# Chapter 10: Established or Speculative? A Checklist for Physics and Math Claims

## Core Idea
In this model popular physics and mathematics mix four very different kinds of statement under one confident voice: **measured facts**, **well-tested theory**, **interpretations and proposals**, and **speculation**. This chapter gives a ladder and a checklist for sorting a claim before repeating it. For general evidence reasoning (base rates, replication, calibration) hand off to `scientific-thinking`.

Fields in the table: **Level**, **What backs it**, **How to phrase it**.

## 10.1 The evidence ladder
| Level | What backs it | Phrase it as | Examples from this skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Measured and repeated | Direct observation, many groups | "has been measured" | Gravitational time dilation; Casimir attraction; the violation of Bell inequalities; mergers detected by gravitational waves; supernova dimming; cosmic expansion |
| 2. Theorem or proof | Logical proof within stated assumptions | "is proved, given X" | Noether's theorem (given a variational formulation); the diagonal argument; undecidability of halting; independence of the continuum hypothesis from the standard axioms; the four colour theorem (computer-assisted) |
| 3. Well-tested theory in its domain | Passes many tests, with known limits | "the best current model, valid for ..." | General relativity; quantum mechanics (formalism); the standard model; Lambda-CDM; statistical mechanics |
| 4. Interpretation or proposal with same predictions | Cannot be told apart by present data | "one reading of the theory" | Copenhagen, pilot wave, many worlds; whether the block universe is real; whether the action is "more fundamental" |
| 5. Hypothesis with partial support | Consistent with some data, contested | "a proposal; here is the main objection" | Evolving dark energy (hints); the firewall argument; timescape; holographic space; fluctuation-type origins |
| 6. Speculation | Internally consistent but no test | "speculative" | String landscape; Planck remnants; baby universes inside black holes; counting versions of yourself |
| 7. Unproven but strongly believed | No proof; community expectation | "believed, not proved" | P is not equal to NP; no observable naked singularities (a cosmic-censorship-type expectation, general knowledge) |

## 10.2 Eight questions before repeating a claim
1. **Which level?** Place it on the ladder; use the matching phrase.
2. **What does it forbid?** A claim that forbids nothing (any outcome fits) explains nothing.
3. **Is it about the formalism or an interpretation?** If no prediction changes, it is an interpretation (ch03).
4. **Which assumption carries the result?** (energy conditions, smoothness, no-hair, independence, closed system).
5. **What did the evidence actually measure?** Distinguish direct measurement from model-dependent inference (ch05.1: acceleration was inferred).
6. **How independent are the probes?** Shared data and calibration inflate confidence (ch01.9).
7. **Is a divergent or idealised quantity being read literally?** (sum of integers equals -1/12; the frictionless dome; an infinite number of monkeys.)
8. **What would change the conclusion?** If nothing, treat the claim as a preference.

## 10.3 Phrases that signal level slippage
| Phrase | Problem | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "Science has proved the universe splits" | Many worlds is an interpretation | "One interpretation says..." |
| "The observer's consciousness collapses the wave" | Detectors do as well; no physical role for mind established | "Measurement means interaction and entanglement" |
| "Dark energy doesn't exist" | One alternative model; other probes disagree | "A proposal reduces the need for it; main probes still favour it" |
| "Black holes destroy information" | The question is open | "Whether information survives is a contested paradox" |
| "Energy is always conserved" | Needs time-translation symmetry | "Conserved where the laws are time-symmetric" |
| "Space is discrete at the Planck scale" | Hypothetical | "Some proposals suggest a smallest length" |
| "P versus NP means computers can't solve these" | Unproven; instances vary | "No fast general algorithm is known" |
| "An incompleteness-type result means some truths are forever unknowable" | Independence is relative to axioms | "Not decidable from these axioms" |

## 10.4 Worked cases
- **"1 + 2 + 3 + ... = -1/12."** Level 2/1 for the regularised statement (measured in the Casimir effect), but false as an ordinary sum. Say: "a divergent sum has a finite regularised part, and that part matters in physics" (ch07.4).
- **"Are we Boltzmann brains?"** A reductio of fluctuation cosmologies, not an observation. Contested resolutions: instability argument, anthropic conditioning (ch06.9).
- **"Is the horizon a firewall?"** Level 5. Competes with other resolutions; physicists divided; the equivalence principle is at stake (ch04.7).
- **"Timescape explains acceleration."** Level 5 and probably wrong per other probes; useful as a reminder to model lumpiness (ch05.3).
- **"Quantum eraser sends information to the past."** No: sorting detections by the partner outcome creates the pattern (ch03.6).
- **"Newtonian mechanics is indeterministic (dome)."** A mathematical fact about non-unique solutions for an idealised force; physical meaning contested (ch09.6).
- **"The universe is a hologram."** A precise duality exists in a model with negative curvature (level 5 for our universe) (ch04.9).

## 10.5 Using this skill in an answer
- State the level in one phrase, then give the mechanism, then the limit.
- Separate "what is measured", "what is theory" and "what is proposed" in three short lines.
- Mark background additions with *general knowledge*.
- Do not give numbers without a date and context (the figures in these chapters are dated snapshots).
- Hand off statistics, replication and calibration questions to `scientific-thinking`.
