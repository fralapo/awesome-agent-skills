# Chapter 03: Quantum Mechanics and Its Interpretations

## Core Idea
In this model quantum mechanics is the best-tested theory known, and **its mathematics is agreed while its meaning is not**. This chapter separates what the formalism and experiments fix (quantisation, superposition, non-commuting observables, violated Bell inequalities) from what the interpretations add (collapse, hidden variables, branching). Compare interpretations by *what extra assumption each adds* and *what it gives up*.

Fields: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**. **(position)** marks interpretive claims.

## 3.1 Quantisation began as a patch: the Planck constant
- **Thesis**: the constant that sets the scale of the quantum world came from a desperate fix for a failed classical prediction.
- **Mechanism**: hot objects glow with a spectrum that looks like a lopsided bell curve. The classical equipartition argument predicted a spectrum that grew without limit at high frequency (the **ultraviolet catastrophe**) because classical energy can be divided infinitely. Allowing only energies in multiples of a minimum (a frequency times a tiny constant) capped what high-frequency modes can hold and reproduced the whole spectrum. The constant was expected to cancel out; it did not. Light itself comes in packets of energy equal to frequency times the constant (photon; photoelectric effect). Value: 6.63 x 10^-34 J s. Because the Planck constant and temperature set the colour, a Sun-like spectrum can be used to measure the constant; if it were 25% smaller the Sun would look violet, all else equal.
- **Trade-off**: the constant appears in essentially every quantum equation (uncertainty, de Broglie wavelength, Schrodinger equation, orbit energies, the Planck length). It acts like a pixel size for reality, but that wording is an image: *the Planck length as "the length below which length loses meaning"* is hypothetical.
- **Does not hold when**: reading the Planck constant as proof that space is discrete; the safe statement is that quantum blurriness limits how well a position can be defined.

## 3.2 Wave-particle duality and quantum tunnelling
- **Thesis**: every object has a matter wave; how well its position is defined depends on its wavelength.
- **Mechanism**: the de Broglie wavelength is the Planck constant divided by momentum. A human has a wavelength orders of magnitude below the Planck length, so is "as right there as possible"; an alpha particle in a nucleus has a probability tail that does not stop at the force barrier, so it can appear outside (**tunnelling**: key to radioactive decay and fusion).
- **Trade-off**: explains decay rates and fusion; the picture of an object being everywhere a little is a statement about probability amplitude, not about spreading material.
- **Does not hold when**: large objects with huge momentum; there the effect is unmeasurably small.

## 3.3 Observables-first mechanics versus wave mechanics
- **Thesis**: two historically different formulations (matrix mechanics built from measured quantities; wave mechanics built from matter waves) turned out equivalent, and a third (path integral, ch01) too.
- **Mechanism**: matrix mechanics works with a table of transition quantities; non-commuting multiplication leads to the **uncertainty principle** and to the Copenhagen stance that the world between measurements is not just unknown but undefined. The wave equation was obtained by replacing the classical action in the wave form of classical mechanics with the wave function.
- **Trade-off**: the second formulation restores a realist-looking picture; the first stays closest to the data.
- **Does not hold when**: used to claim one formulation is the "true" one; they predict the same numbers.

## 3.4 Collapse, the measurement problem and decoherence
- **Thesis**: the central open question is how a superposition becomes one definite result.
- **Mechanism**: the wave function evolves smoothly by the Schrodinger equation; measurement yields one outcome. Decoherence (entanglement with environment) explains why macroscopic superpositions are not seen in practice, but not, by itself, why one outcome is the one observed. Which observation counts as measurement is not special: a detector or a houseplant does as well as a physicist.
- **Trade-off**: all interpretations reproduce the same laboratory predictions, so choice is by economy of assumptions and by what each costs.
- **Does not hold when**: appeals to consciousness as a physical cause; there is no reason to believe a human observer collapses more than any detector.

## 3.5 Comparing the interpretations: what each adds, what each gives up
| Interpretation | Adds | Gives up | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen | Collapse at measurement; wave function is possibility, not a physical thing | Description of what happens between measurements; sharp definition of "measurement" | Mainstream (orthodox) |
| De Broglie-Bohm pilot wave | Definite particle positions guided by the wave ("hidden variables") | Locality: the guidance must act instantly at any distance | Complete and self-consistent but a fringe view **(position)** |
| Many worlds | Nothing beyond the Schrodinger equation | A single observed world: all outcomes persist in branches | Serious minority; cannot be tested by visiting branches |
- **Thesis (position)**: many worlds is the most economical in assumptions even though the least economical in worlds; critics note extravagance. Copenhagen also contains many "alternative realities" (the superposition of paths) but merges them into one timeline at collapse. Pilot wave may be many worlds in disguise (the other branches become empty ghost branches); this possibility is raised without being settled (position).
- **Trade-off**: realism and locality cannot both be kept (3.7); each interpretation picks what to drop.
- **Does not hold when**: someone claims an experiment already rules out the others; none does.

## 3.6 Delayed-choice and eraser experiments do not need backwards causation
- **Thesis**: erasing which-path information appears to restore interference "after the fact", but the pattern only appears when the detections are sorted using the partner photon.
- **Mechanism**: entangled twin photons; a beam splitter scrambles which-slit information for some partners. The interference shows up only in the subset selected by the partner's detector; overall the screen shows no pattern. A signal to the past (a "lottery cheating machine") is not achievable by this.
- **Trade-off**: shows that "observation" is entanglement and information, not mind.
- **Does not hold when**: the description is simplified to "the future changes the past"; that conclusion is unsupported.

## 3.7 Bell inequalities: local hidden variables are out
- **Thesis**: experiments show that nature violates the inequalities that any local, pre-assigned-value (realist) theory must obey.
- **Mechanism**: entangled pairs have correlated outcomes. If each particle carried its answers from creation, correlation could not depend on the later *choice of measurement axis*; observed correlations do. Experiments on entangled photons, repeated over many kilometres, violate the inequalities. So at least one of locality and realism must be dropped.
- **Trade-off**: no pure "classical" story survives; the choices are non-local hidden variables, non-realist (Copenhagen-style) readings, or many worlds.
- **Does not hold when**: loopholes are claimed to remain; many experiments verify the result at larger distances.

## 3.8 Does many worlds explain the Born rule? (position)
- **Thesis (position)**: the claim that probability equals the square of the amplitude can be derived rather than assumed in many worlds, which would count in its favour.
- **Mechanism**: put the observer inside the wave function; after the coin interaction the observer, device and environment all branch. Before looking, the observer's credence is split by the **principle of indifference**: for equal-weight branches it is 1 over the number of branches (so the square of the amplitude). For unequal amplitudes, split the larger branch into several equal orthogonal sub-branches (like splitting a stack of cards so that stacks are swappable), count equal branches, and the proportion reproduces the squared amplitude without assuming it.
- **Trade-off**: gives a reason for a rule otherwise treated as an axiom; relies on indifference, on counting branches and on treating splits as legitimate, all of which critics contest.
- **Does not hold when**: you do not accept that indifference applies to branches, or that "which branch am I in" is meaningful.

## 3.9 Entropy from entanglement (link to ch06)
- **Thesis**: a pure quantum state has zero entropy; a part of an entangled whole has positive entropy.
- **Mechanism**: a superposed quantum coin with known wave function has zero von Neumann entropy: no hidden information, unlike a classical coin that is already heads or tails unseen. For two entangled coins the pair has zero entropy but each alone has positive entropy because the information sits in the correlation. Classical Shannon entropy is a special case of the quantum quantity.
- **Trade-off**: ties the second law to entanglement, a candidate for its deeper origin **(position)**.
- **Does not hold when**: asked to give a testable prediction beyond known entanglement dynamics.

## Quick questions for any quantum claim
1. Is the statement about the formalism (agreed) or an interpretation (not)?
2. What does that interpretation give up (locality, realism, a single world)?
3. Is a human mind invoked as a cause? If yes, treat as unsupported.
4. Does the claim change a prediction? If not, it is an interpretation.
