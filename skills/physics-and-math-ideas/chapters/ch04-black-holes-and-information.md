# Chapter 04: Black Holes, Singularities and Information

## Core Idea
In this model black holes are the sharpest meeting point of general relativity (ch02), quantum theory (ch03) and thermodynamics (ch06). What is **well established**: horizons, the black-hole entropy formula, gravitational-wave observations of mergers. What is **contested**: what happens to information, whether firewalls exist, whether space is holographic.

Fields: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**. **(position)** marks contested claims.

## 4.1 Singularities are unavoidable in general relativity - and signal its limit
- **Thesis**: once matter is compacted enough to form a trapped surface, a singularity forms however messy the matter; the same reasoning applied backward points to a beginning of the expanding universe.
- **Mechanism**: the early collapse solutions needed perfect spherical smoothness, so people doubted they applied to real stars. The singularity theorem removed that assumption. A **trapped surface** is a closed surface from which even outward-pointing light moves inward. Light rays (null geodesics) are focused by normal matter (the weak energy condition). The theorem shows that rays leaving a trapped surface must converge to a focus and cannot be continued as geodesics beyond it, otherwise a shorter path would exist than the "shortest" one, a contradiction. This is **geodesic incompleteness**: in a non-rotating black hole time ends at a point; in a rotating one space ends at a ring. Applied backward through an expanding universe, the geodesics meet, so time cannot be traced beyond the big bang (combined result: the Penrose-Hawking theorems).
- **Trade-off**: an unavoidable result inside pure general relativity, but it relies on assumptions (energy conditions) and on general relativity itself.
- **Does not hold when**: quantum gravity matters. Infinities should make us suspicious; the theorem is most useful as a **statement of where general relativity must fail**, not as proof that infinite density exists.

## 4.2 Black-hole types and the no-hair idea
- **Thesis**: an astrophysical black hole is described by mass, spin and (usually negligible) charge; everything else about what fell in is lost to the outside.
- **Mechanism**: a non-rotating, uncharged hole has only inward-pulling gravity, so a horizon is inevitable. Spin or charge adds a counter-effect that creates an inner horizon, bounded regions of normal-looking space, and a ring (spin) or point (charge). Adding spin or charge until inner and outer horizons merge gives an **extremal** black hole; going past that would give a **naked singularity**. Rotating black holes can donate rotational energy to passing particles or waves in the ergosphere (Penrose process; superradiance when the particle is light). Charged holes can approach extremality by evaporating mass while keeping charge; spinning holes also lose angular momentum, so they cannot.
- **Trade-off**: a very small parameter set makes tests sharp.
- **Does not hold when**: charge is zero in practice (astrophysical holes), so extremality by charge is theoretical; naked singularities are expected to be avoided by some protecting mechanism, which is not proven here.

## 4.3 Gravitational-wave ringdown as a test
- **Thesis**: the "ringing" after a merger encodes mass and spin, and tests whether the final object is a no-hair black hole.
- **Mechanism**: the ringdown is a sum of harmonics (fundamental plus overtones), analogous to identifying an instrument by its overtones. Simulations showed overtones are strong right from the merger and are not a chaotic mess, so overtones from the real merger signal could be used. This "gravitational-wave spectroscopy" gave mass and spin consistent with the full-signal estimate, and oscillation frequencies and decay times consistent with a hole defined only by mass and spin.
- **Trade-off**: precise measurements of the final object.
- **Does not hold when**: significance is low. The overtone detection in a real event was reported at about 3.6 sigma, called "likely" by its authors; the no-hair support is **tentative** and far from confirmation (position, dated).

## 4.4 Entropy proportional to area
- **Thesis**: black-hole entropy scales with horizon area, not volume.
- **Mechanism**: a black hole hides almost all information about what fell in, so its entropy is huge: about one bit per four Planck-length squares of horizon area. The surprise is the area scaling (ordinary systems scale with volume). A black hole is the maximum information density for a volume (**Bekenstein bound**), which, if extended to everything, means all information in a region fits on its boundary.
- **Trade-off**: connects gravity, thermodynamics and information.
- **Does not hold when**: asked what the entropy counts microscopically; that is exactly what remains unexplained.

## 4.5 Hawking radiation: two descriptions and a lab analogue
- **Thesis**: black holes radiate and lose mass; the common "virtual particle pair" story is a picture, not the mechanism.
- **Mechanism**: popular version: a pair appears at the horizon, one falls in with negative energy, the other escapes. More precise: the horizon scatters the vibrational modes of quantum fields whose wavelength is similar to the hole's size; perturbed modes look like escaping particles to a distant observer. For very massive holes the radiation is cold and mostly photons. Analogue systems (water flowing over a sloped obstacle faster than surface waves, or a "bathtub vortex") have event horizons for ripples; Hawking-like behaviour of the ripples was observed, including the expected sapping of the analogue field (back-reaction). The vortex also has an ergosphere region.
- **Trade-off**: analogues test the mathematics of horizons in the lab.
- **Does not hold when**: used as proof that astrophysical black holes radiate; analogue ripples are analogous, not the same. Exactly how back-reaction works is contested.

## 4.6 The information paradox as a trade-off between two principles
- **Thesis**: black holes seem to force a choice between **unitarity** (quantum information is conserved) and the **equivalence principle** (nothing special happens at a large horizon).
- **Mechanism**: if infalling information is swallowed *and* returned in Hawking radiation, the same quantum bit exists in two places, which quantum theory forbids (no-cloning). If infalling information never enters, equivalence is violated. Entanglement view: the horizon entropy rises as the hole grows and then falls once Hawking particles become mostly entangled with other Hawking particles. The turning point is the **Page time**: it occurs when about half of the entropy has been radiated, which corresponds to roughly 29% of the initial mass lost and, in time, roughly 54-65% of the total evaporation time (approximate, *general knowledge*). After it, information can in principle be decoded.
- **Trade-off**: black-hole **complementarity** hedged by saying no single observer can see both copies.
- **Does not hold when**: the hedge is challenged, as in 4.7.

## 4.7 Firewalls: when complementarity fails (position)
- **Thesis (position)**: an argument shows that to keep unitarity one may need a wall of extreme energy at the horizon, which breaks the equivalence principle.
- **Mechanism**: a bit at the horizon late in the evaporation must be entangled with Hawking radiation (to carry information out) and with a partner just inside (to be a normal vacuum mode). Entanglement is **monogamous**: a maximally entangled pair cannot also be entangled with a third system. Breaking the inner link costs a huge amount of energy, which would form a screen so intense that anything falling in is thermalised. Its light would be redshifted to look like faint Hawking radiation to distant observers. An observer who dips near the horizon and comes back out could test both entanglements, defeating complementarity's loophole. So either firewalls exist, or monogamy is violated (with unitarity), or equivalence fails differently.
- **Trade-off**: keeps quantum mechanics, abandons the "smooth horizon" idea.
- **Does not hold when**: you accept other resolutions. Physicists remain **divided**; many strongly dislike firewalls. The proposal's value is showing that the paradox is not fully solved by complementarity.

## 4.8 Speculative resolutions to treat as speculation
- **Planck remnants** (position): evaporation might stop at a tiny relic that stores information; this appears to violate the Bekenstein bound unless the interior space is larger than the horizon suggests (one idea: a new inflationary region per black hole). Primordial relics have also been proposed as dark matter. All of this is **speculative**; none is required by data.
- **Trade-off**: possible escape routes; each adds assumptions.
- **Does not hold when**: used as established.

## 4.9 Holography: space as emergent (position)
- **Thesis (position)**: space may emerge from a lower-dimensional boundary theory.
- **Mechanism**: from entropy-by-area to the holographic principle: the volume (**bulk**) is encoded on its boundary. The AdS/CFT correspondence is the one worked-out case: a boundary **conformal field theory** (laws that do not change with scale) with a scale-like extra degree of freedom produces an extra radial dimension; nested coarse-grained patterns map to nested spheres. The real emergence may come from entanglement across scales rather than scale invariance alone. If the two descriptions are a true **duality**, neither is more fundamental ("ontological democracy"); if approximate, one side emerges from the other (here the bulk, since the boundary is better defined).
- **Trade-off**: a precise calculational tool.
- **Does not hold when**: applied directly to our universe. The example needs a negatively curved universe (negative cosmological constant); ours seems to have a positive one.

## Checklist for black-hole claims
1. Is the claim geometry (horizon, trapped surface) or quantum (radiation, entropy, information)?
2. Which principle is being given up: unitarity, equivalence, locality?
3. Is the evidence observational (waves) or purely argumentative?
4. Does it rely on assumptions (energy conditions, no-hair) that the claim itself might break?
