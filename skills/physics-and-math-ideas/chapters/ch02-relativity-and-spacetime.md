# Chapter 02: Relativity and Spacetime

## Core Idea
In this model relativity replaces "things moving through space over time" with **tenseless geometry in four-dimensional spacetime**, and treats gravity as the curvature of that geometry. The shift is about which statements are objective: clocks, rulers and eyes can mislead, geometric facts cannot.

Fields per concept: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**. *General knowledge* and **(position)** are marked.

## 2.1 Translate dynamics into geometry before comparing theories
- **Thesis**: to judge whether two descriptions of motion disagree, restate both as static facts about world lines in spacetime.
- **Mechanism**: an object's whole history is a curve (world line) in a four-dimensional diagram. Statements such as "accelerates", "is pulled" and "is at rest" become statements about whether the curve is straight (a geodesic) or bent relative to the local geometry. Diagrams of spacetime are not meant to be visualised literally; they are designed to reduce reliance on the eye.
- **Trade-off**: objectivity gained, intuition lost.
- **Does not hold when**: you only need a quick estimate in a weak field; then the dynamical, force-based language is fine as a crutch (see 2.7).

## 2.2 Inertial frames are local, not global
- **Thesis**: in curved spacetime a frame (axes plus clocks) that behaves as inertial is valid only in a small patch; a global inertial frame does not exist.
- **Mechanism**: analogy of a surface ant. A tiny patch of a sphere looks flat and has Euclidean grids, but a large rectangular grid cannot be laid on a sphere without distortion. An ant that extends its grid too far wrongly concludes that another great circle is "bent". In the same way an observer in deep space who extends his inertial frame to the falling apple wrongly says the apple accelerates. Inertial *observers* (no force on them, world line a geodesic) still exist everywhere, and their axes and clocks are valid only locally and must be reset in each successive patch.
- **Trade-off**: dissolves the apparent contradiction that "both frames are inertial", at the price of dropping a single universal frame.
- **Does not hold when**: spacetime curvature is negligible over the region of interest; then one inertial frame works well.

## 2.3 Free fall is straight; standing still on the ground is accelerating
- **Thesis**: in this model a falling apple has no force on it (its world line is a geodesic), while a point on Earth's surface has a net force on it and really accelerates.
- **Mechanism**: two apples released side by side in a falling box approach each other because they start on initially parallel geodesics that cross in curved spacetime (Newtonian language: they fall radially). Comparing different parts of Earth would require one frame spanning several patches, so conclusions drawn that way (such as "the surface must be expanding") need a heavy grain of salt.
- **Trade-off**: gravity "disappears" as a force, but the geometry must carry its effects.
- **Does not hold when**: the equivalence principle fails (it has not been seen to, but black-hole firewalls would require its violation, ch04).

## 2.4 Gravitational time dilation proves curvature
- **Thesis**: the mere existence of gravitational time dilation, however tiny, is incompatible with flat spacetime.
- **Mechanism**: send two light pulses, 5 s apart, from the ground floor to the roof. On a flat diagram the two photon world lines are parallel and congruent whatever gravity does to light. If clocks at both heights ran at the same rate, the vertical lines at the ends would also be parallel and congruent, forming a parallelogram. In reality the pulses arrive on the roof slightly more than 5 s apart (the excess is below a nanosecond). Unequal clock rates make opposite sides non-congruent, impossible in flat geometry. So spacetime is curved, and a flat-plus-force picture is excluded.
- **Trade-off**: a clean, assumption-light argument; it shows curvature exists, not what causes its exact size.
- **Does not hold when**: the effect is attributed to something other than gravity (mis-calibrated clocks); the argument assumes the measurement is clean.

## 2.5 Near Earth, curvature is mostly in time
- **Thesis**: most everyday gravity effects come from curvature of *time*; the three-dimensional space around Earth is almost exactly Euclidean.
- **Mechanism**: a frame has axes and clocks, and near Earth curvature shows up in clocks far more than in rulers. Freefall satellite orbits look circular in space only because the frame covers too big a spacetime patch. Rubber-sheet pictures suggest spatial curvature only and are therefore somewhat misleading.
- **Trade-off**: improves intuition for what "bending" means; at stronger fields space curvature matters too (*general knowledge*).
- **Does not hold when**: near compact objects, where spatial curvature is comparable to time curvature.

## 2.6 Energy tells spacetime how to curve
- **Thesis**: the distribution of energy at a set of events determines curvature and thus geodesics, via the Einstein equations.
- **Mechanism**: input the energy distribution of a star into the equations and the output is a map of geodesics around it; translated back into space and time that map contains planetary orbits and radially inward trajectories. Applied to the orbit of Mercury, the action formulation in proper time (ch01) gives the right precession, with no force needed.
- **Trade-off**: elegant and extremely well tested; full solutions are very hard, so cosmology assumes smoothness (ch05).
- **Does not hold when**: quantum effects become dominant (very small scales, the earliest moments, inside singularities, ch04).

## 2.7 Why people still say "gravity"
- **Thesis (position)**: "gravity" is a convenient word, not a claim that a force exists.
- **Mechanism**: few humans can visualise four-dimensional spacetime, and Newtonian language is easier and usually accurate enough; people remind themselves it is a crutch to use with caution.
- **Trade-off**: speed and communication versus occasional misleading intuition (e.g. the rubber sheet).
- **Does not hold when**: precision matters (clock synchronisation, strong fields).

## 2.8 Determinism and the block picture
- **Thesis**: general relativity, like Newtonian mechanics, is deterministic: matter and energy fix the geometry, which fixes their motion into past and future. Relative simultaneity removes an absolute "now".
- **Mechanism**: if no moment is privileged, past and future are as real as the present "at least in some interpretations", and time becomes an eternal block. Where you are, how fast you move and the light-limited horizon decide what is measured and known, but not whether the world is deterministic.
- **Trade-off**: simple and complete; clashes with the felt flow of time and with quantum randomness (ch03).
- **Does not hold when**: the block view is read as proof (it is an **interpretation (position)**; the physics only says simultaneity is relative).

## 2.9 Time flows differently in different places - and what that can and cannot explain
- **Thesis**: gravitational time dilation is established; claiming it can mimic cosmic acceleration is a proposal (ch05).
- **Mechanism**: clocks run slower in strong gravity and faster in empty regions, so identical regions of the universe can differ in elapsed time by a large factor. The question is whether this alters the average expansion rate.
- **Trade-off**: pushes standard general relativity to its limit without adding new ingredients.
- **Does not hold when**: independent probes (baryon oscillations, microwave background) conflict with the proposal.

## Checks before using this chapter
- Is the claim stated as a geometric fact or as a force story? Convert it.
- Does it quietly extend a local frame beyond its patch?
- Is the weak-field Newtonian answer being used where accuracy needs curved time?
