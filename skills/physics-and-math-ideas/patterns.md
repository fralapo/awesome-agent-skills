# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets.

## 1. A conservation law seems broken
**When**: energy or momentum "disappears" (redshift, dark energy, a hilly road).
**Move**: ask which symmetry is missing (time translation, space translation); restate the law as conditional on that symmetry. [ch01]
**Cost**: the statement gets longer and less catchy.
**Not when**: the symmetry clearly holds; then look for a missed term.

## 2. Two pictures are both self-consistent
**When**: flat spacetime with a force versus curved spacetime; two interpretations.
**Move**: look for a measurement that separates them (gravitational time dilation); if none exists, call it an interpretation. [ch01, ch02, ch03]
**Cost**: may leave the question open.
**Not when**: predictions differ and data already decide.

## 3. "Observation creates reality"
**When**: a quantum claim invokes a conscious observer.
**Move**: restate observation as interaction and entanglement with any detector; list what the interpretation gives up. [ch03]
**Cost**: removes a dramatic story.
**Not when**: the user wants an interpretation-level discussion; label it as such.

## 4. A black-hole puzzle
**When**: information loss, firewalls, "what happens at the horizon".
**Move**: name the colliding principles; check which one each proposal drops; note monogamy of entanglement and the Page time. [ch04]
**Cost**: no single answer.
**Not when**: the question is only about observable mergers.

## 5. A cosmology result "confirms" or "refutes" dark energy
**When**: new survey or alternative model.
**Move**: ask which probes, their degeneracies and covariance, and whether other probes agree. [ch01.9, ch05]
**Cost**: slower verdicts.
**Not when**: a single robust, independent probe disagrees decisively.

## 6. A constant looks arranged
**When**: small Higgs mass or cosmological constant.
**Move**: state the prior explicitly; then offer the two options (hidden link between UV and IR, or many universes). [ch05.5-5.6]
**Cost**: both options are speculative.
**Not when**: a symmetry that explains the value is known.

## 7. Entropy or the arrow of time is invoked
**When**: "why does time flow", "perpetual motion", a demon.
**Move**: identify the entropy lens; check that the system is closed; place the explanation in dynamics or in boundary condition; look for erased memory. [ch06]
**Cost**: the answer references an unexplained special past.
**Not when**: only a local, open system is discussed.

## 8. A divergent sum or infinity shows up in a calculation
**When**: "1 + 2 + 3 = -1/12", vacuum energy, point sources.
**Move**: look for a cutoff or continuation and compare differences; call the value regularised. [ch07.4, ch05.5]
**Cost**: less shocking headline.
**Not when**: the series converges normally.

## 9. A "fundamental" limit is cited
**When**: undecidable, unprovable, "can't be computed", "NP-hard".
**Move**: classify the limit: independent of axioms, uncomputable, or unsolved; add the exact scope (all programs? all inputs? given which axioms?). [ch08]
**Cost**: more qualifiers.
**Not when**: a restricted class avoids the limit; say so.

## 10. A paradox needs resolving
**When**: two valid-looking arguments disagree.
**Move**: state both, list assumptions, find the clash, classify, name the field it created, and say what it does not show. [ch09]
**Cost**: some paradoxes remain open.
**Not when**: it is pure wordplay.

## 11. A proof is done by computer
**When**: exhaustive case checking.
**Move**: say it is valid if checked and trusted; separate "true" from "understood"; name the checking tools. [ch08.9]
**Cost**: the proof gives no insight.
**Not when**: a short human proof exists.

## 12. A claim is repeated with confidence
**When**: popular-science statement, headline, talk.
**Move**: place it on the evidence ladder; use the matching phrase; list the main objection. [ch10]
**Cost**: less punchy text.
**Not when**: a primary source with data is at hand; then follow `scientific-thinking`.
