# Pricing and Sales Negotiation: Cheatsheet

Numbers are illustrative rules of thumb in generic currency; test against the user's market.

## Which Price Model?
| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Duration unknown, support work | Hourly with cap (last resort) |
| Defined days for a studio | Day rate; never under one day |
| Definable deliverables | Output fee: fixed quantities, capped rounds, change orders |
| Repeating scope | Named package, three tiers |
| Measurable client result and a stakeholder | Value-based fee (fraction of gain) |
| Undefined scope | Paid discovery, then range, then fixed bid |

## Bid Build-Up (floor)
roles x days (incl. producer, art direction) → markup for underestimation (~50% example) → overhead and tools → subtotal → contingency (~15% for undefined scope) → profit 10-30% (20% typical) → ONE number to client. Never raw day rates; never show hours.

## Value Math
Gain per period x periods = annual gain → fee about 8-10% of it (about 10% of gross value; up to about 20% if uncertain; one school of thought). Client size check: revenue about 200k per employee; marketing about 10% of revenue. Price larger and riskier clients higher. Each intermediary about 2x markup.

## Money Conversation Order
1. Budget (after a little trust) → 2. timeline → 3. scope. Want all three and can only meet two = weak fit, refer. Use "money you have set aside", not "budget". Magnitude question: "50k or 500k?"

## Anchor and Present
First number yours, high, round. Bracket: higher number first, pause. Three options: premium → core (target, middle) → entry. Say it once, hold it. No reaction to the top = too low.

## Minimum Engagement Fee
Annual target ÷ number of clients served at once (8-15, example 10) = minimum new-client fee (about a tenth of target).

## Payment Terms (common)
50% upfront / 25% at about three quarters / 25% on delivery (net 30). Simpler: 50/50. Deposit before work. Instalments for cash-flow-limited buyers. Small creative jobs: 25%/50%/full deposit, non-refundable once started, final and source files only after payment, hourly rate beyond deadline or scope.

## Objection Moves
| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| Too expensive | "Compared to what?" Ask the result's worth; cost of the problem; options; hold |
| Lower the price | No discount; pre-designed leaner option, or bonus, or one conditional concession |
| Cheaper elsewhere | Agree; ask criteria; risk, reliability; let them go |
| Budget is X (far below) | Effort-vs-result test; scope realign, discovery phase, or refer |
| Not enough experience | Embrace, pivot to focus and access |
| Need free work / exposure | Charity vs client; value-equivalence; measurable and contracted |
| Send me the packet | Confirm price, scope, deliverables first |

## Concession Rules
Decide final number first. Concede once, explicitly, conditionally ("this time only"). Never twice. Never silently cut scope. Give bonuses, not discounts.

## Scope Control
Objective "done"; fixed quantities; capped rounds (typically 3); changes get a separate estimate; approvals documented; early warning on overrun; timeline clause.

## Engagement Shapes
Starter project before a retainer (cold, high-ticket prospect) · intensive (~3 months, fixed) → light (15-20 h/week or 20-40 h/month, capped) → intensive · light async retainer with explicit monthly allowance. See ch11.

## Raise Rates
Three yeses → raise (~2x step). New clients first. Refusals → raise value, not cut price. Track average project value and close rate. Restrict supply; do not be instantly available.

## Positioning Tests
Room (about 10 to 200 claim the same), distance (most clients far away), win-both (advantage + premium). Specialise on what and for whom.

## Red Flags
Price first and no buying intent in 30-60 days · not the decider · commodity mindset · wants all three of budget/timeline/scope · habitual discounting · free work as favour · refuses discovery fee · values clash.

## Hand-offs
Time tracking, CRM, invoices → `freelance-designer-ops` · delivery and persuasion craft → `public-speaking-persuasion` · segmentation, pricing strategy, BATNA → `marketing-mba` · concept → `creative-director` · contracts, tax → professionals.
