# Patterns

Recurring situation → move. Each entry: When / Move / Trade-offs. Chapter in brackets.

## "I need a new logo / site / deck" (first request is a prescription)
**When**: client arrives with a self-prescribed solution and asks for a price.
**Move**: acknowledge, do not quote. Ask who the audience is, what is driving the decision now, what was tried, budget, who else decides. Name the problem type (communication, customer experience, business). Offer a paid diagnosis if needed. [ch05]
**Trade-offs**: slower; a few clients leave. Price becomes defensible.

## "What is your budget?" is the first thing they ask or you ask
**When**: budget unknown, trust low.
**Move**: bracket your range (high first, pause), ask which end feels comfortable; use "money set aside"; ask a magnitude question. Offer a referral if far off. [ch04, ch05]
**Trade-offs**: needs a stated range you can defend; wrong range anchors you.

## "Too expensive"
**When**: price objection.
**Move**: breathe, confirm mutual interest, ask "compared to what" and what the result is worth, quantify the cost of the problem, offer a pre-designed leaner option or bonus, concede at most once conditionally, accept no gracefully. [ch08]
**Trade-offs**: may lose the job; keeps price integrity and good clients.

## "Budget is a fraction of the scope"
**When**: scope implies far more than the money offered.
**Move**: effort-vs-result test; where does this rank against their other spend; realign scope, propose a paid discovery to define scope and budget together, or refer. Never take uncapped revenue share without runway. [ch08, ch07]
**Trade-offs**: more calls; protects margin.

## "Can you do it hourly?" / itemised estimate requests
**When**: client wants line items.
**Move**: price the result as one package; breakdown internal; extras as one lump sum. Offer tiers instead of line cuts. [ch02, ch04]
**Trade-offs**: opaque to some procurement; prepare a scope document.

## Scope is unclear but they want a number now
**When**: undefined scope, pressure for a bid.
**Move**: give a range labelled with assumptions ("not a firm commitment"), say firm needs discovery; sell paid discovery; state contingency openly. [ch03, ch07]
**Trade-offs**: some prospects balk; those who accept are better.

## Everything says yes
**When**: close rate above most.
**Move**: rule of three: raise about 2x on the next new client; if refusals follow, build value before cutting. [ch12]
**Trade-offs**: temporary drop in volume; higher profit.

## Client keeps changing things after approval
**When**: scope creep, post-approval changes.
**Move**: point to objective "done" and approvals; give a separate estimate; raise overrun early as a decision. [ch09]
**Trade-offs**: friction now, fewer disputes later.

## Prospect goes quiet after the proposal
**When**: silence.
**Move**: retreat and follow: no chasing; scheduled low-key follow-ups; ask what holds the decision; accept that no follow means no opportunity. [ch06]
**Trade-offs**: patience; protects position.

## Large, risky commitment and a nervous buyer
**When**: big fee, past bad experience.
**Move**: restate the ask, empathise, offer phased start, checkable proof, instalments, conditional milestone guarantee; ask what would put their mind at ease. [ch06, ch11]
**Trade-offs**: more structure for you.

## Price shopper
**When**: lowest price decides.
**Move**: identify fast; refer out (possibly for a 10-20% fee); do not match; let go. [ch05, ch12]
**Trade-offs**: lost volume; saves capacity.

## Free work, "exposure", favour
**When**: request for unpaid work.
**Move**: triage charity vs client; value-equivalence; require measurable exposure in contract; ask for percentage/royalty only with cap. [ch08]
**Trade-offs**: may offend; avoids a precedent.

## Competitive pitch against a bigger firm
**When**: multi-bid.
**Move**: say the client's doubt first; reframe with a strength (access, focus, named team); do not compete on size or price; do not attack the rival. [ch07, ch08]
**Trade-offs**: needs honesty about size.

## Fixed-fee project with creative clients
**When**: clients treat change as "part of the process".
**Move**: objective done, capped rounds, change-order language, documented approvals, price headroom. [ch02, ch09]
**Trade-offs**: heavier paperwork.

## Hesitant close
**When**: client agrees but will not sign.
**Move**: summarise their conditions, ask "anything else that would give you pause?", conditional close, add a time-bound guarantee, instalments. [ch06, ch11]
**Trade-offs**: guarantee carries risk; keep it bounded.

## Commodity trap
**When**: competing only on price.
**Move**: add time-to-result or certainty; narrow the niche; run the room test; package the difference; consider ERRC. [ch10, ch11]
**Trade-offs**: narrower audience.
