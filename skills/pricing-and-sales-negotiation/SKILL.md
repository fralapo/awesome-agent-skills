# Pricing and Sales Negotiation
**Chapters**: 12 | **Corpus**: ~100 theory notes on pricing, bidding, discovery, negotiation and positioning for creative freelancers and small studios | **Generated**: 2026-11-06

## Role and Boundaries

This skill **decides the money and conversation side of selling creative work**: what to charge, how to build and present the number, how to qualify and question a prospect, how to answer objections, how to hold or trade a price, and which clients to accept, refer or decline. It proposes numbers, scripts and structures; the user owns the final decision and the relationship.

|---|---|

| Discovery questions, qualification, call structure, closing, proposal structure | Delivery craft: voice, stage presence, storytelling, general persuasion psychology, objection psychology → `public-speaking-persuasion` (see ch10 objection handling, ch12 sales pitching, ch13 calls to action) |

| Red flags and when to walk away | Contracts, tax, legal terms, VAT regimes → an accountant or lawyer (this skill is not legal or tax advice) |

**Stay in lane.** Do not rewrite the user's creative concept to justify a price, and do not invent market rates. Numbers in the chapters are illustrative ranges and rules of thumb from the source material, in generic currency units; mark them as such, ask the user for their market and costs, and test before committing. Where teachers disagree, the chapters present both views and say when each fits.

## The Core Principles (recurrence order)

1. **Diagnose before you prescribe.** Questions first; the first request is a symptom, not the brief. The one who asks the most questions is trusted most.
2. **Price the outcome and the client, not hours or deliverables.** The same job is worth more to a larger, riskier, more urgent client. Single number; breakdown stays internal.
3. **Never discount to close a gap.** Hold the price; change the scope or options, add a bonus, or refer. At most one explicit, conditional concession.
4. **Talk money early.** Test budget, timeline and scope in that order; learning there is no fit costs minutes, not weeks.
5. **Anchor first, high, in round numbers; show ranges and three options.** The buyer compares your options with each other, not with outside vendors.
6. **Do not justify; embrace the objection and pivot** to value, scope or fit. Be willing to walk away.
7. **Clients buy the least risky option.** Proof, process and risk-reversal justify a higher price more than craft claims do.
8. **Specialise.** Narrow enough for few competitors, wide enough for prospects; win at a premium, not on price.
9. **Raise rates in steps, on new clients, after yeses.** Charge enough to absorb small changes.
10. **Protect scope in writing.** Objective "done", capped rounds, change orders, documented approvals.

## Scope & Limits

Practical theory for selling creative services, distilled from many practitioners' teaching. Numbers (fractions of value, markups, ranges, payment splits) are single- or few-source rules of thumb and vary by country, industry and client; treat them as starting points to test. The skill gives no legal, tax or contract advice and no guarantee of results; say so when a user asks about contract wording, VAT, withholding or enforcement.
