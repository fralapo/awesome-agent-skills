# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets. Entries marked (position) are practitioner judgment, not consensus. Educational, not medical or coaching advice.

## 1. "A study says X wins, so everyone should do X"
**When**: a headline compares two methods.
**Move**: ask population, duration, what was equated, effect size, and whether individuals differed; treat the result as a default, then test on yourself. [ch01, ch12]
**Cost**: slower than adopting the headline.
**Not when**: the change is trivially cheap and reversible.

## 2. Progress has stopped but volume is moderate
**When**: no gain in several weeks while sets feel comfortable.
**Move**: check set quality (close to failure, good technique), logs, food and sleep; if recovered, add a few weekly sets to the lagging muscle. [ch03, ch09]
**Cost**: more time and fatigue.
**Not when**: you are already run down; then reduce or deload first.

## 3. Progress has stopped and the lifter is fatigued
**When**: performance down, joints achy, sleep poor.
**Move**: look at leading indicators, cut volume and effort for a week or two, fix sleep and food; return and re-measure. [ch08, ch09]
**Cost**: a lighter week.
**Not when**: only one bad session; wait for several signals.

## 4. How many sets, how often
**When**: designing a split.
**Move**: set weekly hard sets per muscle (about 10-20), split into sessions of about 4-8 sets, aim for two or more sessions per muscle; choose the frequency at which you recover between sessions. [ch03, ch04]
**Cost**: more sessions or longer ones.
**Not when**: a beginner, for whom less volume works.

## 5. Failure or reserve?
**When**: deciding how hard to take a set.
**Move**: stop most sets at 0-3 reps in reserve; use failure on isolation and machine work, sparingly; more reserve on heavy compounds (position). [ch05]
**Cost**: some stimulus versus recovery trade.
**Not when**: a beginner who misjudges reserve; use a clear rep target and stop at form breakdown.

## 6. "Only effective reps count"
**When**: someone discounts sets far from failure.
**Move**: accept the soft version (be fairly close), question the precise rule (last five reps equal), keep a record. [ch05]
**Cost**: less tidy numbers.
**Not when**: strength practice, where far-from-failure sets still train skill.

## 7. Time-poor trainee
**When**: sessions must be short.
**Move**: compound-first, full-body two to three times weekly, shorter rests with extra sets if needed. [ch04, ch07]
**Cost**: some volume per muscle.
**Not when**: heavy strength work, where long rests matter.

## 8. Adding weight, the beginner way
**When**: new lifter.
**Move**: easy / medium / hard, two-for-two or small fixed jumps, log every session, expect a slowdown after a few months. [ch07, ch11]
**Cost**: simple but rigid.
**Not when**: an advanced lifter; use autoregulation or weekly increments.

## 9. Is the plateau real?
**When**: "I am stuck".
**Move**: compare like with like over three to four weeks against a recent anchor; use block-start and end calibration sets. [ch07]
**Cost**: patience.
**Not when**: life load is obviously high; fix that first.

## 10. Peaking for a max
**When**: a competition or test date.
**Move**: raise specificity as the date approaches, then taper (halve volume, keep intensity). [ch06, ch08]
**Cost**: less general training.
**Not when**: no fixed date.

## 11. Deload timing
**When**: unsure whether to schedule.
**Move**: schedule every 4-8 weeks if you cannot monitor closely; otherwise deload on signs; make it genuinely easy. [ch08]
**Cost**: lost sessions versus risk.
**Not when**: beginners with little fatigue.

## 12. "My CNS is fried"
**When**: someone blames the nervous system for fatigue.
**Move**: ask what they feel; check sleep, food, stress and total load; note that post-session fatigue in one test was predominantly peripheral after the first day. [ch09]
**Cost**: a conversation.
**Not when**: persistent or unusual symptoms; see a clinician.

## 13. Full range versus partial
**When**: choosing range.
**Move**: use a reasonably full range, with exceptions for free-weight isolation where the top is easy; avoid trading range for load. [ch06]
**Cost**: lighter loads.
**Not when**: pain limits range; adapt and consult a professional.

## 14. Form anxiety
**When**: fear of a posture.
**Move**: practise a consistent, controlled setup; progress load gradually; stop at pain; do not equate every deviation with injury risk (position). [ch10]
**Cost**: some uncertainty remains.
**Not when**: a specific injury or symptoms; use `biomechanics-and-rehab-reasoning`.

## 15. Lifting and endurance together
**When**: combined goals.
**Move**: separate sessions when possible; same-session combinations cost more strength in trained people. [ch11]
**Cost**: schedule.
**Not when**: untrained, for whom interference is small.
