# Patterns: Workshop Facilitation (situation -> move)

## Group stalls in endless discussion
**When**: circular debate on a goal or topic.
**Move**: stop, switch to note-and-vote: 3-5 min silent writing in one sentence frame, fast read-out, silent dots, decider chooses. Park the rest.
**Trade-off**: less nuance; the decider may override the tally (ch05).

## One person dominates
**Move**: acknowledge, park the topic with their name and a return time, ask a probing question ("what is behind that?"), give an easy task (hand out dots), move to silent generation so everyone writes first. Escalate privately only last (ch04).

## Someone is silent or junior
**Move**: use together-alone writing; write their point down, ask why, bridge to a deeper understanding; anonymous notes.

## Participant resists or doubts the method
**Move**: pre-frame the sceptic before the session, ask for suspended disbelief; ask for their input; reassure aloud that the process feels unnatural; frame as an experiment (ch02, ch04).

## Off-topic point arises
**Move**: acknowledge, write it on the parking lot with name and time, return to it before closing. Never dismiss with "not relevant" (ch04).

## The group is in the messy middle and morale drops
**Move**: name it ("this is the groan zone, it is normal"), point to the emotion curve, switch to structured silent work, do not jump to convergence early (ch01).

## Instructions are misunderstood
**Move**: restate in What/Why/How, show a worked example, repeat; do not offer options (ch03).

## Energy sags after lunch or a long block
**Move**: energiser, short walk, alternate active and passive segments; put heavy thinking in the morning (ch03).

## Time is running out
**Move**: cut an exercise, not the decision or the follow-up; use shorter boxes; keep user testing in a sprint (ch07). Hold boxes firmly but kindly.

## Too many ideas, no priority
**Move**: pick top 10 per person, post unsorted, vote with enough dots, reorder by votes, keep the top ten (10 for 10); then effort-impact (ch06).

## Votes are split or unclear
**Move**: increase dot count next time; the decider chooses using the spread as information; allow an extra dot for a question that must be addressed (ch05).

## Group of 30 or more
**Move**: parallel breakouts of about five, spokesperson per group, buffer time (ch04).

## The decider is absent or hesitant
**Move**: stop deciding; run only Collect and Choose that day; schedule the decision session when the decider is present (ch04, ch08).

## Client brief is vague ("surprise me")
**Move**: three agreements (goal, context, parameters); ask for a budget range; ask "what have you seen that reflects this?"; elicit four or five criteria; do not start speculative work (ch01, ch02, ch10).

## Participants have different expectations
**Move**: call each before, write end-point notes at the start, revisit them mid-day, show the emotion curve (ch01).

## Remote group goes quiet or the tool fights you
**Move**: shorter blocks, pre-filled board, copied dot stickers, homework in working hours, a heads-up before pulling everyone to your view (ch09).

## A concept debate stalls the storyboard
**Move**: assign debaters a side task (copy, content); start from the voted test flow, not empty cells (ch07).

## The facilitator has an opinion
**Move**: keep it; ask a question instead. If you must decide or vote, announce the role switch (ch02).

## Ending with no follow-through
**Move**: owner and date for every item, review cadence in the calendar before leaving, parking lot closed, summary promised with a date (ch10).

## Plan breaks on the day
**Move**: stay calm; drop to the phase skeleton and choose the next exercise from the picker; remember facilitation means making it easy (ch02).
