# Chapter 03: Running the Session: Instructions, Timeboxing, Silent Work, Energy

## Core Idea
Execution rests on four habits: explain every exercise with one formula, run individual work in silence before any sharing ("together alone"), hold time boxes firmly but kindly, and manage energy on purpose. Structure and discipline create freedom for creative problem solving; creativity becomes a process you can run on demand rather than a mood to wait for.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **What / Why / How** for every exercise: **What** is its name; **Why** is its purpose, what the output looks like and how it will be used, linked to the previous or next exercise; **How** is concrete steps, tools, quantities, with a visual demonstration. Give one clear way to do it, not a menu of options. Be exact on the "how". Never describe an activity without the why.
- **Repetition rule**: expect to repeat key points. The first explanation reaches roughly half the room, the second about three quarters, the third about 95%. Summarise, then show two or three examples. Embarrassed people do the exercise wrong rather than ask.
- **Worked example before every exercise**: a blank template is not enough. Use a neutral example unrelated to the client's real topic. For tricky homework a short demo video helps.
- **Together alone**: everyone works individually toward one shared goal; the facilitator keeps it that way. Silent generation, then sharing, gives everyone an equal voice and curbs team politics and early anchoring.
- **Note-and-vote**: write silently, vote with dots silently, then the decider chooses. It gives results close to a long debate in much less time (ch05, ch06).
- **Five working principles to tell the team**: together alone; tangible beats discussion (show a visual example rather than describing it, because each listener imagines something different); getting started beats being right (pick a direction, test it, change course, work in testable chunks); do not rely on inspiration (use structured exercises); timebox everything (work expands to the time given, so show a visible timer).

## Step-by-Step: A Standard Exercise Slot
1. **Announce** what, why, how; show the example (about 2-3 min).
2. **Start the visible timer** and say the end time.
3. **Silent solo phase** (typically 3-8 min, see table); walk the room, help anyone stuck; no discussion, quiet background music reduces awkwardness.
4. **Share**: each person posts all at once, or reads their note fast with no explanation (about 30-60 seconds each; wrap up gently at ~30 seconds over).
5. **Vote silently and simultaneously** (countdown, so nobody watches others vote).
6. **Decide** (decider, or the pre-agreed rule), then move on when the box expires. Do not wordsmith numbers or argue details.
7. **Transition**: state the output, name the next exercise, and give a heads-up before pulling everyone to your view.

## Typical Timings from the Material
| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Silent writing of goals or questions | 3-5 min (first instincts are enough) |
| Silent problem or solution generation | 5-8 min (7 min in a problem jam; 6 min remote) |
| Read-out of a note | 30-60 s, no explanation |
| Dot voting | 3-6 min depending on options |
| Lightning demo research | 15-30 min solo, then up to 2 min per person |
| Break | about 5 min in a 2 h remote session; more in-person breaks every ~90 min |

## Managing Energy
- Start with a quick energiser, and again after long breaks; examples: find something blue and touch it, guess who wrote which fun fact.
- Alternate active segments (writing, voting, moving) with passive ones (listening).
- Short walks in breaks; high-fives after milestones. Energy drops after lunch and in long sessions.
- Keep the energy positive over long days and **reassure out loud and often** that the group is doing well: structured exercises feel unnatural and people doubt them.
- **Be firm but fair**: hold time boxes even when the team wants more time (especially sketching); move on when needed.
- **Never turn your back to the group**; ask someone else to scribe or draw.

## Shortcutting Circular Discussion
Park the point on a visible board; or run note-and-vote; or write points down as they emerge. Open talk with "what else? who else?" and close with "is there anything else?". When an off-topic item resurfaces, ask whether it belongs in the parking lot (ch04).

## Variations
- **Facilitator also timekeeper** in short formats; in multi-day work assign a timekeeper so you can watch the room.
- **No devices** except during research exercises.
- **Instruction fatigue**: if instructions get long, split the exercise into two slots with a demo each.

## Common Mistakes
- Offering several ways to do one exercise; explaining once and assuming understanding.
- Open-ended tasks with no time limit; timers nobody can see.
- Discussion creeping into silent phases or into voting.
- Letting debates run past the box; letting one person monopolise pitches.
- Skipping examples; crowding the agenda; no physical breaks.

## Checklist
- [ ] What/Why/How and an example ready for each exercise?
- [ ] Timer visible; end time spoken?
- [ ] Silent phase protected; sharing sequenced?
- [ ] Votes simultaneous and silent?
- [ ] Energiser and break placed after long or passive blocks?
- [ ] Someone else scribing so you face the room?
