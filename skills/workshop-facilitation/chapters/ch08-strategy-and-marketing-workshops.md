# Chapter 08: Strategy, Marketing and Brand Workshops

## Core Idea
A strategy or marketing workshop is a fixed, time-boxed sequence that turns a shared long-term goal into a broad roadmap of concrete, testable concepts, with a decider making the calls. Silent work comes first, then short pitches, then the decision. Brand and positioning work follows design-thinking order: understand users first, then define the brand, keeping weaknesses visible.

## Strategy Workshop (hours to a week)
1. **Welcome and roles**: preview the days, introductions with role cards, name the decider and the facilitator.
2. **Guidelines**: time-box everything, together alone, no multitasking, "move first, polish later".
3. **Long-term goal** (12-24 months): 15 min solo sentence on success; straw poll; decider picks.
4. **Metrics**: 10 min solo, two or three measurable indicators; silent heat-map dot vote; decider picks.
5. **Lightning demos**: 25 min analogous research; each example gets a big idea, why it is interesting, a takeaway.
6. **Concept creation**: 30 min; each concept is a titled hypothesis ("if we do X, we get Y") with a six-week plan.
7. **Day two**: heat map and straw poll on concepts; decider selects three or four to pursue.
8. **Roadmap**: place concepts on horizons (next 30 days, 3-9 months, longer); an owner and a next-check-in date for each; decider signs off.
- Keep the roadmap broad and flexible; granular plans fail. Do not wordsmith numbers.
- Pitfalls: absent decider; concepts too vague to understand without the author.

## Marketing Workshop Pattern: Collect, Choose, Create, Commit
Typical marketing application of the 4 Cs (ch01): collect challenges (sailboat or silent problem list); choose and reframe as HMW scoped tightly (one landing page, not a campaign); create with lightning demos plus 10-for-10; commit as experiments with hypothesis, steps and success criteria, an owner and a date (ch06, ch10).

## Growth Experiment Workshop (90-120 min, remote or in person, 3-10 people)
1. Frame the challenge as HMW (copy to every board section).
2. Lightning demos, 15 min silent solo: 1-2 products each with screenshot, a "what I like" note and a link.
3. Heat map: read all (10 min) and dot anything interesting.
4. Pick a favourite (3 min) and pitch it (1 min each); facilitator captures points.
5. Experiment template: name, hypothesis, action steps, success criteria (15 min).
6. Dot vote (3 each) and decider's final call aloud (5 min); keep the top 3.
7. Copy winners to a clean area; schedule the follow-up now.
Show a worked example before each step. More than 10 participants breaks time management. Do not let one person monopolise pitches.

## Brand and Positioning Workshop Moves
- **Users before brand**: define users (jobs, pains, gains) first, then the brand.
- **Deep on user profiles, broad on customer descriptors** (adjective + noun, for example "concerned activist").
- **Negative words**: capture them, reframe as positives, pair with a positive to create messaging. A mix of about 80% strengths and 20% quirks reads as authentic.
- **Keep weaknesses visible**: a perfect image is easy to disprove online; brands need customers more than customers need brands. Pay attention to conversations customers already have. One illustration: a company sent its most vocal critics a reformulated product for free and asked for honest feedback; several became advocates.
- **Transparency** about your agenda builds trust.
- Hand-offs: positioning and strategy theory -> `marketing-mba`; visual identity -> `art-director`; messaging copy -> `creative-director`.

## Design-Thinking Introduction (about 2 h, a team new to the method)
1. Icebreaker "My first job": name, first job, one lesson, on a note, shared in small groups.
2. Creative-confidence demo: 45-second drawing of a neighbour; debrief feelings.
3. Explain five stages: Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test. They loop and alternate divergent and convergent thinking; do not treat as linear.
4. **Empathy map** for a persona with a goal: Say, Think, Do, Feel (plus Pains, Gains, Jobs); 3 min silent individual notes, then 7 min group discussion and placement.
5. Point-of-view statement, then HMW questions.
6. Crazy Eights (1 min per sketch), present a favourite, refine 3 min.
7. Prototype 20 min with basic materials; keep it cheap and avoid building or coding before feedback.
8. Test: someone from another group uses it, optionally under a constraint (for example simulated limited hand mobility); feedback on what worked, what did not, what to improve.
In real projects the challenge comes from research, not assumptions.

## Variations
- Time scale: strategy sequence runs from hours to a week; compress by dropping concept depth, never the decision step.
- Client-facing: remove the long-term goal exercise if the client has an approved goal; start at metrics.

## Common Mistakes
- Letting the decider be absent; vague concepts; granular plans.
- Hiding weak points; copying a framework rigidly.
- Skipping examples; too many experiments for bandwidth; no follow-up.

## Checklist
- [ ] Decider and metrics agreed before concepts?
- [ ] Concepts written as hypotheses with a six-week or similar plan?
- [ ] Roadmap horizons with owner and check-in date?
- [ ] Users defined before brand?
