# Chapter 06: Ideation and Decision Techniques

## Core Idea
Structured ideation separates generating from judging: frame the challenge as a "How might we" question, generate silently and individually, then select by silent voting, then let the decider decide. Meetings without structure lose ideas (a group remembers only 5-6 of 50); priority matters more than volume. Inspiration from analogous solutions beats inventing from scratch, and cheap visible output beats talk.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **How might we (HMW)**: turn a problem or negative statement into an open opportunity question ("we are not getting enough customers to checkout" -> "how might we increase customers reaching checkout?"). Write "HMW" at the top of the note; post the challenge visibly and, in digital boards, copy it onto every section.
- **10 for 10**: HMW posted; 5 min silent ideation, one idea per note; 1 min each picks their 10 strongest and discards the rest; everyone posts their 10 at once, unsorted, duplicates fine; 3 min silent dot vote with 10 dots each (self-votes allowed, stacking allowed); clear dots, reorder by votes, keep the top 10 as shared output. Six people x ten notes = 60 notes is normal. Tell people to use all their dots.
- **Lightning demos** (analogous research): each person finds 1-3 products, companies or people that solved a similar problem, in any industry (cross-industry gives distance from the obvious). Solo, silent, "together alone", 15-30 min depending on the format (15 min growth workshop; 20-30 min sprint; 25 min strategy). Note format: example name top-left, big idea underneath, a few bullets on what applies; as few words as possible; save a link or screenshot. Facilitator models one example first. Each person presents up to 1-2 min; a note-taker captures one big idea per note; notes go on the wall for the sketching or concept step. Scope the challenge tightly (a single landing page, not a whole campaign) and add stakes and context so examples are judged against real constraints.
- **Heat map**: after reading all demos or concepts, everyone places dots silently on anything interesting; the facilitator places dots first to model generous use.
- **Crazy Eights**: fold a sheet into 8 frames; 1 min per sketch (8 min total), quantity over quality; then each person picks a favourite and refines it (3 min in a 2 h intro). Sketching is communication, not drawing skill.
- **Four-part sketch**: notes (~20 min, copy what is on the walls, collecting not creating) -> ideas (~20 min, rough sketches of the most interesting notes, avoids a blank page; about 5 min in a one-day format) -> Crazy Eights -> **solution sketch**, a three-panel poster showing the concept, direction and the "bet" to test, annotated with short stickies. Rules: anonymous (no names, give the concept a nickname), words matter more than drawing, one big idea per sketch, self-explanatory because nobody can ask the author later.
- **Lightning Decision Jam (LDJ)**, a structured mostly-silent problem-solving sequence (about 1 h):
  1. Moderator runs the timer and keeps silence.
  2. Silent problem generation, 7 min.
  3. Post and explain, max 30 s per note.
  4. Silent vote, 2 dots each (moderator included); rank, discard the rest.
  5. Reframe top problems as HMW notes in priority order.
  6. Silent solutions, 7 min, quantity over quality, contrasting colour.
  7. Silent vote, 6 dots each; rank.
  8. **Effort-impact matrix**: facilitator places each solution; participants only say "higher" or "lower"; the high-impact, low-effort corner is the sweet spot for immediate action. Park low-impact ideas whatever the effort, and high-effort ones with low impact.
  9. Turn sweet-spot items into concrete next actions with one owner each.
  Remote variant opens with a 6 min positives round and 3 dots for negatives (ch09). Keep to the top few problems.
- **Experiment template** (growth or product experiments): name, hypothesis, granular action steps, success criteria; about 15 min, at least one per person; dot vote 3 each; decider keeps the top 3; copy winners to a clean area and schedule follow-up immediately. Growth hacking here means building the product so users promote it (referral loops), not running a campaign.
- **Crazy-eights-to-prototype loop** for design-thinking intros: empathy map -> point-of-view statement -> HMW -> Crazy Eights -> build -> test (ch08).

## Decision Sequence (concept selection)
1. **Reveal**: concepts (taped face-down) turned over and spaced widely on a wall.
2. **Heat map / art museum**: silent walk with many or unlimited dots; questions go on notes under the concept, not asked aloud; no neighbour chat.
3. **Speed critique**: facilitator summarises each concept focusing on where dots cluster (they must have studied it); a note-taker places 3-4 key-idea notes above it; the author may correct misreadings.
4. **Straw poll**: each non-decider casts one vote silently (~5 min), then a one-minute pitch with reasons tied to the sprint goal. Called "fake" or non-binding in one source and "binding" in another; treat it as strong input, final authority sits with the decider.
5. **Super vote**: decider alone, timeboxed to ~10 minutes. For a first run pick one concept, or one plus a single feature from another; two head-to-head prototypes double the build and test work.
- Judge concepts against the goal and key questions, not against excitement. Seek a timeboxed decision, not consensus.

## Dot-Vote Cheat Rules
| Situation | Dots |
|---|---|
| Goal statement | 1 each (decider separate) |
| Key / sprint questions | 3 each |
| Problems (LDJ) | 2 each |
| Solutions (LDJ) | 6 each |
| 10 for 10 | 10 each, stackable |
| Retro blockers | 8 each |
| Experiments | 3 each |
| Concept heat map | many / unlimited |
Rule: size the dots so votes differentiate; vote silently and simultaneously.

## Common Mistakes
- Starting with open discussion; no time limit on any step; jumping to solutions before problems are ranked.
- Limiting research to direct competitors; long demo presentations that eat the box.
- Over-collecting notes (two hundred are hard to consolidate); too many experiments for the team's bandwidth; no follow-up.
- Over-attachment to one idea; adding complexity to rescue a weak concept.
- Judging drawing skill; cramming several concepts into one panel; letting the author's identity influence the vote.
- Discussing during voting; facilitator summarising concepts they have not studied.

## Checklist
- [ ] Challenge written as an HMW and visible?
- [ ] Silent generation before any share?
- [ ] Dot counts sized; "use all your dots" said?
- [ ] Output ranked and visible (top 10, top 3)?
- [ ] Decider's role explicit; follow-up named?
