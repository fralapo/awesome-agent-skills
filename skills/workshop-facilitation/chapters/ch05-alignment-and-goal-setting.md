# Chapter 05: Alignment and Goal-Setting Exercises

## Core Idea
Teams align fastest when they write silently, vote silently, and let one decider choose, using the same sentence frame so options are comparable. Optimism first (a best-case long-term goal), then pessimism (what could stop us, as answerable questions). The pair of a goal plus a few testable questions gives a one-page north star that makes a vague goal checkable. The same logic applies to retrospectives and objectives.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Long-term goal + key questions** (the core alignment exercise).
- **Note and vote**: write -> read fast, no explanation -> vote with dots -> decider chooses.
- **Sentence frames**: goals begin "In two years (time) we will..."; questions begin "Can we...?" (yes/no, answerable). Use "Can we" for key questions and "How might we" for open-ended solutions.
- **Three agreements** (goal, context, parameters) for briefing a team or a client (ch01).
- **Sailboat retrospective**: sail = what moves the team forward, anchor = what holds it back, waterline between.
- **Objectives and key results (OKR) workshop**: a roughly two-hour session turning many candidate goals into three objectives with measurable key results and a review cadence.

## Step-by-Step: Long-Term Goal and Key Questions (about 45-60 min)
1. Frame: "Two years from now, best case." Silent writing, 2-3 optimistic statements each (3-5 min), keep one.
2. Post the goal notes; each person reads theirs fast, no explanation.
3. Vote: one dot each (or a silent straw poll with a written reason, everyone voting at the same moment). The decider does not vote, or casts one vote and may merge phrases from other notes. They choose using the spread as information.
4. Questions: "what could stop us?" Each person writes challenges as "Can we..." questions for about 5-8 minutes (up to 2-3 or as many as possible, keep three).
5. Vote: three dots each (decider excluded), 5-7 min. The decider picks one "ultimate" question or adds one extra dot to a question that must be addressed.
6. Write the goal and the top three questions on a wall sheet, most important first, and keep them visible for the whole project. Output: one page.
- With 7 people and 3 questions each you get about 21 notes; aim for three winners (four at most if the decider insists). Give enough dots that votes differentiate; one dot each with many options yields no signal.

## Step-by-Step: Sailboat Retrospective (about 30 min; full retro about 90 min)
1. Draw the boat. Set ground rules, parking lot and a neutral example.
2. Positives: 3-6 notes each (about 3 min), posted one by one on the sail.
3. Blockers: silent, individual writing (about 8 min, second colour); everyone sticks notes at once; **nothing is read aloud** (preserves anonymity).
4. Optional: group names themes; leftovers under "Other".
5. Dot vote: 8 dots each, about 6 min, silent, simultaneous, self-votes allowed.
6. Prioritise: top item on top; items with 3+ votes below; 1-2 vote items parked.
- Facilitator stays neutral and does not vote; if the leader must decide too, announce the role switch. About 7 participants, buffer for large groups.

## Step-by-Step: Objectives and Key Results (about 2 h, 3-10 people)
1. Brief with examples; silent objective writing (~10 min), quantity over quality, within a given focus area.
2. Remove empty notes; silent dot vote (3 dots, stacking allowed) on criteria: understandable, realistic, clear value.
3. Decider picks the top three, may ignore the vote.
4. Key-result ideation per objective (~10 min, silent).
5. Silent vote per objective; decider selects three key results each and edits wording on the fly (for example raising a 25% target to 40%).
6. Visualise and share; set the review cadence and send calendar invites.
- **Objective**: short, concrete, action-oriented, inspiring. **Key result**: measurable (yes/no or 0-100%), an outcome not an activity, time-bound, a stretch. Set dot counts to match the number of picks.

## Variations
- **Alignment with a single stakeholder** (client): use the three agreements and criteria elicitation (ch01, ch02) instead of dot voting.
- **Marketing or product team**: derive the goal's measurable indicators with the strategy sequence (ch08).
- **Anonymous variant** for senior groups: collect notes face-down or digitally before posting.

## Common Mistakes
- Long circular debate on the goal; discussing while people vote.
- Treating the vote as binding; letting the decider be absent.
- More than three key questions (diluted focus); vague goals with no test.
- Key results that describe activities; too many participants; skipping the review schedule.
- Reading blockers aloud; letting people watch each other's votes; adding an unplanned extra vote or "fix it now" discussion instead of parking.

## Checklist
- [ ] Same sentence frame handed to everyone?
- [ ] Silent writing, read-out without explanation, silent vote?
- [ ] Dots sized to options; decider convention announced?
- [ ] Goal and top three questions visible for the whole project?
- [ ] Review cadence scheduled before leaving?
