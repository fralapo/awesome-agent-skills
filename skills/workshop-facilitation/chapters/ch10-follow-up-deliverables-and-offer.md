# Chapter 10: Follow-Up, Deliverables and Offering Facilitation

## Core Idea
A workshop ends only when ideas become owned actions with dates and a review point. Commit is a phase, not an afterthought: what, who, when. For a facilitator working as a service, the offer should be priced on the client's risk and investment rather than effort alone, with scope, budget and the reference picture agreed before any speculative work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Commit phase**: turn solutions into owned actions. Each chosen item becomes a named experiment with an owner and timeline, entered into a shared document. Owner is the idea's author or a volunteer.
- **Experiment/action template**: name, hypothesis, granular steps, success criteria, owner, date.
- **Effort-impact action board**: pick high-impact, low-effort items for immediate action; leave vague items out or make them a specific next step.
- **Review cadence**: set it before leaving the room and send calendar invites; each concept on a roadmap has an owner and a next-check-in date. Schedule follow-up immediately.
- **Parking-lot closure**: revisit every parked item before closing, assign a time and owner or drop it explicitly (ch04).
- **Retrospective**: one short exercise (sailboat, ch05) or a last-day review; ask what to change next time.

## Step-by-Step: Closing and Handing Over
1. **Visualise final outputs** on one wall or board: goal and questions, ranked ideas, selected experiments, roadmap horizons.
2. **Sign-off aloud** by the decider; keep votes as information.
3. **Assign owners and dates** for every item; limit the number to the team's bandwidth.
4. **Close expectations**: return to the notes written at the start and compare.
5. **Review**: note what you would change next time (ch02).
6. **Document**: photograph the walls, transcribe the winning notes to a shared document within the agreed time, send with the schedule of check-ins. Group by phase, not by exercise order.
7. **Follow up** at the first check-in: what happened, what was learned, next step.

## Deliverables (what the client should get)
- One-page summary: goal, top three questions or objectives, chosen experiments, owners, dates.
- The parking-lot list and its resolution.
- The wall photos or a cleaned digital board, the recorded decisions.
- For sprints: the storyboard, the prototype, and the test findings grouped by step (ch07).
- Where deliverables must be presented to stakeholders, hand off formatting to `presentation-design`.

## Offering Facilitation as a Service
These points come from a few reference remarks and vary by country and experience; use as orientation, not as a price list.
- **Positioning**: the facilitator's value is a low-friction process with an actionable outcome; measure by whether the team wants you back.
- **Reference points (per workshop day)**: beginners may start around a minimum of 500; active practitioners around 2,500 per workshop day plus 300-400 per preparation day.
- **Pricing basis**: price by the client's risk and investment, not by effort alone (for a sprint, a high-stakes decision justifies more than the hours suggest).
- **Budget and speculation**: ask for a budget range; if the client does not know it, send them to whoever does. Do not do exploratory work for free; explain the team's time is limited. Anchor with past projects and their price bands. To uncover the mental picture ask "what have you seen that reflects this idea?" and "what resonates with you?", not "what do you like?"; show references. Do not split the difference before scope is known. State terms calmly; use the best-alternative concept to decline an unfair option and restate what you need ("give me a budget and I will explore it").
- **Scope**: client snapshot and three agreements (ch01), including deadline, constraints and secondary expectations.
- For negotiation tactics, proposals and rates, see `pricing-and-sales-negotiation`; for freelance operations see `freelance-designer-ops`.

## Common Mistakes
- No follow-up; no owner; too many experiments for the team's bandwidth.
- Leaving sweet-spot items as vague ideas instead of a specific next step.
- Giving ideas away before a deal; accepting "just wow me" as a brief.
- Treating output volume as success.

## Checklist
- [ ] Every item has what, who, when?
- [ ] Review dates in calendars before the room empties?
- [ ] Parking lot closed?
- [ ] Summary document promised with a date?
- [ ] Offer states scope, budget, deadline, constraints?
