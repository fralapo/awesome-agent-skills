# Chapter 09: Remote and Online Facilitation

## Core Idea
A remote workshop keeps the in-person logic (silent solo work, voting, a decider) but reshapes time, tools and artifacts for screen fatigue and digital whiteboards. Shorter sessions, pre-filled boards, copy-based dot voting and homework replace long days and blank canvases. The facilitator curates the board between sessions and treats it as a living document.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Timetable**: instead of two full in-person days, plan several half-day sessions (for a sprint: three half-day sessions in week one, two more the next week). For a short jam: one hour; for a growth workshop: 90-120 min with a 5-minute break in a 2-hour session.
- **Onboarding**: teach only frame navigation (outline), zoom and pan, select, copy, and editing notes. Nothing more is needed.
- **Pre-filled boards**: map, HMW notes, goal and questions prepared from pre-workshop interviews; never a blank slate. A blank page invites debate.
- **Voting by copy**: copy a dot sticker (for example decider four dots, others two) instead of the tool's built-in voting widget, which hides other votes and cannot target parts of an image. Use clearly colour-coded dots.
- **Homework**: lightning demos as homework, submitted the same day so the facilitator can prepare them; set deadlines within working hours; never let clients work overtime.
- **Concept sketching** on paper, timed on a call, photographed and voted on with dots.
- **User tests**: two people per session (interviewer and note-taker), colour-coded notes.

## Remote Problem-Solving Jam (about 1 h)
1. Positives round: 6 min silent writing, then share back (constructive tone).
2. Negatives round: 6 min silent writing; 3 dots each on the most urgent.
3. Reframe the top challenge as HMW.
4. Solution brainstorm: 6 min silent, one idea per note; dots (several allowed on the best ideas).
5. Effort-impact matrix: pick high-impact, low-effort.
6. Turn the top three into experiments: map steps, one owner each (the author or a volunteer).
Keep discussion out of silent phases; share after the timer. Work only on the top few problems.

## Remote Preparation and Attention Management
- **Before the day:** call the decider about goals, challenges and expectations; build connection through 1:1 calls, a group call or an icebreaker; test every piece of technology in every browser; prepare a plan B for the tool, since a client may ban a platform; reread tactics for dominant people and circular discussions.
- **Setup:** two screens (board on one, video and chat on the other); a co-facilitator handles tech while the lead facilitates. Design the board with progressive disclosure: hide sections not yet in use and embed slides in the board instead of switching tools.
- **During:** exaggerate energy, since remote participants disengage easily; open with a paired icebreaker; play low-volume music in individual exercises; encourage video; ask permission before moving to the next exercise and buffer more discussion time than in person.
- **Length:** cap a session at about four hours including breaks; break every 50-60 minutes remotely (about 90 in person).
- **Closing:** have everyone zoom out to see the completed board, run a short takeaway round, then follow with a handover call and a written report (ch10).

## Step-by-Step: Running an Online Session
1. Send the pre-brief 2-7 days before with role, schedule and tools; ask for a 5-10 min pre-activity; remind the day before (ch01).
2. Set up the board: sections, HMW copied onto each, example notes, timer visible.
3. Start with a quick energiser and a tool warm-up (one note, one dot).
4. Give instructions as What/Why/How with a demo and a worked example (ch03). Give a heads-up before pulling everyone to your view.
5. Keep individual phases silent; re-enable cameras when the timer ends.
6. Place breaks; keep sessions short, screen fatigue sets in fast.
7. Between sessions, update the board and send the summary (ch10).

## Variations
- **Hybrid** (not covered in depth by the material): work on one shared digital board so remote and in-room people see the same artifacts.
- **Large online groups**: breakout rooms of about five with a spokesperson (ch04).

## Common Mistakes
- Relying on built-in voting that hides results and cannot target details.
- Long sessions without breaks; homework outside working hours.
- Blank boards; teaching too many tool features.
- Discussion creeping into silent phases because cameras are on.

## Checklist
- [ ] Sessions split into short blocks?
- [ ] Board pre-filled and numbered?
- [ ] Voting by copied dots; decider's dot count decided?
- [ ] Homework deadlines in working hours?
- [ ] Tool onboarding limited to five actions?
