# Chapter 03: Typography Fundamentals

## Core Idea
Clarity comes from strong, simple contrast: a fixed ratio between sizes, a jump of more than one step between weights, one shared alignment edge and one typeface. Sophistication starts from simplicity and adds complexity gradually; complex pieces still rest on strong fundamentals. Graphic design is largely about how supplied content is presented (semiotics), so a designer's depth comes from knowledge and interests outside design as well as within it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Size by ratio**: divide the headline size by a fixed factor to get the body size (75 pt headline / 5 = 15 pt body). For posters with huge display type use a larger divisor so secondary text becomes very small yet legible; adjust until contrast feels neither timid nor extreme.
- **Skip a weight**: if the body is regular, pair it with extra bold, not bold; adjacent weights look almost identical and read as a mistake. Limit a piece to one or two weights; a poster can keep every size equal and rely on weight alone to highlight key words.
- **One edge**: align all text to the same grid line (typically flush left) regardless of size, style or position; mixed alignments look cluttered. Even in radial or diagonal grids a shared edge reads clean.
- **Anchored centring**: if text is centred, anchor blocks with horizontal lines so they relate and reading stays unambiguous.
- **Grouping devices**: lines, background shapes or a single oversized letter that physically separates blocks; a shape behind text groups more clearly than a thin rule.
- **Single typeface**: one family per design; a second only if it pairs well. Beginners overuse fonts and lose cohesion.
- **Type selection**: choose from curated sources, check the designer's and foundry's other work, shortlist about ten candidates and compare. Judge a face by whether its other published samples carry the tone you need.
- **Custom display letters from strokes**: draw with the pen, vary stroke width, expand to outlines, cut with subtractive pathfinder, smooth corners for clean curves.

## Rules and Numbers
- Avoid force-justified text, especially in narrow columns: uneven word spacing makes rivers of white space. Prefer flush-left, flush-right or centred; if justification is unavoidable, reduce type size in half- or quarter-point steps until the line length suits the column and the rivers vanish.
- In flush-left text, shape the ragged edge so long and short lines alternate; blurred, the right edge should read as an even grey rectangle.
- Never leave a single word alone on the last line of a paragraph (keep at least two) and never begin a paragraph with the last word of the previous one.
- Letter anatomy carries period and tone: a high or low crossbar, stroke weight and letter height shift the era feel (heavy reads immediate, narrow and graceful reads classical).
- Line spacing is a refinement tool that can strengthen the main point of a text block.
- For the nine typography laws, UX writing and numeric readability on screens, see `ux-ui-expert` ch21.

## Common Mistakes
- Three or more typefaces competing.
- Bold next to semibold: contrast too weak to look intentional.
- Mixed alignments within one layout.
- Rivers, widows and orphans left in the final.
- Picking unvetted free fonts as the typographic foundation.
