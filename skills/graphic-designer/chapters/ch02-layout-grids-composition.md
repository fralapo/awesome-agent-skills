# Chapter 02: Layout, Grids and Composition

## Core Idea
A grid is a framework of columns, gutters, margins and rows. It makes layouts consistent, speeds decisions and improves readability while leaving room for creativity. A real grid carries an embedded logic (specific proportions and constrained positions); a regular field of columns that allows nearly any placement is not a meaningful grid. Composition work is systematic experimentation: the viewer gives only a few seconds, so test crop, orientation, scale, contrast, proximity, alignment and margins before committing.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Choose the grid by content**: one column for continuous reading (books, essays); two columns for magazines and newsletters; three or more for newspapers and websites; a modular grid (columns plus rows) for brochures and posters with mixed content.
- **Modular grid**: a matrix of cells; a block can take one cell, two, a double-height column or the full area, giving varied layouts that stay coherent.
- **Master-page setup**: set margins, grid, running footers and automatic page numbers on the master so every page inherits them.
- **Thumbnail-first**: sketch small thumbnails on the grid (headline, image, text positions) to explore many options in minutes (about 20 minutes covers a set of pages), then rebuild the best ones digitally.
- **Cover testing**: keep the grid, vary background colours and image scale to compare options quickly.
- **Image-led page recipe**: gather several images from different angles and distances; choose one strong image over several similar ones; test several orientations and crops; fill the page with the image instead of centring a title above a small picture; keep subject lines near the rule of thirds.

## Rules and Numbers
**Practical A4 print setup**: 3 mm bleed; 15 mm margins on all sides with a larger bottom margin (about 25 mm) for footnotes and page numbers; four columns with a 5 mm gutter; eight rows with the same 5 mm gutter.
- Fit row guides to the margins, not the page, or unwanted narrow boxes appear at the edges.
- Use the same gutter for columns and rows so cells keep consistent proportion.
- Strong photography wants larger images and less text.
- Typographic hierarchy before layout: very large cover headline; smaller second heading; light-weight subhead at body size; body copy; caption slightly smaller (for example 8 pt on 10 pt leading). One family in a few weights.

**Text on photos**
- Guarantee contrast: change text colour or image treatment until it reads.
- Move away from centred alignment; align to left or right margins or inside a calm area of the photo.
- Keep padding between text boxes and the page edge.
- Adjust line spacing as part of refining a block.
- Titles and slogans carry more weight and scale than secondary text.

**Experimental grids for posters**: keep information legible but leave enough open space for pattern, shape and colour to develop; recurring work (monthly event posters) is a playground for a non-conventional grid, and a series can vary by season or theme while belonging to a family.

## Common Mistakes
- Default centred title over a small photo.
- Margins left at software defaults; text hugging the page edge.
- Different gutters for columns and rows.
- Treating the grid as a cage instead of a framework you break on purpose, once.
