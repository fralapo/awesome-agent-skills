# Chapter 09: Monoline and Custom Lettering Construction

## Core Idea
Custom lettering stays clean when it is built from measured, repeated geometry rather than eyeballed, and when the negative shapes (counters, gaps) are judged as carefully as the strokes. Structure comes first: in monoline work, thick and thin, rhythm and legibility all come from construction, not from effects. Concept and brand fit come from other roles; this chapter is the vector-craft procedure. For outline surgery and experimental type see ch04; for logo-level checks see ch08.

## Procedure: Template and Build
1. **Set up a template**: baseline, x-height, cap height and a slant guide. A blend between two lines with many steps produces a slant grid.
2. **Sketch and iterate before polishing**; do not perfect a direction that may be thrown away. Import sketches as guides.
3. **Draw the easiest letter first** and use it as the unit (its angle, stroke length, curve radius). Then lowercase, then uppercase.
4. **Measure, then constrain**: measure the angle of an existing stroke and constrain new lines to that exact value. Reuse geometry by duplicating, rotating or scaling instead of redrawing.
5. **Align curve handles with the construction guide** so transitions have no kink. Use few anchor points on curves; avoid anchors that cross over earlier ones.
6. **Round caps** soften monoline terminals.
7. **Turn off pixel snapping** when fine-tuning small drawings; it can pull points off position.
8. **Refine early**, then switch off the sketch and judge the form itself, without the crutch of the guide.
9. **Balance optically**: place the lockup in a circle or frame and check weight, including flourishes.

## Rules for the Letters
- Keep one stroke angle across letters; inconsistency reads as error.
- Curved letters overshoot the baseline; align the flat part to it, not the curve.
- Apply **optical correction**: a point that is mathematically aligned may need a small shift to look aligned.
- Check **counters** and spaces: circles drawn inside adjacent spaces should match; an off counter makes a letter read wrong.
- Solve structure before weight: in monoline, thick and thin emerge from how strokes join.
- Drop any idea that hurts legibility (a symbol inside a letter that makes it hard to read).
- Judge the drawing at the size it will be used, not only at working size.

## Weight, Pairing and Focal Point
- **Monoline: equal size means equal stroke weight.** Scaling an element down changes its perceived weight against the rest; re-balance strokes after scaling instead of trusting the number.
- **Pair a script with a type lighter than the script strokes**, so the script stays the focus.
- **Squint at the lockup**: whatever dominates the blurred shape is the focal point; check it is the intended one.
- Judge the letterform, not the sketch: switch the sketch off early.

## Monograms and Interlocking
- Break the mark into parts, use offset paths to cut gaps, and remove cuts where there is little space.
- Interlocking forms work better with more crossing parts; ribbon-like forms must fold in a physically plausible way or they look wrong.
- Fussy small counters and triangles disappear at small sizes; a tiny delicate counter in a monogram turns fussy and reads as an icon.

## Colour Notes for Lettering
For print avoid placing neighbouring hues (red and orange) side by side, they merge in poor reproduction; warm colours advance, so the key element goes in the warm colour. Test two versions with the audience before deciding.

## Common Mistakes
- Pen-tool kinks at joins and handles that are not aligned.
- Strokes at slightly different angles across the word.
- Judging only at small working size.
- Polishing before the structure is decided.
- Ribbons or interlocks that cannot exist physically.

## Checklist
- [ ] Template with baseline, x-height, slant guides
- [ ] Easiest letter drawn first and reused as the unit
- [ ] All angles measured, not eyeballed
- [ ] Handles aligned, few anchors, no kinks
- [ ] Counters and spacing checked, overshoots and optical corrections done
- [ ] Judged without the sketch and at use size
