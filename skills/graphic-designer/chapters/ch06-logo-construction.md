# Chapter 06: Logo Construction and Delivery

## Core Idea
The graphic designer builds the mark: choosing the logo type, shaping it with intent, constructing it cleanly, testing it and delivering it properly. A mature logo gets its power from form, shape psychology and composition, not from effects. Brand strategy, positioning and the identity system around the mark are owned by the art director (see `art-director` ch03); the concept behind the brand comes from the creative director.

## Construction Workflow
1. **Read the strategy** you were given (purpose, audience, values, chosen direction). If it is missing, ask for it instead of inventing it.
2. **Keyword method** (if concept work is yours): collect keywords with the client, expand each with related words, combine into pairs and think of each pair visually (grip becomes a fist); this yields shapes and even names.
3. **Sketch on paper first**: dump rough ideas, do not finish a logo; blend concepts freely. Import sketches as locked guides when building digitally, snapping tolerance low.
4. **Avoid clichés**: skip obvious category icons and drop ideas whose cultural associations conflict with the brand story.
5. **Iterate**: the second iteration of a concept is almost always better than the first.
6. **Grid late**: use a grid only in refinement. Circular grids help organic logos, straight and 45-degree grids help rigid ones; the grid keeps micro white space at a constant unit (1x, 2x), repeats angles consistently and keeps symbol-to-wordmark spacing precise.
7. **Vectorise and clean**: consistent line thickness (unless deliberately hand-drawn), even spacing, alignment, clean proportions.
8. **Fake overlaps cleanly**: outline strokes, add an offset path in the background colour, delete unwanted areas with a shape-merging tool; outline view verifies alignment.
9. **Apply and test**: mock up in real applications (business card, letterhead, banner) and test at many scales; a logo can look very different outside the design software.
10. **Deliver**: several versions (symbol alone, type alone, combined), vector formats, correct colour modes, clear file names, careful presentation. Over-deliver: finish early and offer more concepts than agreed.

## Logo Types
| Type | Use | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Lettermark (initials) | very long names | must be made iconic |
| Wordmark | startups; puts the name in the open | keep the name short |
| Brandmark (symbol) | language-independent | hard to associate with the brand alone |
| Combination mark | most flexible | needs separate symbol and type versions |
| Emblem | authority, togetherness | fine detail fails at small sizes |
| Mascot | personality | hard to keep timeless |

## Shape and Type Psychology
- Squares and rectangles read as strong and reliable; circles and ovals as community and friendship; angled straight lines as motion and speed; triangles as strength and stability.
- Man-made or industrial subjects call for straight geometric edges; natural or living subjects for curves.
- Typeface by feeling: a bold condensed italic sans suggests strength and forward motion, a light refined roman suggests elegance and class, a serif suggests luxury and prestige. Ask what the brand sells, who buys it and what it must feel like.
- Layer meaning in one simple symbol: one shape can read at once as a letter, a marker, a heart and an abstract person. Let form express the brand promise (a curved, forward-moving shape implies speed). Simple shapes are more memorable.
- If a mark is overly detailed and illegible small, strip back: remove colour, go to black and white or a simple typographic logo.
- Typographic anatomy can carry the concept: reshaping a letter to evoke rails, or using the full weight range in one wordmark to express variety.

## Stress Tests
Favicon size; one colour; reversed on dark; embroidery; animation; on three very different formats. If it fails, it is not ready; report to the art director if the failure comes from the direction, not the construction.

## Common Mistakes
- Gradients, drop shadows, bevels and complex type effects that date the mark.
- Mark only tested on a white screen at large size.
- Inconsistent stroke weights and uneven spacing between symbol and wordmark.
- Inventing brand strategy because none was provided.
