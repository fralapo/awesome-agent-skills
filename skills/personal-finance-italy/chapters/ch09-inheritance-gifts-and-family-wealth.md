# Chapter 09: Inheritance, Gifts and Family Wealth

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. Rules are snapshots (Italy, 2013-2025, year stated), to be re-checked against the rule in force. Succession is a legal matter: a notary or lawyer decides individual cases.

## Core Idea
In this model, Italian succession law **fixes shares**: specific assets can be allocated only by will, and a protected part of the estate cannot be disinherited. The tax side is separate and, for securities, turns on one question: does the instrument produce capital income (funds, ETFs, zero-coupon bonds) or miscellaneous income (stocks, ETC/ETN, ordinary bonds)? That category decides how the heir's cost basis is set, which matters only when the heir later sells.

## 9.1 Statutory shares without a will
- **Thesis**: absent a will, the law decides the split.
- **Mechanism**: spouse alone takes all; spouse and one child half and half; spouse and several children one third to the spouse and two thirds to the children; no spouse or children: half to ascendants and half to siblings. Under community property regime half the assets already belong to the spouse.
- **Trade-off**: simplicity versus no flexibility.
- **Does not hold when**: the spouse waives rights or special statutes (family business pacts) apply.

## 9.2 Reserved quotas
- **Thesis**: a protected part of the estate cannot be disinherited; only the rest ("disponibile") is freely allocable.
- **Mechanism**: spouse alone 1/2; spouse and one child 1/3 each; spouse and several children 1/4 and 1/2; one child 1/2; several children 2/3; spouse and ascendants 1/2 and 1/4; ascendants 1/3. Siblings are not protected. Free disposal is limited to the remaining quota.
- **Does not hold when**: the protected heir waives or does not claim the right in time.

## 9.3 Cost basis at death: the category decides
- **Thesis**: the heir's cost basis depends on the income category; succession tax and income tax are separate levies (Italy, snapshot 2025; verify for the tax year).
- **Mechanism**: for instruments producing **capital income** (funds, ETFs, zero-coupon bonds) death is not a realisation event: no income tax arises at death, the heir inherits the original cost basis with no step-up and pays 26% on the whole gain when selling; losses lapse. For instruments producing **miscellaneous income** (stocks, ETC/ETN, ordinary bonds, certificates) the basis is generally the value declared for succession tax (verify); a loss lapses. Succession tax (rates and allowances by kinship) applies to the estate value independently of this. Government bonds avoid both inheritance tax and capital gains. Heirs receive securities, not cash.
- **Worked example**: an ETF bought at 100,000 and valued 200,000: no income tax is due at death; if the heir sells at 200,000 the taxable gain is 100,000 (26,000 tax), because the basis stays 100,000 (verify). The succession-tax allowances apply separately to the 200,000 value.
- **Trade-off**: selling before death crystallises tax at once, while holding defers it to the heir's sale; the basis rules differ by category.
- **Does not hold when**: law or administrative guidance changes, or the heir is the spouse with exemptions.

## 9.4 Inheritance tax in outline (general knowledge, snapshot 2025)
| Heir | Rate | Allowance |
|---|---|---|
| Spouse and direct line (children, parents) | 4% | on the part above about 1,000,000 per heir |
| Siblings | 6% | on the part above about 100,000 |
| Other relatives and others | 6% (relatives to fourth degree) or 8% | no general allowance |

The same schedule applies broadly to gifts. Real estate carries mortgage and cadastral taxes on top. Government bonds are exempt; exemption of life-insurance payouts is a weak argument for choosing an insurance wrapper when direct-line succession is already exempt up to the allowance. Verify the thresholds for the year.

## 9.5 Accepting, renouncing, minors
- **Thesis**: inaction and acts of an heir can accept an inheritance silently; renunciation and benefit of inventory protect from debts.
- **Mechanism**: acting as heir (closing accounts, using assets) is tacit acceptance. Benefit of inventory limits debts to inherited assets. Renunciation passes the share to descendants; for minors, a court authorisation is needed. Rights prescribe after 10 years; those in possession of assets face a 3-month term to decide. Sanctions are not inherited; tax and interest are.
- **Trade-off**: certainty versus cost and time.
- **Does not hold when**: debts are known to be zero.

## 9.6 Ownership and capacity of a minor
- **Thesis**: money given to a minor belongs to the minor and can be used only for the minor's benefit.
- **Mechanism**: acts of extraordinary administration (investments, property) need judicial authorisation, so the practical route is that an adult buys and gifts the asset. An investment held in a parent's name is the parent's: it enters the estate (legitimate shares) and is exposed to the parent's creditors unless formally donated. Bonds issued to a minor are protected from the buyer's creditors; the drawback is the lock-up until majority and early redemption only with a judge. A step-up yield table on a savings bond is cumulative (ch01).
- **Does not hold when**: a legal exception applies or the family accepts the risk of keeping assets in the parent's name.
- **Worked example**: a state-guaranteed savings bond taxes interest at 12.5%, against 26% on ordinary financial income.

## 9.7 Joint accounts, securities and bank procedures at death
A joint account ("conto cointestato") is frozen by many banks until succession is settled; a delegation to operate avoids this. Securities held at a foreign custodian require heirs to reconstruct the basis and file in the declarative regime (ch06); the dead holder's loss carry-forward lapses (ch05).

## 9.8 Allocation for older investors and heirs
- **Thesis**: age alone does not dictate prudence; effective horizon, projected spending (especially care costs) and the wish to leave assets do.
- **Mechanism**: remaining life expectancy at 70 is around 16-17 years and rises with age survived. The investment horizon for those with heirs is longer than their life. Spending in the next few years should be matched with bonds held to maturity; the rest can bear equity risk if it will not be needed. Couples benefit from economies of scale in care. When assets are very small, simplicity and low cost beat any investment account.
- **Worked example (stylised)**: with a comfortable pension and house, 100% equities may be acceptable; with no heirs and high pension, half or more in bonds; with small assets and care needs, bonds for the next several years.
- **Does not hold when**: spending needs absorb all income and assets or health is very poor.

## 9.9 Life and long-term-care insurance
Pure term cover pays a large sum on death for a small premium and fits households dependent on one income or with a mortgage: 100,000 euro for accidental death at about 30 euro a year versus 400,000 including illness from about 100 euro a year (Italy, snapshot 2025). Long-term-care cover has steeply rising premiums because usage probability goes from negligible to about 70% by age 80. Investment-style branches layer costs.

## Common mistakes
- Believing a will can override reserved quotas.
- Selling an ETF before death "to avoid tax" without noting that death itself triggers no income tax on it (verify).
- Acting as heir (using accounts) before deciding to accept.
