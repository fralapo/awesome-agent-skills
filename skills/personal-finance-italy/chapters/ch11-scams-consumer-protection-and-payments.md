# Chapter 11: Scams, Consumer Protection and Payments

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. Figures and rules are snapshots (Italy/EU, 2023-2025, year stated), to be re-checked against the rule in force.

## Core Idea
In this model, most personal-finance losses come from three places: scams that exploit urgency and irreversible payment, advice and products that transfer value to the seller through cost, and timing or emotion errors. Defences are mostly **mechanical**: cheap verification rules, reading the cost stack, matching money to horizon, and knowing how card and payment rails behave when something goes wrong.

## 11.1 Anatomy of a financial scam
- **Thesis**: scams share a small set of mechanics: unsolicited contact, urgency, irreversible payment, escalating fees.
- **Mechanism**: a fake endorsement (cloned profile, deepfaked advertisement, group chat) leads to a platform showing invented gains; withdrawals require "tax" or "fee" payments that grow (100, 200, 50 euro) until the victim stops. Sunk-cost thinking makes victims pay one more fee; loss-chasing makes them seek risky recovery; poorly written mass emails select naive targets on purpose. Later waves target the same victim: recovery services asking advance fees, tax release, customs release. Romance scams invest months because attacker time is cheap. Pyramid schemes fail by arithmetic: 5 invitees per member give 5^10 = 9,765,625 people at level 10.
- **Trade-off**: strong verification rules cost opportunities and time.
- **Does not hold when**: contact is initiated by the recipient with an authorised intermediary.
- **Worked example**: a fraudulent "tax release" asks 20% of 3,000 euro = 600 euro before paying out; real withholding is deducted from payment, never prepaid.

## 11.2 Verification heuristics
- **Thesis**: cheap, mechanical checks defeat most scams.
- **Mechanism**: nobody seeks you out to offer profitable investments; urgency is the lever; hang up and call a number you looked up yourself; search "name + scam"; read the real domain from the first single slash backwards; two confirmations from the same channel are one confirmation; caller ID and display names can be spoofed; identical bank names in a transfer are not checked against the account number. Guaranteed returns above about 10% are impossible; realistic guaranteed returns track low risk-free rates (3-4% gross in Italy, snapshot 2023). Use authorised intermediaries listed in official registries.
- **Does not hold when**: the attacker already controls both channels (SMS and phone number).
- **Worked example**: a 50% deposit for a holiday rental requested to a third party's account fails the "would I hand cash to a stranger" test.

## 11.3 Cards, chargebacks and irreversibility
- **Thesis**: reversibility differs sharply by rail.
- **Mechanism**: cards and bank transfers can be disputed or recalled in many cases; a crypto transfer cannot after confirmation, so custody and fraud risk dominate price risk for beginners. A new withdrawal address with a 24-hour wait at serious venues gives time to cancel a scam. Credit cards give float and holds; prepaid cards cannot support pre-authorisations. Redundancy (two cards, two accounts) limits a suspension (ch01).
- **Does not hold when**: the user authorised the payment under deception; recovery then depends on speed and the institution's policy.

## 11.4 Why bank advice and wrapper products underperform
- **Thesis**: product-selling advice and complex wrappers transfer value to the seller.
- **Mechanism**: an adviser inside a bank recommends the bank's funds or ones paying retrocessions; an investment-style life policy costs 2-3% a year with exit penalties; wealth management adds about 1%; "capital guaranteed" is paid for through lower upside and issuer risk. A fee-only independent adviser is paid by the client, so conflicts are lower. If 4% of cost is taken from a 7% gross return, about 57% of the return disappears; at 8% equity return and 4% total cost half is lost; bonds at 3% with 2% cost lose two thirds.
- **Cost ladder (worst to best)**: investment-wrapped life policies (entry about 4%, annual cost, funds of funds, penalties), managed accounts (about 1% plus underlying 1-3%), guaranteed-capital policies, complex certificates, unlisted funds (entry up to about 4%, ongoing 1-3%), government bonds and savings bonds, index funds (0.1% bonds, 0.2-0.4% equities). Frequent switching (more than once a year) is a warning.
- **Worked example**: 1 million at 2% cost = 20,000 a year; about 219,000 over ten years with lost reinvestment. 100,000 in a policy costing 2-3% loses 2,000-3,000 each year.
- **Does not hold when**: pure protection insurance (term life) is bought for risk transfer, not as an investment.

## 11.5 Suitability, appropriateness, cost reporting
- **Thesis**: rules force disclosure and profiling, but legal duties are narrower than banks' practice.
- **Mechanism (Italy/EU, snapshot 2026)**: with advice, the intermediary must collect knowledge, experience, financial situation, loss capacity and objectives. With execution-only trading, it asks only knowledge and experience (appropriateness); if inappropriate, the law requires a warning, not a ban, but many banks block the purchase to limit litigation. Cost and charges reporting is due at least yearly: services, third-party payments and instrument costs, aggregated and in percentage points. The gross return shown excludes costs. Current-account stamp duty is due above a 5,000 euro average balance per account; securities stamp duty is 0.2% a year.
- **Does not hold when**: a different jurisdiction applies.

## 11.6 Choosing a securities account: fees and regime
- **Thesis**: commission structure and the tax regime matter more than headline price.
- **Mechanism**: fixed per-order fees suit large orders, percentage fees suit small ones. About 0.2% is good, above 0.7-1% is out of market. Break-even between fixed and variable = fixed fee / percentage rate. In the administered regime the domestic intermediary pays the tax; foreign brokers are declarative (ch06).
- **Worked example**: 0.19% with min 2.95 and max 19 euro: the minimum applies up to about 1,550 euro (2.95/0.0019), the cap from 10,000; a 9.95 flat fee beats it only above about 5,240 euro per order (Italy, snapshot 2025).

## 11.7 Insurance: expected value versus maximum loss
- **Thesis**: insure what could break the balance sheet, not what is merely annoying.
- **Mechanism**: expected loss = damage x probability; premiums exceed it. For a 15,000 euro car with 0.2% theft probability the expected loss is 30 euro; a premium of 60 earns the insurer 30 on average while it pays 14,970 on a hit; it works through pooling. Deductibles limit moral hazard; limits (caps) bound payouts; liability cover has very high limits because damage to third parties is open-ended.
- **Worked example**: home liability (third-party damage 40,000-200,000 euro) is worth it; theft or fire cover on a replaceable car is not.
- **Does not hold when**: the insured has more information than the insurer or values peace of mind.

## 11.8 Gambling: expected value and bookmaker margin
- **Thesis**: every bet has negative expected value for the player, and the loss grows with the number of plays.
- **Mechanism**: expected value = sum of outcome x probability. Bookmaker odds: the sum of 1/odds exceeds 100% and the excess is the margin; actual margin = 1 - 1/sum. Each outcome of a 1X2 bet is a separate bet. Roulette has no memory; doubling after losses needs capital that grows as 2^n for a tiny gain. Instant lotteries return only a fraction of stake.
- **Worked example**: odds 1.45, 4.75, 5.75: implied 69.0 + 21.1 + 17.4 = 107.4%; margin 6.9%; expected value about -0.069 per euro. A 5 euro instant ticket with expected return 3.53 loses about 1.47 (29%).
- **Does not hold when**: playing against other players (poker), where skill and rake dominate.

## 11.9 Behaviour: forecasts, panic, urgency
Most losses come from timing mismatches. Selling a ten-year product after three years crystallises losses; money needed soon belongs in safe assets. An investment pitched with urgency should be refused: another will come. Expense tracking at macro-category level, with cost per use for purchases; housing, car and energy dominate savings potential (typical household: housing about 40%, car about 33%).

## 11.10 Digital security
Each tool moves trust somewhere; none removes risk. A second factor must not transfer automatically to a new device; a six-digit code with three attempts has a 3-in-a-million guess chance. A VPN hides traffic from the local network and shifts visibility to the provider; it does not stop phishing. Anonymising networks are slow and often blocked; illegal marketplaces rely on escrow and untraceable coins and are largely scams.

## Common mistakes
- Paying a "fee" to release winnings.
- Judging a product by the guarantee without reading its cost.
- Calling back a number from the message instead of one you looked up.
