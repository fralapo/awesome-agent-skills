# Chapter 10: PIR, Flat Regimes and Income Structures

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. Rules and figures are snapshots (Italy, 2022-2026, year stated), to be re-checked against the rule in force. Choice of tax regime for a business is a professional accounting decision.

## Core Idea
In this model, three things are bundled here because they share one logic: **a tax wrapper or regime is worth it only if the tax it avoids exceeds its fixed costs and constraints**. The domestic personal savings plan ("PIR"), the flat-rate regime for small self-employed ("regime forfettario"), the progressive personal income tax ("IRPEF"), occasional self-employment ("prestazioni occasionali") and a limited company all follow the same arithmetic: net benefit = tax avoided minus fee, compliance cost and lost flexibility.

## 10.1 PIR: a tax wrapper with allocation constraints
- **Thesis**: the exemption is worth its constraints only if the product inside is competitive after fees.
- **Mechanism**: the domestic personal savings plan exempts gains and dividends (and, in the usual design, inheritance tax) if about 70% is invested in specified firms and holding periods and caps are respected. Rules include a minimum holding period, caps on annual and total contributions, allocation rules (a minimum share in domestic issuers, sub-shares outside the large-cap index, a cap per issuer) and timely reinvestment after sales. Failure typically makes all past tax due. Funds that guarantee compliance are the practical route because index changes can break a self-built portfolio. Idle cash can break compliance. Rules are fragile (nationality of firms, index changes).
- **Worked example (Italy, snapshot 2023)**: 5,000 at 5.9% gives gain 295, tax avoided 76.7 (26%), fee 60 under a flat quarterly fee with minimum, net benefit 16.7. Gross 4%: fund with 1.27% fees = about 2.73%; same bond directly taxed 26% = about 2.96%; in sample data the wrapped fund still did better because of the exemption.
- **Trade-off**: zero tax and diversification versus fees, concentration in domestic issuers and compliance risk.
- **Does not hold when**: capital is large enough (about 10,000+) and returns exceed the fee; then the wrapper pays. Below that, it may not.

## 10.2 IRPEF and the marginal rate
- **Thesis**: the progressive tax is the reference point for every deduction decision.
- **Mechanism**: snapshot 2025: marginal rates 23% / 35% / 43% by income bracket, plus regional and municipal surcharges (general knowledge). Deductions ("deduzioni") reduce taxable income at the marginal rate; credits ("detrazioni") reduce tax by a fixed percentage regardless of bracket (for example 19% on certain expenses). Employment-income allowances scale with taxable income.
- **Trade-off**: higher brackets make deductions more valuable and exits from flat regimes more expensive.
- **Does not hold when**: income is fully absorbed by allowances: marginal rate effectively zero.

## 10.3 Moving income into the ordinary base to use deductions
- **Thesis**: taxpayers under flat-tax regimes or with zero net tax cannot use deductions; selectively moving some income into the ordinary base can unlock them.
- **Mechanism**: substitute-tax income carries no deductions. Options include electing ordinary taxation where the law allows (for gains realised with a foreign intermediary), one-off occasional services clearly separate from the main activity, renouncing the flat rate on rental income, and treating an advance lump-sum of unemployment benefit as employment income. Each shifts income to a base where the marginal rate may exceed the flat 26%, so it pays only when offsettable deductions exceed the extra tax.
- **Trade-off**: higher nominal rate on that income versus use of otherwise wasted deductions; extra compliance.
- **Does not hold when**: deductions are small or the new base triggers other costs (higher social contributions above a threshold, loss of rent-cap protections).
- **Worked example**: compensation 100, documented expenses 50, withholding 20: taxable 50; if deductions zero the tax, the withholding is refunded.

## 10.4 Occasional self-employment
- **Thesis**: occasionality is a qualitative judgement, not a threshold.
- **Mechanism (Italy, snapshot 2022)**: continuity, advertising, dedicated tools and being the main income all indicate a non-occasional activity. Withholding is 20% of the gross as an advance; expenses can be fully deducted in the return; social contributions arise above 5,000 of annual fees; stamp duty applies above a small receipt amount.
- **Trade-off**: no VAT registration versus risk of reclassification.
- **Does not hold when**: the activity is continuous.

## 10.5 Flat regime for small self-employed
- **Thesis**: the flat regime replaces ordinary tax with a substitute tax computed on a deemed-profit percentage of revenue, at a reduced rate for the early years and a higher flat rate afterwards (general knowledge, snapshot 2025: 5% for the first five years if conditions are met, otherwise 15%; revenue ceiling around 85,000 euro with exit rules above it).
- **Mechanism**: no deduction of actual expenses; a coefficient by activity code gives the deemed margin; social contributions still apply. Because substitute tax carries no deductions, a taxpayer under it cannot use deductions or credits that need a tax base (ch07 pension deduction, ch08 energy credit).
- **Trade-off**: simplicity and low rate versus loss of deductions and ceiling rules.
- **Does not hold when**: actual expenses are high relative to revenue (ordinary regime may win).

## 10.6 Company versus sole proprietor
- **Thesis**: the cost of a limited company is double taxation unless income is retained or extracted through deductible channels.
- **Mechanism (Italy, snapshot 2026)**: corporate income tax 24% then 26% on dividends gives about 44% on distributed profit without the regional production tax; adding that tax (about 3.9%, simplified here as levied on profit although its base differs) gives about 47%; personal brackets reach 43% plus contributions to a ceiling. Alternatives to dividends: director fee (contributions about 26%), expense reimbursements, meal vouchers, fringe benefits, royalties with a forfeit deduction, severance. Fixed costs of a micro company can be around 5,500 euro a year.
- **Trade-off**: liability shield and deductible spending versus fixed costs and double tax.
- **Does not hold when**: income is low and a flat regime applies.

## 10.7 Participation exemption for companies holding shares
- **Thesis**: a capital company can reduce tax on share gains from 24% to about 1.2% if shares are held long enough.
- **Mechanism (Italy, snapshot 2025)**: only 5% of the gain is taxable at 24%. It applies to shares, not fund units. Simulations gave roughly 1.4% a year extra with 50 shares over 10 years, yet the index still won in about 18% of cases.
- **Trade-off**: fiscal gain versus stock-specific risk, corporate costs and administration.
- **Does not hold when**: the holding period or other conditions are missed.

## 10.8 Bundled insurance-investment and wealth products
Wrapping an investment in an insurance policy charges for the wrapper (entry load, insurance charge, management fee, surrender penalty). Compare the net IRR on dated cash flows (XIRR) with the plain components. A "guaranteed capital" is paid for through lower returns (ch11).

## 10.9 Tax drag from inflation
Taxes apply to nominal gains, so inflation raises the effective tax burden (fiscal drag). Contributions should be modelled in real terms (growing with inflation). Example: 1,000 real per month for 10 years at 7% nominal: about 121,000 contributed (real) and about 155,000 real capital; savings rate dominates (halving it roughly doubles time to a target).

## Common mistakes
- Joining a wrapper for the exemption without computing the fee against the avoided tax.
- Moving income to a regime without checking which deductions disappear.
- Treating "occasional" as "below a number".
