# Chapter 05: Capital Losses and Loss Harvesting

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. Figures and rules are snapshots (Italy, 2023-2025, year stated), to be re-checked against the rule in force.

## Core Idea
In this model, a realised loss is not cash: it is an **expiring tax credit** worth rate x loss, usable only against gains of the right category, in the realisation year plus four. Because it is worth only a fraction of the loss and selling at a loss still destroys capital, "harvesting" losses is a bookkeeping tool, not a goal. The valuable skill is knowing which losses are usable at all.

## 5.1 The loss as an expiring voucher
- **Thesis**: a loss is a credit of 26% (or 12.5% on government-type income) times the amount, valid for the realisation year plus four.
- **Mechanism**: 10,000 of loss shelters 2,600 of tax at 26%. Losses are personal, lapse at death and are portable between intermediaries only with a certificate. Use order: realise the loss before the gain; in the administered regime a gain realised first is taxed immediately and a later loss cannot undo it.
- **Trade-off**: selling at a loss saves tax but still destroys capital; the credit is a fraction of the loss.
- **Does not hold when**: the security disappears without any consideration (no onerous disposal means no loss).
- **Worked example**: loss 1,000 on one stock, gain 1,500 on another: tax on 500 only (130 instead of 390).

## 5.2 Which losses can offset which gains
| Loss from | Can offset | Cannot offset |
|---|---|---|
| Single stocks, certificates, options, ETN/ETC, ordinary bonds (miscellaneous income) | gains of the same family | capital income (fund gains, coupons, dividends) |
| Harmonised funds and ETFs (the loss is miscellaneous) | gains on stocks, certificates, etc. | gains on funds and ETFs |
| Costs and fees | treated as part of the loss | capital income |

- **Thesis**: the category of the **gain** matters as much as the loss.
- **Mechanism**: capital income is taxed gross. A fund gain therefore cannot be absorbed by a stock loss; only gains on miscellaneous-income instruments can.
- **Does not hold when**: a reform unifies categories (2023 enabling law proposed one basket with full netting; implementation unconfirmed), or a non-harmonised fund is involved.

## 5.3 The 12.5 / 26 scaling
- **Thesis**: losses on government-bond-type income are stored scaled so they offset 26%-taxed gains correctly.
- **Mechanism**: ratio 12.5/26 = 48.08%; 1,000 of loss is stored as 480.77 and then used against 26%-taxed gains. A loss on a government bond is therefore worth less as headline euro but exactly the 12.5% tax it would have saved elsewhere.
- **Trade-off**: fair in tax terms, confusing in the ledger.
- **Does not hold when**: the gain is also on government-type income (equal rate, no scaling needed).

## 5.4 Bonds, par and where the loss comes from
- **Thesis**: a bond bought above par generates a capital loss at maturity that is rarely usable.
- **Mechanism**: the investor pays tax on the larger coupons (capital income) and holds a loss usable only against same-category gains within about four years (Italy, snapshot 2025). A bond issued far below par or zero-coupon is taxed mostly as capital income on the difference with the issue price, with a theoretical-price interpolation (see ch03). A bond issued at par and bought below 100 shifts return from coupon to price gain, which helps an investor holding loss carry-forwards.
- **Worked example**: coupons 5%, 3%, 1% priced 109.22, 100, 90.8 for the same gross IRR near 3%: net about 2.42%, 2.64%, 2.65%.
- **Does not hold when**: large realised gains exist to offset, which makes the loss valuable immediately.

## 5.5 Regimes change the netting rules
- **Thesis**: administered and declarative regimes net differently.
- **Mechanism**: in the **administered** regime the intermediary nets at each sale in order and holds the ledger. In the **declarative** regime, gains and losses of the same year net regardless of order and payment falls a year later. A foreign broker forces declarative; losses can be carried forward by declaring them in the return.
- **Trade-off**: declarative gives timing and ordering freedom; administered gives zero paperwork and lower error risk.
- **Does not hold when**: losses sit at a different intermediary and no certificate has been issued.

## 5.6 Does harvesting pay?
- **Thesis**: harvest only when the loss is real, the sale was planned anyway, and a usable gain exists.
- **Mechanism**: simulations over random baskets compared selling the biggest winner, the smallest gain, or recycling losses to shield a big sale. Smallest gain was best in median terms; recycling lost to fees and complexity. "Take profit at target" is not a strategy; valid reasons to sell are overweight and need for cash.
- **Anti-avoidance caution** (general knowledge, to verify): the framework does not contain a statutory rule that forbids repurchasing after a loss sale, but selling and rebuying purely to produce a tax effect (or to dodge year-end stamp duty) risks being treated as avoidance and costs commissions.
- **Does not hold when**: losses are large and abundant or fixed commissions are tiny relative to size.

## 5.7 Average-cost manipulation
- **Thesis**: buying at a high price to raise the average cost before selling shifts tax forward rather than avoiding it.
- **Mechanism**: average cost = (cost x q + price x q')/(q + q'). It lowers tax per share but raises the quantity that must be sold if cash is needed, so total euro tax can rise (about doubled in one case). In the limit, average cost approaches the sale price and almost all proceeds are taxed like a dividend.
- **Does not hold when**: there are losses to offset anyway.

## 5.8 Exiting a costly product: the sunk-loss trap
- **Thesis**: if annual cost exceeds roughly 1%, leaving a fund or policy usually beats staying, even after an exit penalty.
- **Mechanism**: compare terminal wealth of exit-now plus a cheap index fund versus staying K years, using after-penalty proceeds, compounding at net return and subtracting tax net of usable losses. Penalties by tranche require annual recomputation.
- **Worked example**: 100,000 fell to 80,000; fund cost 2%, ETF cost 0.3%, 6% gross, 10 years: exit now about 129,661 versus waiting until break-even about 123,869.
- **Does not hold when**: costs are about 1% or less and losses are offsettable, or penalties step down very soon.

## 5.9 Tax at death and corporate holders
Losses lapse at death and are not inherited (ch09). For a **capital company**, the participation exemption makes only 5% of a share gain taxable at 24% (about 1.2%) if holding-period and other conditions are met; it applies to shares, not fund units (ch10).

## Common mistakes
- Believing a fund loss shelters a fund gain.
- Realising the gain before the loss in the administered regime.
- Letting a loss expire after the fifth year.
- Counting the nominal loss, not rate x loss, as the benefit.
