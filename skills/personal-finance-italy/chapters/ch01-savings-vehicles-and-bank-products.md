# Chapter 01: Savings Vehicles and Bank Products

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. Figures are snapshots (Italy, 2021-2026, year stated), to be re-checked against the rule in force.

## Core Idea
In this model, the bank layer of personal finance is decided by **structure and fragility** before price: which contract you hold (debit, credit, prepaid), how many single points of failure you have, what is guaranteed and what is merely segregated, and what a headline rate hides (lock-ups, conditions, stamp duty). Fees are small and negotiable; a frozen account is not.

## 1.1 Debit, credit and prepaid cards are different contracts
- **Thesis**: the three card types differ in who extends credit and what the issuer can reserve, not in the logo.
- **Mechanism**: a debit card ("carta di debito") debits the account at once; no balance, no payment. A credit card ("carta di credito") accumulates spending and settles in one block, typically around the middle of the following month, so the issuer lends interest-free until the due date; unpaid balances and instalment plans carry very high rates. A prepaid card ("carta prepagata") holds only preloaded money, extends no credit and cannot support a pre-authorisation hold. Merchants that reserve a deposit (car rental, some hotels, fuel pumps that block around 100 euro) need a true credit card.
- **Trade-off**: credit gives float, emergency liquidity and the ability to be held; it also invites overspending.
- **Does not hold when**: a combined "hybrid" card is used; the merchant may route it to the wrong network and it costs nearly as much as two cards.
- **Worked example**: a rental deposit of 1,000-2,000 euro is possible on credit and usually refused on debit or prepaid.

## 1.2 Redundancy, suspensions and know-your-customer reviews
- **Thesis**: one card or one account is a single point of failure, because issuers can suspend without explaining.
- **Mechanism**: recurring payments (subscriptions, utilities, insurance, tolls) pile up on one card; if it is suspended each must be found and re-pointed. Anti-money-laundering rules force periodic document requests, delivered inside the secure area, not by email. Blocks follow declared income that is inconsistent with inflows (declared 0-9,000 euro, then a 15,000 euro transfer), expired identity documents, or spending that no longer fits the creditworthiness assessed at issue. Suspensions often land at weekends despite "24/7" support claims.
- **Trade-off**: more products means more places to monitor and more annual fees; honest higher-band declarations avoid blocks but slightly raise the issuer's risk view.
- **Does not hold when**: the suspension is operational (maintenance, forgotten password).
- **Worked example**: a local-branch account suited to life events combined with a free online account with its own card gives redundancy if one is blocked; stale declared income or unanswered document requests are common triggers for restrictions, while declaring a higher band raises the issuer's risk view slightly.

## 1.3 Current accounts: costs, switching, joint accounts, stamp duty
- **Thesis**: ordinary fees are small and negotiable; the real decisions are structure and fragility.
- **Mechanism**: cost items are fixed fee, card fees, transfer fees and cash withdrawals. A branch account at about 60 euro a year all-in is reasonable, above 100 expensive (Italy, snapshot 2025). A statutory switching right lets the new bank close the old account and move balance and standing orders, but not securities or mortgages. A joint account ("conto cointestato") is indivisible property: a creditor of one holder can freeze all of it, and many banks freeze it on a death until succession is settled; a delegation to operate avoids this. Stamp duty ("imposta di bollo") of 34.20 euro a year is due on average balances above 5,000 euro, per account, so several accounts do not avoid it per se (Italy, snapshot 2023-2024), but splitting 7,000 euro across two accounts can.
- **Trade-off**: free online accounts cost less and offer weaker contact when blocked.
- **Does not hold when**: a joint account is used only for shared expenses with zero fees.

## 1.4 Deposit guarantee and securities custody
- **Thesis**: deposits and securities are protected by different mechanisms.
- **Mechanism**: deposits are covered up to 100,000 euro per depositor per bank by a guarantee scheme (slow to pay, not sized for large failures). Securities are not "guaranteed" but must be segregated from the intermediary's own assets; the risk is an irregular intermediary. Securities can be transferred between custodians free of charge but manually, blocking them for about a month. Above 100,000 euro in securities, a second custodian guards against blocks, lost credentials or forced transfer to another bank.
- **Trade-off**: more accounts add admin and, for foreign custodians, tax filings (see ch06).
- **Does not hold when**: balances are small and held at a single well-regulated custodian.
- **Worked example**: cash of 250,000 euro needs at least three banks to remain under the guarantee.

## 1.5 Deposit accounts and what the headline rate hides
- **Thesis**: a headline deposit rate is only as good as its lock-up, cancellation terms and stamp duty timing.
- **Mechanism**: interest is taxed at 26% (Italy, snapshot 2025), and the securities or deposit stamp duty is 0.2% of value at 31 December where it applies. Terms of 366 versus 360 days change the year-end duty. Promotional rates for new money carry conditions; an unconstrained "free" rate can be lowered at will. A lock that can be broken loses interest but not capital. A deposit linked to a current account is reachable only through transfers to the holder's own account, so it is harder to drain by third parties. Compare the net IRR over the same term and include the early-exit rate (near zero).
- **Worked example**: 3.25% gross over 366 days is about 2.20% net IRR; a comparable short government bond about 1.98% net, a difference of roughly 220 versus 196 per 10,000. A deposit at 2.8% with duty paid by the bank beats one at 3% with duty charged to the client (20 per 10,000 a year).
- **Trade-off**: short bonds are flexible but carry spreads and commissions (0.10% is already notable for one year).
- **Does not hold when**: promotional rates end or conditions change.

## 1.6 Postal savings and minors' bonds
- **Thesis**: state-guaranteed savings bonds ("buoni fruttiferi postali") trade liquidity for capital certainty and a lighter tax rate.
- **Mechanism**: interest is taxed at 12.5% against 26% on ordinary financial income (Italy, snapshot 2025). Step-up tables of "effective annual yield by holding period" are **cumulative**: 6% for 18 years means 1.06^18 = 2.85x from day one, not 6% on the last stretch. They cannot be sold, so there is no price risk and no liquidity. Bonds issued to a minor are protected from the buyer's creditors and locked until majority, with early redemption only through a judge (see ch09).
- **Worked example**: 1,000 at 6% for 18 years = 2,854 gross, about 2,622 after 12.5% tax on the gain.
- **Does not hold when**: rates change after issue or early redemption is needed.

## 1.7 Bonuses, referral income and cashback
- **Thesis**: annual-fee cards and promotional cashback pay only above a spending threshold, and referral bonuses may be taxable.
- **Mechanism**: break-even spend = annual fee / cashback rate; a 35 euro fee at 1% breaks even at 3,500 euro a year. Promotions capped monthly are small (3.5% on up to 250 euro = 8.75 euro a month for six months). Referral bonuses are commonly treated as miscellaneous income at the marginal rate (10 euro at 43% leaves about 5.7); cashback has been described as non-taxable after a tax-authority position (position, to verify).
- **Trade-off**: chasing promotions raises the chance of triggering risk reviews.
- **Does not hold when**: the spending would happen anyway and the card has no fee.

## 1.8 Cash, reserve and the time-horizon buckets
- **Thesis**: split money by **when it is needed**, not by product.
- **Mechanism**: (1) cash for a few months of ordinary spending; (2) a reserve for unpredictable expenses, sized from a list of last year's surprises plus the largest foreseeable shock, not "N months of salary"; (3) known spending in 1-10 years in instruments that return a known amount on a known date (see ch02); (4) money not needed for 10+ years. A fully "withdrawable" account that pays a month after the request fits urgent deadlines poorly. Under about 5,000 euro a deposit captures most of the benefit; with per-trade fees, purchases below roughly 1,000-3,000 euro lose a larger share to costs, so the trade-off is cost drag against delay.
- **Does not hold when**: wealth is so large that no plausible outflow forces a sale.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on one card or one bank for all recurring payments.
- Reading a promotional rate without the lock-up and the duty.
- Assuming securities are covered by the deposit guarantee.
- Declaring income that does not match actual inflows.
