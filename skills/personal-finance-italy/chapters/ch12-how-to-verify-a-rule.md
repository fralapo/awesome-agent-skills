# Chapter 12: How to Verify a Rule

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice. This chapter is about method: it contains no rate or deadline to rely on.

## Core Idea
In this model, every figure in this skill is a **dated snapshot**. Tax and financial rules in Italy change often: by budget law, by decree, by administrative guidance, by court ruling, and by the quiet change of an annual threshold. The reliable skill is not remembering a number but knowing how to find the rule in force for the **right year**, the **right person** and the **right instrument**, and how to document what you found.

## 12.1 The three dates that decide a rule
- **Thesis**: a rule has an effective date, a tax year, and a payment year, and they differ.
- **Mechanism**: a measure published in the official gazette may apply from the day after, from the next 1 January, or only to transactions after a stated date. The **tax year** is the year of the income or the transaction; the **payment year** is when the tax falls due (often the following year). Transitional rules protect holdings acquired earlier. Always ask: when was the asset bought, when was it sold, what was the rule at each date?
- **Trade-off**: more questions to answer versus a wrong application.
- **Does not hold when**: a rule is purely descriptive (a definition) and not date-bound.

## 12.2 Source hierarchy
| Level | What it is | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary law | law, decree, code published in the official gazette | ground truth, but may be amended |
| Consolidated text | an updated version of the law published by the state | current wording |
| Administrative guidance | circulars, rulings and answers by the national tax authority, the pension institute, the insurance and pension supervisors, the securities regulator | how the rule is applied in practice |
| Official forms and instructions | tax-return instructions for the year, payment-form codes | the operational detail and the current layout |
| The intermediary | broker, bank, fund manager, accountant | their own application and software behaviour; may be wrong |
| Secondary material | articles, forums, social posts, this skill | pointers only, never proof |

Rule: **go up the ladder until you reach primary law or official guidance for the year**. A forum thread that quotes the law is a pointer; the law is the proof.

## 12.3 A verification routine
1. **State the question precisely**: which instrument, which person (resident, employee, self-employed, company), which year, which event (buy, hold, sell, inherit).
2. **Find the rule**: official consolidated text and the tax authority's guidance for that year; for pensions, the pension supervisor's rules and the fund's regulations.
3. **Read the effective date and transitional rules.** Check whether a later measure amended it.
4. **Cross-check with the form instructions** for the same tax year; a rule that cannot be filled into the form is probably misremembered.
5. **Recompute with numbers**: build a one-page example by hand or in a spreadsheet and compare with what your intermediary or software shows. A gap of more than rounding means a method, FX or classification difference worth explaining.
6. **Write down the version**: source name, date read, year of application. This turns a memory into an audit trail.
7. **Escalate when stakes or ambiguity are high**: an accountant, a notary or a lawyer; a written answer to a formal inquiry to the authority.

## 12.4 Signals that a "rule" is shaky
- It is quoted without a year.
- It comes from a seller (a bank, a platform, an insurer) describing its own product's tax advantage.
- It sounds like a loophole.
- It differs from what your own return software shows.
- The number is a round percentage of an old threshold (thresholds and caps are updated by decree).
- It mixes categories: "capital income" and "miscellaneous income" are different legal bases (ch04).

## 12.5 Typical things that change
| Item | Why it moves |
|---|---|
| Rates of substitute taxes, exit taxes | budget decisions |
| Thresholds (stamp duty, allowances, caps on deductions, revenue ceilings of flat regimes) | decrees, indexation |
| Coefficients (pension transformation, mortality tables) | periodic revision |
| Form sections and layouts | yearly |
| Treatment of crypto-assets, wrappers and new products | new legislation, still settling |
| Deadlines | calendar, decrees and extensions |

## 12.6 Using intermediaries without delegating judgement
- **Thesis**: the intermediary is a source of data, not of truth.
- **Mechanism**: an administered-regime intermediary withholds tax by its own interpretation; if you disagree, ask for the basis in writing. A foreign broker's tax report is a starting point; the Italian rule on method (LIFO for shares, bonds and ETC/ETN versus weighted average cost for funds and harmonised ETFs) and FX may differ. A fund's "tax report" for residents may cover only part of the position.
- **Does not hold when**: the intermediary issues a formal certificate on a defined point (for example, a loss-transfer certificate).

## 12.7 Doing the arithmetic yourself
Many rules reduce to one line: net = gross x (1 - t), equivalent-yield = net/0.875, scaled loss = loss x 12.5/26, annualised return = (final/initial)^(1/n) - 1. A one-line check beats an hour of searching when the question is "does this make sense?". If the order of magnitude does not match, one of the inputs is probably wrong.

## 12.8 Using this skill responsibly
- Quote the **snapshot year** with any figure.
- Present rules as "in this snapshot" and say which official source to check.
- Never turn a rule into an instruction ("do this") for a person; frame as concepts and what to verify.
- State openly what is not known; do not fill gaps with plausible numbers.
- Differences between this skill and the current rule are resolved in favour of the current rule.

## Common mistakes
- Applying today's rule to an asset bought years ago.
- Trusting an intermediary's summary without the primary source.
- Forgetting to record where and when the rule was read.
