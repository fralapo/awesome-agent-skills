# Concept and Insight in Design Practice

Practitioner notes on the *idea* side of design work: how a creative director frames the problem, finds a concept, makes it memorable and hands it over. Visual direction belongs to `art-director`; craft and production belong to `graphic-designer`; screens and usability belong to `ux-ui-expert`.

## Frame the Problem
- A vague request ("fresh", "stands out but stays professional") is a communication problem, not a styling request. Ask what the work must make people think, feel or do, and who they are.
- Lead with insight, like a consultant: "what is the real problem we are solving?" beats "what design do you want?".
- Break a brand down beneath its literal product: a travel or rental service is about belonging, not about houses and beds. The concept lives one level below the product.
- **Problem sprint**: list every problem you can see in an existing design (too detailed, generic, dated, lost at small sizes); turn each problem into a fix; each fix is a candidate concept.

## Make It Memorable
- Layer meaning in one simple symbol: a single shape that reads as a letter, a place, a heart and a person carries several brand values with no extra detail.
- Give the viewer something slightly unresolved: a visual metaphor (objects arranged into a heart instead of a plain grid), an eerie absence (empty clothes holding a body shape), or a single twist on a familiar image (a head replaced by an off switch). The moment of working it out encodes the memory.
- Safe, clean, unoriginal ideas are forgotten; metaphorical, slightly odd ideas stick.
- Predictive empathy: judge the idea by what the audience feels a few seconds later, not at first glance.

## Generate Ideas
- Creativity is recombination of existing ideas, not innate talent. Absorb widely, keep a running log (including unrelated items), make something rather than only consuming, and look for unexpected overlaps between two ideas.
- Do not wait for inspiration: a repeatable system of stacking ideas and looking for overlaps produces results on demand.
- Swap a format's default context (where is it usually done?) and try the opposite for a fresh variation.
- Method-led exploration: tight time limits and rule sets generate concepts that rational planning cannot; meaning can emerge after the fact from accidental repetitions and resemblances. If your default instinct is safe, engineer the process (odd prompts, constraints) toward surprise.
- Concept and execution produced at the same instant, with no research or sketching, leave little room for discovery.

## Present and Persuade
- Expectations peak when you open well and drop when objections arrive; concede small points, close the meeting, avoid endless counter-proposals.
- A simple idea can be found quickly; persuading several stakeholders is the long part.
- Anticipate human reaction: layout and alignment can change real outcomes, not just taste.

## Hand-off
Give the art director the concept in one sentence, the audience, the single feeling it should produce and the strategic filters. Ask for the visual direction; do not specify fonts and colours yourself.
