# Glossary

**Absolute / conditional contraband** - goods usable only for war versus goods usable for both; a category of blockade law (ch11)
**Alternation (consuls)** - monthly or daily rotation of the lead between two equal magistrates (ch10)
**Amplifier** - a condition that makes a shock worse without starting it (ch01, ch08)
**Axe war** - low-level Maya raid to take captives and kill nobles; contrasted with total war (ch05, ch06)
**Baray** - large embankment reservoir of the Khmer water system (ch03, ch06)
**Blank cheque** - promise of unconditional support given to an ally before a crisis (ch12)
**Blockade, distant** - closing the enemy's sea routes far from its ports (ch11)
**Bypassing** - neutralising a garrison by air and sea and leaving it cut off (ch09, ch14)
**Cascading failure** - one failure shifts load and causes the next (ch02, ch04)
**Client ruler** - local king who accepts a stronger state's influence in exchange for protection (ch09)
**Collapse** - loss of a defined variable (court, city, trade, writing, population); name which (ch01)
**Coloni** - late Roman tenants bound to the land, forerunners of serfs (ch13)
**Complexity** - number and cost of specialised roles and institutions a society carries (ch02)
**Continuity** - what people, places and memory persist after the break (ch02, ch08)
**Correlated shock** - one that hits many partners at once, defeating mutual aid (ch04)
**Dark age** - period with few records; the silence is a fact to explain (ch01, ch06)
**Design range** - the set of conditions a system was built to withstand (ch02, ch03)
**Destruction layer** - ash and rubble stratum showing fire; it does not identify the cause (ch01)
**Diminishing returns** - each added unit of output costs more (poorer soil, shorter fallow) (ch02)
**Disanalogy** - a difference that limits an analogy (ch07)
**Elite competition** - nobles and local families compete for status and revenue (ch05)
**Export-commodity dependence** - reliance on one product sold abroad (ch04)
**Fallow** - rest period for farmland between crops (ch03)
**Feedback loop** - effect that feeds its own cause; accelerates decline (ch02)
**Final decree** - Roman senate's emergency authorisation to magistrates, in effect martial law (ch10)
**God-king** - ruler whose authority rests on a ritual identifying him with a god (ch05, ch06)
**Hub** - the port or city through which a lifeline passes (ch04)
**Hydraulic city** - settlement whose food depends on engineered water control (ch03, ch06)
**Ice core** - cylinder of ice recording dust, salt, sulphate, snow (ch01, ch03)
**Imperium** - Roman magistrate's authority to judge and command (ch10)
**Inner and outer frontier** - zone where an army can be supplied and tolerated versus zone of influence only (ch09)
**Interdependence** - mutual reliance among states through trade, alliance, marriage (ch04)
**Karst** - porous limestone terrain with few surface rivers (ch03, ch06)
**Legitimacy** - acceptance of authority, tied to outcomes in sacral rule (ch05)
**Lifeline** - the supply link a place cannot do without (ch04)
**Little Ice Age** - cold period starting in the late thirteenth century; causes debated (ch03, ch06)
**Long fuse** - decades-long background causes of a war (ch12)
**Marginal land** - land near the range limit of the crop or livestock system (ch03)
**Medieval Warm Period** - warm spell roughly 900 to 1300 in the north Atlantic region (ch03, ch06)
**Minimum viable size** - population needed to maintain a system (ch02)
**Monument series** - dated carved monuments counted over time (ch01, ch05)
**Mos maiorum** - Roman customary norms of conduct, unwritten (ch10)
**Mutual veto** - each of two officials can block the other (ch10)
**Narrative layer** - what is told by sources or later retellings, not corroborated (ch01)
**Neutral intermediary** - non-belligerent state through which an enemy's trade continues (ch11)
**Overextension** - commitments exceed the capacity to replace losses (ch02, ch09)
**Pollen record** - plants indicated by pollen in sediment (ch01, ch03)
**Proxy** - physical record standing in for climate or environment (ch01)
**Reparations** - payments imposed on a defeated state for damage, set by a later commission (ch11)
**Replacement rate** - how fast a side restores manpower, equipment and inputs (ch09, ch11)
**Sacral kingship** - rule justified by the ruler's role toward gods and nature (ch05)
**Sea Peoples** - name given in Egyptian texts to groups of seaborne raiders; identity debated (ch04, ch06)
**Serfdom** - tied tenancy with obligations to a lord and some legal protection (ch13)
**Simplification** - reorganisation to a simpler form rather than extinction (ch02)
**Slash-and-burn** - farming by clearing and burning forest patches, then fallow (ch03)
**Star war** - Maya total war aiming at conquest and destruction (ch05, ch06)
**Stress accumulation** - build-up of strains released by a trigger (ch02)
**Substitution shock** - fall in value of a good when a better substitute enters the market (ch04)
**Supply radius** - distance an army can operate from its base given supply (ch09)
**Survivorship bias** - studying only what remains or survived (ch02, ch07)
**Terminal Classic** - late phase of the Classic Maya, when monument records end (ch06)
**Theravada** - strict Buddhist school with austere monks; spread widely in the Khmer lands (ch05, ch06)
**Trigger** - event that starts a break in a vulnerable system (ch01, ch02)
**Turnip winter** - winter of 1916-17 famine in the central empires (ch11)
**Underlying vulnerability** - a long-present weakness that makes a trigger lethal (ch01)
**Unity of command** - one authority directs all forces in a theatre (ch09)
**War aims** - political and economic goals a government states for a war, changing over time (ch12)
