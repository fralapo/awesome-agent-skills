# History and Geopolitics: Collapse, Resilience, War and Institutions
**Chapters**: 14 | **Generated**: 2026-11-14

## Role and Boundaries

This skill **decides how to reason about the decline and end of societies**: which layer of evidence a claim rests on, which model fits, which causes are underlying, which amplify and which trigger, what is contested, and how far a past case can inform a present one. It gives models, case sheets and checklists; the user owns the question and the conclusion.

| This skill decides | This skill hands off |
|---|---|
| Evidence layer of each claim (find, inference, narrative, debated) | Reading specialist literature or primary texts in the original languages → specialist sources or professionals |
| Role of each proposed cause and the grade of its evidence | Writing a full narrative history, a school essay or fiction → writing skills |
| Which collapse model applies and what to look for | Forecasting a present-day crisis or investment outcome (the history gives mechanisms, not probabilities) → domain specialists |
| Case sheets for about twenty-four societies, wars and crises, and a method for new ones | Visual layout of maps, timelines or a deck from the analysis → `presentation-design` |
| Strategy and logistics, institutions of a republic, war and economy, causes of a systemic war: as models with contested claims | Current military, political or economic decisions and forecasts → domain specialists; operational planning → professionals |
| Limits of analogy to modern systems | Technical failure patterns in software systems (cascades, retries, load shedding) → `software-architecture` |

**Stay in lane.** This skill does not invent dates, figures or quotations. Figures in the chapters are estimates; where the evidence is unclear the chapter says "unclear". Statements that come from general knowledge rather than the case sheets are marked as such. The cases are a modest sample: four societies in depth and about twenty shorter sheets, drawn from sources that mix evidence with storytelling, so the chapters flag which points are weaker.

## Facts vs Positions

- **Evidence**: something found or measured (burn layers, skeletons, tree rings, isotopes, dated monuments, a letter's text).
- **Inference**: a reasoned step from evidence; name the step.
- **Narrative**: told by sources or later retellings, not corroborated (epics, sagas, anecdotes, rulers' boasts).
- **Debated**: credible alternatives exist; name them. Never present a debated cause as settled, and never offer a single cause.
- Positions are written "In this model..." or "(position)". Every concept in the chapters has four fields: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**. Use the last one before applying the idea: if the limit applies, drop it.

## The Core Principles (recurrence order)

1. **Separate the layers.** Material record, inference, narrative; label each claim.
2. **Name what collapsed.** Court, city, trade, writing, population: they move at different speeds.
3. **Several causes with roles.** Underlying, amplifier, trigger, symptom; no single villain.
4. **Where it is debated, say so.** Report positions and the evidence each rests on.
5. **Variability, not averages.** Societies fail at the extremes and at swings their design did not cover.
6. **Surplus and complexity.** A thin surplus carries a thin top; a fall in surplus removes the top first.
7. **Coupling carries shocks.** Trade, alliances and shared water systems lower local risk and raise regional risk.
8. **Maintenance is a standing cost.** Neglected infrastructure and a shrinking workforce form a loop.
9. **Legitimacy is a buffer.** Authority tied to outcomes weakens when outcomes fail; watch indicators.
10. **Compare with survivors.** Ask who took the same shock and did not fall, and why; allow for luck.
11. **Look for continuity.** Most collapses are simplifications; say who stayed, what was reused.
12. **Analogy argues about mechanism.** Translate, test, list disanalogies; no forecasts.
13. **Radius, return, command, replacement.** Ask what supply can reach, what holding ground yields, who commands, who replaces losses.
14. **Norms need enforcement.** A system run on restraint fails when a powerful actor breaks it at no cost.
15. **Stated war aims and blame are later layers.** Aims declared during a war, and post-war blame, are not causes of its start.
16. **A contested claim stays contested.** Blockade effects, reparations, moral decline and premeditation are positions; report each with its evidence.

## Scope & Limits

Practical theory about the collapse and resilience of societies with four detailed case studies (the classic Maya, the Khmer empire and Angkor, the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean, the Norse colonies of Greenland), plus chapters on strategy and logistics, the institutions and crisis of a republic (Roman case), war and economy and the causes of a systemic war, and about twenty shorter case sheets (Athens and Sparta, British decline, Yugoslavia, the third-century Roman crisis, a steppe empire and its successors, the Inca, the Ottoman rise, Iranian administration, slavery and serfdom, Iran-Iraq, the Imjin War, a river siege, Rome's two frontiers, the Eastern Front in 1941, the central Pacific, the pre-1914 naval race, the Great Northern War, the Irish famine). Do not extend a sheet past what it states. The case literature mixes evidence with storytelling; the chapters keep the analytical content, flag weaker or debated claims, and drop narrative and personal detail (people are referred to by role). Contested topics (the weight of the blockade, whether reparations were payable, whether the settlement caused the next war, moral decline of a republic, premeditation of 1914) are given as positions with evidence. Not covered: island societies such as Easter Island, many other collapses (Han China, Egypt, the Aztecs), the Yugoslav wars themselves, the later course of most wars, economic modelling, quantitative climate reconstruction, and any detailed reading of original inscriptions or documents. Dates, populations and figures are estimates as reported; the skill gives no forecast and no claim about any present-day outcome.
