# Chapter 8: Checklist for Analysing a New Case

## Core Idea
A fixed sequence prevents the usual mistakes: starting from the ending, choosing one cause, mixing story with finds. Use it on any society, city, empire or colony that declined or ended, or on a claim about one. The steps are **position**; the tests in each step come from the cases.

## 8.1 Step order
1. **Frame the question.** What exactly ended (court, city, trade, settlement, writing, language, whole population)? Over what span? Who is asking and why (teaching, comparison, decision)?
2. **List the sources by layer.** Material finds, natural archives, documents, later narrative. Note who produced each and what is missing (perishable records, unexcavated areas).
3. **Date the sequence.** Last dated object, last record, last archaeological layer, traditional "fall" date. Give errors. Mark the difference between event and process (2.8).
4. **Describe the baseline.** Environment envelope, food base, water, trade lifelines, political structure, ruler's legitimacy basis, military setting (chapters 3 to 5).
5. **Extract stresses and triggers.** For each: underlying, amplifier, trigger; evidence; status (strong, plausible, speculative, debated).
6. **Run the models.** Stress and trigger (2.1), surplus (2.2), cascade (2.3), feedback (2.4), fragility (2.5), simplification (2.6), survivors (2.7), contingency (2.9). Note which fit.
7. **Compare with neighbours.** Who was under the same shock and did not fall; why (2.7)?
8. **Check continuity.** Who stayed, what was reused, what was remembered, who moved on.
9. **Write the sheet** (below) and label each claim: Evidence, Inference, Narrative, Debated (1.10).
10. **If making an analogy**, apply 7.11.

## 8.2 Questions by theme
| Theme | Questions |
|---|---|
| Environment | What is the worst year the system was built for; what actually happened; at what scale does each proxy speak? |
| Food | Which staple, how long storable, what share of non-producers, what protein source? |
| Water | What infrastructure, who maintains it, what minimum labour, what happens in the opposite extreme? |
| Trade | Which goods are essential, how many sources, who is the hub, who is the buyer of exports? |
| Alliance | Are partners hit by the same shock; where are their forces? |
| Legitimacy | What is the ruler's promise; what indicators of its loss; how often did religion or dynasty change? |
| Elites | What are the incentives for extraction; who pays; how large is the non-producing class? |
| War | Ritual, total or opportunistic; symptom or cause; destruction layer attributed how? |
| Technology | Who gains or loses from a new tool; is the timing before or after the break? |
| Adaptation | What did people change, with what evidence; what could they not copy and why? |
| Records | Whose voice survives; what perishable records are lost? |
| Aftermath | What continued; where did people go? |

### 8.2b Extra questions for wars and republics
| Theme | Questions |
|---|---|
| Logistics | What is the supply radius; which lane is critical; who controls it (ch09.1, 9.2)? |
| Return | What does holding the ground yield; what lever does the attacker have (9.4)? |
| Command | Who commands; who can overrule; what is the political order (9.8)? |
| Replacement | Can each side replace losses and buy inputs; who is the patron or creditor (9.9, 11.5)? |
| Norms and enforcement | Which rules are customary; who enforces; what is the emergency rule (ch10)? |
| Armed force | Whose loyalty; how paid; what happens to an official who leaves office (10.8)? |
| Origin of a war | Layer table: long fuse, medium term, trigger, decisions, aims after the fact (ch12) |
| Peace and economy | What did the settlement do to debts and trade; what is the counterfactual (ch11.6)? |

## 8.3 Evidence grading
| Grade | Meaning | Example form |
|---|---|---|
| A | Physical, dated, several independent finds agree | Burn layers at many sites plus letters |
| B | One strong physical record at the site | Tree rings at the city; isotopes from burials |
| C | A document by an interested party | A king's boast; a chronicle written later |
| D | Inference from analogy or from absence | "Probably a rebellion" |
| E | Later narrative, legend, folk tale | Epic poem; saga; an anecdote of a body in animal skins |

## 8.4 Sheet template
- **Scope**: unit, place, dates, what ended.
- **Setting**: environment, base economy, structure.
- **Causes table**: cause, role, evidence, grade, status.
- **Narrative layer**: stories to treat with care.
- **Contested**: positions and why the record cannot settle them.
- **Continuity**: who and what persisted.
- **Analogy notes**: what can and cannot be carried over.

## 8.5 Red flags in a claim
- One cause stated with certainty ("the invaders did it", "climate killed them").
- A date for the fall to the year, without the underlying record.
- A destruction layer assigned to a named enemy without a text or weapon type.
- "Disappeared without a trace" without a search for continuity.
- A population figure without a basis.
- A moral lesson that fits too neatly.
- A reading of a ruler's boast as a report.

## 8.6 When the evidence is thin
Say what is known, list what would settle the question (a core, a dated layer, a text), and give the most probable reading with its competitors. Never fill the gap with a vivid story. If the case is not covered by the sheets in chapter 6, use steps 1 to 10 and say that no prepared sheet exists.
