# Patterns

Concrete, repeatable techniques. Each entry: When to use / How / Trade-offs.

## Organise / Design Split
**When**: estimating weekly capacity.
**How**: plan admin (timelines, quotes, invoices, email, calls) and design as two explicit jobs in the week, instead of treating admin as interruption.
**Trade-offs**: shows that only part of the week is billable design time, which can feel like less output.

## Build Your Freelance Dimension
**When**: deciding which services to keep, drop or add.
**How**: map strengths, weaknesses, aspirations, context and skills; start somewhere, set limits, allow change, get help, borrow ideas without copying.
**Trade-offs**: the shape keeps changing; there's no fixed model to follow.

## Awareness Before Sale
**When**: a niche service gets no requests even though it's on offer.
**How**: publish educational content on the niche (bad vs good, why design isn't just pretty/ugly), convert attention into a first event (webinar, workshop), then package services.
**Trade-offs**: much slower than selling a recognised service like a logo.

## Few Things Done Well
**When**: services scatter energy or blur your positioning.
**How**: drop misaligned services (e.g. brand identity), refer them to trusted colleagues, keep one adjacent service you enjoy.
**Trade-offs**: turning down paid work in the short term.

## Scope-First Quote
**When**: a client says "it's just a few slides".
**How**: discovery call on audience, occasion, deadline, slide count, brand assets, who edits later; probe hidden scope; quote immersion, style research and revisions as phases; plan ~1 month part-time.
**Trade-offs**: longer pre-sale; some clients expect days, not a month.

## One Project at a Time Under Deadline
**When**: a tight deadline (e.g. ~2 weeks for a deck).
**How**: dedicate every working day to that single project and decline parallel tight requests.
**Trade-offs**: lost revenue from declined work.

## Hands-On Consultation
**When**: freelancers or small businesses need help with their slides.
**How**: receive the deck beforehand, choose the slides with the biggest gain, redesign them on screen while explaining, leave a before/after file; cap at 1–1.5 h.
**Trade-offs**: tiring (constant topic switching, no depth), but transformative for the client.

## Email as the Official Channel
**When**: onboarding any client.
**How**: state working hours and channels at the start; move WhatsApp/DM conversations to email; share your phone only if you choose.
**Trade-offs**: some clients find email slower; traceability wins.

## Person / Professional / Project Separation
**When**: harsh or reason-less feedback.
**How**: treat criticism as aimed at the role and the project, not you; explain the why of each choice to build trust.
**Trade-offs**: takes practice; emotional client management remains a challenge.

## Delegate with a Fixed Budget
**When**: admin or footage editing eats creative time.
**How**: hand fiscal work to an accountant from day one and editing to an editor with a set budget.
**Trade-offs**: a cost that must be planned, repaid by mental space.

## Learn → Schematise → Apply
**When**: setting up Notion or any organisational tool.
**How**: study templates for their reasoning, answer the four questions on paper (databases, relations, properties, views), then build from zero.
**Trade-offs**: slower than duplicating a template, but the result fits your needs.

## Projects as the Hub
**When**: designing a freelancer workspace.
**How**: one Projects database with generic properties for all service types, related to Clients (limit 1) and Notes (many-to-many); hours, invoices and CRM hang off it.
**Trade-offs**: generic statuses can't capture every service-specific step.

## One View per Question
**When**: adding any database view.
**How**: name the question (what's active? when am I free? how much this year?), show only the properties it needs, filter and sort for it; use a Timeline for date questions.
**Trade-offs**: more views to maintain.

## Explicit Waiting Statuses
**When**: projects stall without visibility.
**How**: add *Feedback* (waiting for the client) and *To invoice* to the project status flow.
**Trade-offs**: statuses must be updated by hand.

## Linked Notes Inside the Project
**When**: client calls and meetings.
**How**: add a Linked view of the Notes database in each project, filtered to that project; notes created there are pre-linked.
**Trade-offs**: the filter must be set on each project page.

## Minutes-Based Hour Tracker
**When**: tracking time against hour budgets or packages.
**How**: Tracker database with Start/End; `dateBetween(End, Start, "minutes") / 60`; rollup Sum into Projects; Hours remaining and Hours % with a percent bar; filter views by End (Today, Week, Yesterday).
**Trade-offs**: logging discipline needed; treat it as a guide, not an obligation.

## Energy Field Gate
**When**: deciding whether to accept a new project.
**How**: rate each project Low/High energy; overlap two Lows freely, decline when High projects already overlap.
**Trade-offs**: subjective rating.

## Quarter Gantt Before Promising
**When**: a new client asks for a start date.
**How**: open the Gantt at Quarter zoom; if several deliveries close in the next month, propose a later start.
**Trade-offs**: an honest later date may lose impatient clients.

## Cash-In Invoice Year
**When**: income counts on payment date (e.g. Italy's forfettario).
**How**: store issue and cash-in dates; compute Year from cash-in; rollup project amounts plus a manual amount for stamp duty and deposits.
**Trade-offs**: duplicates data also held in the accountant's portal.

## CRM Pipeline with Past Clients
**When**: managing leads and quiet periods.
**How**: Call → Quote → Awaiting reply → Client → Lost, plus Past clients; log a dated step timeline and to-dos; recontact past clients when work is slow.
**Trade-offs**: needs monthly pruning of silent leads.

## Yourself as a Client
**When**: content or personal projects silently eat billable time.
**How**: create a project with you as client and track its hours like client work.
**Trade-offs**: makes the real cost of content visible, which can be uncomfortable.

## Single Content Pipeline
**When**: publishing on several platforms.
**How**: one content database with Calendar, per-platform, Ideas by series and Pipeline (To write → To design → To record → To edit); mark sponsored pieces with a checkbox.
**Trade-offs**: one database grows large; views keep it usable.

## Calendars as Colour Labels
**When**: setting up any digital (or paper) calendar.
**How**: one calendar per area with its own hex colour; never recolour single events; optional emoji in titles.
**Trade-offs**: setup time; many calendars to toggle.

## Block + Goal
**When**: every design, writing or recording block.
**How**: 1–4 h blocks (3–4 h on one design project), each with a Google Task stating its goal, partial or deliverable-based.
**Trade-offs**: goals may be missed; move leftovers without guilt.

## Energy-Mapped Week
**When**: building the weekly template.
**How**: creative work in your freshest hours, calls in the afternoon, calm tasks on draining days; 15-min buffers between events; fixed stop time.
**Trade-offs**: client calls don't always fit the afternoon slot.

## Batching
**When**: recording, editing, admin, email.
**How**: group similar tasks in one block (e.g. 2–3 content pieces in a row).
**Trade-offs**: requires scripts or material ready in advance.

## Notebook Triage
**When**: days full of small interruptions.
**How**: write micro-tasks in a paper notebook, clear them at day end or next day, and record what you actually did.
**Trade-offs**: another place to check besides the digital tools.

## Good-Enough Loop Breaker
**When**: a slide absorbs hours without converging.
**How**: return to the paper sketch, gather one limited batch of references, make a bounded number of versions, choose the one that best serves the client's audience and stop.
**Trade-offs**: delivering something you find imperfect.

## Paper First
**When**: the start of every design task.
**How**: wireframes and storyboards on paper or a synced tablet notes app before opening software.
**Trade-offs**: an extra step that makes the computer phase linear.

## Design in Your Tool, Export to Theirs
**When**: the client's software isn't your favourite.
**How**: build the first version in the tool you master, then export to PowerPoint or Google Slides.
**Trade-offs**: conversion work after export.

## Low-Friction Recording Corner
**When**: content production keeps getting postponed.
**How**: camera and softboxed LED panel left set up, heavy curtain for reverb, screen-recording app and phone tripod, monitor light.
**Trade-offs**: dedicated space and gear.

## "Why" Document Anchor
**When**: follower numbers start driving decisions.
**How**: write your target audience and reasons for creating; re-read before creating and in the yearly review.
**Trade-offs**: none significant.

## Yearly Review with Two Goals
**When**: January or any natural pause.
**How**: one wellbeing goal, one revenue goal (beat last year by any amount), one lesson per main project, channel review, costs named, resolutions.
**Trade-offs**: takes a quiet half-day of honest reflection.

## Pause at the Goal
**When**: the year's revenue target is met early.
**How**: use the slack to recharge, adjust strategy and do the postponed personal project instead of grinding out of fear.
**Trade-offs**: less revenue that year; a business that never pauses never evolves.
