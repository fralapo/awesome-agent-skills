## Core Frameworks

**Two jobs in one week**: *organising* (timelines, quotes, invoices, email, calls) and *designing*. Plan both explicitly. A typical week puts own projects and content on Monday and client work Tuesday to Thursday.

**Your freelance "dimension"**: shape the job from your strengths, weaknesses, aspirations, context and skills, and allow yourself to reshape it. **Niche by specialisation** (e.g. presentation design) works, but **awareness comes before the sale**: educate the market first.

**Scope before you quote**: a client deck takes ~1 month part-time (learning the subject, researching a style, revisions). Ask scope questions up front, because "small" projects grow. Take one project at a time when deadlines are tight. Email is the official client channel. Set working hours and tell clients what they are.

**Service formats**: consultations of 1–1.5 h, with slides received in advance and fixed live so the client sees a before/after. Training from 2–4 h up to multi-day programmes (in-person is worth more, so cap travel). Content creation is treated as a project where you are the client.

**Person / Professional / Project**: criticism targets the work and the role, not you. Do a few things well and pass off-topic jobs to other designers. Delegate admin to an accountant and video editing to an editor, each with a set budget.

**Notion workspace: Learn → Schematise → Apply**: answer four questions on paper (which databases, which relations, which properties, which views), then build. The minimum system is **Projects** (the hub) ↔ **Clients** (1 per project) ↔ **Notes** (many-to-many). **One view per question**: Overview, a Status board, a Gantt at Quarter zoom, and a Revenue view filtered to active or done projects. Make the statuses *Feedback* and *To invoice* explicit. CRM pipeline: Call → Quote → Awaiting reply → Client → Lost/Past.

**Hour tracker**: `dateBetween(End, Start, "minutes") / 60`, because "hours" rounds 30 minutes up to 1. Per project, track hours done, hours remaining and % used (over 100% means an overrun). Add an Energy (Low/High) field to decide whether to accept new work.

**Time management**: one Google Calendar per area, used as a colour label. Work in **1–4 h blocks (typically 4 h; 3–4 h on one design project)**. Every block gets a **goal** in Google Tasks. Batch similar tasks, put high-energy work where you're freshest and calls in the afternoon, and leave buffers between events. A paper notebook catches micro-tasks, cleared at day end, and records what you actually did. Move leftovers to tomorrow without guilt.

**Good-enough loop breaker**: once you've given 100%, stop iterating. Perfectionism loops cost more than they add.

**Tools and workspace**: keep organisation tools (email, Notion, Calendar, Drive) separate from design tools. **Paper first**: wireframes and storyboards. Design in the tool you master, then export to the client's. Take inspiration from websites (Behance, Dribbble, Awwwards, Pinterest boards per project), not other decks. Set up the workspace to remove friction: gear left set up, diffuse light, basic acoustics, sit/stand.

**Growth**: consistency plus a format strategy grows an audience. Keep a written "why" for whom you create. Rough split: 2 days a week for content, 3 for clients. **Yearly review**: one wellbeing goal, one revenue goal (beat last year by any amount), one lesson per project. When you hit the goal early, pause and rethink instead of grinding out of fear. Say no to work that clashes with your values.
