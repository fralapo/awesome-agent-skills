# Patterns

Educational, not medical advice. Recurring situation -> reasoning move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets. Entries marked (position) are practitioner judgment, not settled evidence. No entry gives a dose, a diagnosis or an exercise for a condition.

## 1. "The test was positive, so it is that structure"
**When**: a manoeuvre provokes pain and a label follows.
**Move**: ask for base rate, sensitivity, specificity and likelihood ratio; remember that many muscles are active in one manoeuvre; prefer clusters after a narrowing history. [ch01]
**Cost**: slower, less certain answers.
**Not when**: a rare strong sign with a high specificity.

## 2. Testing when you are already almost sure
**When**: the estimate is above about 80% or below about 20%.
**Move**: skip tests that cannot cross the decision line; keep screening for serious conditions at much lower thresholds. [ch01]
**Cost**: patient expectation of a hands-on exam.
**Not when**: the cost of missing it is high.

## 3. "The abstract says it works"
**When**: a finding is shared from a summary.
**Move**: read methods and results, effect size, comparison, risk of bias; ask whether it is statistically or clinically significant. [ch01]
**Cost**: time.
**Not when**: a guideline or systematic review has already done it (still skim methods).

## 4. A group difference presented as a cause
**When**: people with pain differ from people without on some measure.
**Move**: ask direction (pain changing the measure?) and whether a prospective design exists. [ch01, ch09]
**Cost**: fewer tidy stories.
**Not when**: a clear acute mechanism.

## 5. Pain taken as a damage reading
**When**: "it hurts, so something is wrong with the tissue".
**Move**: separate nociception from pain; check context, sleep, stress, beliefs, and whether pain spreads or sensitises. [ch02]
**Cost**: nuance may sound dismissive; validate the experience first.
**Not when**: an acute injury with clear tissue involvement.

## 6. Structural language that frightens
**When**: "your pelvis is out", "bone on bone", "crumbling".
**Move**: replace with accurate, context-framed wording; ask what the person understands; build trust before challenging beliefs (position). [ch02, ch03]
**Cost**: takes sessions.
**Not when**: a real structural problem should be named plainly.

## 7. Spread pain with light-touch sensitivity
**When**: pain beyond a segmental area, evoked hypersensitivity, comorbid symptoms.
**Move**: consider a nociplastic mechanism as a hypothesis and screen with a symptom questionnaire; do not label from a questionnaire alone. [ch02]
**Cost**: classification needs expertise.
**Not when**: local pain only.

## 8. Two similar patients with different risk
**When**: same findings, different life context.
**Move**: look for yellow flags (beliefs, avoidance, mood, support, work) and address modifiable ones early with validated tools. [ch03]
**Cost**: broader assessment.
**Not when**: used to dismiss a physical problem.

## 9. A pattern that looks like serious disease
**When**: night or rest pain, weight loss, fever, trauma plus severe pain, steroid history, neurological change.
**Move**: read the pattern, not one flag; refer for medical review; keep screening during care. [ch03]
**Cost**: some unnecessary referrals.
**Not when**: a single weak flag with an obvious benign explanation.

## 10. "Too much too soon"
**When**: pain after a sudden jump in volume, frequency or intensity.
**Move**: ask whether load exceeded current capacity, and what the recent change in volume, frequency or intensity was; consider that the "just right" zone is individual. [ch05]
**Cost**: patience.
**Not when**: fracture or acute structural injury.

## 11. Complete rest after overload
**When**: previously tolerated activities start to hurt.
**Move**: ask whether the load-tolerance envelope has shrunk, which activities are still tolerated, and what a clinician would advise about re-expanding it. [ch05]
**Cost**: slower perceived recovery.
**Not when**: protection is medically required.

## 12. Pain gone, rehab dropped
**When**: relief appears before tissue has finished remodelling.
**Move**: remember remodelling continues for weeks to years, longer for poorly vascularised tissue (position). [ch05]
**Cost**: sustained effort.
**Not when**: the person has returned to full chosen activity without relapse.

## 13. "Tendonitis, so rest and ice"
**When**: persistent tendon pain.
**Move**: consider the term "tendinopathy"; ask about loading rate and dose relationship; consider how the next-day response is read and by whom; question how much imaging adds. [ch05, ch08]
**Cost**: less certainty about the tissue state.
**Not when**: sudden loss of function or possible rupture.

## 14. Held stretch before a power task
**When**: warm-ups before jumping or sprinting.
**Move**: prefer dynamic movement through range; leave held stretching for other times. [ch05]
**Cost**: habits.
**Not when**: no power requirement.

## 15. "Three by ten for everyone"
**When**: a default exercise dose is applied.
**Move**: ask about the goal, intensity, effort and specificity; hand dosing to the exercise-science skill. [ch05, ch10]
**Cost**: more work per plan.
**Not when**: no tailored plan is needed.

## 16. Shoulder instability explained by "weak muscle"
**When**: a dislocation leads to a single-muscle story.
**Move**: use the ball-and-saucer mechanism and the anterior restraints; remember the cuff centres the head and many muscles share abduction. [ch06]
**Cost**: more complex narrative.
**Not when**: an individual has lax tissues or traumatic injury.

## 17. A knee injury mechanism
**When**: a pivot or valgus event.
**Move**: use each ligament's restraint direction to predict structures at risk; rapid swelling hints at a well-supplied ligament. [ch06]
**Cost**: simplification.
**Not when**: major instability after trauma needs medical assessment.

## 18. "Flat feet must be fixed"
**When**: a foot is called collapsed or pronated.
**Move**: note that variation is the norm, pronation is natural, "excessive pronation" lacks a definition; look at modifiable contributors such as training load. [ch09]
**Cost**: disappointing a person who expected a fix.
**Not when**: a specific, reproducible foot problem.

## 19. A frightening scan
**When**: a report lists degeneration, protrusion or tears.
**Move**: ask the base rate in people without pain; remember readers differ; ask whether the image would change the plan. [ch08]
**Cost**: reassurance may feel dismissive; acknowledge the finding.
**Not when**: trauma, progressive neurological deficit, suspected serious pathology.

## 20. "A repaired image will fix it"
**When**: surgery proposed for a degenerative finding.
**Move**: questions to ask: what do trials against placebo or non-surgical care show; what is the procedure expected to change; does the image match the clinical picture. Decision to take with the treating clinician or surgeon. [ch08]
**Cost**: a harder decision.
**Not when**: a locked joint, a displaced fracture or a clear indication.

## 21. A passive treatment that feels good
**When**: ultrasound or similar with no specific effect in sham trials.
**Move**: ask what the active ingredient is; note value of care and placebo; watch for dependency and displacement of active self-management. [ch08]
**Cost**: appears to criticise something the person enjoys.
**Not when**: the person chooses it with full information and alongside active care.

## 22. "Fix your posture"
**When**: a postural flaw is blamed for pain.
**Move**: use the evidence on weak associations, adaptation and variation; consider time in one position and lack of variation; ask what would change. [ch09]
**Cost**: may sound dismissive of a person's experience.
**Not when**: a posture reliably provokes a symptom in a task.

## 23. "Butt wink is dangerous" or "harmless"
**When**: a pelvic tuck at the bottom of a squat.
**Move**: separate unloaded from heavily loaded; note the anatomical end range at the hip; state the disc-stress theory and the adaptation counter-argument. [ch07]
**Cost**: no simple rule.
**Not when**: an individual history of back pain needs a clinician.

## 24. "Squat as deep as possible" or "parallel is enough"
**When**: depth debates.
**Move**: tie depth to the goal (sport rules, specificity); note anatomical limits; stop at pain or loss of control (position). [ch07]
**Cost**: depends on context.
**Not when**: pain in range.

## 25. "Movement fault causes pain; corrective exercise fixes it"
**When**: a movement screen finds a weak link.
**Move**: treat it as a hypothesis; note that most people without pain have weak links; compare with simple training; avoid threat language. [ch07, ch09]
**Cost**: fewer neat explanations.
**Not when**: a pain-provoking task changes with a modification in a reproducible way (still a hypothesis).

## 26. A myth with a catchy claim
**When**: "never exercise with pain", "no pain no gain", "bulletproof", "bad exercises".
**Move**: run the checklist; state the best counter-argument; replace absolutes with dose and context. [ch10]
**Cost**: longer answers.
**Not when**: a medical restriction applies.

## 27. An individual asking for a plan
**When**: a person describes symptoms and wants treatment advice.
**Move**: give mechanisms and ways to think, name red-flag situations, and refer to a qualified clinician; hand programming questions to `exercise-science-and-programming`. [ch03, ch10]
**Cost**: less satisfying.
**Not when**: never; this skill does not diagnose or prescribe.
