# Institutions and Geopolitics
**Chapters**: 11 | **Generated**: 2026-11-14

## Role and Boundaries

This skill **decides how to structure an analysis of political and strategic outcomes**: which rule, constraint or incentive is at work, which layer of evidence a claim rests on, which positions compete, and what a forecast would have to show. It gives mechanisms, case sheets and a checklist; the user owns the question and the conclusion.

| This skill decides | This skill hands off |
|---|---|
| Which institution, constraint, incentive or structural shift explains an outcome, with trade-offs | Collapse and resilience of ancient or past societies and the reliability of claims about the past → `history-geopolitics` |
| Labelling of facts, estimates, inferences, positions and forecasts | Investment decisions, valuation, market history → `investing-and-financial-history`, `economics-finance` |
| Case sheets for electoral rules, money, crises, state failure, geography, strategy, empire, ideologies | Legal, diplomatic or military advice, and ruling on who is right in a dispute → professionals and primary documents |
| Checklist and output template for a conflict or regime | Slides, maps and layout of the result → `presentation-design` |
| Neutral description of live disputes (dated, unverified) | Partisan evaluation of parties, leaders or governments (not offered) |

**Stay in lane.** This skill does not invent dates, figures or quotations. Numbers are dated estimates and must be re-checked in primary statistical sources before reuse. Statements from general knowledge are marked as such. Where the evidence is unclear the chapter says "unclear". Statements about recent events are dated snapshots, marked unverified; forecasts appear only as "(position, dated)" with indicators. Gaps are named in the chapters.

## Facts vs Positions

- **Fact**: a rule, treaty, event or measurement. **Estimate**: a number with a date. **Inference**: a reasoned step; name it.
- **Position**: written "In this model..." or "(position)": for example "geography sets costs, not outcomes", "colonial design explains weak states", "an alliance web can turn a local crisis into a general war".
- Every concept in the chapters has four fields: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**; many have a **Counterargument**. Use the last two before applying an idea; if the limit applies, drop the idea.

## The Core Principles (recurrence order)

1. **Rules shape outcomes.** Ask what a rule rewards before judging the people under it.
2. **Mechanism before verdict.** Explaining an actor's behaviour is not endorsing it.
3. **Geography sets costs, not destinies.** Name the choices that stayed open.
4. **State means law, capacity and consent.** They can come apart.
5. **Incentives explain crises.** Perverse incentives, moral hazard, credit and expectations.
6. **Several causes with roles.** Underlying, amplifier, trigger, symptom.
7. **Structure versus incident.** Find the shift in relative power behind the spark.
8. **Commitments carry shocks.** Automatic alliances and mobilisation timetables.
9. **Dependence is two-sided.** Exporters, importers, routes and rents.
10. **Inherited structures persist.** Borders, infrastructure and institutions outlive their makers.
11. **Labels hide plurality.** Capitalism, communism, nation, empire: define before arguing.
12. **Forecasts are positions.** Replace dates with indicators; no partisan verdicts.

## Scope & Limits

Practical theory about how institutions, economies, geography, strategy and empire shape political outcomes, with case sheets. The chapters keep the analytical content, flag weaker or debated claims and drop narrative and personal detail. Not covered: detailed party systems and law of any single country, game-theoretic models, quantitative forecasting, military operations in detail, religious history, and the full treatment of democracy and liberalism (ch02.11 is a thin general-knowledge sketch). Figures are dated estimates; statements about recent events are unverified snapshots; the skill gives no forecast and takes no side on current politics.
