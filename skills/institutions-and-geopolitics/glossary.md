# Glossary

**Alliance chain** - web of treaties that can turn a local conflict into a general war (ch08)
**Bank run** - sudden mass withdrawal of deposits, prevented by reserves and deposit insurance (ch02, ch04)
**Blank check** - unconditional promise of support to an ally in a crisis (ch08)
**Bubble** - price rise detached from fundamentals that depends on new buyers (ch04)
**Bypass** - alternative route or pipeline around a chokepoint (ch07)
**Chokepoint** - narrow passage whose closure can cut a flow (ch07)
**Core, periphery, semi-periphery** - world-systems categories of wealth and dependence (ch09)
**Cost-push inflation** - price rise caused by a rise in input costs or a supply shock (ch04)
**Credit default swap** - contract insuring against default; unbacked ones spread losses (ch04)
**Demand-pull inflation** - too much money chasing too few goods (ch04)
**Dependency theory** - view that extraction creates lasting reliance of former colonies (ch09)
**Discount rate** - rate at which the central bank lends to banks (ch02)
**Duverger's law** - tendency of plurality, single-member systems to produce two parties (ch02)
**Empirical statehood** - actual capacity to govern territory (ch05)
**Extensive growth** - growth from more inputs rather than higher productivity (ch05)
**Extraterritoriality** - foreigners tried under their own law on another state's soil (ch09)
**Faithless elector** - elector voting against their pledge (ch02)
**Forces of production** - tools, technology, skills and resources used to make things (ch03)
**Fractional-reserve banking** - banks hold only part of deposits as reserves (ch02)
**Geo-economics, geo-strategy, geo-technology** - the three parts of a strategy web (ch08)
**Hegemon-challenger trap** - war risk when a rising power threatens an established one (ch08)
**Heartland** - area of a country holding its people and wealth (ch06)
**Historical materialism** - view that material production shapes society and history (ch03)
**Ideology** - cultural beliefs justifying a way of organising society (ch03)
**Independent spending** - uncapped political spending not given to a campaign (ch02)
**Juridical statehood** - recognition in law, regardless of capacity (ch05)
**Lifeline** - supply link a state cannot do without (ch06, ch07)
**Lorenz curve** - plot of cumulative income share against share of households (ch03)
**Means of production** - land, raw materials, tools and machines used for labour (ch03)
**Moral hazard** - taking more risk because someone else bears the cost (ch04)
**Mortgage-backed security** - share in a pool of mortgages (ch04)
**Negative integration** - building group unity by dehumanising an out-group (ch05)
**Neocolonialism** - formal independence with external control of the economy or politics (ch09)
**Nominal vs real** - values unadjusted vs adjusted for inflation (ch04)
**Open-market operations** - central bank buying or selling government debt (ch02)
**Perverse incentive** - rule or reward that produces the opposite of its aim (ch04)
**Plurality rule** - the candidate with the most votes wins, even without a majority (ch02)
**Quantitative easing** - large-scale central bank asset purchases (ch02)
**Relations of production** - how people organise labour, property and surplus (ch03)
**Reserve requirement** - cash a bank must hold against deposits (ch02)
**Resource curse** - weak institutions turn resource wealth into conflict or stagnation (ch05)
**Securitisation** - bundling loans into tradeable securities (ch04)
**Skill-biased technological change** - technology that favours skilled labour (ch03)
**Spoiler effect** - a small candidate draws votes from a similar big one and helps their rival (ch02)
**Stratification** - ranking of people by wealth, power and prestige (ch03)
**Superstructure** - in one theory, politics, culture and religion built on the economic base (ch03)
**Surrogate representation** - donating to a candidate outside one's own district (ch02)
**Winner-take-all** - the leader in a unit gets all its electors or seats (ch02)
**Undemarcated border** - frontier never marked or legally fixed (ch07)
**Cost-exchange ratio** - cost of the defender's interceptor relative to the attacker's weapon, times the numbers fired (ch07.11)
**Production node** - a single cluster or processing step on which a whole supply chain depends (chips, rare-earth processing) (ch07.12)
**Pipe gas versus liquefied gas** - rigid, cheap, mutual dependence versus flexible, dearer, market-priced supply (ch07.10)
