# Institutions and Geopolitics: Cheatsheet

Figures are dated estimates unless marked. "Position" = judgement, not fact. Chapter references in brackets.

## Where to Start
| Situation | First move |
|---|---|
| "Why does X behave this way?" | Actors, stakes, rules, constraints; checklist [ch11] |
| A statistic or forecast is quoted | Label layer, add date, replace countdown with indicators [ch01] |
| Two parties dominate | Plurality plus single-member districts, and voter explanation [ch02.1, ch02.2] |
| Winner lost the popular vote | Elector formula plus winner-take-all [ch02.5] |
| Campaign money debate | Direct vs independent spending; access vs votes [ch02.7, ch02.8] |
| Prices rising | Demand-pull vs cost-push; bubble vs fundamentals [ch04.2, ch04.3] |
| Financial crisis | Securitisation, incentives, opacity, freeze [ch04.4, ch04.5] |
| Failed or fragile state | Law, capacity, consent; stakes of office [ch05] |
| Strait or canal in the news | Width, share of flows, bypass, who can interdict [ch07.1] |
| Border dispute | Who drew it, what it follows, what is undemarcated [ch06.4, ch07.8] |
| Rising vs established power | Structure vs incident; commitments; indicators [ch08.3, ch08.4] |
| "Colonialism caused it" | Mechanism, counter-case, causal weight as contested [ch09.5] |
| A famous ideology or episode | Doctrine vs practice; who tells the story [ch10] |

## Models and What to Look For
| Model | Look for |
|---|---|
| Duverger-type tendency | Plurality rule, single-member districts, strategic voting, spoiler fear |
| Indirect election | Elector formula, winner-take-all, narrow wins in pivotal units |
| Independent spending | Outside committees, non-disclosing groups, issue ads |
| Central bank independence | Mandate, tools, appointment rules; link from money to prices |
| Bubble | Price vs fundamentals, easy credit, "only up" story, new-buyer dependence |
| Securitisation crisis | Rating inflation, loosened standards, uninsured insurance, opaque web |
| Deflation spiral | Bank failures, credit freeze, falling prices, layoffs, fixed exchange rate |
| Juridical vs empirical state | Recognised borders, authority beyond the capital |
| Extractive colonial design | Corridor infrastructure, thin interior, trade pointing outward |
| Union failure | Control of army, foreign policy, rival cores, outward trade |
| Planned-regime collapse | Extensive growth, military burden, shadow economy, price shock, satellites |
| Chokepoint | Width, flow share, bypasses, interdiction tools |
| Mutual-hostage deterrence | What the actor loses; regime-survival threshold |
| Hegemon-challenger trap | Relative shift, fear, alliance obligations, small incident |
| Alliance chain | Automatic commitments, ultimatum, mobilisation timetable |
| Order-building | Currency, lending, market access, navy, say in policy |
| Dependency / core-periphery | Primary exports, processing elsewhere, financial reliance |
| Fragmentation invites intervention | Many small rivals, mercenaries, local allies of the invader |

## Evidence Types: What They Show and Do Not
| Type | Shows | Does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Treaty text, law, constitution | Formal rules and claims | How they were applied |
| Official statistics | Measured aggregates, with definitions | Distribution, informal economy |
| Budget, trade and energy data | Dependence and flows | Intent |
| Maps, topography, population grids | Constraints, density | Political will |
| Leader statements, claim maps | Positions and signals | Capabilities, intent |
| Military order of battle, exercises | Capability and signalling | Decision to attack |
| Inquiry reports, court rulings | Documented findings | Findings not examined |
| Memoirs, victors' histories, hostile authors | Perspective | Neutral account |
| Popular explainers | Framing, hypotheses | Proof |

- Strait widths: about 21 miles (Gulf), about 28 km (Red Sea), about 1.5 miles (southeast Asia) [ch07.1].
- Canal: about 5% of seaborne trade; the alternative route adds about 8,000 nautical miles and 18 days [ch07.3].
- Population line: about 94% of people on about 43% of land in one country [ch06.5].
- Cost-exchange: a drone in the tens of thousands of dollars versus an interceptor above a million (dated, unverified) [ch07.11].
