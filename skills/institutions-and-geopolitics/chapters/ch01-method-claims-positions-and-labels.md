# Chapter 1: Method: Claims, Positions, Labels and Neutrality

## Core Idea
Institutions and geopolitics are full of claims that sound like facts and are positions. This chapter sets the reading discipline used in the rest of the skill: classify each claim, date each number, mark predictions, expect a counterargument for each position, and keep description apart from partisan judgement. It also says when to hand off to `history-geopolitics`.

## 1.1 Five layers of a claim
- **Thesis**: every statement in this field sits in one of five layers, and the same sentence can mix them.
- **Mechanism**: (1) **Fact**: a documented event, a rule, a treaty text, a dated measurement. (2) **Estimate**: a number with a method and date (population shares, trade shares, reserve shares). (3) **Inference**: a reasoned step from facts; name the step. (4) **Position**: a judgement or contested model ("geography decides", "the order serves the leader"). (5) **Forecast**: a statement about the future.
- **Trade-off**: labelling takes space; it prevents a story from borrowing the authority of facts.
- **Does not hold when**: the audience needs a short summary; then at least keep forecasts and positions marked.

## 1.2 Dating numbers
- **Thesis**: shares of trade, reserves, budgets and wealth change; a number without a date is a claim about the wrong year.
- **Mechanism**: figures in this skill are kept only if clear, and given with a year or marked "estimate". Where a figure is unclear it is omitted or marked "unverified". Before reuse, check a primary statistical source (central bank, statistical office, international agency).
- **Trade-off**: fewer numbers; fewer errors.
- **Does not hold when**: a ratio (the order of magnitude) is the point, not the value.

## 1.3 Positions and the counterargument rule
- **Thesis**: a position without its strongest counterargument is advocacy.
- **Mechanism**: each concept in chapters 2 to 10 has four fields: **Thesis**, **Mechanism**, **Trade-off**, **Does not hold when**. Positions are written "In this model..." or "(position)". Use "Does not hold when" before applying an idea. Where the strongest opposing view is known, a "Counterargument" is given; where only general knowledge is available it is marked (general knowledge).
- **Trade-off**: a more cautious style.
- **Does not hold when**: a decision is needed under time pressure; then say which position you adopt and why.

## 1.4 Mechanism versus verdict
- **Thesis**: an analysis can explain why an actor behaves as it does without saying the actor is right.
- **Mechanism**: a state's geographic anxiety explains a claim; it does not make the claim legitimate. A theory of the colonial roots of a border explains a dispute; it does not assign title. A rule's effects on parties do not say whether the rule is just.
- **Trade-off**: less satisfying for those who want a ruling.
- **Does not hold when**: the question is explicitly normative; then separate the factual premises from the value premises.

## 1.5 Neutrality on current politics
- **Thesis**: current party politics, elections and live conflicts get descriptive treatment only.
- **Mechanism**: the chapters use rules, mechanisms and dated, anonymised illustrations; they avoid naming parties or leaders; they do not rank governments; where a live conflict is mentioned, it is a dated snapshot labelled unverified. A user's question about a present-day dispute gets the checklist (ch11), not a side.
- **Trade-off**: less colour.
- **Does not hold when**: the user asks for a summary of a party's stated platform (give it from primary documents, attributed, with a date).

## 1.6 Forecasts and countdowns
- **Thesis (position)**: a date offered for a future event is a statement of intent or concern, not evidence.
- **Mechanism**: the skill gives forecasts only as "(position, as of month year)" with observable indicators that would change them. A "countdown" (a year by which something will happen) should be translated into indicators (mobilisation, shipping, sanctions preparation, reserves).
- **Trade-off**: less dramatic.
- **Does not hold when**: a deadline is set by a calendar the actors control (an election, a treaty expiry).

## 1.7 Source interest and hostile narratives
- **Thesis**: ask who wrote, when, for whom and what they gained.
- **Mechanism**: victors' accounts, elite writers about rulers they feared, and wartime propaganda are not neutral. A myth can serve to excuse failure ("a stab in the back"). A label created later (a name for a group of raiders, "imperial madness", "artificial borders") can create a single actor or cause. See ch10.
- **Trade-off**: skepticism can be used to dismiss real events.
- **Does not hold when**: independent evidence supports the claim.

## 1.8 When the question belongs to another skill
| Situation | Go to |
|---|---|
| Why an ancient society or a colony collapsed | `history-geopolitics` |
| Capital markets, valuation, investing history | `investing-and-financial-history`, `economics-finance` |
| Slides or a report from the analysis | `presentation-design` |
| Brand, marketing or messaging about a conflict | the relevant marketing skill; keep this skill's neutrality rules |

## Diagnostics
1. Label each key claim by layer.
2. Add a date to each number.
3. For each position, write its counterargument.
4. Separate mechanism from verdict.
5. Convert dates into indicators.
