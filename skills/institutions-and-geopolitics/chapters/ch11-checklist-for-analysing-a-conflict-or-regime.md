# Chapter 11: Checklist for Analysing a Conflict or a Regime

## Core Idea
A short sequence of questions that puts the chapters to work. It helps a user, student or analyst structure an analysis of a war, a dispute, a state, a party system or a collapse. It does not give verdicts, and it does not make forecasts. If the case is a historical collapse of a society, also use `history-geopolitics`.

## The sequence
1. **Define the question.** What exactly is being explained: a war's outbreak, a regime's durability, a border dispute, a crisis, a policy? Over what time span? What will the answer be used for? Ask for the claim to be stated as a sentence that could be wrong.
2. **List the actors and their stakes.** State, faction, army, party, firm, outside power. For each: what it wants, what it fears, what it can do, what it loses by waiting. Distinguish leaders from the state, and the state from the people. [ch05, ch08]
3. **Identify the rules.** Electoral and constitutional rules, party system, central bank, courts, who controls the army. What does each rule reward? [ch02]
4. **Check the state.** Law (recognition), capacity (tax, police, courts), consent (legitimacy). Is the army the strongest institution? What are the stakes of holding the government? [ch05]
5. **Map the geography.** Heartland, barriers, lifelines, chokepoints, depth, frontier weaknesses, sea exits. What is cheap and what is costly? Which constraints did technology change? [ch06, ch07]
6. **Trace the money.** Revenue source, dependence on one customer or route, debt, inflation, credit conditions, bubbles, inequality and who gains. [ch03, ch04, ch07.6]
7. **Look for inherited structure.** Colonial borders, partition lines, extractive infrastructure, treaties, claims never settled. [ch07, ch09]
8. **Find the structural shift.** Relative power, technology, demographic or economic change, an order losing its leader. Separate this from the trigger incident. [ch08]
9. **Check commitments.** Alliances with automatic obligations, mobilisation timetables, ultimatums, guarantees; who believes the other will back down? [ch08.4]
10. **Classify the causes.** Underlying, amplifier, trigger, symptom. Do not accept a single cause. Ask who experienced the same shock and did not fail. [ch01]
11. **Mark the evidence.** For each claim: documented fact, estimate, inference, narrative, or position. Date every figure. Mark each author's interest. [ch01]
12. **Give the counterargument.** For each position, state the strongest opposite one and what evidence would decide between them. [every chapter's "Does not hold when"]
13. **Check ideology and labels.** Is the label (communist, liberal, nationalist) one thing or several? Is a nation treated as one actor? Is an episode told as a morality play? [ch10]
14. **State forecasts as positions.** Only "(position, dated)" with observable indicators that would change the view; no dates as certainties. Say what is not covered. [ch08.7]
15. **Neutrality check.** Describe parties, disputes and current events without taking a partisan side; where a dispute is about rights, say that the analysis explains incentives and constraints and does not settle who is right. [ch06.6]

## Output template
- **Question** (one sentence).
- **Actors and stakes** (table).
- **Structure**: rules, state, geography, money, inheritance (one paragraph each).
- **Causes by role**: underlying, amplifier, trigger, symptom.
- **Contested points**: position, counter-position, deciding evidence.
- **Indicators to watch** (observable).
- **Limits**: what the evidence does not show.

## Common traps
| Trap | Fix |
|---|---|
| Event described as inevitable ("geography decides") | Name the choices that remained open [ch06] |
| One villain | Split causes by role [ch01] |
| Nation as single actor or a "national character" | Disaggregate: factions, leaders, institutions [ch08.4] |
| A date as a forecast | Replace with indicators [ch08.7] |
| Old analogy | List disanalogies (nuclear weapons, trade, institutions) [ch08.3] |
| Heroic or villain narrative | Incentives and independent evidence [ch10] |
| Using a rule's label as a judgement | State the mechanism, then the value judgement separately [ch02] |
