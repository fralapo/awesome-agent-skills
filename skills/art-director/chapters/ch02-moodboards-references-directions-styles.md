# Chapter 02: Moodboards, References, Directions and Styles

## Core Idea
Visual direction moves from logic to instinct: strategy narrows the field, research and moodboards open it, and a small set of distinct routes is presented so one can be locked. Any style can be described by four attributes (palette, typography, texture/imagery, layout), which makes routes comparable and lets the graphic designer reproduce them.

## Process
1. **Strategy filters**: purpose, mission, long-term vision, target audience and a short list of non-negotiable values. Judge routes by alignment with these.
2. **Industry research**: study the client's field to learn its conventions (for example martial-art and sports brands lean on bold sans serifs rather than formal serifs) and to decide where to follow or break them.
3. **References and moodboards**: collect references from several sources; search by precise style names and keyword pairs (a style plus an adjacent term), which finds relevant inspiration faster than generic queries.
4. **Keyword expansion**: gather keywords with the client, expand each with related words, combine into pairs and think of each pair visually.
5. **Three distinct routes**: present about three directions (for example playful, editorial, minimalist heritage). Evaluate each against the desired positioning, discard those that miss it, borrow selected elements from runners-up.
6. **Lock early**: committing to a direction early shows homework, sets expectations on look and feel, avoids unnecessary revisions and gives a reference for later decisions.
7. **Avoid clichés**: skip generic category icons and symbols; drop ideas whose cultural associations conflict with the brand story, however attractive the structure.
8. **Test with the designer**: ask the graphic designer to sketch quickly on paper before digital work; the aim is to dump rough ideas, not to finish.

## Style Description Template
Palette · Typography · Texture/imagery · Layout. Add a one-line feeling and two or three do/don't rules.

## Style Catalogue (palette / type / texture / layout)
- **Early-2000s optimistic tech**: glossy gradients, sky and water imagery, soft transparency, clean humanist sans.
- **Dark academia**: muted browns, dark greens, burgundy; classical serif; aged textures (parchment, leather). **Light academia**: same sources in cream, beige and soft yellow for a warmer, airier mood.
- **Wabi-sabi**: imperfection and ageing; earthy palettes, natural textures, minimalist layouts, irregular shapes and spacing for calm.
- **Regional or thematic**: recognisable palette and motif sets (warm terracotta and turquoise with geometric patterns for desert themes; navy, white and a red accent with stripes and anchors for nautical).
- **Rebus**: icons inserted within or in place of words for visual puns; keep layout clean so the viewer can decode.
- **Literal object**: imitate tickets, labels or receipts with monospace type, paper creases, barcodes and printed textures. Receipt layouts: black on white or off-white, timestamps, long narrow vertical format.
- **Glassmorphism**: blurred semi-transparent layers, soft shadows, glowing edges, bright gradients behind the glass, minimal sans.
- **Modular typography**: letters built from repeated basic shapes on a strict grid; custom, geometric, futuristic; minimal colour, maximum structure.
- **Narrative neo-brutalism**: text-led and grid-based; bold pastel colours, heavy sans, visible borders and boxy sharp elements; message over imagery.
- **Modern nostalgia**: retro print type and palettes blended with modern sans, grain, halftone and soft shadows; retro yet clean.
- **Punk-influenced Swiss typography**: sans fonts and grids rotated, layered, duplicated and misaligned on purpose; black and white with one bold accent; clashing sizes and weights.
- **Brutalist and grunge posters**: loud type, abrasive image treatment, layered texture (execution details in `graphic-designer` ch05).

## Rules and Numbers
- About three routes: fewer limits choice, more dilutes it.
- Keep a short list of do/don't rules per route; they are what the designer uses at 2 a.m.
- When a style becomes widely imitated, change direction deliberately (opposite mood, weight or composition) to stay distinctive.

## Common Mistakes
- A moodboard of pretty images with no stated feeling or rules.
- Presenting routes that differ only in colour.
- Reopening the direction in every review after it was locked.
