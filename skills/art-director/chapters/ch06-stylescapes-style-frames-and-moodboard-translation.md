# Chapter 06: Stylescapes, Style Frames and Moodboard-to-Design Translation

## Core Idea
A stylescape (a moodboard of curated images, type, colour, texture and logo) is a cheap way to test whether a look fits before costly production. Its job is a fit check, not a final design. The next step is translation: assembling the approved elements into a real piece (a page, a pack, a film) and tightening in stages. For motion, key style frames play the same role and must show an arc. Ch02 covers building moodboards and choosing routes; this chapter covers using them to check and to translate.

## Procedure: Stylescape Fit Check
1. **Gather ingredients** from the strategy: brand attributes, customer needs, value proposition (for example: fast, healthy, fits budget and lifestyle), logo, typefaces, photography, texture.
2. **Audit references and competitors**: note what to emulate and what to avoid, matched to the client's **price tier**. A high-contrast fast-food look applied to a mid-tier brand sends the wrong tier signal. Audit both directly competing brands (vertical) and unrelated industries that solve a similar customer problem (horizontal); see the creative-director discovery reference.
3. **Assemble the stylescape** from curated images only; curation matters more than quantity.
4. **Run the fit check** with the client against the agreed words and attributes, before the mark is drawn. Revisions here cost almost nothing.

## Procedure: Translate a Stylescape into a Design
1. **Sketch the structure quickly** (for a web page: menu, hero, sections, footer; for a pack: front, sides, back; for a booklet: cover, rhythm of spreads).
2. **Set the grid** and check elements against it (grid values for screens belong to `ux-ui-expert`; the direction-level point is that a grid is fixed before polishing).
3. **Carry across** logo, fonts and photographs from the stylescape; write copy from the brand attributes.
4. **Tighten progressively**: early comps may be loose; later comps snap to the grid. Do not show loose comps to a client already past the concept stage.
5. **Consolidate feedback** from several directions into one direction, then comp that. Present one consolidated direction, not several comps.
6. **Work on a broader canvas** than the final crop, not inside the final crop.
7. **Hand off** to `graphic-designer` (craft), `ux-ui-expert` (screens, small-screen-first behaviour, asset resolution) with the stylescape and written rules.

## Procedure: Mood Board and Style Frames for Motion
- **Mood board**: a highly curated image set conveying story, aesthetic and idea before custom work starts.
- **Style frames**: key frames from key moments (for example three acts) that show how the aesthetic evolves from beginning to end.
- Open the review with a **one-sentence concept and references** so the reviewer knows the intent.
- Give the **opening frame context**; a standalone first frame with no cues confuses.
- Make the palette or treatment **shift visibly across acts** so the progression reads; let a motif **change form with the story** (hard angles that become flowing lines).
- Ask of every motif what it means, not only whether it looks good. Decorative elements with no story role are removed.
- Add an **insert frame** where a cause or mechanism is unclear (the origin of a visual element).
- Count the **technical cost** of the motion each motif requires before locking it.

## Examples (generic)
- A health-food delivery brand: ingredients are "fast, healthy, fits budget"; the audit shows premium competitors in muted serif and discount competitors in loud colour; the stylescape sits between, with fresh photography, one warm accent and a friendly sans; the client approves the fit; only then does the web comp begin.
- A three-act explainer film: act one cold and angular, act two transitional, act three warm with flowing lines; the style frames show this shift, and an insert frame explains where the central object comes from.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating the stylescape as a finished design, or as decoration for the pitch.
- Stylescape of pretty images unrelated to the agreed attributes.
- Skipping the fit check and drawing the logo first.
- Style frames that all look the same, so the story has no arc.
- Motifs with no narrative role.
- Ignoring the tier of the client's price point.

## Checklist
- [ ] Ingredients listed from the strategy
- [ ] Competitor audit with emulate and avoid lists, matched to price tier
- [ ] Fit check approved before the mark is drawn
- [ ] Structure sketched, grid fixed, comps tightened in stages
- [ ] One consolidated direction presented
- [ ] Motion: concept sentence, context frame, visible arc, cost of motifs
