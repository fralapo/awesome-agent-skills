# Patterns

Operational techniques extracted from the 7 sections.

## 1. The 4 Elements of Indirect Selling
**When to use**: every piece of content has to touch at least 1-2 of these
**How**:
1. Direct experience — "I saw / did X"
2. Principle of need — situation / desire gap
3. Hero's journey — values, journey, payoffs
4. Social proof as garnish — testimonials everywhere
**Trade-off**: slower content to produce but builds a lasting asset. A single "DM me info" CTA is fast but saturated

## 2. Social Proof Sprinkle (garnish)
**When**: you have case studies / testimonials but they only sit in Drive
**How**: insert screenshots / results into Stories, descriptions, comments, B-roll. Never standalone "here is my case study"
**Trade-off**: requires discipline; a single case study = 5-10 scattered micro-uses

## 3. Manifesto Filter
**When**: you have the wrong clients / want to select the target
**How**: write a public manifesto (pinned post) with values, modus operandi, who you want (and who not). Example: "I reply within 48h, I'm not saving lives"
**Trade-off**: you will lose misaligned clients = win, not loss

## 4. Distribution by Interest — content adaptation
**When**: you see engagement falling at constant follower count
**How**:
- Recognizability + domain in every piece of content
- Complementary resources (recurring lead magnet)
- Never assume the viewer knows you
**Trade-off**: less "in-group" content but much more scalable

## 5. Stories for Loyalty
**When**: the feed no longer reaches the community
**How**: an intentional communication plan for Stories, not compulsive posting. Show values, payoffs, behind the scenes, client cases
**Trade-off**: daily time investment vs a real relationship channel

## 6. Complementary Channels
**When**: you depend 100% on Instagram
**How**: add at least 1 channel among:
- YouTube (click intent, long-form loyalty-builder)
- LinkedIn (different target, B2B)
- Newsletter (owned channel, powerful sales)
- School / Circle (private community)
**Trade-off**: more channels = more time, but anti-fragility

## 7. Revenue / Hours Formula
**When**: you are assessing whether content "pays you"
**How**: `€_generated_from_the_channel / hours_dedicated`. €500/hour beats any consulting
**Trade-off**: needs correct attribution; in the short run low revenue is normal (compound effect)

## 8. 3 Personal Brand Delegation Stages
**When**: you want to scale without losing "personal"
**How**:
- Stage 1 — one-man-show (necessary to validate your voice)
- Stage 2 — + editor (delegation with faster ROI)
- Stage 3 — microteam (director + recording support + editor)
**Trade-off**: a generalist SMM = tempting shortcut but costs the brand

## 9. Inside Out (ideation)
**When**: you are creative, you write naturally
**How**: weekly journaling + extract pillars from client-call transcripts (Gemini Meet → Claude project → "extract content pillars")
**Trade-off**: requires a writing habit

## 10. Reference (ideation)
**When**: you don't write spontaneously, you love scrolling
**How**: dedicated account + Reels scrolling + "Interested" on the 3 dots + "next contents" collection in saves
**Trade-off**: risk of getting lost in scrolling, discipline needed

## 11. Pillar (ideation)
**When**: you want a structured method, an endless list
**How**: start from macro-topics of your domain → break into sub-topics → break further → stop when you have a single specific piece
**Trade-off**: the list can get huge; the opposite problem = choosing

## 12. Reference Based Scripting (Turbo Scribe + AI)
**When**: you freeze in front of a blank page
**How**: link to a reference video → turboscribe.ai → skeletal structure → fill in with your points (optionally AI prompt "extract the structure from this script, give me a skeleton to fill with my ideas")
**Trade-off**: risk of being derivative if you don't add personal experience

## 13. Author Mode (Ken Follett)
**When**: you like writing but want quality
**How**: dump your thoughts, let it sit, re-read, prune, structure. Multiple drafts, not a perfect version on the first try
**Trade-off**: slower but more "yours"

## 14. Full Day Blocking
**When**: you can never find time to record
**How**: block 1 day / month on the calendar WITH MONTHS OF LEAD TIME (e.g. in January block March). 1 full day = more than 1 month of content
**Trade-off**: rigidity that becomes freedom

## 15. Minimum Recording Setup
**When**: you want to start with €0-100
**How**: recent iPhone + €6-30 light from Amazon + tripod + decent background (home wall). Outdoor too (park, Airbnb)
**Trade-off**: none, really

## 16. 4 Video Dynamism Techniques
**When**: your reels are static talking heads and don't perform
**How**: combine at least 2-3 of:
- Objects / visual metaphors
- Camera angle changes + movement (record standing)
- Custom B-roll (record 10-30 scenes of "you doing things")
- Trailer-style script (short sentences)
**Trade-off**: setup takes 1h extra but engagement much higher

## 17. Vertical Positioning
**When**: target is generic, conversion is low
**How**: reduce the domain to 1 precise thing. "Personal brand" → "personal brand in organic". Face ↔ domain in 2 seconds
**Trade-off**: you give up wider audience but sell 10x better

## 18. Need Gap (sales content structure)
**When**: useful content but no one writes
**How**:
1. Show the target's current situation (the problem)
2. Show the desired situation (the client result)
3. Create the perceived gap (what's missing to get there)
4. Optional CTA (by that point self-evident)
**Trade-off**: requires real case studies, no generalities

## 19. Sell Execution, Not Information
**When**: you are afraid of "giving away content"
**How**: redesign the service around SUPPORT + ACCOUNTABILITY + APPLICATION to a specific context. Then you can say everything for free
**Trade-off**: redesigning the offer = a big one-off job

## 20. Algorithm — Optimize for 3 Metrics
**When**: you post regularly but don't scale
**How**: design every piece of content to maximize at least 1 of:
- Saves (= "I'll come back here")
- Watch time (= deep content, long-form explainers, slow carousels)
- Shares (= "you have to see this too")
Likes and followers = ignore as primary KPIs
**Trade-off**: formats like carousels with audio reborn; like-bait dead

## 21. Diagnostic Matrix "Lots of X but Few Y"
**When**: you want to figure out what to fix by reading the numbers on a post
**How**: compare 2 metrics and apply the fix:
- High saves / low likes → explicit CTA before the halfway mark
- High views / low followers → visual identity + repeated distinctive format
- High followers / low interactions → personal content + talk about the target
- High followers / low bio clicks → strong reason to click + final CTA
- High likes / low comments → polarizing questions + personal experiences
- High profile visits / low followers → clear bio + 3-sec hook + verticality
- High saves / low shares → emotional lever + identity-laden topics
**Trade-off**: requires looking at insights regularly; reveals what the real gap is

## 22. Repost Strategy (post-August 7, 2025)
**When**: your followers no longer see your posts (discovery-first algorithm)
**How**:
1. Publish at the time your audience is actually online
2. Do a **repost within 3 hours** of the initial publication
3. Repost bypasses topic-centric distribution → reaches followers as a new post
**Trade-off**: used poorly it becomes spam; bonus: influences distribution in the following hours

## 23. Non-Inflated Hook Pattern
**When**: your hooks sound like everyone else's
**How**: replace clichéd formulas with specific framing:
- "Tips for X" → "Everyone tells you to X, but no one explains HOW"
- "How I did X" → "In [time] I took X from [A] to [B]"
- "X tips for Y" → "If you are going through [moment], these are the actions to avoid ending up with [Z] within [T]"
- "My method for X" → "If tomorrow I woke up with [problem], here's exactly what I'd do"
**Trade-off**: specific hooks filter the audience (whoever doesn't have that problem scrolls)

## 24. Diagnosing Recognizable Brands (3 levers)
**When**: you want to understand why a brand dominates on social
**How**: look for these 3 things:
1. **Strategic trend use** — adapted to their TOV, no copy
2. **Mascot / face** — non-replicable visual identity
3. **Active social listening** — personalized ironic / coherent replies to comments
**Trade-off**: replicability for a personal brand = substitute mascot with your face

## 25. I See You / I Validate You / I Associate You (Personal Brand Triangle)
**When**: you want to understand why a personal brand doesn't convert
**How**: diagnose the 3 vertices:
- **I See You (who you are)**: do you have a defined ethos? Do you have recurrence elements? Do you document your storytelling?
- **I Associate You (what you do)**: is the bio understandable in 5 seconds? Is the offer clear?
- **I Validate You (why believe you)**: uncopyable experience in the content? Social proof as garnish? Visible authority?
**Trade-off**: months of work, not a quick fix; but without this no funnel works

## 26. Profile Funnel (conversion from bio)
**When**: you have a short purchase process (target already aware "who do I go to?")
**How**:
- Bio first line = reason to give money (understandable to an elementary-school kid)
- Pinned posts: authority + proof + personal story
- Bio link: 1-3 links max
- Visual coherence in covers
**Trade-off**: works without virality if the foundations are solid

## 27. Sales Set in Stories (structure)
**When**: you want to convert your followers without being pushy
**How**:
1. Hook that creates a perceived gap ("do you know what people in the position you want do?")
2. 3-4 necessary elements, little text per slide
3. **Closing slide** that connects the dots (people don't do it on their own)
4. Varied CTA (not just "DM me info")
**Trade-off**: 1 / week if you do Stories consistently, max 1 / 2 weeks if inconsistent

## 28. Lead Magnet + ManyChat Funnel
**When**: you want to scale lead acquisition without a dedicated setter
**How**:
- Content / Story with keyword → ManyChat trigger
- Always **double confirmation** (never send the resource on the first message)
- Use **buttons**, NOT quick replies (invisible)
- Attraction resource → conversation reactivation on a sales resource
- Manual reactivation after X hours = where the actual conversion happens
**Trade-off**: needs technical setup; if the newsletter is not managed, do NOT ask for email

## 29. Pillar Method for Content Ideas
**When**: you have macro-topics but a creative block on the single post
**How**:
1. List macro-topics of the domain (target problems / pains / goals / desires)
2. Break each macro into sub-topics
3. Question: "can I be resolutive in 60 seconds?" YES → flag it / NO → break further
4. Filter the ideas by purchase process + target awareness level
**Trade-off**: disproportionate output (the problem becomes choosing)

## 30. Try-and-Real Tactic
**When**: you want more movement without creating new content
**How**:
- Publish past best performers as Trial Reels
- Create **variations** (title / font / cut) to bypass deduplication
- Volume 1-3 per day
- It's a long shot: needs high volume so that 1 hits
**Trade-off**: frustrating (most flop); the content that hits = data on a cold audience

## 31. DM Management — Skeleton
**When**: people DM you and you don't know how to handle the conversation
**How**:
1. **Opening**: "Before sending info, tell me briefly about it so I can see if I can help"
2. **Deep dive**: directional questions (problem, what you've already done, goal)
3. **Close**: "Looks in line, I'd say let's book a call. What do you think?" (control to them)
4. **Reactivation**: chats DON'T end, they pick up hours / days / months later
**Trade-off**: takes time; but the 90% of conversations that die = money lost

## 32. 5 Target Awareness Levels
**When**: you want to understand why a piece of content / funnel doesn't convert
**How**: map your target to the current level:
- Unaware / Problem-aware / Solution-aware / Product-aware / Most-aware
**Fix by level**:
- Unaware → educational content full of metaphors (harder target)
- Problem-aware → awareness content ("this isn't working")
- Solution-aware → case studies + unique mechanism
- Product-aware → direct sales sets
**Trade-off**: start from "problem-aware" upward for greater efficiency

## 33. Tactical Defense Organic vs ADV
**When**: a client / team says "organic content is useless"
**How**: defend with 5 arguments:
1. Experimentation (test formats for free before spending budget)
2. Community + loyalty (ADV brings, organic retains)
3. Values + USP (no-ADV-clone differentiation)
4. Coherence (whoever sees the ADV checks the profile)
5. Social listening (understand the audience = understand what will work in ADS)
**Trade-off**: needs a composite ROAS + brand-lift metric, not just the single channel's CPL

## 34. Bio That Converts — the 3-4 Line Structure
**When**: writing / auditing an Instagram bio (the first conversion outpost; a confused prospect leaves for the clearer competitor)
**How**:
1. **Line 1** — clarity + specificity. Who you are + what you do + what you do DIFFERENTLY, in the target's own words. **Kill the verb "help"**: use "They call me when…", an imperative ("Acquire steady clients organically"), a first-person verb ("I build personal brands that sell organically"), or "You find me when…" (coaching/mental niches)
2. **Line 2** — differentiation. Deepen line 1 / show *how* you impact them. No empty fillers, no invented method names unless certified. The distinctive tail clause = the core of the method
3. **Line 3** — authority. Case studies / years / client numbers (uncopyable). No big case yet → drop it, be more specific above
4. **Line 4 (optional)** — CTA: "write INFO in DM" (mass/aware B2C) · book-now (short purchase window) · simple "click" + arrow
5. **Bio link** — max 3 ideally 2: a free resource + a direct-contact funnel (phone/WhatsApp/Calendly). Profile = the landing page, reachable in ≤2 clicks. No Linktree clutter
**Test**: "1-10, from this bio alone, would I give this person my money?"
**Trade-off**: ~150 chars feels always too few; expect frequent rewrites. Emoji are purely stylistic (cost characters)

## 35. Read the Market Sophistication Stage
**When**: positioning / promise isn't landing despite good content
**How**: locate your market on the 5-stage ladder and phrase the promise accordingly:
1. First to market → plain claim
2. Few competitors → amplify the claim
3. Claim saturated → lead with the mechanism (the *how*)
4. Mechanism saturated → amplify / differentiate the mechanism
5. Everything heard → pull back to identification + you (storytelling, alignment)
**Rule**: in a saturated market (4-5) a generic promise won't enter no matter how good the content. Awareness = the prospect; sophistication = the market — work both before content
**Trade-off**: forces honest competitor study; the higher the stage, the more "you" has to carry the differentiation

## 36. Stand Out Under Topic-Centric Distribution
**When**: reach is fine but content blends in / doesn't convert new viewers
**How**: you compete with EVERYONE on the interest, not 3 rivals → 
- Open with a **contrarian line** that breaks a common myth (not "X tips for Y")
- **Differentiate visually** so videos don't read as interchangeable talking heads
- Carry **your** experience / vision — what ChatGPT can't write
- Stay **directional** (speak to people in the purchase window), don't chase views/trends — exception: mass markets where volume is the play
**Trade-off**: directional content filters out the merely curious; that's the point

## 37. Build the Acquisition Ecosystem (non-linear flow)
**When**: relying on a single path (`post → inquiry`) that no longer fires
**How**: place **1-3 touchpoints** on the account and prioritize the **3 highest-leverage funnels** (~90% of results):
1. Stories (majority indirect → direct sales set / CTA)
2. Lead magnet OR quiz funnel (capture + qualify + reactivate)
3. DM management (welcome → handle → reactivate)
Plus: open DMs proactively; deploy free resources **early**, run them as a real funnel (not "post + ask email + hope")
**Trade-off**: more moving parts to instrument, but conversion no longer depends on one fragile path

## 38. Two Reasoning Frames Before the CTA
**When**: "my CTAs don't work" — content is correct on paper but no one writes
**How**: stop tweaking the CTA (it's only a gentle nudge) and build the need first:
1. **Real reason they pay** — name the core lever (the situation worth paying to solve), not every pain. A real problem ≠ a *buying* problem
2. **Desire over pain** — show the desired situation (where they could get), not "knife in the wound". Result/situation Stories convert best → create the "how did they do it?" gap
With both in place the CTA is optional; people reach the link and act unprompted
**Trade-off**: requires honest diagnosis of why people actually pay + real client results to show

## 39. The Loop (attraction → authority sequencing)
**When**: you post regularly but new followers never seem to turn into clients
**How**:
1. Publish attraction content 1-3x/week to pull new people INTO the loop
2. Once someone interacts, assume their Stories tray + feed will preferentially show you next — fill that space with authority/recognizability content (case studies, unfiltered talk, recognizable style), not more attraction content
3. Vary format/setting/video type across both so the platform has more material to match against different viewers ("provide more elements")
**Trade-off**: requires planning two distinct content jobs instead of one undifferentiated stream

## 40. 3-Error Content Diagnostic
**When**: content is on-topic and still gets no distribution / no conversion
**How**: check in order before touching positioning:
1. Communication/ethos — are you reading a script / flat on camera?
2. Market analysis — have you checked what the target is actually watching/saving, or are you guessing?
3. Value — does this say anything beyond what's already been said, or is it generic?
**Trade-off**: tempting to jump straight to "fix positioning"; skipping these 3 first wastes a rewrite on the wrong problem

## 41. YouTube Showcase → Sponsor Pipeline
**When**: a channel grows in views/subscribers but revenue doesn't follow, or sponsors never reach out
**How**:
1. Find and train your real on-camera energy instead of performing a persona — flop videos are almost always missing energy, not missing a "character"
2. Treat one piece of tone/language feedback from your real audience as a positioning signal, not noise (e.g. cutting swearing to read as more mature/professional)
3. Build a downstream offer (consulting, coaching, a productized service) and sell THAT off the channel's proof-of-expertise, rather than relying on ad revenue alone
4. Only then pitch or expect sponsors — they buy brand-safety and positioning, not raw reach
5. Sustain output (~2x/week) for years, through the discouraging middle period, before expecting any of the above to compound
**Trade-off**: the payoff (showcase credibility, sponsor deals) is gated by multi-year constancy; there is no shortcut past the low-feedback period

## 42. The Documentary Method (4 Steps)
**When**: starting or relaunching a YouTube channel in the content-first era, unsure what to post or who to post it for
**How**:
1. **Target** — name ONE specific viewer persona (age, job, needs, what they want to see), not a demographic bracket; you need the right few, not the most
2. **Document, don't create** — base videos on your own lived process/current work, not on AI-suggested generic topics; say what YOU think, not what a model would say if asked directly
3. **Educate** — while documenting, explicitly explain what you're learning/doing so the viewer gets usable value alongside the story
4. **Share vision & mission** — state your own point of view and method repeatedly, even against prevailing format trends, to build a "micro-cult" following rather than a generic info channel
**Trade-off**: slower to scale than chasing trends/volume, but matches exactly what content-first distribution (and the Hype Score, for 500-500,000 subscriber channels) now rewards

## 43. Profile Funnel Build (links, pinned posts, Stories)
**When**: traffic arrives but profile visits do not become chats or calls
**How**:
1. Bio: specific, clear, deliberately different from the other bios in the industry
2. Links (max 3, each with a job): direct contact (simplest option for the audience) / gated free resource that always leads somewhere / optional assist (event, hiring, portfolio, reviews); link a focused landing page, not the whole site
3. Pinned posts: authority (1-2) + who you are (video intro or hero's-journey carousel) + a free resource consistent with the service sold
4. Check visual perception: no clutter, covers with titles
5. Keep Stories active with social proof and method detail; optionally open chats with a welcome audio ending in a question fitting the target's current moment
**Trade-off**: effort is front-loaded and the profile needs frequent rewrites; skipping it makes every content win leak out of the funnel

## 44. Content by Awareness Level and Time in Orbit
**When**: content gets views but not requests, or all content feels identical for everyone
**How**:
1. Locate the majority of the target on the awareness ladder (usually problem-aware on social platforms) and open with the situation they live in; show process and cases to solution-aware buyers
2. Split the reached audience: new (~0-15 days) → profile, useful content, active Stories, a second channel; middle (~15-60 days) → social proof, transformations, plain statement of the service, vertical resources, indirect selling; veterans → very specific resources, detailed PDFs, live events, case studies, direct conversations
3. Do not hard-sell the middle group, do not use scarcity on veterans
**Trade-off**: needs a planned mix per week; the strategy part (months of content) is contextual per brand

## 45. Ranking-Aware Content Check (late-era IG)
**When**: a video does not get distributed
**How**:
1. Categorisation: is the topic one people already watch or search? Join the existing conversation your own way
2. Stand-out: hook, audio, light, baseline professionalism
3. Fit: does the message match what the target wants and their awareness level?
4. Add originality (no recycled uploads) and, where possible, an industry-current topic or format
5. For carousels: natural look with photos underneath, strong slide 1 AND slide 2, test weekly
6. Use long-form sparingly as in-app sales letters, one per awareness level, linked from Reels
**Trade-off**: more upfront market research; no extra production time

## 46. Funnel Flow Tracking and Backward Planning
**When**: monthly revenue swings, no clue why a month was weak, or a revenue goal with no plan
**How**:
1. List the steps and count survivors per touchpoint: profile traffic, follow conversion, chats opened, chats in target, chats moved forward, booking link sent, call booked, call closed
2. Work backwards from the goal: clients needed ÷ closing rate = calls; calls ÷ chat-to-call rate = chats; chats = Stories, resources, outreach and content volume needed
3. Translate unrealistic goals into capacity (clients that can be served at once) before pushing harder
4. Keep several flows alive; test one new flow about every three months
**Trade-off**: needs tracking discipline and a few months of data; ratios differ strongly by niche, so measure your own instead of copying anyone's

## 47. Five-Trigger Check on Sales Communication
**When**: content or Stories are correct "on paper" but no one writes
**How**: check for at least one of: (1) self-reference (opens from the felt situation), (2) similarity (a stated belief or recurring payoff line), (3) information gap (current vs desired situation plus "you know something I don't"), (4) distinctiveness (a recurring detail others cannot copy), (5) authority (lived-experience delivery, visible context, results with the story behind the number)
**Trade-off**: a conscious design step per piece; improves conversion more than adding CTAs

## 48. Directional Content Brief (alarm bell, reasoning, goals)
**When**: views arrive but not the right people
**How**:
1. Describe the alarm-bell situation in which the target feels they need help
2. Describe their reasoning and where they look for a solution (alone, a book, a professional)
3. Describe goals and desire (not only pain)
4. For each piece: what should they know, do or think to leave that situation, and how can you start helping now?
5. Add at least one conversion element: security + competence from experience, or validation with client results
**Trade-off**: slower topic choice; stops "viral but off-target" pieces from diluting the profile

## 49. Creative Director Operating Cycle
**When**: delegating content strategy and recording to one person beside the founder
**How**:
1. Kickoff: align direction, tone, references and revenue goals; plan about three months of run-in
2. Shared reference channel all year; weekly call
3. Build an input document from references + target file + founder concepts; founder reviews alone or in a short joint session
4. Recording: blocked day(s), group by location, decide orientation and format per input, the director is responsible for getting the material shot
5. After: sort files, calendar, editor notes; founder only approves alignment
**Trade-off**: heavy at the start, then light; requires a senior, committed person (not an agency with many clients)

## 50. Paid Multi-Evening Workshop as Conversion Layer
**When**: a warm audience plus a high-touch offer and a team able to run a project
**How**:
1. Separate event profile; waitlist ads about a month earlier; official funnel 3-4 weeks before to a landing page with a short video
2. Price ladder and an upgrade tier; email nurture and a messaging community
3. Five ~90-minute evenings, each building on the last; pitch only at the end of the last two; bonuses instead of discounts
4. Plan sales capacity, delivery slots, slides and rehearsal; keep access rules simple
**Trade-off**: heavy workload and emotional load; first-event figures flatter repeats

## 51. Starting-From-Zero Sequence
**When**: a small or new account with no acquisition
**How**: (1) offer and positioning: specific, priced to the market, unique angle; (2) four to six directional pieces a week in series and varied formats; (3) proactive conversations: service talk, soft Stories polls, new-follower outreach with a resource; (4) then the sales structure; read the cause if no clients after about four months (positioning, communication, proactivity)
**Trade-off**: front-loaded effort and no early data; boost accelerates only what already works

## 52. Lead Magnet Design
**When**: a free resource gets downloads but no conversations, or conversations but no clients
**How**: choose its job (movement: practical and solution-giving; sales: awareness of what must change, solution inside the service); outcome-and-proof title; topic from the target's top problems; a follow-up (chat flow or email) that turns it into conversations; alternate with indirect and direct need-creating content
**Trade-off**: more design work per resource; the magnet alone never converts

## 53. Angle and Nesting-Doll Positioning
**When**: a bio or promise reads like everyone else's
**How**: confirm the offer is competitive; find a reading key (angle); make the promise progressively more specific until it describes only you; match tone (direct vs welcoming) to the market and to your real self; end videos with an extra resource instead of the stock "like, comment, follow"
**Trade-off**: narrows the audience on purpose

## 54. Trial Reels Categorisation Routine
**When**: views are stuck for months or the account looks mis-categorised
**How**: check Explore for alignment; republish last year's best reels (most views and followers) as Trial Reels only; test three intros on one body; verify the incoming audience is in target; attach a keyword automation; run for several weeks
**Trade-off**: platform behaviour is reported, changeable and may carry limits not yet visible

## 55. Moving From Word of Mouth to an Owned System
**When**: clients arrive only by referral, price is stuck and timing is out of control
**How**: ask clients what they value; rebuild services into a few standardised packages; make every content piece experiential using the offline history; build touchpoints and tracking
**Trade-off**: initial reorganisation of the offer; a head start rather than a restart

## 56. Objective-Labelled Editorial Review
**When**: the editorial plan is guesswork and results look random
**How**: give each new piece one objective (growth, awareness, community, positioning, sales); label past posts by objective; compare the best and worst within each group; keep what the best share; check engagement, not only reach
**Trade-off**: needs a few weeks of labelled data; objectives that overlap blur the comparison
