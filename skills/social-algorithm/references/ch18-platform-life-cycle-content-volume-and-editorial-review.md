# Chapter 18: Platform Life Cycle, Content Volume and Editorial Review

## Core Idea
Platforms follow a recurring life cycle, and each stage changes what a creator can expect from organic reach. Inside that frame, a common position is that volume is the starting point and quality the destination, that a recognisable format beats imitation, and that an account is run best by giving every piece one objective and reviewing the results by objective. This chapter collects those mechanics. It extends Ch 2 (algorithm shift), Ch 7 (Instagram knowledge base) and Ch 17 (revenue layers). Every figure is a dated or single-source snapshot; platform rules change every year, the principles last longer than the tactics.

## 1. The Platform Life Cycle
- **Typical sequence (a pattern, not a law)**: younger users and light content arrive first, then early adopters, then professionals and companies, and the average age rises. Each stage brings advice that the platform is "too old" or "too noisy"; the pioneers of earlier cycles benefited from starting while it was still early.
- **Ecosystem effect**: a feature introduced by one platform (ephemeral stories, short vertical clips) pushes the others to copy it. As a platform fills with people and ads it becomes "dirtier": feeds get more commercial and less personal.
- **Organic reach opens, then closes**: many platforms offer broad free distribution while they grow and later narrow it so that businesses pay. A mature page may see growth rates far below the early years (a reported drop from low single-digit percent to a fraction of a percent for very large pages, dated about 2018). Treat reach on a mature social page as a paid channel and keep it as an advertising tool and a targeting database.
- **What the platform sells**: attention, not raw data. Advertisers rent access through aggregated targeting; the platform keeps the data.
- **Lookalike audiences**: a platform can find people similar to existing customers or subscribers, which reduces the need to define a buyer persona in detail. With a steady conversion rate, doubling the traffic roughly doubles the sign-ups (a simple proportionality, illustrative).
- **Zero-click search**: a reported share of searches (about half in one dated estimate) ends without a click because the engine answers on its own page. The consequence is presence on several channels, not one.
- **Selling model**: monthly billing lowers the entry barrier; annual prepayment improves cash flow and filters for commitment (`marketing-mba` ch03).

## 2. Volume, Quality and Format
- **Quantity as a starting point, quality as an arrival point**: quality is not objective; the audience decides it, so publish, observe and learn. Moving from one piece a week to one a day changes the context in which the audience meets you.
- **Fear of saturation**: in this model, people who tire of your content were not the right audience; superfans want more. A seller of a product also becomes a media outlet and can repackage the same material in several formats.
- **Continuity over perfection; own format over cloning**: a recognisable format of your own beats copying others. If a piece does not work, start from the assumption that the content was not strong enough (substance and the way it was told) before blaming the platform.
- **Sustainable production**: a simple set-up that allows recording every day, a backup plan, and not confusing effort with result. The idea matters more than equipment; audio and light come before extra cameras.
- **Luck can be enabled**: constant output creates the occasions in which luck can occur (the setter who keeps passing the ball).
- **Format language differs per platform**: the same clip can perform very differently on separate short-form surfaces. On an interest-led platform the single piece counts more than the follower count and old pieces can be revived. For brands: native content, not an advertisement transplanted onto the platform.
- **Narrated clip**: go to the point at once; the first seconds decide (a first 30 seconds is often cited); combine a hook (a challenge, an interesting person) with a place or topic; the format "I did this, here is the result" follows a hero-journey shape.

## 3. A Practitioner's Four Rules (hypotheses to test)
1. **Daily publishing** as the default rhythm.
2. **One platform at a time**, then expand.
3. **Tribe by psychographics**: group the community by shared traits and values, not by age and city.
4. **Teach most, sell little**: a ratio of roughly 98 percent useful content to 2 percent selling is quoted by one practitioner; another quotes 90/10. Both are heuristics, not measured rules. The brand acts as a parachute; selling is an adaptation that comes later.

## 4. Scoring a Piece and Reviewing by Objective
- **Four-parameter score (one practitioner's method, untested)**: rate a piece from 1 to 10 on value, rarity, hard-to-reproduce and hard-to-replace. You compete for attention against every form of entertainment, not only against peers.
- **One objective per piece**: growth, awareness, community, positioning or sales.
- **Retrospective by objective**: label past posts by objective, compare the best and worst inside the same group, and derive the editorial plan from the difference. Look at insights and engagement; fans without engagement are inactive or fake.

## 5. Creator as a Company
- **Averages mislead**: earnings statistics for creators mix a few very high values with many low ones, so the mean is far above the median.
- **Own assets outside the algorithm**: email list, events, a members' area. A members' area you control with a monthly fee gives recurring revenue; third-party tiers pay less and depend on the platform (a position, not a fixed rule).
- **Build a business on the media**: income comes more from products, services and partnerships built on the audience than from views.
- **Credibility and niche count more than numbers**: followers are vanity, fans are the income; see `brand-strategy-storytelling` ch07 for the true-fans arithmetic.
- **Honest warnings for newcomers**: competition is huge, hostile comments can last for years, most creators earn little, the work is solitary, platforms change, burnout is common. Starting for money alone is a weak motive.

## Common Mistakes
- Expecting early-era organic reach on a mature platform.
- Running several platforms at once with none done well.
- Waiting for quality before publishing.
- Copying another creator's format.
- Giving a piece several objectives at once.
- Judging by follower count instead of engagement and income.
