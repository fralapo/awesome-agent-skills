# Chapter 15: Starting From Zero — A Phased Playbook

## Core Idea
A new or near-new account (about a thousand followers, no acquisition) should be built in a fixed order: **offer and positioning → attention and movement → conversations → sales structure**. Skipping ahead (paid traffic, a funnel, a newsletter) on top of an unclear offer or silent profile produces a "house of cards". In this model, a project that is set up correctly tends to close its first clients within roughly **three to four months**, sometimes in the first month if the starting point and market are favourable.

---

## Step 1 — Offer and Positioning First (the step most often underweighted)
- **Two ways to position**: (a) pick a *group of people* with a set of problems (classic niche: dentists, doctors, social-media managers); (b) pick a *specific problem* and solve it for several groups (positioning on the problem, not the niche). Either works; what fails is a broad market ("help personal brands in general").
- **Start specific**: specificity gets a name and first case studies faster; breadth feels like more options but limits all of them.
- **Value of the offer must fit the market**: if buyers are unaware and unwilling to pay more than a small amount to solve the problem, a high-ticket offer has a structural problem. Choose a market where the service has high added value and people already spend to solve it.
- **Anchor on what makes you unique** (character, approach, process) even before you have cases; a generic promise with no case studies does not clear a saturated market. This feeds the bio, the content and the whole communication.

## Step 2 — Content That Creates Movement
- **First goal: attention and movement**, measured first in social metrics and then in opened chats. Without movement there is nobody to sell to.
- **Contents that are highly attractive**: very useful, very practical, so that the target feels foolish not to follow or save (in abstract niches: highly relevant and emotionally weighty rather than technical).
- **Frequency**: not fewer than four to six pieces a week if you want cruising speed (fewer is acceptable without urgency); about four a week is a practical minimum just to produce enough data.
- **Be directional** and avoid generic topics so the profile is categorised correctly.
- **Use references, formats and series**: create vertical series, test several formats, add a recurring unique element; a plain talking-to-camera 60-second video with default editing is not enough from zero.
- **Do not start with paid boosts on unoptimised content**: it is a traffic strategy, not an acquisition strategy. Boost accelerates something that works; it does not fix a missing strategy (a reported experience: some requests arrive, but within 6-12 months the build is a "house of cards").

## Step 3 — Turn Movement Into Conversations (be proactive)
Do not worry about calls, closings or revenue yet; the aim is conversations and data, and to learn whether the audience is in target.
- **Talk about the service** from the start (direct, sales-oriented Stories included): until people know what you sell and how you work, CTAs and content do not convert. You are a professional with a service, not just a content creator.
- **Soft actions in Stories**: polls about the situation the target lives in, then you open the conversation from the answers. Avoid "write INFO" Stories while the profile is still cold.
- **Reach out to new followers** with a free resource coherent with the service; the aim is a relationship, not a call: worst case the person knows what you do and stays warm, best case a match appears and a no-pressure call is proposed.
- **Free resource with an automation** (keyword → guide) to open chats; an email list is useless until there is an email strategy.
- Expect to feel you are doing a lot: in the early stage the engines need to run at full power in every direction because there is no data yet. A frequent client error is to publish and wait.
- Reaching out may reveal that followers are not in target; better to learn this in weeks than after months of content.

## Step 4 — Sales Structure
Add contact points (profile funnel, sales-oriented Stories and posts, lead magnets, automations), then the sales process (chat → pre-call screening → call). First make sure steps 1-3 exist; without movement and conversations a sales process has nothing to process.

## Content Mix by Account State
- **Small account, few people in target** → mostly attraction (for example around 80%), the rest awareness; sales content later.
- **Stalled but with a base** → balanced mix.
- **Big numbers, cold audience (people do not know what you do)** → increase awareness content.
- Many accounts already sell through awareness content and sales Stories without hard-sale posts once movement exists.
- Example weekly plan reported: 10 attraction, 2 awareness, 2 sales pieces over a planning block.

## If No Clients After About Four Months
Usually one of three causes: (1) **positioning** (market new to you, unvalidated offer, unknown target: more time to validate); (2) **communication** (not consistent, not effortful in scripts and messages); (3) **not proactive** (publishing and waiting). Diagnose in this order.

## Anti-Patterns
- Boosting posts before the strategy exists
- A broad promise to "everyone"
- A high-ticket offer in a market that does not pay for the problem
- Resource + email capture with no follow-up or no email flow
- Waiting for DMs instead of opening conversations

## Key Takeaways
1. Order: offer/positioning, movement, conversations, sales structure
2. Start specific; the market and the price must match
3. Four to six pieces a week, directional, in series and varied formats
4. Be proactive: service talk, soft Stories, new-follower outreach, resource automations
5. Boost accelerates; it does not replace strategy
6. First clients usually within 3-4 months when the phases are done properly
