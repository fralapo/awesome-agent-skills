# Chapter 14: Paid Live Events as a Conversion Layer

## Core Idea
A multi-evening paid workshop is a way to convert an accumulated audience (and cold paid traffic) through hours of real value instead of a few seconds of content, then move interested people into a high-touch offer. In this model it works because the organic personal brand lowers the cost of every ticket and the event lets the audience experience the method before any pitch. It is a heavy operation: treat it as a project with a team, not as a content format.

> The figures below are **one operator's reported results for a first event** and are given as orders of magnitude to calibrate expectations, not as benchmarks. A first event tends to outperform later ones (novelty, a warm base).

---

## 1. Why Combine Organic and Ads Here
- A dated event is a natural place to run **paid and organic funnels together**; the two are complementary, not rival religions (see Ch 4, ADV integration).
- Reported effect: roughly **half of the tickets** came from organic and the paid spend per result was about **half** of what it would have been without an established personal brand and positioning. The organic channel also produced slightly more revenue than the paid one in the end.
- Practical lesson: even a launch-driven business gets a cost advantage from an organic asset built earlier.

## 2. Protect the Core Asset: Use a Dedicated Profile
- Promote the event from a **separate, event-specific profile**, not the main personal-brand account. Reasons: the main account is a company asset and heavy pre-event promotion would drag engagement and annoy a community that follows for other reasons; and a first-time ad account could be restricted, which must not endanger the main profile.
- Trade-off accepted: less authority and trust on the new profile, compensated by organic mentions from the main one.

## 3. Funnel Structure and Timeline
1. **Waitlist ads, about a month before** — tests the ground and collects data. Reported: low cost per lead, high intent (the event, its paid ticket and its format were already explained).
2. **Official funnel about 3-4 weeks before** — organic and paid both land on a **landing page with a short (~5 min) presentation video**, the full agenda, and a direct ticket purchase. Tag traffic so organic and paid are measurable separately.
3. **Price ladder** — the ticket price rose roughly weekly. Pro: purchase spikes at each step, many tickets sold early. Con (to verify): price changes on the landing page may disturb ad optimisation; test before relying on it.
4. **Upgrade tier** — a higher ticket with extras (a private pre-session, lifetime recordings, a private group) helped amortise ad costs.
5. **Nurture** — an email flow so strangers learn who you are, plus a messaging-app community for materials and reminders over the dead days before the event; direct support contact.
6. **Offer** — no discounts. Value was added on the **bonus** side instead (for example free access to a later in-person event), which raises perceived value without cheapening the core price.

Reported project timeline: roughly 7-8 weeks from first alignment (concept, funnel, landing pages, scripts) to event day; waitlist ads in week ~3-4; teaching materials and slides started late (the biggest underestimation).

## 4. Event Format
- **Five evenings of about 90 minutes**, each laying the base for the next, so attending night one makes skipping night two irrational. The arc: positioning and communication → content strategy → organic funnels → organisation/production system → AI integration, with a guest on the paid-advertising side for the last evening.
- The **offer is mentioned only at the end of the last two evenings**. The aim is a real event with takeaways, not a free webinar that opens with a hero's-journey story and a cold pitch after 20 minutes.
- Because the events are live, recordings are secondary (the paid tier gets them for longer).

## 5. Reported Funnel Numbers (first event, order of magnitude)
- Tickets: about 585 sold vs a 400 goal (~1.4x). Refund requests: ~2%.
- Show rate: ~90% the first evening, ~60-70% on average across evenings (a reference figure given to the operator was ~50%).
- ~27% of attendees booked a call; call no-show ~13%.
- Overall closing rate including calls that happened around the event: ~45% (a first-event reference given was 30-40%).
- Sales continued for about two weeks after the event.

## 6. Lessons Learned
- **Start ads earlier than you think** when you have no paid data: otherwise you record ad creatives every evening and edit them in a rush to test variants.
- **Emotional load**: cold paid traffic brings hostile, baseless comments; the pressure on the person who is the face of the brand is real and was underestimated. Plan for moderation and recovery.
- **Slide production** takes far longer than expected (hundreds of slides, then rehearsals to stay in time). Start early; rehearse every evening.
- **Size the sales and delivery capacity before the event**: booked calls exceeded forecast, discovery calls spilled over two weeks, onboarding of new clients arrived at the same time. Reserve delivery slots in advance and add sales capacity.
- **Keep access rules simple**: different recording-expiry rules for two ticket types created a flood of support questions even though the rule was written everywhere. People do not read; reduce complexity instead of repeating warnings.
- **Do not attempt it without a team** that can dedicate most of its focus for weeks.
- Send a **post-event satisfaction survey**.
- Funnels and automations will break under load at the peak moment; assume it and have people ready.

## Anti-Patterns
- Promoting from the main account until engagement drops
- A price ladder with no check of its effect on ad delivery
- Pitch in the first minutes of every evening
- Two ticket types with different access rules and no simple message
- No sales capacity plan for the 1-2 weeks after the event
- Treating an event as a content shoot rather than a project

## Key Takeaways
1. A paid multi-evening workshop converts through lived value; the pitch comes last
2. Organic assets roughly halve the paid cost per result (reported)
3. Use a separate event profile to protect the main asset
4. Start with a waitlist ~1 month before; collect data early
5. Plan sales, delivery and slide production before announcing the date
6. First-event numbers flatter later ones; expect lower repeats
