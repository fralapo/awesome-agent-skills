# Chapter 07: Data: Charts, Tables, Numbers & Timelines

## Core Idea
Tables, charts and numbers are the most important and the worst-designed content in business decks: 2000s-style table themes, 3D charts, key figures buried in bullet lists, screenshots pasted from Excel or dashboards in a foreign style. Every data slide is redesigned with the same three moves: **remove** what does not serve the spoken message (duplicates, extra labels, gridlines, legends, decimals), **create hierarchy** (one message per slide, written in the title; big number, small label), and **highlight only the datum being cited**, preferably by dimming everything else rather than adding arrows and boxes. Before adding, try removing. Data must be rebuilt in the deck's own fonts and colors so it never looks "pasted on". When a table cannot be avoided (it often can't in companies), make it light: no fills, thin horizontal hairlines only, a heavier rule under a distinct header, generous cell padding, and one focus. Long sequences (timelines, offers, feature lists) are split across several slides and revealed one step at a time, because the slide should show only what is being said at that moment.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Remove → Hierarchy → Highlight:** the three-step clean-up for any table, chart or number slide. First strip the superfluous, then build size/weight/color hierarchy, then point the eye at the one datum that supports the slide's claim.
When to use: first pass on every data slide, before any aesthetic work.

**Highlight by subtraction:** to make one series/country/bar stand out, lower the opacity or desaturate the others (e.g. other bars at 80% transparency, focus bar solid) instead of adding frames, arrows or callouts that create clutter.
When to use: comparison charts, tables with one winning value, any "look at this one" moment.

**Five table best practices:** (1) no cell fills; (2) only horizontal lines, no verticals, no outer border; (3) thin, light lines, heavier only under the header; (4) header bigger, colored, bold, body smaller, neutral, regular; (5) highlight the key datum with color/bold or a rounded rectangle.
When to use: every table that must stay a table.

**Five chart best practices:** (1) no 3D, only the detail needed; (2) colors with meaning, taken from the brand/theme; (3) legend integrated next to the line/series (or removed); (4) highlight the datum by dimming the rest; (5) animate the chart one piece at a time following the voice.
When to use: every native chart.

**Three number best practices:** (1) never inside a bullet list; (2) hierarchy: big number + small description; (3) add an icon or image that makes the figure immediate.
When to use: KPI, "key numbers", results, percentages.

**One step per slide (timelines and sequences):** one event/date per slide with the timeline as a small reference bar; states of each milestone are *to come* (outline/grey), *active* (filled accent), *past* (filled dark or empty).
When to use: company histories, roadmaps, process steps, biographies.

**Value before price (offer slides):** tell what is in the offer category by category, on separate slides, and show the price last; the more value is built before, the more a (high) price feels justified.
When to use: pricing, sponsorship packages, commercial proposals.

## Rules & Numbers
Canvas is 1920×1080 px unless stated; sizes in pt and aligned with ch04 type scale (title on data/chart slides 40–48, data descriptions 24, key numbers ≥56, hero numbers 120–200, sources 18–20).

**General**
- One chart or one table per slide. Two unrelated charts on stage make the eye hunt for data and stop listening.
- Chart title and slide title: one or the other, never both. The slide title can carry the takeaway ("80% of…").
- Remove the legend when there is one series; remove the Y axis when data labels are present; remove duplicated info (years both in subtitle and legend); put "%" on the numbers, not in a separate note; spell out obscure acronyms.
- Reuse the colors of the titles inside the charts. Use theme colors so "Change colors" offers the brand palette. A monochrome palette (one hue, lighter/darker variants made in HSB) works especially well for pies and donuts.
- Don't communicate categories by color alone: add labels or icons.
- Tables, charts or schemes arriving from Excel, Google Images, dashboards or other documents are rebuilt in the deck's fonts and colors. A slide that looks fine alone but clashes with the deck is a failure.
- Before cleaning a table, ask whether it is needed at all or whether one key number would do.

**Charts (line/bar)**
- Data labels only on the cited datum when focusing (e.g. 32 pt bold with a colored leader line); otherwise labels above the line at ~18 pt.
- Axis labels 18–20 pt, light/medium grey or same color as the text, same font as the deck. Bar-chart X-axis text can go up to 28 pt black when there is no Y axis or grid.
- Gridlines: lighten (white at 50%, or very light grey) rather than necessarily delete; delete entirely in a simple bar slide.
- Line series: ~4 pt, square caps, no shadow. Average line as a separate series in a contrasting color (orange/red) with the word "Average" written next to it, not in the legend.
- Percent axis: enter values as decimals (0.82, not 82, or you get 8200%), format as percentage with 0 decimals, max 1.0 (=100%), major unit 0.1.
- Bar slide layout: label + max two-line key message top-left; chart below in the white space; no chart title, legend, Y axis or grid; widen bars (gap width).
- Source line: bottom, resting on the margin, medium grey, 18 pt, text box inner margins 0.
- Legends you keep: recreate by hand if needed (small borderless color squares + label in the same color), or in a template put them at the bottom, enlarged to be legible.

**Alternatives to the pie chart**
- Parts of a whole (sums to 100%): pie, donut, single-row stacked bar or treemap. Single percentage or comparison of independent percentages: bubble chart, waffle chart, area-fill.
- Area-fill: a colored rectangle covering the same share of the slide as the percentage (e.g. 74% or 81% of the width) over a grey full-slide rectangle, number on top in a contrasting color.
- Donut variant: arcs of different thickness/color built with the Arc shape over a hidden real pie chart used as an exact reference (hide it, don't delete it, so data can be updated).
- Bubble chart for one percentage: two rows, X = 1 for both, Y = 0, sizes 100 and 33; X axis min 0/max 2, major unit 1; Y axis min 0 (cuts bubbles in half = semicircle effect), max 1. Several percentages: X = 1…5, X max 6, Y min −1 to see whole bubbles. Bubble scale 300 for one pair, 200–250 when several sit side by side. The 100% reference bubble is a dashed outline with no fill; the highlighted one is filled (gradient allowed, transparent where overlapping). Labels show bubble size, not Y value.
- Never 3D.

**Big numbers**
- Number large, description small: hero numbers 120–200 pt with a 24–32 pt caption, e.g. number 120 pt in accent color + description 32 pt black; or number 200 pt + 32 pt caption; description in a secondary color with ~16 pt space after. In dense data slides key numbers drop to ≥56 pt with 24 pt descriptions (see ch04 type scale).
- Three-number slide (sparse): 24 pt label top center → 80 pt introductory sentence (message title) → three blocks side by side (120 pt number, 32 pt description), separated by thin grey lines.
- Percent sign smaller than the digits, optionally in color. Bold inside a bullet is not enough to make a figure emerge.

**Tables**
- No fills; remove all borders, then reapply only inside-horizontal + bottom lines in medium/light grey, very thin; the rule under the header heavier (in white on dark decks).
- Header: bigger, bold, accent color (e.g. header 32 pt bold); a two-level header uses merged cells (group "Q1 2024" once instead of repeating it) with the second row in a lighter HSB tint of the accent; keep header rows shorter than data rows. Optional thin 2 pt white divider between groups is the only vertical allowed.
- State context once ("/2024" in the title) instead of repeating it in every cell.
- Cell margins: left/right 0 (there are no vertical lines), top/bottom ≈0.5–1 cm (0.2 is too tight); add 0.3–0.5 right margin only on data cells if highlight boxes would touch. Vertically center text. Numbers right-aligned; currency label not bold.
- Body text neutral, regular; e.g. 24 pt in a dense competitor table. Avoid ALL CAPS and heavy alternating row stripes.
- Semantic emphasis: best value bold green, worst red, optionally a small rectangle behind the number at 85% transparency. Add highlight shapes last (they must be moved by hand if the table moves).
- Optional: rounded white rectangle under the whole table, very soft blurred shadow, 85% transparency, when other content surrounds it; on dark decks a dark rounded rectangle under the table fakes rounded corners.

**Competitor / feature-comparison tables**
- Yes/no: brand main color for "present", grey ("disabled") for "absent", always with check and X icons. Avoid traffic-light green/red: it clashes with the identity.
- Our brand in the header must be the most visible; logos in identical white rounded boxes so all have the same proportion.
- Delete columns with no information (a column that says "high" everywhere). Reveal the differentiators in a second step (click/morph) when the speaker focuses on them.
- Quick variant: white borders everywhere, feature column 28 pt on very light grey, cells filled with two colors for has/hasn't.
- Two-group comparison: criteria listed once on the left with horizontal separators, the two groups' values in adjacent columns so they can be compared; one accent (colored circles) for the variations.

**Timelines**
- Never all dates on one slide: even with fades the eye goes everywhere. Max ~3 dates per slide if they must share one; ideally one per slide.
- Dates are the message: shrink and center the title; date very large (hero-number size, e.g. 200 pt serif), unit/era small in the sans in accent color, description 32 pt aligned to the date's baseline.
- Thin timeline at the bottom as a reference; milestones as outline circles; active one filled accent with its date; past ones filled dark. Variant: dashed grey line, accent dots with a thick lighter ring; or one highlighted dot with previous/next dates in transparency.
- Palette: one light, one dark, one accent. Never all the colors of a flag on one slide; multiple identity colors can become thin bars at top/bottom where each segment is a chapter that rises for the current section.
- No cut-out faces or decorative photos on every timeline slide: they steal focus.
- Between timeline slides a plain fade is enough; for a continuous strip, use a Push transition left→right and let the last line segment exit the slide to connect to the next. Line thickness ~1.5–2 pt. Balance dates above/below the line optically.

**Laws and norms**
- If the full text must appear, show it in grey and color only the cited passage, or reveal passages one at a time with fades. Always read it aloud and comment it, otherwise don't include it.

**Gantt / project schedules**
- No Gantt-specific rules exist in this method; apply the two closest ones together: the table rules (no fills except the bars, horizontal hairlines only, distinct header) and the timeline state logic (current phase in accent, others grey; one phase discussed per click/slide).

## Procedures
**Clean a native table (any software)**
1. Remove all fills. 2. Remove all borders; reapply only horizontal inner + bottom, thin, light grey. 3. Make the header rule heavier. 4. Format header (bigger, bold, accent); merge cells for groups. 5. Set custom cell margins (L/R 0, T/B ≈0.5–1 cm), vertical center, numbers right. 6. Highlight the key value (bold/color or rounded shape added last). 7. Text box inner margins 0 for surrounding labels.

**Clean a native chart**
1. Delete chart title (if the slide title exists), legend (single series), Y axis (if data labels), 3D and shadows. 2. Apply theme colors; dim the non-focus series (≈80% transparency) and keep the focus series solid. 3. Data label only on the focus point (double-click to select a single point). 4. Lighten gridlines; set axis number format and bounds. 5. Write the takeaway in the slide title. 6. Animate piece by piece in sync with speech.

**Save chart templates (brand consistency for clients)**
1. Set custom theme colors in the slide master first. 2. Build one chart per type as complete as possible (several series, legend, labels, axes): a template saved from a one-series bar chart will not color a three-series chart. 3. Save as chart template, named with a brand prefix ("Brand line", "Brand bar", "Brand pie"). 4. Insert new charts from Templates, or Change chart type → Templates. One template per chart type.

**Linked charts**
Every native chart sits on an Excel sheet. Paste from Excel keeping the link so updates flow into the slide; on Mac keep folders organized because links cannot be re-pointed (on Windows they can). Build the chart small and enlarge it at the end.

**Screenshots of charts you cannot rebuild**
Capture with margin around the chart → crop to rounded rectangle → place on a larger rounded rectangle in light grey or the accent color → optional soft shadow. Rebuilding natively is still better.

**Table → slides/cards**
1. Understand the content first, then visualize. 2. Separate narrative from data: left half explains, right half previews. 3. If a table holds one key result, turn it into columns context → action → result, with the result in an accent box. 4. Index slide built as a table: big "Index" title (~120 pt, menu headline size) left; table ~2/3 of the slide on the right with chapter numbers (64 pt, grey or accent) and titles (64 pt, black), horizontal lines only.

**Offer/pricing sequence (sent or presented)**
1. Intro slide: small grey label, big bold sentence, grey secondary paragraph, packages previewed in a grey panel. 2. One slide per category: packages pinned top-right, item titles bold, details smaller with hairlines, accent check marks per plan. 3. Price slide last: plans side by side, price large, discount stated explicitly ("−50%, €X if confirmed by date") instead of an ambiguous highlight. When presented aloud: show the struck-through price then the new one on the next click. Take style from the client's website; take structure from web pricing pages (they are better designed than slides).

**Minimal animated timeline**
1. Design the base tile (date + description group). 2. Duplicate for milestones. 3. Decide how the tile behaves when its date is spoken (moves, fills, info enters). 4. One slide per milestone with the progress bar at the bottom, fade between slides; or lines drawn with a wipe in the direction of the stroke, connector line "with previous" +0.2 s, date group fade 0.3 s.

## Common Mistakes
- Key figures in a bullet list → isolate as big number + small label + icon.
- 3D charts and default table themes → flat, theme colors, hairlines.
- Adding arrows/boxes to highlight → dim the others instead.
- Labels on every data point → label only the cited one.
- Chart title duplicating the slide title → keep one; put the takeaway in the slide title.
- Colors too similar (average line confused with a series) → contrasting accent for the reference line.
- Table pasted from Excel/Canva in another style → rebuild in deck fonts and colors.
- Traffic-light green/red for yes/no in a branded table → brand color vs grey + check/X.
- Vertical lines, outer border, filled header rows, zebra stripes, ALL CAPS → remove.
- Cramped rows → add top/bottom cell margins.
- All dates on one timeline slide; title as the biggest element → one date per slide, shrink the title.
- Pricing shown first or in one dense table → value by category, price last.
- Full legal text with no focus → grey text, colored cited passage, read it aloud.

## Mental Models
- **Every number has an identity:** in a bullet list everything looks equally important.
- **The grid serves, it doesn't attract:** if the eye lands on lines before data, the lines are too strong.
- **Focus comes before the data:** the audience should see the point at first glance, then the table.
- **More detail = less attention** on what matters.
- **Each chart type has its own proportions:** reuse a "text left / chart right" layout but adapt space to the chart's shape (horizontal charts below a top message; donuts on the right half).
- **A template is a contract:** chart templates and theme colors keep data consistent even when the client fills the deck.

## Examples
- **Revenue by product table (three products, four years):** fills and verticals removed, 32 pt bold header, best seller of each year in a rounded box, product icons; companion line chart with labels above the line and lightened grid; a "key numbers" slide (revenue, headcount, % satisfied clients) as three outlined boxes with icons.
- **Tax-gap bar chart across European countries:** inverted title/subtitle hierarchy and duplicated years fixed; takeaway in a 48 pt bold title; legend merged next to the title; one country's bar solid with a 32 pt bold label, other bars 80% transparent; average as a red/orange line labeled "Average"; source 18 pt grey.
- **Quarterly revenue table:** two-level merged header (accent row with white text, lighter tint below), grey hairlines, 2 pt white divider between quarters, numbers right-aligned, best month green bold, worst red.
- **Startup pitch competitor slide (8×7 table):** fills/verticals removed, white 24 pt text, accent header, thicker white rule under the header, logos in equal white rounded boxes, brand color vs grey checks; differentiators revealed with a 3 pt yellow outline box via morph + 0.5 s fade.
- **Ice-cream consumption percentages:** bubble chart with a dashed 100% reference and a filled 33% bubble cut in half by the axis; flavors compared as five bubbles in subject-coherent pastel colors.
- **Sponsorship offer:** from one paragraph + Excel table to intro slide → one slide per category with accent checks per plan → price slide with explicit early-bird discount.
- **Historical biography timeline:** from a crowded slide with a cut-out portrait to one date per slide, big serif date, small sans description, outline-circle milestones at the bottom, flag colors turned into chapter bars.
