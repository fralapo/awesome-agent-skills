# Chapter 02: Deck Structure & Slide Types

## Core Idea
A good deck is built from a small set of recurring slide types, each with one job: cover, agenda/index, break (section) slide, focus slide, content slide and back cover. The types are told apart by their background, which sets up a visual pattern. Repeating that pattern tells the audience what to expect, so they concentrate on the content; breaking it (a coloured focus slide, a section divider) grabs attention. Content changes from deck to deck, but this form stays the same, so a personal or brand template with these layouts saves energy and keeps decks coherent. Peripheral navigation elements (footer, page number, small logo symbol) support the audience, especially when slides are photographed, shared or revisited during Q&A. They must never compete with the content. The last slide is the most valuable one in a business context. Use it for a call to action, not "thank you for your attention".

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 5 slide types (+ index)**:
  - *Cover*: graphics different from the rest (colour or image); contextualizes the topic or introduces it.
  - *Break slide*: divides the topics like a chapter marker; number + title; a different background.
  - *Focus slide*: a phrase or number to remember; a different colour; the speaker lingers on it.
  - *Content slide*: the majority of the deck; light background, a fixed frame, varied layouts inside.
  - *Back cover*: a call to action, contacts, or the main concept to remember.
  - The *agenda/index* sits between cover and content when the deck has chapters.
  When to use: as the skeleton of every template and every deck longer than a handful of slides.
- **Background rhythm scheme**: cover in the main colour; content slides white or off-white; break slides in the secondary colour (or a desaturated, darker tint of the main colour with the main colour as accent); focus slides in a contrasting colour (e.g. black); closing slide identical to the cover. Use a background image only when it adds weight to the text.
  When to use: always. Apply it rigidly in long sessions (2–3 h trainings); play more freely with colour in 5–10-min presentations.
- **Content-slide frame**: fixed peripheral references (deck or chapter title, author, year, page number, small logo symbol). Free, varied layouts inside the frame.
  When to use: for any deck that will be photographed, shared, printed, or discussed in Q&A.
- **Label + message title hierarchy**: the generic category ("Problem", "Mission", "Revenue 2025", "Idea") becomes a small label top-left. Directly below it goes a large sentence carrying the key message. Charts, tables and diagrams only support that sentence.
  When to use: on every content slide; it also makes an emailed deck readable without a speaker.
- **Slide master hierarchy (PowerPoint)**: the master controls the layouts and each layout controls its slides. Guides set in the master apply everywhere.
  When to use: setting up any recurring structure (footer, guides, light and dark layouts).

## Rules & Numbers
**Cover**
- Design it *last*. It stays on screen for a few seconds and needs no special effects. Usually enough: a very large title in a font with personality, a little context information to complete the layout, a background image that adds weight to the theme, and a good composition.
- It doesn't have to be slide 1. First ask how the speech begins: a question, a figure or an image can open, with the cover after the intro, or the cover can be dropped. The cover is essential when introducing topic and speaker at an event, when it stays on screen before the start, and in decks sent by email (there it stays first).
- Standard contents: title, optional subtitle, name and surname (every group member), organization or client, date. Add context fields by setting (class/subject/school, supervisor/co-supervisor, company).
- Cover hierarchy example: large title, smaller subtitle, supervisors' names larger than the date, date small bottom-right, logos bottom-left. Split the title into a **Bold** part (the important one) and a Regular part. Leave more air between secondary details.
- Logos: white on a coloured background, or black. A logo that has its own white background goes inside a white band or box (e.g. along the bottom), never pasted onto the colour. The full logo appears only on the cover (and back cover), with whitespace around it.
- Background: a full-bleed photo blurred (sharpness reduced) and darkened (brightness reduced), or a photo under a semi-transparent black rectangle, or a solid brand colour. A mesh gradient (from a free generator) gives an instant "tech/AI" feel. Keep text and image clearly separated.
- Treat it like a website hero section, minus the menu, logo bar, buttons and CTA (buttons belong only in interactive decks). Use white, not dull grey.

**Agenda / index**
- List only first-level macro-chapters (about 4 entries), never every slide title. Group topics into sections and mark them with break slides.
- Table-based index: "Index" top-left at 120 pt (a menu headline, inside the 80–160 range; see ch04 type scale). On the right, a table covering about 2/3 of the slide with only horizontal lines visible and top/bottom cell margins. Column 1 holds chapter numbers in grey or the main colour at 64 pt; column 2 holds chapter titles in black at 64 pt. Adding a chapter means adding a row.
- Keep title and entries on the same alignment.
- In decks sent as PDF, make the index clickable: arrows or buttons linking to each section, plus a home/menu icon at the top of each slide that returns to the index. Add a short legend at the start explaining that the PDF is interactive. Buttons must look clickable and anticipate the destination; never write "click here". Test every link, and have someone else test them too.
- Announce the topics at the start of a lesson or talk and avoid jumping between topics.

**Break / section slides**
- At most two levels of numbering (1, 1.1); deeper levels lose the audience.
- Use a different background colour from the cover and from content slides; it can change per topic. Show the number and title. Asymmetric composition (title left, subtitle right) with everything white except the number in the accent colour.
- A summary slide that previews the 3–4 points of a section works as a break slide too, and is better than jumping straight into detail.
- Dividers are where brand personality is allowed: a colour per section, a mascot entering from the bottom, geometric elements looping slowly. Keep content slides sober.
- Dynamic alternative: chapter boxes of equal height under a top-placed footer, the first coloured and the others grey. With Morph, the active box grows to fill the slide (it can turn white for content) while the others slide off-canvas. This works as a running index through the whole deck.

**Focus slides**
- One sentence or number to remember. A contrasting background (e.g. near-black), a short phrase on the left, an abstract (optionally animated) illustration on the right. Hold it longer than other slides.
- A big number: large and centred, with a small description. In corporate decks it can sit on a different background or have different graphics.

**Content slides**
- Light background: off-white, not pure #FFFFFF; text in very dark grey or very dark blue, not pure black. One accent colour for titles, highlights and dividers. Dark backgrounds also weigh heavily in print.
- One key message per slide. Change the layout from slide to slide (text + image, diagrams only, big number, quote): each change regains attention and "labels" the content type, which makes the content hierarchy readable in long decks.
- Place primary elements on the left (read first) and secondary ones (images to look at afterwards) on the right.
- The deck title must not compete with the slide title: keep it small and in the same place on every slide.
- Long lists inside content: one item per slide with a small progress indicator (a "slider"), instead of 10 rules on one slide. The same applies to any bullet list.
- Project/work decks: a header with author, deck title and chapter title; a summary slide previewing the section's points; then deep-dive slides with summarized text top-left and an image on the right focused on the feature. Write specific text per slide; never repeat the same generic paragraph while only the images change.
- A break can also be a curiosity slide ("Did you know…?") that resets attention between dense content slides.
- Keep unused slides hidden rather than deleted; they may be needed later (Canva: ⋯ on the page > Hide page).
- Template minimum: more than "title + text". If title + text is the only content layout, people will paste only text. Provide text + image, data, quote, section and dark variants.

**Footer, page numbers, logos**
- Standard footer: small company logo on the left (symbol-only version if one exists), deck title in the centre, page number on the right.
- Training-deck frame variant: deck title top-left, author name bottom-left, year, page number. Any variant is fine as long as it stays fixed.
- Footer text 18–20 pt Regular on 1920×1080 (Light becomes invisible); see ch04 type scale. Inner margins of the footer text box at 0, text aligned top, and the box inside the bottom margin. Remove the date.
- The page number goes in a quiet corner (bottom-right), never top-left or on a strong colour block. A chapter reference can go top-left and a subject/course reference bottom-left, both small. Right alignment is reserved for captions and the page number.
- Page numbers help in Q&A ("go back to slide 12") and when slides are photographed. They are optional in a short deck (about 13 slides, e.g. a pitch). In portfolios use simple sequential numbers, not project-internal numbering, and put your name in the footer to mark authorship.
- No full logo on every slide, no giant logo in the footer, no filled footer band, no name watermark over content. A footer must never be larger than the content (e.g. a wide photo strip across the bottom makes a template unusable).
- Put the logo in the slide master only if it works on both light and dark; otherwise place it per layout, or use two masters (light/dark).
- Partner/client logo walls: put every logo in identical rectangles (or a hairline grid) so proportions match. Render all logos monochrome (all black, all white or greyscale to suit the background), with the brands' approval, to avoid a "rainbow".

**Back cover**
- Answer: "What do I want the audience to do when I finish speaking?" Give the next step, the contact (email), where to keep learning (social channels), the decision requested, or the main concept to remember.
- "Thank you for your attention" wastes the slide; it appears because we don't know how to close. In school contexts a plain "Thank you" (not "for your attention"), a reflective question or a quote is acceptable. In business, always use the CTA. In a thesis or project defence, close with future developments to show the work continues. In a portfolio, close with contacts (LinkedIn, email) and repeat your name and role.
- Same background as the cover, to close the loop.

## Procedures
**A. Set up the structure in PowerPoint's slide master**
1. View > Slide Master. Delete unused layouts; right-click > Grid and Guides > add vertical and horizontal guides for the margins (plus a centre guide) on the main master.
2. Set title and body fonts on the master and define custom theme colours (main colour plus tints).
3. Footer: remove the date, place the footer text in the centre and the number on the right (or the chosen variant), set text-box margins to 0. Then Insert > Header & Footer: tick slide number and footer, type the deck title, Apply to All. Changing it there updates every slide. If a layout doesn't update, untick and re-tick it.
4. Build layouts: cover, index, break, focus, content variants and back cover. Use Background Styles for the dark ones (with title and footer colours adjusted), and Insert Placeholder for image/text slots.
5. Save as a template (.potx): opening it creates a new .pptx, so the template is never overwritten. In Google Slides follow the same order (theme colours → footer → layouts → ready-made extras such as tables, charts and connectors → a usage-guidelines slide).

**B. Check the rhythm**
1. Zoom out to see all slides at once: Keynote light table, PowerPoint Slide Sorter, Google Slides grid view.
2. Verify the colour sequence (cover/main → content/light → break/secondary → closing = cover) and that no slide type breaks the pattern by accident.

**C. Quick index from a table**
1. Type "Index" top-left at 120 pt (menu headline size).
2. Insert a 2-column table on the right 2/3 of the slide, with no fill and only horizontal borders.
3. Set the cell margins (top/bottom) for air. Numbers in the accent colour or grey at 64 pt, titles in near-black at 64 pt.

## Common Mistakes
- An index that lists every slide → about 4 macro-sections plus break slides.
- Background colours alternating with no rule → assign one background per slide type.
- The same background image repeated on every slide → plain light background for content; colour for cover/break/back cover; images only when they add weight to the text.
- Hours on the cover, rushed content → design the cover last, in a short time.
- The deck title as the biggest text on every slide → shrink it into the frame; the slide's own message gets the space.
- Generic titles ("Problem", "Idea") with the message hidden below → small label (20–28 pt) plus a big message sentence (64–88 pt; see ch04 type scale).
- A huge logo or photo strip in the footer → small symbol only; full logo on cover and back cover.
- Page number top-left or on a colour block → bottom-right, small.
- "Thank you for your attention" as the final slide → a call to action.
- Break slides with three or more levels → maximum two levels.
- A template with only cover + title/text → several content layouts, a light footer, and a test before distribution.

## Mental Models
- **Pattern, then break the pattern:** the repeated background lets people relax into the content; a deliberate break (focus slide, divider) is how you shout without shouting.
- **The frame is for the audience, not the designer:** navigation elements exist so a viewer can say "slide 14" or find the chapter in a photo.
- **The last slide is prime real estate:** the final image stays on screen during Q&A. Make it work.
- **Cover = website hero; index = navigation menu; break slide = chapter page.**

## Examples
- **Training-deck system:** a coloured cover visible before the session; content slides framed with title top-left, name bottom-left, year and page number; black focus slides with a short phrase on the left and an animated abstract illustration on the right; numbered break slides in a different colour; a back cover with "Need a consultation?" plus email, and "Keep learning" plus social handles.
- **University paper redesign:** a cover with the name top-left, the title split bold/regular inside a shape, and a white band for the colour logo. Break slides in a desaturated petrol tone with the number in the main light blue and a title-left/subtitle-right layout. Content slides on white with a small chapter reference and page number placed discreetly.
- **Design-project index redesign:** many entries cut to 4 grouped sections, with a filled circle reused as the bullet for each entry. Background scheme fixed as green for the cover and dividers, beige for the index and content, replacing an arbitrary alternation.
- **Brand with a strong personality:** each section divider has its own background colour, slow looping geometric shapes and a mascot that enters from the bottom with a simple animation. Content slides stay on a single darker colour.
- **Corporate event template rejected:** a heavy photo footer, only three layouts, and white titles with black shadows on white. The fix: a light footer and multiple content layouts.
- **Partner slide:** logos of mixed shapes and colours placed in equal white rounded boxes and converted to monochrome; the slide reads as ordered at a glance.
