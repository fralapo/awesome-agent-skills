# Chapter 10: The Redesign Playbook (Before → After)

## Core Idea
Redesigning a slide is a diagnosis before it is a makeover: first understand the content and the one message the slide must deliver, then list what is wrong, keep what already works, and only then change the form. Almost every weak slide fails in the same handful of ways — the wrong element is the biggest (deck title, page number, slide title instead of the data), too much is shown at once, the same idea is repeated, items have no visual identity, a pasted table/diagram/screenshot breaks the deck's style, colors are too many or off-brand, alignment and margins are random. The fixes are equally repeatable: cut and merge duplicate text, split content across several slides (one point, one date, one category at a time), rebuild alien material in the deck style, give each item an anchor (icon, number, mark), restore a three-level hierarchy, dim what is not being discussed, and derive style from the subject's own identity (brand site, logo, packaging, book cover, flag, product UI) instead of from other slides. A good redesign does not overturn a brand: it keeps background, colors and the original good idea, and improves structure and legibility.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Five-area audit (Organization – Text – Color – Style – Resources)**: Organization = slide types and their sequence (cover, break slides, content, back cover; recurring layouts). Text = quantity, hierarchy, alignment, column width, emphasis. Color = number of colors, contrast, brand fit, role per slide type. Style = font(s), shapes, corners, shadows, consistency. Resources = images, icons, diagrams, mockups: quality, coherence, origin.
When to use: reviewing any whole deck (paper presentation, portfolio, corporate deck) before touching a single slide.

**Keep – Kill – Change**: list what works and must survive (a good idea, brand colors, a progress element), what must go (duplicates, decorative shapes, unused columns, filler images), and what must be transformed (table → layout, bullets → cards, many dates → one per slide).
When to use: every single-slide redesign; it prevents throwing away the client's strengths.

**Split instead of squeeze**: "more slides doesn't mean talking longer." One item per slide with an indicator beats one crowded slide with fades, because even with fades the eye wanders and the half-built slide looks unbalanced.
When to use: timelines, lists of 5–10 rules, concept maps, offer tables, handouts.

**Value before price (offer sequence)**: introduction → one slide per category of what is included → price last. The more value is built first, the more a high price is justified.
When to use: sponsorship packages, pricing/plan slides, commercial proposals.

**Identity extraction**: when no brand guidelines exist, pull a graphic system from what the subject already has — logo shapes, website/blog layout, packaging colors and type, book cover typography/texture, a flag's colors, a product's interface.
When to use: school/territory topics, product decks, clients without a brand book.

**Talk deck vs send deck**: a deck to be projected stays sparse; a deck to be emailed can hold more text but must be self-explanatory, with small titles introducing paragraphs and a clear grid.
When to use: deciding how far to cut text in any redesign.

## Rules & Numbers
Diagnosis
- Start by naming the single message; if you must read everything to get it, the slide fails.
- Check what is the largest element. If it is the deck title, page number or a generic slide title while the content is the dates/numbers/results, invert the hierarchy.
- Look for repetition: the same word in eyebrow and title, title and subtitle saying the same thing, years in subtitle and legend, a generic paragraph repeated on every slide while only images change.
- Check whether items are identifiable: points without a distinctive element "get lost."
- Check for two clashing styles on one slide (e.g. colored gradient background vs full-color photo with shadow).
- Check origin of resources: diagrams from search engines, tables pasted from Excel, schemes from different papers (gradients vs flat, serif vs sans) are inconsistent by definition.

Text fixes
- Merge title and subtitle into one sentence; key words in the accent color; the concept word appears once.
- Remove colons after labels; label small on top, information large beneath.
- Uniform list item length (same words/lines); max 3 lines per item; six items → two columns of three separated by a 1–1.5 pt grey line.
- Shorten long titles; title of a content slide not bigger than the content it introduces (message titles 64–88 pt, titles over lists/data/charts 40–48 pt; see ch04 type scale).
- No ALL CAPS rows; emphasis with bold, not uppercase; no orphan short words at line ends.
- Plural/singular must match the content (four problems → "problems").

Layout fixes
- Default split: label + title + one visual on the left; points on the right with icons in consistent boxes.
- Problem slide: short large title (center or left), the 3–4 problems around or to the right, each with a marker (an X, or an icon in a colored circle); photo desaturated behind a brand-colored gradient veil.
- Results table → three columns: context (sector, first highlighted) → action (small blue label, large info) → result in an accent box.
- Comparison slide: criteria listed once on the left separated by horizontal lines; the two groups' values side by side on the right; one accent (colored circles) only on the differences.
- Index: few entries (≈4 macro-sections, not every slide title) plus section dividers; reuse an existing graphic motif functionally (a selected circle beside the active entry); background scheme by slide type.
- Timeline: one event per slide; thin line at the bottom as reference; stages as outline circles, current stage filled in the accent color with its date above, past stages filled dark; big date + small event at center; simple fade between slides — no need to move objects.
- Portfolio: neutral container so projects speak; recurring layouts (cover, about, per project: title, intro, materials); clickable index linking to each project; consistent mockups (same shadows/style) for every section.
- Long list (e.g. 10 rules): one rule per slide with a slider-like indicator.
- Concept map: one box per slide, arrows become spoken transitions; circles instead of arrows if a connector is needed.

Table fixes
- No cell fills; only horizontal lines, no verticals, no outer border; thin light lines, thicker line under the header; header larger, bold, accent; body smaller, neutral.
- Delete columns that carry no information (a column that says "high" in every row).
- Yes/no: brand color + check for "yes", grey + X for "no" — not traffic-light green/red unless semantic (best/worst).
- Logos inside identical white rounded boxes so proportions match.
- Rounded-table effect: a dark rounded rectangle (small radius) behind the table.
- Differentiators revealed on a second, duplicated slide (morph + 0.5 s fade), when the speaker focuses on them.

Color and style fixes
- Black text on blue/strong color, grey on blue → white text. Black on a saturated orange base → black base, orange as accent.
- Too many colors (orange, red, green) → one dark homogeneous background or white, one accent.
- Color-photo clashing with colored background → black-and-white photo with brand gradient overlay (85–90% opacity, 4 colors sampled from the original and lightened).
- Starry/complex photo backgrounds → lower exposure and saturation plus a semi-transparent black rectangle.
- Green highlighted text unreadable → keep the color as an underline, and reuse that color to link text to the matching part of a diagram.
- Rounded-terminal fonts look unprofessional in serious contexts → geometric sans with clean terminals; one font across the deck.
- Dark-blue text instead of black next to a light-blue primary; grey instead of black for a softer portfolio.
- Background by slide type: cover and dividers in the main color (dividers desaturated/darker tint with the main color as accent on the number), content on white/beige.

Navigation fixes
- Page number out of top-left → bottom right; top-left gets the chapter reference; bottom-left the subject/deck title (≈20 pt Medium, low contrast: footer/label size; see ch04 type scale).
- Footer essential: small logo symbol, site, page number; no photo strips or filled bands.
- In short decks (≈13 slides) the page number can be dropped; in long ones keep chapter + page so people can ask to go back.

Effort
- Rebuilding external diagrams is slow: do it fully only for important presentations; for serious standard contexts (university) a standard style is fine — distinction comes from details.

## Procedures
**The redesign loop (single slide)**
1. Collect the slide plus its neighbors (style context) and the spoken script if available.
2. Write the message in one sentence; mark what the audience must see first.
3. Run Keep–Kill–Change; note the brand assets to preserve (hex colors, background, glows).
4. Cut text: merge duplicates, shorten title, equalize item lengths (an AI chatbot can balance lengths; correct by hand).
5. Decide the split: does this need 1 slide or a sequence (overview + one per point)?
6. Sketch 2–3 versions by hand; pick the one that makes the message visible at a glance.
7. Rebuild on the deck's margins/guides: hierarchy (label → title → body), anchors (icons from one set, one style), grouping by proximity.
8. Rebuild alien objects (tables, charts, diagrams) natively in deck fonts and colors.
9. Add focus (dim the rest, accent box, progressive reveal) only where the speaker talks.
10. Compare before/after side by side; check in grid view against the rest of the deck.

**Whole-deck audit**
1. Map slide types; ensure cover, break slides, content, special/focus slides, back cover with call to action (not "Thank you for your attention"; "Thank you" alone is acceptable).
2. Assign a background rule per type.
3. Five-area audit; produce a fix list ordered by impact: structure → text → color → style → resources.
4. Fix one representative slide per type, then propagate via master/layouts.

**Extract an identity when none exists**
1. Gather: logo, website/blog, packaging, cover, flag, product UI.
2. Pick 3 constants (type contrast, palette, one graphic motif) and apply them systematically.
3. Tone down saturated source colors (flags) and add one color tied to a distinctive element as main; white as support.
4. Draw motifs natively (pen tool, merged shapes), don't paste downloads.

## Common Mistakes
- Redesigning without understanding the content → read and summarize first; "know it, then enhance it."
- Overturning a brand in the redesign → keep background, palette, existing good ideas.
- Leaving a slide "stuck on" in a different style (Canva/Excel/web table) → rebuild it in the deck style.
- Fixing a crowded slide by animating it → split it into several slides.
- Icons from different sets (line + filled, mono + color) → one set, one stroke, outline only; custom-drawn icons for a company's services.
- Neutral text bullets for key services/numbers → infographics; number + title + subtitle hierarchy for every figure.
- Big decorative rectangles with no function → remove.
- Ambiguous yellow highlight on a price → explicit discount ("-50%, X € if confirmed by date").
- Too many dates in one slide, huge stage circles → one date per slide, thin line in the deck's style.
- Cut-out portrait or mascot repeated everywhere → use once; it steals attention in a timeline.
- Footer info repeated on every slide (speaker name, audience) → present once.

## Mental Models
- Structure before style: chaotic content collapses any design, like a house without foundations.
- The original author usually had a good idea; the redesign's job is to accentuate it, not replace it.
- Same content, many valid representations: the question is not "what to write" but "what to communicate."
- Pricing pages and SaaS plan pages are always designed better than offer slides — borrow their structure.
- Decorative elements can become functional: a flag-colored strip becomes a chapter indicator that rises for the current section.
- A redesign is judged in the sequence, not slide by slide.

## Examples
- Index slide of a design project: from one entry per slide in monospace black-on-green to 4 grouped entries with section dividers, a selected circle beside the active entry, large page numbers; green cover/dividers, beige content.
- Startup pitch problem slide: duplicated "problem" word removed; title on two lines at ~80 pt dark blue with the key word underlined by a 7 pt white line; four problems in fully rounded white boxes with icons in gradient-colored circles; photo with lowered exposure/saturation under a 4-color diagonal gradient at 85–90%.
- Pitch-deck competitor table built in another tool: rebuilt as 8×7 table, 24 pt text, 40–48 pt slide title, header in accent, only horizontal lines, unused "importance" column deleted, brand color vs grey for yes/no, logos in identical white boxes, differentiators revealed by morph.
- Sponsorship offer of an event deck sent by email: from paragraph + Excel table (all caps, off-brand green, heavy alternating rows) to intro slide (small grey "Offer" label, bold sentence, packages in grey boxes) → one slide per category with red checks and thin lines → price slide with explicit early-confirmation discount.
- Campaign results hidden in a large table: title shortened, three columns (sectors / strategy / result in light-blue box).
- Historical timeline with every date at once and a dominant portrait: one event per slide, title reduced and centered, Inter + Playfair Display, single green accent, flag colors as thin top/bottom strips acting as chapter markers.
- School geography presentation: palette from the regional flag (desaturated) plus sea blue; hand-built tiles, anchor and region silhouette; serif display title split around the silhouette; motifs linking slides with Magic Move.
- University paper deck: margins equalized, one geometric font, dark-blue text, petrol-tinted break slides with accent numbers, external schemes redrawn on a very light grey panel, 3-point list turned into 4 slides.
- Designer portfolio: cover with name, role sought, small photo, contacts and a "Portfolio" tag; clickable index; per-project intro merging client, goals and strategy; product words highlighted one at a time with the mockup moving.
- Space-themed school slide: oversized text reduced; condensed huge title; points right with simple violet/white icons; two large B&W planets on opposite sides over a darkened star photo.
- Geometry slide: everything left-aligned, one recognizable style per text role, green moved from text to underline and reused on radius/circumference in the diagram.
- Book presentation: style taken from the book cover (grain texture, cover typography), colons removed, three slightly rotated boxes with worn edges, inactive boxes at lower opacity.
- Corporate services deck: seven custom icons coherent with each other, bullet lists turned into infographics, number/title/subtitle hierarchy everywhere.
- Software-house showcase: a visible grid from the logo with colored rectangles moving in order; calm logo wall; feature text left, screenshot right with zooms and blurs guiding focus.
- Community deck without brand book: overlapping transparent logo shapes reused as icons and backgrounds; editorial line-separated layout taken from the client's blog.
- Same dark, small-text slide redesigned three ways from three hand sketches: all valid, because each started from the message.
