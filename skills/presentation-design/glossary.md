# Glossary

**80/20 reuse** — about 20% of the designed elements make up 80% of the deck; copy-paste them instead of redesigning (Ch01)
**Accent colour** — the single base hue used for small elements: key words, icons, dividers, page number (Ch05)
**After previous** — animation start option that waits for the previous effect to finish; used rarely (Ch08)
**Agenda / index** — slide listing only first-level macro-chapters (about 4), placed between cover and content (Ch02)
**Align & Distribute** — the final alignment pass that fixes objects still a few pixels off (Ch03)
**Analogous harmony** — three neighbouring hues on the colour wheel: one main, the other two only as accents (Ch05)
**Anchor icon** — a simple symbol that guides the eye through the points of a list (Ch06)
**Animation** — an effect acting on one object: entrance, emphasis, exit or motion path (Ch08)
**Area-fill** — a coloured rectangle covering the same share of the slide as a percentage, over a grey full-slide rectangle (Ch07)
**Audience window** — the presentation-only window shared in online sessions, keeping presenter view private (Ch01)
**Autofit** — text-box sizing option; use "resize shape to fit text", never "shrink text on overflow" (Ch04)
**Back cover** — the last slide: call to action, contacts or main concept to remember; same background as the cover (Ch02)
**Background rhythm** — a fixed background per slide type that creates a recognizable pattern (Ch02)
**Body text** — running text on a slide, 24–32 pt, minimum 32 pt when read across a large room (Ch04)
**Break slide** — a section divider with number and title on a different background; max two numbering levels (Ch02)
**Bridge sentence** — a sentence linking two sections of a speech so it flows (Ch01)
**Bridge slide** — an identical intermediate slide that regroups objects for a clean morph, auto-advancing after 0 s (Ch08)
**Bubble chart (percentage)** — a pie alternative showing one percentage as a filled bubble against a dashed 100% reference (Ch07)
**Call to action (CTA)** — the action requested from the audience, placed on the back cover (Ch02)
**Cell margins** — internal padding of table cells: left/right 0, top/bottom ≈0.5–1 cm (Ch07)
**Chart template** — a saved chart style with brand colours, one per chart type, stored on the computer (Ch07)
**Complementary harmony** — opposite hues; daring, never small and adjacent or they vibrate (Ch05)
**Constraint-first briefing** — asking the commissioner for format, slide cap, template and software before designing (Ch09)
**Content slide** — the majority slide type: light background, fixed frame, varied layouts inside (Ch02)
**Content-slide frame** — fixed peripheral references (deck title, author, year, page number, small logo) (Ch02)
**Contrast – Emphasis – Breath** — final three-word check: text detached from background, clear reading order, white space (Ch03)
**Counter change** — morph technique fading a new number in at the same position as the old one (Ch08)
**Cover** — the opening slide with distinct graphics; designed last (Ch02)
**Descriptive image** — an image showing the real thing: product, views, gallery (Ch06)
**Dim the rest** — focus by lowering the opacity or saturation of everything not being discussed (Ch03)
**Display text** — oversized cover or statement type, up to 200–300 pt (Ch04)
**Duarte matrix** — presentation-context matrix of audience size vs stage/spontaneity, used to decide whether slides are needed (Ch01)
**Duotone** — an image recoloured in two brand colours; less realistic but coherent (Ch06)
**Effort-to-stakes ratio** — spectacular work only for pitches, launches, events and talks; care and standard layouts elsewhere (Ch09)
**Emotional slide** — a slide that acts as a stage set and amplifies the voice (Ch01)
**Evocative image** — a large image (half slide or more) that reinforces a concept and moves the audience (Ch06)
**Explanatory image** — a screenshot or diagram with zooms, circles or callouts on the relevant part (Ch06)
**Expressive animation** — animation that expresses the brand or topic; the hardest kind (Ch08)
**Eyebrow** — a small label above a title, 20–28 pt, often with increased tracking (Ch04)
**Fade** — the default animation and transition that "never goes wrong" (Ch08)
**Fade-and-move** — a fade combined with a very short movement, 0.3–0.5 s (Ch08)
**Five-area audit** — deck review across Organization, Text, Color, Style and Resources (Ch10)
**Focus slide** — a slide with one phrase or number to remember on a contrasting background, held longer (Ch02)
**Footer** — peripheral band with small logo symbol, deck title and page number, 18–20 pt (Ch02)
**Functional animation** — animation that focuses attention on what is being discussed (Ch08)
**Glass panel (glassmorphism)** — a blurred, semi-transparent panel over an image for legible text (Ch06)
**Gradient overlay** — a full-slide rectangle from opaque black at the text to fully transparent elsewhere (Ch06)
**Guides** — margin and centre lines set in the master before anything is drawn (Ch03)
**Handout** — the denser emailed version of a deck, built to be read alone (Ch01)
**Hero number** — a key figure set at 120–200 pt with a small caption (Ch04)
**Hierarchy** — reading order built from size, weight, colour and position; max three levels (Ch03)
**Highlight by subtraction** — making one datum stand out by dimming the others instead of adding arrows or boxes (Ch07)
**Horizontal scroll** — Push transition from the right on all slides to mimic a scrolling page (Ch08)
**Identity extraction** — deriving a graphic system from a subject's logo, site, packaging, cover, flag or UI (Ch10)
**Indent levels as styles** — body-placeholder levels formatted as named paragraph styles in the master (Ch11)
**Inspire + educate** — a talk arc combining a personal journey with a practical part (Ch09)
**Keep – Kill – Change** — redesign triage of what survives, what goes and what is transformed (Ch10)
**Key message** — the single thing a slide must communicate (Ch01)
**Key number** — a figure isolated as a large number, at least 56 pt (Ch04)
**Label** — the small generic category word above a message title (Ch02)
**Layout** — a reusable slide arrangement governed by the master (Ch02)
**Light table / slide sorter** — zoomed-out view of all slides to check rhythm and consistency (Ch02)
**Line length (measure)** — characters per line: 40–50 on projected slides, 50–80 in documents and emailed decks (Ch04)
**Line spacing** — vertical distance between lines: big titles 0.8–0.95, body 1.1–1.2 multiple, never 1.5 (Ch04)
**Loop** — a slow, continuous, barely perceptible animation (5–10 s) for backgrounds and waiting slides (Ch08)
**Magic Move** — Keynote's equivalent of Morph (Ch08)
**Mask** — a shape subtracted from a full-slide rectangle so an image or video shows through (Ch08)
**Master → Layout → Slide** — the template hierarchy: the master commands layouts, layouts command slides (Ch03)
**Menu headline** — large index or menu title, inside the 80–160 pt headline range (Ch02)
**Mesh gradient** — a multi-point gradient image used for tech/AI covers and glows (Ch05)
**Message title** — a sentence stating the slide's takeaway, 64–88 pt on content slides (Ch04)
**Monochrome palette** — one hue with light and dark tints assigned to roles (Ch05)
**Moodboard** — references collected from non-slide sources to extract fonts, colours and shapes (Ch01)
**Morph** — PowerPoint transition animating objects present on two consecutive slides, ~1 s (Ch08)
**Near-black** — very dark grey or deep colour used for text instead of pure black (Ch05)
**Near-white / off-white** — very light grey or tinted white used for backgrounds instead of #FFFFFF (Ch05)
**Neutral triad** — white plus black or grey plus one colour; the easiest harmony (Ch05)
**On click** — animation start option for anything synced with speech (Ch08)
**One family, many weights** — a typographic system built from the weights of a single family (Ch04)
**One step per slide** — splitting sequences so each slide shows only what is being said (Ch07)
**Orphan / lonely word** — a short word alone at the end of a line or title (Ch04)
**Pattern break** — a deliberate change in the repeated scheme that captures attention (Ch03)
**Peripheral navigation** — footer, page number and chapter references that support the audience (Ch02)
**Persuasive arc** — problem → alternative → proof → call to action, with a circular close (Ch09)
**Placeholder** — a layout slot for text or images that reflows content when layouts change (Ch11)
**Presenter view** — speaker-only view with notes and next slide (Ch01)
**Progress indicator** — a small line, pills or numbers showing position in a sequence (Ch02)
**Progressive focus** — duplicated slides stepping through items with the active one at full opacity (Ch03)
**Proximity** — closeness read as grouping: space inside a group smaller than between groups (Ch03)
**Ready-made template** — a pre-designed deck balanced for one amount of text, images and palette (Ch11)
**Recolour by roles** — transposing a template's background, text and accent roles into a new palette (Ch05)
**Reduce-then-amplify** — cut a slide to one sentence, set it large, say the rest (Ch04)
**Remove → Hierarchy → Highlight** — the three-step clean-up for any data slide (Ch07)
**Reveal** — background-coloured rectangles that move away so content appears to rise (Ch08)
**Script vs outline** — word-for-word speech for short talks; outline for long sessions (Ch01)
**Shadow rules** — light opacity, blur proportional to distance and size, adapted to object and background colour (Ch06)
**Shadowlord** — a tint/shade generator used for monochrome palettes (Ch05)
**Slide master** — the top-level template element holding guides, fonts, colours and footer (Ch02)
**Slide moment** — a point in the script marked as needing a slide (Ch01)
**Slide vs document** — a slide supports the voice ("empty"); a document says everything ("full") (Ch01)
**Slider** — one item per slide with a progress indicator, or a morph carousel (Ch02)
**Smooth start / smooth end** — easing that keeps movement from looking jerky (Ch08)
**Source line** — 18–20 pt grey text on the bottom margin citing the data (Ch07)
**Space before / after** — paragraph spacing setting used instead of empty returns (Ch04)
**Split instead of squeeze** — spreading crowded content over several slides rather than animating one (Ch10)
**Staging animation** — entrances and exits that compose or decompose the scene (Ch08)
**Statement slide** — a sparse slide with one short phrase or key word at 80–160 pt (Ch04)
**Storyboard** — hand-drawn sketch of each slide, with no colours or fonts (Ch01)
**Style · Timing · Sequence** — the three properties of every animation (Ch08)
**Talk deck vs send deck** — sparse projected deck vs self-explanatory emailed deck (Ch10)
**Technical slide** — a chart, table or data slide that builds authority (Ch01)
**Text mask** — a word cut out of a rectangle with an image or video underneath (Ch06)
**Theme colours** — palette stored in the theme so it propagates to shapes, charts and recolouring (Ch05)
**Theme fonts** — title and body fonts set once in the master (Ch11)
**Three foundation questions** — who is it for, what is the goal, what file is needed on the day (Ch01)
**Tint system** — base 0% accent, ~90% darker text, ~90% lighter background, plus 20–80% steps (Ch11)
**Transition** — an effect acting on the whole slide change, 0.5–1 s (Ch08)
**Type scale** — fixed set of text sizes chosen by role, preferably multiples of 8 (Ch04)
**Value before price** — offer sequence telling what is included category by category before the price (Ch07)
**Vignette (radial)** — a radial gradient darkening the edges to focus on a detail (Ch06)
**Visual metaphor** — an image or composition expressing an abstract concept (Ch11)
**Visual pattern** — a repeated scheme that lets the audience predict and concentrate on content (Ch03)
**Watermark** — a mark on stock images; never acceptable on slides (Ch06)
**White space** — empty area that creates focus, order and grouping; an element, not a gap (Ch03)
**With previous** — animation start option that runs together with the previous effect, used for staggered builds (Ch08)
