# Patterns

Concrete, repeatable techniques. Each entry: When to use / How / Trade-offs.

## Script First, Slide Moments Second
**When**: any talk, especially 1–20 minutes.
**How**: write the speech word for word in a text tool, validate it with an outside reader or an AI assistant, then mark where a concept needs emphasis or showing. Only those marks become slides.
**Trade-offs**: slower start; long trainings (1–4 h) get an outline instead and are largely improvised.

## Storyboard on Paper
**When**: before opening the software, for every slide.
**How**: sketch each slide by hand with no colours, fonts or pixel distances; answer which elements, what key message, where the eye lands. Sketch 2–3 versions of hard slides and pick the one that shows the message at a glance.
**Trade-offs**: feels like extra work, but saves hours of moving objects; slides added later should still be sketched first.

## Inspiration from Websites, Not Slides
**When**: building the moodboard, unless a mandatory brand fixes the style.
**How**: collect references from award-winning websites, Dribbble, Behance, Pinterest, apps, dashboards, posters and packaging onto one blank slide; ask how colour, hierarchy, positioning, consistency and motion work; transform and restyle, never copy.
**Trade-offs**: needs translation from a vertical, interactive medium to a horizontal static one.

## Message Title + Small Label
**When**: every content slide, especially decks that will be read without the speaker.
**How**: the generic topic ("Problem", "Revenue 2025") becomes a 20–28 pt label top-left; directly below, a 64–88 pt sentence states the takeaway. Charts, tables and images only support that sentence.
**Trade-offs**: the title is no longer the shortest text; requires rewriting content, not just restyling. Reading only the titles must tell the story.

## Reduce, Then Amplify
**When**: a slide that started as a paragraph or page of notes.
**How**: summarize the slide to one sentence or 1–3 words, set it large, reinforce with one icon or image, and say everything else aloud or keep it in speaker notes.
**Trade-offs**: the speaker must know the content; not suitable for emailed decks, which must be self-explanatory.

## One Point at a Time (Slider)
**When**: lists of 5–10 rules, any bullet list, long sequences.
**How**: one item per slide with a small progress indicator (1 2 3 4, a line or pills); or an overview slide first, then one slide per point.
**Trade-offs**: raises slide count (irrelevant to duration); loses the at-a-glance overview unless an overview slide opens the sequence.

## Dim-the-Rest Focus
**When**: lists, card grids, charts, tables, maps, screenshots where the speaker talks about one part.
**How**: active item 100% opacity, inactive ~30%; inactive chart bars ~80% transparency; or a semi-transparent rectangle in the background colour over the group to mute. Never add arrows or callout boxes.
**Trade-offs**: don't over-focus: the rest must stay readable as context.

## Card Light-Up (Hover Metaphor)
**When**: dashboard-style or card-grid slides.
**How**: all cards "off" (grey or low opacity); the card being discussed lights up via colour, shadow, position or size, like a website hover.
**Trade-offs**: needs one duplicated slide or animation step per card.

## Big Number + Small Label
**When**: KPIs, results, percentages, any key figure.
**How**: never inside a bullet list. Number 120–200 pt in accent with a 24–32 pt description (key numbers ≥56 pt in dense data slides), optionally an icon; percent sign smaller than the digits.
**Trade-offs**: one number dominates; supporting detail moves to the voice or the handout.

## Three-Number Slide
**When**: 2–3 headline figures that belong together.
**How**: 24 pt label → 80 pt framing sentence → three horizontal blocks (120 pt number, 32 pt description) separated by thin grey lines.
**Trade-offs**: requires figures of similar weight; more than three breaks the rhythm.

## Area-Fill Percentage
**When**: one percentage that must hit (thesis, pitch).
**How**: a coloured rectangle covering the same share of the slide as the percentage, over a grey full-slide rectangle, number on top in a contrasting colour; text appears after.
**Trade-offs**: works for one value per slide; not a comparison tool.

## Minimal Table
**When**: data that must stay a table.
**How**: no fills; only thin light horizontal lines, heavier under the header; no verticals, no outer border; header bigger, bold, accent; cell margins L/R 0, T/B ≈0.5–1 cm; numbers right-aligned; highlight the key value last.
**Trade-offs**: highlight shapes must be moved by hand if the table moves; first ask whether one number would do.

## Chart Clean-Up (Remove → Hierarchy → Highlight)
**When**: every native chart or pasted data visual.
**How**: delete chart title (if slide title exists), single-series legend, Y axis (if data labels), 3D and shadows; apply theme colours; dim non-focus series; label only the cited datum; write the takeaway in the slide title; animate piece by piece.
**Trade-offs**: hides data that may be asked for in Q&A; keep the full data in the document or notes.

## Integrated Legend and Average Line
**When**: line or bar charts with a reference value.
**How**: put series names next to their lines instead of a legend; draw the average as a separate series in a contrasting colour labelled "Average" next to it.
**Trade-offs**: manual work when data changes.

## Bubble or Donut Built over a Hidden Chart
**When**: a pie alternative or a custom-shaped percentage.
**How**: build arcs or bubbles over a real chart kept hidden (not deleted) as an exact reference, so data can be updated.
**Trade-offs**: more setup time; slide carries an invisible object.

## One-Event-per-Slide Timeline
**When**: company histories, roadmaps, biographies, process steps.
**How**: one date per slide, date very large (e.g. 200 pt) with a small description; thin line at the bottom; milestones as outline circles, active filled in accent, past filled dark; plain fade between slides.
**Trade-offs**: many slides for long histories; max ~3 dates if they must share a slide.

## Offer Slide: Value, Then Price
**When**: pricing, sponsorship packages, commercial proposals.
**How**: intro slide (small grey label, bold sentence, packages previewed) → one slide per category with accent ticks per plan → price slide last with an explicit discount ("−50%, €X if confirmed by date"). Presented: struck-through price, then the new one on the next click.
**Trade-offs**: longer sequence; when emailed, keep the price on one legible slide.

## Competitor Slide: Brand vs Grey
**When**: pitch competitor or feature-comparison tables.
**How**: minimal table in the deck style; logos in identical white rounded boxes, yours most visible; brand colour + check for "has", grey + X for "doesn't"; delete uninformative columns; reveal differentiators on a duplicated slide (3 pt outline box, Morph + 0.5 s fade).
**Trade-offs**: avoids traffic-light colours, which investors may expect; semantic green/red only for best/worst values.

## Two-Group Comparison
**When**: before/after or group-versus-group data.
**How**: criteria listed once on the left with horizontal separators; the two groups' values in adjacent columns; one accent (coloured circles) only on the differences.
**Trade-offs**: limited to two groups.

## Back Cover CTA
**When**: the last slide of any business deck.
**How**: answer "what should the audience do when I finish?": next step, email, where to keep learning, decision requested or main concept; same background as the cover.
**Trade-offs**: in school contexts a plain "Thank you", a reflective question or a quote is acceptable; thesis decks close on future developments.

## Background Rhythm by Slide Type
**When**: every deck.
**How**: cover in the main colour, content on off-white, breaks in the secondary colour or darker tint, focus slides in a contrasting colour, closing = cover. Check in sorter view.
**Trade-offs**: rigid in long trainings; can feel monotonous if never broken on purpose.

## Template Recolour by Roles
**When**: adapting a ready-made template to a brand or topic.
**How**: map its three roles: white background → very light tint (e.g. beige), black text → very dark brand colour (brown, dark green), accent → a brand hue of similar lightness; optionally invert light/dark; swap fonts globally.
**Trade-offs**: the template still dictates text amount; don't lengthen paragraphs, shrink titles or move elements.

## Monochrome Palette
**When**: the fastest coherent palette, especially from a single brand colour.
**How**: tint/shade generator on the base HEX; base = accent, ~90% darker = text and dark slides, ~90% lighter = background, 20–80% steps for charts; invert for dark slides.
**Trade-offs**: calm but can lack energy; add one contrasting accent only if needed.

## Identity Extraction
**When**: no brand guidelines exist (school topics, small clients, products).
**How**: gather logo, website, packaging, book cover, flag or product UI; pick 3 constants (type contrast, palette, one motif); tone down saturated sources; draw motifs natively.
**Trade-offs**: time-consuming; must stay consistent once chosen.

## Text on Photo Overlay
**When**: titles or phrases over images.
**How**: text in a uniform/blurred zone; or a semi-transparent black rectangle; or a gradient from black (0% transparency) at the text to fully transparent; or brightness −40%; or blur for covers.
**Trade-offs**: overlays mute the photo; colourful images may need B&W or duotone first.

## Brand Gradient Veil
**When**: a photo that clashes with the palette (pitch problem slides).
**How**: desaturate the photo (lower exposure/saturation), cover it with a 4-colour diagonal gradient sampled from the slide and lightened, at 85–90% opacity.
**Trade-offs**: the photo becomes texture rather than information.

## Glass Panel
**When**: modern legible text boxes over complex images.
**How**: background picture blurred (sharpness −100%, brightness −10%); same image as a normal picture on top; rounded rectangle filled with "slide background fill"; white→transparent gradient border; centred shadow ~60–74% transparency.
**Trade-offs**: use sparingly and consistently; skip "liquid glass" distortion, which hurts legibility.

## Readable Cover in Three Steps
**When**: an image cover with a title.
**How**: full-slide image → lower sharpness until blurred → lower brightness → large title.
**Trade-offs**: the image becomes mood only.

## Logo Wall in Equal Cells
**When**: partner, client or sponsor slides.
**How**: every logo in identical rectangles or a hairline grid; all monochrome (all black, all white or greyscale) with brands' approval; align and distribute.
**Trade-offs**: requires approval to alter logos.

## Screenshot Frame
**When**: a chart or interface you cannot rebuild.
**How**: capture with margin, crop to a rounded rectangle, place on a larger rounded rectangle in light grey or the accent colour, optional soft shadow.
**Trade-offs**: rebuilding natively is still better.

## Image Focus by Duplicate
**When**: highlighting a detail of a product or screenshot.
**How**: duplicate the image; bottom copy darker and blurred; top copy cropped with a circle/rectangle mask on the detail, optional outline and small label.
**Trade-offs**: two images to keep aligned.

## Text Mask
**When**: covers or destination/event launches.
**How**: cut a word out of a slide-sized rectangle (merge shapes: combine/subtract) and put a video or image underneath.
**Trade-offs**: needs very large, heavy type to read.

## Morph Zoom
**When**: moving from overview to detail.
**How**: duplicate the slide and enlarge the image on slide 2; or enlarge a full-slide frame with a hole until only the image remains; add a circle on a detail and a connector to a callout. Morph ~1 s.
**Trade-offs**: same objects must exist on both slides; test on the presenting machine.

## Morph Slider / Carousel
**When**: feature lists, product variants, portfolios.
**How**: first item centred and highlighted, second smaller and transparent, third off-slide; duplicate and shift by one position; a 4 pt grey progress line with a black segment advances. Morph ~1 s.
**Trade-offs**: one slide per state; layer order and grouping must match.

## Horizontal Scroll
**When**: continuous strips, timelines, website-like sequences.
**How**: Push transition from the right on all slides; timeline lines exit the slide to connect to the next.
**Trade-offs**: every slide must be built to join its neighbour.

## Morph Reveal Mask
**When**: text that should rise "from nothing".
**How**: rectangles in the background colour cover text or images and move away on the next slide.
**Trade-offs**: layer order mistakes show the trick.

## Counter Change (01 → 02)
**When**: numbered sequences with a big counter.
**How**: on the arriving slide put "2" with the old "1" at 0 opacity; on the departing slide paste "2" at the same position at 0 opacity; morph.
**Trade-offs**: fiddly to maintain if the order changes.

## Bridge Slide for Morph
**When**: morph objects jump or invent paths.
**How**: insert an identical slide where groups are rearranged, auto-advance after 0 s.
**Trade-offs**: hidden extra slides complicate editing.

## Progressive Build with Staggered Fade
**When**: populating a slide fluidly without many clicks.
**How**: group elements; with-previous fades of 0.3 s with 0.1–0.2 s delays (cascades 0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8 s); content named aloud stays on click.
**Trade-offs**: automatic builds can run ahead of the voice.

## Slow Background Loop
**When**: big-statement slides, waiting slides, brand glows.
**How**: path animation with auto-reverse, repeat until end of slide, easing off, 5–10 s; only on selected slides.
**Trade-offs**: heavy on weak machines; must never become the focus.

## Two Outputs: Talk Deck vs Send Deck
**When**: content must be both presented and emailed.
**How**: presented version spreads content over many sparse slides; emailed version is denser, on a fixed grid, with message titles and small headings so it reads alone.
**Trade-offs**: two files to maintain.

## Clickable PDF Index
**When**: decks or portfolios sent as PDF.
**How**: index entries link to sections; a home/menu icon on each slide returns to the index; buttons look clickable and anticipate the destination; a short legend explains the PDF is interactive; test every link.
**Trade-offs**: link maintenance when slides move.

## Visual Metaphor via Chatbot
**When**: a hard concept with no obvious visual.
**How**: ask "How would you visually build a slide expressing the concept of [sentence]?"; collect 3–5 ideas, pick one, sketch it yourself.
**Trade-offs**: ideas are generic; the visual interpretation must stay yours or decks look alike.

## Custom Brand Template (.potx)
**When**: companies whose staff produce decks often.
**How**: master guides, theme fonts, theme colours (tint system), light footer, indent levels as named text styles, layouts for the five slide types plus variants, usage-guidelines and extras slides; test with real content; save as .potx.
**Trade-offs**: slowest part of the work; PowerPoint limits (chart templates saved on the computer, not in the file).
