# Chapter 03: Wallets and Custody

> Educational concepts, not investment, tax or legal advice.

## Core Idea
In this model, coins live on the ledger; what you hold is a **key**, or the 12 to 24 word phrase that regenerates all keys. Custody is therefore a choice about **who controls the key**, and each choice swaps one risk for another. Operational error (lost phrase, wrong network, fake software) causes more losses than cryptographic failure.

## 3.1 Loss taxonomy
- **Thesis**: the main loss channels are few and different from each other.
- **Mechanism**, in rough order of perceived frequency: (1) lost keys or phrase; (2) scams that convince the owner to hand over the phrase or sign something (ch10); (3) malware on the signing device; (4) physical coercion; (5) failure of a third-party custodian (ch05).
- **Trade-off**: a dedicated signing device mitigates mainly the third channel. It does not protect against a lost phrase or a persuaded owner, and for the first it is equivalent to a software wallet.
- **Does not hold when**: the holder is a high-value target for targeted attacks; then dedicated hardware, multi-party schemes and operational secrecy matter more.

## 3.2 Wallet types
| Type | Who holds the key | Main risk | Main benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custodial account | The provider | Provider failure, freeze, hack | No technical burden, recovery by identity |
| Software wallet (phone/computer) | You, on an online device | Malware, device loss | Convenient |
| Hardware signing device | You, on an offline device | Supply-chain tampering, phrase loss | Key never leaves device |
| Paper or metal backup | You, offline | Fire, flood, theft, decay | No electronics |
| Multi-signature | Several keys, k-of-n required | Complexity, coordination | No single point of failure |

## 3.3 The seed phrase
- **Thesis**: one phrase deterministically derives unlimited keys and addresses.
- **Mechanism**: the phrase encodes a random number; a standard function expands it into a tree of keys. For 12 words, the last word is partly a checksum (about 1 in 16 candidates is valid for a given 11-word prefix), so a typo is usually caught. **Derivation paths** determine the address format (native SegWit-style, legacy, nested); the same phrase under a different path produces different, equally valid addresses, which is why a restored wallet can look "empty" until the right path is selected. Some wallets use their own, incompatible phrase standard.
- **Trade-off**: one backup covers everything, but whoever sees the phrase controls everything.
- **Does not hold when**: a wallet uses a non-standard scheme and the software that created it disappears.

## 3.4 Randomness
- **Thesis**: a phrase is only as secret as its randomness.
- **Mechanism**: use physical randomness (dice, or a device with a reliable generator). Never use meaningful sentences, song lyrics or book quotations: they are searchable. Avoid summing dice: sums are not uniform. A phrase generated by a web page or a program you did not verify may be recorded.
- **Does not hold when**: a trusted offline device with a reviewed generator is used; this is the common path.

## 3.5 Passphrase (the extra word)
- **Thesis**: an optional memorised passphrase makes a found phrase useless on its own.
- **Mechanism**: the passphrase is mixed into the key derivation, so each different passphrase opens a different wallet. A wrong passphrase does not error; it opens an empty wallet.
- **Trade-off**: protection against theft of the paper versus a second thing that can be forgotten, and heirs who do not know it cannot recover anything.
- **Does not hold when**: no recovery plan for the passphrase exists. A secret nobody can recover is a loss waiting to happen.

## 3.6 Recovery testing
- **Thesis**: untested backups are not backups.
- **Mechanism**: verify that the phrase plus passphrase recreate the same first address in independent wallet software. The safest practice is: test with the phrase, confirm the address, then generate a **fresh phrase never entered in any software** and use only that one. Keep at least three copies on paper or steel in different locations; never store a phrase on a computer, in a photo or in a password manager.
- **Trade-off**: more copies lower loss risk and raise theft risk.
- **Does not hold when**: heirs, a trusted party or a legal arrangement can retrieve a sealed copy: succession is part of custody (3.9).

## 3.7 Rehearsal on a test network
- **Thesis**: most losses come from operational error, so rehearse with worthless coins.
- **Mechanism**: a test network mirrors the real one with valueless coins from faucets. Create the seed exactly as you will in production, import it, send, delete the wallet, wait days to forget the steps, then redo from the written procedure. Before large real transfers, send a small test amount, check the destination on a block explorer, and verify the last characters of the pasted address (clipboard-swapping malware replaces addresses). Send on the asset's **native network**: sending on the wrong network is a common loss.
- **Trade-off**: more procedure means safer against attackers but more chances of self-inflicted error.
- **Does not hold when**: complexity itself becomes the risk, because no tested written plan exists.

## 3.8 Untrusted machines and software provenance
- **Mechanism**: on an untrusted computer use an amnesic live operating system booted from a USB stick (saves nothing, routes through an anonymity network) or a virtual machine. Download wallets only from the official source and verify the release signature; look-alike domains distribute trojaned wallets. Buy hardware devices directly from the maker; a reseller or second-hand unit may be tampered with and a pre-filled "recovery card" is a scam.
- **Does not hold when**: the signature cannot be checked against a key you obtained by an independent route.

## 3.9 Custodial vs self-custody
- **Thesis (position)**: neither is right in general; each moves risk.
- **For self-custody**: no counterparty can freeze, lose or lend your asset; the 2020-2022 failures of large platforms (ch05) were custodial failures.
- **For custodial**: no technical burden, recovery by identity, simpler tax and succession handling (a regulated intermediary can act as withholding agent in some systems, ch09), and an explicit responsible party.
- **Hybrid**: a **withdrawal whitelist with a delay** (for example 24 hours after adding a new address) gives time to cancel a theft; multi-signature spreads control across devices or people.
- **Does not hold when**: holdings are small enough that setup cost exceeds loss risk, or large enough that a single failure is catastrophic and professional custody with insurance is warranted.

## 3.10 Succession and incapacity
A key that dies with its owner is a permanent loss. A plan should describe what exists, where the recovery material is, how it is accessed and by whom, without writing the secret in a document that could be found. Legal arrangements differ by jurisdiction; this is an area for qualified advice.

## Common mistakes
- Photographing or cloud-saving the phrase.
- Entering the phrase into a "validation" site.
- Skipping the test transfer.
- Using a second-hand hardware device.
- Forgetting that a passphrase is part of the backup.
