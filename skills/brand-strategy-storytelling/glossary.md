# Brand Strategy & Storytelling: Glossary

**A/A test**: running the same variant against itself to measure the noise level of a test before reading any real result (ch09).

**ADAP**: four-move web-copy structure: attention hook, detail/proof, desired state, call to action (ch09).

**AIDA**: attention, interest, desire, action; sequence for carousels and emails (ch09).

**Asynchronous trust**: trust built by publishing, so people warm up before meeting you; opposite of synchronous (meetings, meals) (ch07).

**Attribute pair**: the two brand attributes (often in tension, such as refined plus rustic) that serve as the filter for every reference and sketch (ch02).

**Authenticity gap**: distance between private self and public persona; wider gap, less trust (ch06).

**Beats and gaps**: story units; each beat opens or closes a question in the audience's mind (ch05).

**Binary critique**: story feedback in yes/no questions (personal, feeling, struggle, one takeaway) instead of opinions (ch05).

**Brand**: the shared gut feeling and reputation in customers' heads; a result, not a deliverable (ch01).

**Brand core**: one word to be known for + the enemy + the rallying cry; the brand sits where they overlap (ch03).

**Brand ladder**: satisfaction, delight, engagement, empowerment; measured yearly by the share at the top (ch01).

**Brand platform**: the plan before naming or logo: why we exist, what we stand for, how we differ, how we position, who the customers are, verbal toolbox (ch01).

**Brand premium / equity**: what customers will pay above a cheaper alternative because of the brand (ch05, ch06).

**Branding**: how the brand promise is articulated and communicated across channels; includes strategy, positioning, messaging, experience (ch01).

**Brand point of view (POV)**: a consistent, human stance a brand speaks from; the opposite of a committee voice (ch04).

**Capabilities document**: a short document with a few case studies and services, sent for early enquiries before a proposal (ch08).

**Case-study anatomy**: title with outcome hook, visuals, three-part summary, body with the client as hero (ch08).

**Cold / hot lead**: a request for a price on a deliverable (cold) vs a prospect describing a problem you solve (hot) (ch02).

**Commodity (matrix cell)**: good but not different; competes on price (ch03).

**Dilution**: gradual loss of a distinctive voice into safe, correct copy as an organisation grows (ch04).

**Director's zoom**: open with a wide establishing shot, then tighter ones (ch05).

**Emotion audit**: labelling each paragraph with its emotion; none means rewrite (ch05).

**Enemy**: the practice, belief or way of working a brand stands against; rallies a tribe (ch03, ch06).

**Establishing shot**: opening line giving a specific time, place and situation (ch05).

**Gap framing**: naming the two sides of the gap an offer bridges and writing across it (ch04).

**Gift economy**: giving value with no short-term expectation, trading money for time, attention, status and support (ch07).

**Gimmick (matrix cell)**: different but not good (ch03).

**Good-and-different matrix**: knock-off, commodity, gimmick, target (ch03).

**Hero arc (brand)**: origin, defining moment, transformation, opposition (ch05).

**Identity design**: designing symbols (mark, type, colour, system); one part of branding (ch01).

**Inverted pyramid**: lead (headline plus subhead), body (evidence), tail (extra context); for portfolios and case studies (ch08).

**Inversion**: brainstorming the opposite or "dumbest" version of the category's approach to find a distinctive angle (ch03).

**Known-unknown twist**: changing one component of a familiar phrase to make a fresh hook (ch04).

**Messaging layers**: hook, subhead, body, one call to action, each with a single job (ch04).

**Normal / explosion / new normal**: three-part story arc (ch05).

**Ownership test**: can you be number one in the lane within one to two years? (ch03)

**Persona (living hypothesis)**: a named customer profile refined with market feedback (ch02).

**Point-first editing**: write the point in one sentence, tell the story toward it, cut what does not serve it (ch05).

**Positioning**: the one claim you own: for whom and what result; a place in the customer's mind (ch03).

**Promise formula**: result + time frame + the objection it overcomes (ch09).

**Promise-behaviour gap**: difference between what a brand says and what it does (ch01).

**Prism**: one consistent self showing different spectra on different platforms (ch06).

**Self-selecting name**: a name the right audience recognises itself in and others skip (ch04).

**Smallest viable audience**: the narrowest group you can picture clearly, with their problems (ch03, ch07).

**Stickiness test**: asking the audience for one small action to measure loyalty (ch07).

**Attribute slider**: a pair of opposite poles per brand attribute, with a forced choice, used to plot a brand against competitors (ch03).

**Imagination gap**: the distance between what a buyer can picture and the result of a service; closed with real documented proof (ch08).

**Repeatable story test**: a story is short and vivid enough for a customer to retell it unaided (ch05).

**Story set**: about three prepared, rehearsed stories per product or audience (ch05).

**Symptom vs root cause**: the requested fix vs the underlying problem (ch02).

**T-skill**: deep specialisation in one thing plus broad curiosity (ch03).

**Tone filter**: running dull copy through several tones to find the voice (ch04).

**Touchpoints (trust)**: repeated interactions that accumulate into trust (ch07).

**True fans**: a small number of people who buy repeatedly and advocate; income = fans x profit per fan (ch07).

**Verbal toolbox**: words, tone and how the brand wants to be described (ch01).

**Wedge**: one specialty claimed first, with adjacent disciplines around it (ch03).
