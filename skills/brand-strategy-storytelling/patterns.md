# Brand Strategy & Storytelling: Patterns

Operational techniques. Each: when to use, how, trade-off.

## Discovery and definition

### Discovery Session (four parts)
**When**: any project that arrives as a deliverable request.
**How**: goals and success in numbers → challenges → customers and pain points → attributes. Mirror back, do not persuade.
**Trade-off**: takes a session and facilitation skill; a questionnaire is faster but generic. (ch02)

### Why-Now Probe
**When**: a brief says "we need a new X".
**How**: ask why now; ask for an example for each adjective; ask the three-years-later look-back question; ask "what else" until empty.
**Trade-off**: can feel intrusive; frame it as making the work cheaper to get right. (ch02)

### Attribute Pair Filter
**When**: before moodboards or logos.
**How**: free brainstorm, opposing pairs, group, reduce to two, agree with the client; test every reference against the pair.
**Trade-off**: a pair narrows exploration on purpose. (ch02)

### Persona as Hypothesis
**When**: any design or copy review.
**How**: behavioural types → choose target → named profile → top three needs; ask "for her, or for us?"; refresh with market feedback.
**Trade-off**: wrong personas mislead; review them regularly. (ch02)

### One-Sentence Offer Statement
**When**: aligning a team or filtering features.
**How**: offering + audience + problem + secret sauce; every feature must map back or be dropped.
**Trade-off**: several drafts; vague answers expose missing research. (ch02)

## Positioning

### Claim First, Proof Later
**When**: entering or repositioning.
**How**: choose focus (discipline x market), state it as a claim, build skills and method to prove it, codify the method.
**Trade-off**: the claim may be slightly ahead of reality; close the gap quickly. (ch03)

### Forced-Choice Narrowing
**When**: the audience is vague.
**How**: ask concrete either/or questions until you can pick the person out of a crowd.
**Trade-off**: excludes people; that is the point. (ch03)

### Attribute Slider Plot
**When**: checking that a position differs from the two nearest competitors.
**How**: opposite poles per attribute, forced choice (no centre), plot brand and both competitors; if the shape matches one, redo with an extreme.
**Trade-off**: extremes repel some buyers; that is the point. (ch03)

### Good-and-Different Placement
**When**: checking a positioning.
**How**: place in the matrix; commodity → invert or change about 3 percent; gimmick → raise quality.
**Trade-off**: difference can alienate some buyers. (ch03)

### Brand Core Triangle
**When**: the brand needs a stance.
**How**: one word, one enemy (a practice), one rallying cry that links them.
**Trade-off**: repels some people; do not attack individuals. (ch03)

### Three-Circle Fit
**When**: choosing what a person or studio should specialise in.
**How**: love / good at / pays well; add industry fit and access routes.
**Trade-off**: the centre may be small at first; start with the wedge. (ch03)

## Story and proof

### Repeatable Story Test and Story Set
**When**: any brand, founder or product story.
**How**: could a customer retell it in a sentence? Keep one true specific detail, place it where the buyer sees it, prepare about three stories and rehearse beats, not scripts.
**Trade-off**: shorter stories drop nuance; keep nuance for the long form. (ch05)

### Process-Heavy Case Structure
**When**: design or product projects whose value is in the decisions.
**How**: one-slide summary, problem-and-process sentence repeated per section, labelled process diagram, captioned sketches, proto-personas, annotated deep-dive screen.
**Trade-off**: high-level on purpose; link a detailed version for deep readers. (ch08)

## Voice and messaging

### Tone Filter
**When**: finding or testing a voice.
**How**: take three dull sentences, rewrite each in five or six tones, pick what fits the audience, write do/don't rules.
**Trade-off**: tone follows the audience, not taste. (ch04)

### Message Layers
**When**: any page, post or deck.
**How**: hook → subhead → body → one CTA; each layer gets the reader to the next.
**Trade-off**: skimmable but demands discipline in cutting. (ch04)

### Known-Unknown Twist
**When**: writing a short brand line.
**How**: take a familiar phrase and change one word, letter or order; or borrow a known concept and swap one element.
**Trade-off**: can slip into a pun; run the "tell me more" test. (ch04)

### Three-Phrase Reduction
**When**: positioning copy.
**How**: write the line about 15 times, pick the best, reduce to three short phrases.
**Trade-off**: slow; worth it for text repeated everywhere. (ch04)

### Typing and Speaking Name Test
**When**: choosing a name or domain.
**How**: write the email address four times; say the name to ten people and watch them type.
**Trade-off**: small sample; combine with an availability check. (ch04)

## Story craft

### Normal-Explosion-New Normal
**When**: structuring a brand or launch story.
**How**: establishing shot → explosion → stakes → sensory detail → lesson.
**Trade-off**: lesson last, so the tension has time to build. (ch05)

### Point-First Edit
**When**: a draft is long or aimless.
**How**: write the point in one sentence; cut details that do not serve it; start near the conflict.
**Trade-off**: may cut charming detail. (ch05)

### Emotion Audit
**When**: a draft feels flat.
**How**: label each paragraph with its emotion; rewrite those with none; delay relief.
**Trade-off**: overuse feels manipulative; keep emotions true to the facts. (ch05)

### Binary Story Critique
**When**: reviewing someone's story.
**How**: four yes/no checks (personal, feeling, struggle, one takeaway); say where the struggle is missing.
**Trade-off**: tests structure, not taste. (ch05)

### Beat Question Pass
**When**: pacing a story or video.
**How**: for each beat write what the audience now wonders; add a beat where nothing is wondered.
**Trade-off**: can over-engineer short pieces. (ch05)

## Personal brand and audience

### Origin Excavation
**When**: building a personal story.
**How**: list peak-emotion moments, transformative people, things called "too much"; write origin, defining moment, transformation.
**Trade-off**: private detail needs a filter: only what serves the point. (ch06)

### "Too Much" Flip
**When**: finding differentiators.
**How**: list traits people call too much, no judgment; write the advantage each gives a specific audience.
**Trade-off**: some traits remain private. (ch06)

### Endorsement Not Pitch
**When**: a personal brand fronts a business.
**How**: personal channel builds authority; the business is endorsed by it; set the boundary between the two.
**Trade-off**: slower to convert; stronger trust. (ch06)

### 30-30-30 Experiment Plan
**When**: starting content or changing format.
**How**: 30 experiments without checking results → repeat the 2-3 winners → refine for 30 days each.
**Trade-off**: patience required; do not judge results early. (ch07)

### Stickiness Test
**When**: judging audience loyalty.
**How**: ask for one small concrete action; read the response.
**Trade-off**: requires an existing audience. (ch07)

### Show-Your-Work Filter
**When**: deciding what to post.
**How**: would someone share it, or learn from it? Process over product.
**Trade-off**: less polished; builds trust. (ch07)

## Portfolio and copy

### Headlines-Only Read
**When**: finishing a portfolio or case study.
**How**: read only headlines; fix any break in the story.
**Trade-off**: none; cheap check. (ch08)

### Outcome-Hook Case Study
**When**: writing a case study.
**How**: title with outcome; three-part summary; body with client as hero; process visuals; result.
**Trade-off**: needs a measured result or a stated qualitative one. (ch08)

### Diagnose-First Page Review
**When**: a page underperforms.
**How**: check offer and traffic; first-visitor read; fix largest lever; A/A before email tests.
**Trade-off**: slower than rewriting. (ch09)

### Four-Question Dissection
**When**: learning from a strong sales piece.
**How**: problem, benefit, objections, action; copy the sequence, not the words.
**Trade-off**: patterns, not formulas; adapt to the audience. (ch09)

### Copy Work Loop
**When**: building writing skill.
**How**: transcribe a strong author by hand, note patterns, write your own under them; 30-minute sprints.
**Trade-off**: slow; builds style awareness. (ch09)
