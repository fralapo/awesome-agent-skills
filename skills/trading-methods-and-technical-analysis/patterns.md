# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets. Entries marked (position) are contested. Educational, not advice or signals.

## 1. "Is it going up or down?"
**When**: a direction is asked or asserted.
**Move**: name the timeframe; read the higher timeframe's structure first, then the lower one for timing; state what would invalidate the reading. [ch01]
**Cost**: no one-word answer.
**Not when**: the question is about value rather than price path (ch09).

## 2. "It's overbought, it must fall"
**When**: a high oscillator reading is used as a reversal call.
**Move**: recall what the formula measures (close near the top of its range); check trend, levels and higher-timeframe divergence before reading anything into it. [ch02]
**Cost**: fewer contrarian trades.
**Not when**: the market is a well-defined range, where extremes are the method's premise (position).

## 3. A breakout on the chart
**When**: price crosses a level.
**Move**: wait for the close; compare the candle's range with normal range; note shadows and the next bar; plan the retest and the level at which the break is false. [ch01, ch05]
**Cost**: worse entry price or missed moves.
**Not when**: the method is explicitly a stop-order breakout with a predefined exit.

## 4. "Seasonality says buy" (position)
**When**: a calendar statistic is offered as a reason to trade.
**Move**: count how many windows and instruments were scanned, compare the win rate with the asset's baseline, treat nested horizons as one sample, and require technical timing and a stop. [ch04, ch08]
**Cost**: most seasonal "edges" disappear.
**Not when**: there is a physical or institutional mechanism tested out of sample.

## 5. Choosing a strategy
**When**: a method is picked for an instrument.
**Move**: identify the regime (trend, range, expansion, stable relation), pick the rule family built for it, and write its invalidation before its entry. [ch05]
**Cost**: standing aside often.
**Not when**: the rule is a diversified, long-horizon system meant to run through all regimes.

## 6. Sizing a trade
**When**: an order is about to be placed.
**Move**: stop at the invalidation level, target at the next level, check reward-to-risk, then size = risk money / (stop distance x value per point). [ch06]
**Cost**: smaller positions than intuition suggests.
**Not when**: the stop cannot be honoured (gaps, illiquidity); then reduce size further or skip.

## 7. Several open trades "in different markets"
**When**: positions look diversified.
**Move**: check shared currencies, sectors and drivers; estimate combined risk with correlation; assume correlations rise in stress. [ch06]
**Cost**: fewer simultaneous ideas.
**Not when**: the positions are a designed spread with both legs managed together.

## 8. "My strategy wins 85% of the time"
**When**: a hit rate is offered as proof.
**Move**: ask for average win, average loss, worst trade, maximum drawdown, number of trades and costs; compute expectancy. [ch06, ch08]
**Cost**: an uncomfortable conversation.
**Not when**: losses are capped by an honoured stop and expectancy is positive after costs.

## 9. A spectacular track record
**When**: a course, indicator, signal service or product shows past returns.
**Move**: apply the ch08 checklist: fixed rules, out-of-sample, costs, number of trades, drawdown, benchmark, and the seller's interest. [ch08]
**Cost**: most claims become unverifiable.
**Not when**: results are audited, long and published in advance (rare).

## 10. Panic in an open position
**When**: a trade causes fear or the urge to move the stop.
**Move**: treat it as a sizing signal; follow the written exit; reduce standard size for future trades. [ch07, ch06]
**Cost**: accepting the loss.
**Not when**: new information invalidates the trade; then the exit rule applies, not the emotion.

## 11. A "cheap" stock on a screen
**When**: a fair-value gap or a high yield attracts attention.
**Move**: check core revenue, notes to the accounts, shareholder yield, solvency and manipulation scores (not for banks), growth acceleration; treat model outputs as hypotheses. [ch09]
**Cost**: time spent reading reports.
**Not when**: the screen is used only to build a watchlist.

## 12. A leveraged or structured product
**When**: a turbo, fixed-leverage product, barrier certificate or margin contract is considered.
**Move**: find its death event (barrier, reset, margin call, issuer default), its daily cost, the notional behind it, and its liquidity in stress; read the key information document. [ch10]
**Cost**: fewer products pass.
**Not when**: the exposure is a small, defined-loss amount the holder is willing to lose entirely.
