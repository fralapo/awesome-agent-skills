## Ground rules (read before answering)

1. **Present it as a model, not as settled science.** This is a psychoanalytic worldview ("in this model…"). Every chapter ends with a *Caveats* section separating ideas with mainstream support (repression, projection, attachment, harm of physical punishment, conformity, coercive control) from the model's own single-cause or contested claims (no heritability of mental illness, illness as purely psychosomatic, dismissal of CBT, unsourced abuse statistics, gender generalisations). Use the Caveats whenever the answer matters.
2. **A lens, not a diagnosis.** Use it to understand motives, write, communicate and reflect. Never diagnose a real person, never use it to accuse someone (especially of child abuse) and never present it as therapy. Real symptoms → a qualified professional; risk of self-harm → emergency services or a crisis line, immediately.
3. **No home remedies.** Don't pass on self-help tips for acute panic or illness (flagged in ch05).
4. **Esoteric material is out of scope** (magic, "astral", alchemy as practice, reincarnation). If asked, treat it as belief, not fact.

## Core Model

**The psyche.** A *reasoning mind* (useful, keep it) sits beside a *conditioned mind*: the rules, fears and "description of the world" absorbed from parents and society (the "Mind Matrix"). Beneath both live the *soul* (non-theological: the felt emotional life) and *instinct* (an innate "wisdom of the species"). Suffering comes from the conditioned mind overruling soul and instinct. Awareness = knowing what you actually feel, doubting what you were taught, judging only from experience ("did you live it or read it?").

**Ego is healthy; the false ego isn't.** The plain "I" should be kept. The *false ego* is a defensive identity built to please the parents' mirror: the child prefers "I'm not worth it" to "they don't love me", then props up a *secondary identity* with career, looks, money and approval. Hostility, arrogance and the need to be at the centre are read as defence of that fragile construct → ask **"what is this person defending?"** before answering the attack. The **"can I change my mind?"** test separates a real opinion from a defended identity.

**Repression → projection → distorted reality.** What isn't felt is repressed; repressed material is projected onto partners, bosses, leaders, groups and gods. People then flee unhappiness through *disapproved escapes* (alcohol, gambling, ultras) or *approved* ones (work, study, shopping, career). **Symptoms are containers**: insomnia, phobias, compulsions and addictions are read as signals of unprocessed childhood experience, not vices. The **fear gradient**: fear → anxiety → anguish → panic → madness; a phobia is a *false target* for a fear whose real object is hidden.

**Love replays childhood.** The partner often stands in for a parent: jealousy, fear of abandonment, affective dependency and "choosing the bad boy" re-enact early bonds. **Reciprocity** is the health thermometer of a relationship; love and forgiveness **cannot be commanded** (you can require behaviour, never feeling). Understanding ≠ forgiving ≠ condemning.

**Family as the first institution of power.** The traditional family is read as training for obedience. The alternative: *side with the child* (explain rules, allow dissent, apologise, no hitting, ask real questions, don't presume to know your child). No duty to forgive parents who harmed knowingly; forgiving ≠ excusing a good-faith error.

**Therapy and dreams.** Effective therapy works on causes, not effects. Good-analyst checklist: boundaries, non-directive, confidential, welcomes the patient's aggression, empathy without collusion. Dreams are *personality integrators* that slip past the mind's censor; symbols (water, house, tree, animals, snake, death, teeth, money) mean something only **relative to the dreamer**.

**Power, manipulation, groups.** Authority attracts the projection of the parent: idealisation and demonisation are two sides of one split; mature judgment means **staying on the line** (evaluating, neither idolising nor condemning). Victims can become perpetrators through *identification with the aggressor*. Coercive-control red flags: isolation, induced guilt, escalating demands, "only I understand you". Majorities, parties and fandoms trade belonging for thought (*the majority myth*). Aggression should be **proportionate** to the harm; insistence signals insecurity. At work, a **respect ladder** applies: "what you let pass becomes the new normal".

**Myths and belief, read psychologically.** God as a projected, stronger parent; faith as an inherited *package*; angry defence of a belief as a sign of inner doubt; the *just-world* belief. Myths (Medusa, Orpheus and Eurydice, Penelope, the Gordian knot, the triple goddess, Parsifal's unasked question) are reusable story templates — and myths get rewritten by whoever holds power.
