# Glossary

**Abandonment in love** (abbandono in amore) — this model's formula that fear of being left by a lover is fear of being left by a parent; out-of-scale grief after a modest bond is its signal (Ch3)
**Adult to adult** — the stance the model proposes against social anxiety: meet bosses, clients and audiences as one adult to another, not as a child facing a parent (Ch5)
**Affective dependency** (dipendenza affettiva) — in this model's definition, "depending on someone who doesn't love you, hoping they will"; a continuation of dependency on the parents (Ch3)
**Aggression as fear** — this model's reading that much hostile behaviour defends against fear and can soften quickly once the person is reassured (Ch7)
**Anger vs. hatred** — the model separates anger (a child's helpless pain at a wrong) from hatred and rancour (wanting the other to suffer) (Ch7)
**Anguish** (angoscia) — third step of this model's fear gradient: paroxysmal anxiety with racing heart and sweaty hands (Ch5)
**Anima** (soul) — in this model's secular usage, the felt life of emotions, sensations and inner movement; explicitly not a theological entity (Ch2, Ch6)
**Anthropocentrism** — in this model, the deepest cultural schema, a species-level egocentrism from which other hierarchies (man/woman, adult/child, race/race) descend (Ch2)
**Anxiety** (ansia) — second step of this model's fear gradient: fear without an object that attaches to any event (Ch5)
**Approved vs. disapproved escapes** — this model's pairing of socially rewarded flights (work, study, career, shopping) with frowned-upon ones (alcohol, gambling); in this model both serve to avoid feeling (Ch2, Ch7)
**Army of parents** (esercito di genitori) — this model's image for the invisible judging parents a stage-frightened speaker sees in the audience (Ch5)
**Attitude precedes content** — this model's claim that people perceive the speaker's inner state before the words; polite hostility leaks through (Ch7, Ch8)
**Authenticity is detectable** — this model's point that children, and audiences, sense when a rule or message is repeated without conviction (Ch4)
**Awareness** (consapevolezza) — in this model's sense, knowing what one actually feels and accepting only what one has experienced, as opposed to erudition (Ch2)
**Behaviour over words** — this model's rule that what the partner *does* answers "do you really love me?" (Ch3)
**Being found out** — the insecure person's expectation that partner or employer will eventually discover they are worthless (Ch1)
**Benevolence** — in this model, the "carrier wave" of any teaching or therapy: content without goodwill toward the receiver does not land (Ch4, Ch6)
**Black-and-white thinking** ("chest of drawers") — this model's image of the mind filing people into fixed "good" or "bad" drawers and bristling when a "bad" person does good; overlaps the recognised distortion of dichotomous thinking (Ch7)
**Bully hypothesis** — this model's reading that the bully was raised with too much harshness and hates in weaker peers the fragility his parents despised in him; contested as a single cause (Ch1, Ch7)
**Caduceus / "two in one"** — this model's image (Mercury's two intertwined serpents) for holding inner opposites in balance rather than transcending or cutting one off (Ch3)
**"Can I change my mind?" test** — this model's check: whatever one cannot question (parents, party, God, people) is a received schema, not a conviction (Ch1)
**Child-before-adult** (sentirsi bambini davanti a un adulto) — this model's account of social anxiety: the adult feels like a child facing a parent again, especially before authority or fame (Ch5)
**Child's choice** — this model's central mechanism: a dependent child concludes "I am not worth it" rather than "my parent does not love me", preserving hope at the cost of lifelong self-devaluation (Ch1, Ch5)
**Comparison as self-devaluation** — this model's view that constant comparison already signals low self-worth; one can only measure oneself against oneself (Ch1, Ch5)
**Compassion without fusion** — taking part in another's pain while staying separate, so it does not reawaken one's own past (Ch6)
**Compensatory euphoria** (euforia compensatoria) — this model's term for staying always "up" to avoid depression, then collapsing under a serious event (Ch5)
**Conditioned mind** (mente condizionata) — this model's core term: the layer of rules and beliefs received from family, school, religion and media that acts as an inner judge and runs the false ego; to be dismantled, unlike the reasoning mind (Ch1, Ch2, Ch6, Ch7)
**Conditioning vs. plagiarism** (condizionamento / plagio) — this model's distinction: the conditioned person keeps a sense of right and wrong; the "plagiarised" (coercively persuaded) person has lost it and depends on someone else to tell them (Ch7)
**Conscious mask** (persona) — the mask built in childhood to be accepted; this model's aim is to take it off to know oneself, then wear it again knowingly for social life (Ch1, Ch4)
**Containment** — warmth, contact and a quiet voice for the newborn; the model cites Harlow's cloth-mother experiment (Ch4)
**Conversion** (conversione) — in this model's reading of gambling, swapping an original pain for a less painful event (a match, wins and losses) whose fears are lived out instead; not the clinical conversion disorder (Ch5)
**Cover figures** — figures standing in for the parents in dreams so the dream can criticise them; the model contradicts herself elsewhere by saying dream parents are always literal (Ch6)
**Cruelty as a transgenerational chain** — this model's view that parents who did their duty without loving pass on what they received (Ch7)
**Cycles vs. control** — this model's contrast between Penelope's woven-and-unwoven web (accepting cycles) and Alexander cutting the Gordian knot (forcing a solution) (Ch8)
**Defensive faith** ("house of cards") — this model's claim that rage at people who contradict a belief signals that one does not fully hold it (Ch8)
**Deficient identity** (identità carente) — this model's term for the absent identity left by negative parental messages; hoarding is read as building an artificial boundary of things (Ch5)
**Demonisation** — seeing only the bad in a figure; for the model, venting rage at a failing parent one cannot accuse (Ch7)
**Depersonalisation** (spersonalizzazione) — this model's term for family and school flattening a child's innate personality; **not** the clinical depersonalisation disorder (Ch1)
**Descent into Hades** (discesa nell'Ade) — this model's metaphor for re-examining one's past even when it means questioning the parents (Ch6, Ch8)
**Descent-into-reality disorder** (disturbo di discesa nella realtà) — this model's coinage for people brilliant in the abstract but chaotic with bills and paperwork, because the concrete brings old pain up; not a clinical category (Ch2)
**Description of the world** (descrizione del mondo) — a received world-picture that replaces direct experience; the model borrows the phrase from Castaneda (Ch2)
**Devil as rebellion** — this model's symbolic reading of the devil as rebellion against parent and power, not absolute evil; a psychological reading, not theology (Ch8)
**Displacement** — aggression or fear moved from a dangerous target (the parent) to a safer one (a sibling, a promoted colleague, an object) (Ch2, Ch4, Ch5)
**Disproportion as a signal** — this model's recurring heuristic that the part of a reaction that exceeds its trigger "belongs to the past" (Ch3, Ch5, Ch7)
**Doubt is the engine** — this model's stance that questioning everything, including the speaker ("listen to me, don't believe me"), drives awareness (Ch2)
**Dream censor** — Freud's "guardian of the castle" that edits a dream's message; the model combines it with Jung's view that the unconscious speaks in images (Ch6)
**Dreams as personality integrators** (integratori di personalità) — this model's view that dreams join what others told us we are with what we would be without conditioning, in small steps (Ch6)
**Dynamic engrams** (engrammi dinamici) — this model's term for emotional grooves from early experience applied automatically to the whole outside world (Ch5, Ch6)
**Effect-level therapy** — this model's label for approaches (CBT, EMDR are named) said to swap one conditioning for another; this claim is contested, since CBT has strong evidence (Ch5, Ch6)
**Ego** (io) — in this model's usage, the healthy self: body, self-respect and the capacity to see others; not to be "demolished" (Ch1)
**Emotions as fuel** — this model's image of emotions as the "petrol" of psychic and physical energy; repressing them drains energy (Ch2, Ch4, Ch6)
**Endogenous jealousy** — this model's term for jealousy that arises even when the partner behaves impeccably, traced to an early abandonment (Ch3)
**Escapes from reality** (evasione dalla realtà) — any activity used not to feel one's own emotions; the model says it is fine as a pause, destructive as a way of life (Ch2)
**Evaluating vs. judging** — the model separates evaluating a behaviour ("this person is dangerous") from judging the whole person ("he is evil") (Ch2, Ch4, Ch7)
**Exogenous jealousy** — this model's term for jealousy that responds to the partner's real behaviour (flirting, "a spare tyre") (Ch3)
**Experience vs. events** — in this model, experience is what one understood about events, not the events themselves (Ch2)
**Exploitation as being special** — this model's view that exploiting others feeds the illusion of not being "one of many", and so of not dying (Ch7)
**External reference** — measuring one's worth by parents, institutions, audiences or grades, an internalised parent that always answers "not enough" (Ch1)
**Fall of the conditioned mind** (caduta della mente condizionata) — this model's name for the end point of inner work: conditioning (not intellect) falls, bringing inner silence and an end to comparison; a narrative arc, not a milestone to promise (Ch2, Ch6)
**False ego** (falso ego) — a defensive self built to please parents who could not love the child as it was; for the model a defence, not a moral fault (Ch1)
**False target** (falso bersaglio) — this model's reading of a phobia as fear moved onto a containable object so the real cause can stay hidden (Ch2, Ch5)
**Fear** (paura) — first step of this model's fear gradient: fear with a known object (Ch5)
**Fear gradient** — this model's scale fear → anxiety → anguish → panic attack → madness; descriptive vocabulary, never a diagnostic scale (Ch5)
**Fear of death** (paura della morte) — in this model, after Freud, the deepest fear, carrying all the childhood fears and hidden by modern culture (Ch2, Ch5, Ch8)
**Fear vs. pleasure test** — this model's question whether someone is aggressive because they feel threatened (reassure) or because they enjoy it (set limits) (Ch7)
**Feeling beautiful or ugly** — in this model, an inner verdict on one's acceptability, not an aesthetic fact (Ch1)
**Feet on the ground** — this model's grounding: feeling one's body and handling concrete tasks (Ch2)
**Foreign territory** — this model's image for true love, which keeps a free part that looks at the other as unknown land (Ch3)
**Forgiveness** (perdono) — in this model, an outcome of maturity or a gift that requires the other's request and repentance, never a duty; imposed forgiveness is, in this view, a form of repression; many therapists disagree (Ch3, Ch4, Ch5, Ch7)
**Fragility as sensitivity** — this model's reframing of fragility as a capacity to feel, carrying both the risk of suffering and the capacity for joy (Ch1)
**Free will** (libero arbitrio) — the model denies real free will; one can only "widen the walls of the prison" by admitting one is not free (a philosophical stance) (Ch2)
**Friction, not walls** — workplace-assertiveness principle: raise the cost of disrespect instead of escalating (Ch7)
**Ghosts** (fantasmi) — this model's image for harsh inner parental figures that make solitude feel sad; solitude itself is not (Ch3)
**God as projected parent** — this model's psychological reading of belief in a protective, judging God as the need for a parent stronger than the real ones; no claim about religious truth (Ch8)
**Good analyst criteria** (il bravo analista) — this model's checklist for a helper: empathic without colluding, clear boundaries, plain language, non-directive, confidential, lets the patient decide when it ends (Ch6)
**"Good" as flattening** — this model's claim that conventional goodness is often obedience; the real question is "am I aware?", not "am I good?" (Ch7)
**Good parent / bad parent split** (genitore buono / genitore cattivo) — the child keeps an idealised parent and projects the "bad" one onto others; the note links it to Melanie Klein (Ch3, Ch5)
**Gordian knot** — in this model, the conditioned mind's impatience and wish to solve by force (Ch8)
**Guilt is not fault** (senso di colpa ≠ colpa) — this model's distinction: guilt feelings are inadequacy passed on by parents, society and religion, not evidence of wrongdoing (Ch5)
**Hidden parent** (genitore occulto) — the strong, winning inner figure a rejected child may ally with, giving up its own sensitivity (Ch4)
**Historical child** — in the model's inner-child layers, ourselves as children, especially the part we do not remember (Ch4)
**Hypnosis critique** — this model's view that hypnosis does not reach serious trauma because waking defences stay active and the wish to please the hypnotist shapes answers; "past lives" recalled under hypnosis are called inventions (Ch6)
**Idealisation** — seeing only the good in a figure; for the model, a search for the good parent one never had (Ch1, Ch3, Ch7)
**Identification** (immedesimazione) — picking up the other's emotional identity in the moment without judging or absolving; this model's method is asking "what would I feel if I acted that way?" (Ch3, Ch8)
**Identification with the aggressor** — a mistreated child sides with the violent parent so as not to feel like a victim; this model's "victim-to-perpetrator switch", which is said not to run backwards (Ch4, Ch5, Ch7)
**Imprinting** (impronta) — borrowed from Konrad Lorenz: the parents' early mark on one's sense of worth and expectations of the world; this extension to humans goes beyond what ethology supports (Ch1, Ch4, Ch5, Ch6)
**Improbable hero** — this model's Tolkien reading: change comes from someone who is not the strongest (Frodo) (Ch7)
**Individuation** — after Jung, stepping out of family and social conditioning to find one's own answers; not individualism (Ch8)
**Induced needs** — status needs (car, designer clothes, career, the "right" family) that, in the Mind Matrix reading, rest on the belief that otherwise "we are nobody" (Ch2)
**Inherited belief package** — beliefs absorbed as a bundle from family, place and media rather than tested (Ch8)
**Inner child** (bambino interiore) — the repressed, sensitive child part that, for the model, must be welcomed rather than made to grow up; a therapeutic metaphor, not a clinical construct (Ch1, Ch3, Ch4, Ch5)
**Inner complementary** — this model's revision of anima/animus: men seek their soul, women their instinctual mind; finding it ends the *need* for a partner (Ch3)
**Inner judge** (grillo parlante / giudice interno) — the self-monitoring voice that says how to please; the model says it removes spontaneity and makes us less likeable (Ch5, Ch7)
**Instinct** (istinto) — in this model's model, an innate "wisdom of the species" that includes perceiving people and danger, not just fight/flight (Ch2)
**Internal parents** — the part of the child that "passed to the parents' side" and became the inner critic (Ch5, Ch7)
**Internal reference** — judging by "how it resonates in me" while still listening to others (Ch1)
**Internalised observer** — the judging gaze carried even when alone; this model's example is the gentleman who uses sugar tongs when nobody is watching (Ch1)
**Just-world belief** ("simulacrum of justice") — in this model, the expectation that good conduct buys protection, modelled on the parent who rewards obedience (Ch8)
**Karma reframed** — the model rejects karma as reward and punishment; its psychological core is dropping merit/guilt bookkeeping (Ch8)
**Knowing vs. awareness** (sapere saputo / sapere sapiente) — this model's contrast between erudition in the head and awareness in feeling (Ch2)
**"Laziness does not exist"** (la pigrizia non esiste) — this model's claim that persistent apathy signals low energy or suffering, not laziness (Ch4, Ch5, Ch6)
**Levels of judgement** — citing Russell's logical types, the model holds that on the practical level a violent person must be stopped; from a higher level nobody is guilty of what they received (Ch8)
**Living half in the past** — this model's view that we live half in childhood, so "living in the present" requires knowing one's past (Ch2, Ch6)
**Living through proxy events** (vivere per interposti avvenimenti) — living through matches, films or stars without asking "how does this concern me?" (Ch5)
**Logorrhoea and mutism** (logorrea / mutismo) — this model's readings of compulsive talking (addressing an imagined listener) and near-silence (believing one must be brilliant to be accepted); quietness is not treated as pathology (Ch5)
**Love vs. fear** — this model's maxim "you cannot love someone you fear"; fear produces aggression (Ch3)
**Majority myth** (mito della maggioranza) — this model's point that a majority passes laws but does not guarantee justice, and psychologically stands in for the parents (Ch7)
**Male di vivere** (ache of living) — this model's near-universal background pain compensated by love, shopping, gambling and other pursuits (Ch5)
**Manipulation red flags** — this model's signs of coercive influence: telling people what to feel, blaming followers, building dependence on one figure, guru poses, promising "the secret" (Ch7)
**Medusa** — in this model's reading, a former snake goddess of becoming and death, demonised by later culture; her image on a shield turns fear into protection (Ch8)
**Mental contagion** (contagio mentale) — the model, citing Le Bon: attitudes pass from the stronger to the weaker, not the better to the worse (Ch2, Ch7)
**Mental vs. deep dreams** — this model's split between dry "mental" dreams (lists, numbers) and enigmatic deep dreams about childhood (Ch6)
**Mentalisation** (mentalizzazione) — this model's thesis of humanity shifting from feeling to organising; **not** Fonagy's clinical mentalization, which means almost the opposite (Ch3)
**Mind Matrix** (mente Matrix) — this model's moral use of *The Matrix* for a collective conditioned mind that makes us see through others' ideas (Ch2)
**Mind vs. soul speaking** — this model's markers: speaking from the soul has varied pace, pauses and tone; speaking from the mind is monotone and full of jargon (Ch2, Ch6)
**Mirror statements** — "what you said hurt me because it reminded me of…" instead of "you hurt me" (Ch3)
**Myth rewritten by power** — this model's thesis that old myths told the story of the soul and were rewritten when power changed hands; ask who tells a story and why (Ch8)
**Narcissism** (narcisismo) — in this model's popular sense, a defensive strategy of the unloved child who trades love for admiration; the word is used loosely, not diagnostically (Ch1, Ch5, Ch7)
**Nervousness** (nervosismo) — this model's term for repetitive discharge of tension (nail biting, tics, bouncing leg); never moving at all is read as anaesthesia (Ch5)
**Neurotic vs. rational sacrifice** — this model's split between purposeless self-denial as a barter with authority and sacrifice that serves a real goal (Ch7, Ch8)
**Nigredo** (opera al nero) — this model's alchemical *metaphor* for deep therapy as descent into the dark part leading back to childhood (Ch2, Ch6)
**Obedience training** — this model's name for the purpose attributed to Western family and school education (Ch1, Ch4)
**Opinion vs. need to believe** — this model's test: what you can lose without harm is an opinion; what you cannot bear to lose is a need to believe (Ch8)
**Orpheus and Eurydice** — in this model's reading, a descent to recover one's soul; the reading inverts the ban: to leave the underworld one *must* look back at childhood (Ch8)
**Others respond to your inner state** — this model's claim that people react to what one emanates more than to words (Ch5, Ch7)
**Panic attack** (attacco di panico) — fourth step of this model's fear gradient: strong anguish with no known cause (Ch5)
**Parent projection onto authority** — this model's view that people who never looked at their parents transfer an idealising stance onto God, leaders, bosses and parties (Ch4, Ch7, Ch8)
**Parsifal's unasked question** — this model's recurring image: Parsifal fails the Grail because he does not ask; failure through incuriosity (Ch2, Ch7, Ch8)
**Partner-as-parent** — this model's model that partners are chosen, idealised and feared as stand-ins for the parents (Ch3)
**Passing the time** (far passare il tempo) — filling every empty moment to cover the unease that surfaces when the mind is idle (Ch2)
**Penelope's web** — in this model, an image of nature's cycles and their acceptance (Ch8)
**"Perfect childhood" as a defence** — this model's claim that whoever insists their childhood was perfect is usually protecting someone (Ch5, Ch6)
**Personal importance** (importanza personale) — the conditioned mind's need to feel special and immortal; the model warns self-development seekers against "getting a big head" (Ch1)
**Personality** — in this model, an innate base (introversion/extraversion) that family and school can "depersonalise" (Ch1)
**Positive thinking without inner positivity** — this model's critique that forced optimism is self-conditioning that lasts minutes (Ch2)
**Power as temptation** — this model's Tolkien reading: wanting power "for good" (Gandalf refusing the Ring) is still the conditioned mind (Ch7)
**Primary mirror** — the parents' gaze, mother first, in which the model says identity starts (Ch1)
**Projection** (proiezione) — attributing what one repressed to others, especially reading them through the parents' template (Ch2, Ch3, Ch6, Ch7)
**Proportionate aggression** — this model's view that aggression is legitimate when it answers real harm and is proportionate to it (Ch7)
**Psychosomatics, three planes** — this model's split of hereditary predisposition, environment and personal reaction; the model's stronger claims (all illness has a psychic cause) are unsupported (Ch5)
**Quantity, not kind** — after Freud, the model treats disorders as matters of degree: a trait becomes a problem when it damages relationships or work (Ch1, Ch5)
**Reasoning mind** (mente ragionante) — the useful, problem-solving part of the mind, distinct from the conditioned mind (Ch1, Ch2, Ch7)
**Reciprocity** (reciprocità) — in this model, answering in the same tone as what one receives; a gauge of psychic health that does not apply in the same way between parent and child (Ch3)
**Repetition compulsion** (coazione a ripetere) — in this model's reading, choosing partners like the parents in the hope that this time the good will come (Ch1, Ch5, Ch6, Ch8)
**Repression** (rimozione) — Freud's defence, treated by the model as universal: the memory is erased but the feeling stays active; a real but debated concept (Ch2, Ch5, Ch6, Ch7)
**Respect ladder** — a three-level response to workplace disrespect: pause and resume, one private question, remove the extras (Ch7)
**Ritual overlay** — grafting a new meaning onto a loved existing ritual; this model's example is Christmas over Sol Invictus (Ch8)
**Sacrifice as barter** — this model's reading of sacrifice as "I suffer, so you spare me"; it rejects the "suffering ennobles" myth, against research on post-traumatic growth (Ch7, Ch8)
**Sadism and masochism** — this model's readings: the sadist identifies with the tormenting parent; the masochist's formula is "do what you want with me, but save me"; the claim of a single origin is contested (Ch5)
**"Saving the past"** — choosing cold partners who resemble the withholding parent, so winning them feels like rescuing one's emotional history (Ch3)
**Say it once** — this model's model of calm authority: explain clearly once, twice at most, then stop (Ch3, Ch4)
**Schemas** — mental frameworks that change with era and place; this model's example is medieval witch-hunting (Ch2)
**Screens as symptom** — this model's view that video games and smartphones are a defence against unhappiness, not its cause; research finds mixed effects (Ch7)
**Secondary identity** — an approved or opposite identity built in answer to a parental verdict and propped up by roles, looks and possessions (Ch1)
**Separation from the mother** — in this model, the fundamental, rarely completed separation: not ceasing to love her but no longer laying childhood images over her and others (Ch3)
**Sessuofobia** (sex-phobic society) — this model's thesis that family, Church and school treat sex as taboo because people who find pleasure do not sacrifice for authority (Ch3)
**Shyness vs. introversion** — in this model, introversion is innate and fearless while shyness is fear passed on by the parents; temperament research is more nuanced (Ch1, Ch4, Ch5)
**Siding with the child** (dalla parte del bambino) — this model's call to choose the weaker party (the child, instinct) over the stronger (the parents, power) (Ch4)
**Silence is a move** — workplace-assertiveness point that a two-second pause, or one question followed by silence, shifts the burden back onto the other person (Ch7)
**"Spoken from experience or from reading?"** — this model's filter for judging guides and sources (Ch2)
**Staying on the line** — this model's healthy middle between idealisation and demonisation: credit merits and faults, separate known from assumed (Ch7)
**Subconscious vs. unconscious** — in this model, the subconscious holds what one cannot recall right now; the unconscious holds what was repressed in childhood (Ch2, Ch6)
**Supremacy desire** (desiderio di supremazia) — the wish to be superior in something, starting with being the parents' favourite; this model's antidote is feeling "as important as a plant in the woods" (Ch7)
**Symptom as container** (sintomo-contenitore) — this model's reframe of habits and compulsions as containers for an unknown anguish, not vices (Ch5)
**Symptoms as signals** — this model's view that a child's sudden symptoms signal a real fear; usable only as "ask why", never as a diagnostic key (Ch4)
**Thirst for justice** (sete di giustizia) — in this model, an illusion traced to wanting justice from one's parents; it prefers "I want this" to "this is just" (Ch5)
**Tragedy of not noticing** — this model's claim that the tragedy is not being unloved but failing to realise it (Ch4)
**Transference** — the patient's projection of the parents onto the therapist; the model says it is real but should not be encouraged (Ch6, Ch7)
**Triple Goddess** — this model's nymph–mother–crone triad; refusing the third aspect, death, drives, in this view, status-seeking and specialness (Ch8)
**True maternity** — in this model, an attitude of care toward every weak creature, not the act of having a child (Ch4)
**Trust is risk-weighted** — the model separates identifying with someone from how much one entrusts to them (Ch3)
**Understanding is not justifying** — this model's recurring rule: understanding why someone acts does not excuse the act or stop one from halting harm (Ch2, Ch3, Ch4, Ch5)
**Victim selection** — this model's claim that bullies pick the psychologically fragile; it risks victim-blaming if applied to real cases (Ch5)
**Violence ≠ strength** — this model's view that violence is an inability to master aggression, so a weakness; some partners confuse it with strength (Ch3)
**"Void to fill"** — this model's reading of compulsive consumption and entertainment as compensation for repressed pain (Ch7)
**"Water up to the throat"** — this model's image of the little-loved person clutching a relationship like a drowning person, without seeing the partner (Ch3)
**"What you let pass becomes the new normal"** — Workplace-assertiveness rule: tolerated disrespect sets the baseline (Ch7)
**"Whoever insists on converting you isn't convinced"** — this model's reading of over-insistence as the persuader's own insecurity (Ch7)
**Wisdom** (saggezza) — in this model, knowledge of self and others that comes from feeling, not the head (Ch2)
**Wish to please** — this model's point that under hypnosis, in groups and around authority, people produce what the conductor seems to want (Ch6)
