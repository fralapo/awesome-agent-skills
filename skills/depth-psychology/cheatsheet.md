# Cheatsheet

Everything below describes this model's positions. It is a lens for reflection, communication, research and fiction: not clinical guidance, not a basis for diagnosing or labelling anyone. Anyone in distress should be pointed to a qualified professional; anyone at risk, to emergency services or a crisis line.

## The Model of the Psyche (Ch1, Ch2, Ch6, Ch7)
| Part | What it is in this model | Healthy role | When it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ego (io) | The plain "I": body, self-respect, capacity to see others | To be kept firm, not "demolished" | Replaced by a false ego built for approval |
| Reasoning mind (mente ragionante) | Deduces, organises, solves problems | A tool in service of the I | Cut off from feeling, it obeys conditioning without knowing |
| Conditioned mind (mente condizionata) | Rules and beliefs received from family, school, religion, media; the inner judge ("mente doppia", "grillo parlante") | Began as protection against parents, enemies, death | Outlives its purpose: judges, needs certainty, runs the false ego |
| False ego / secondary identity | Defensive self built to please parents; propped up by roles, looks, money, group | — | Rigid, wounded by criticism, measured by an external reference |
| Soul (anima) | Felt life: emotions, sensations, inner movement (secular sense) | United with the mind, neither prevailing | Regresses to an "empty shell" of pure mind |
| Instinct (istinto) | Innate "wisdom of the species": perceiving people and danger; fight/flight/freeze/flatter | Tells you whom to trust and when to defend yourself | Overridden by conditioning ("family is always good"); "closed doors" after trauma |
| Subconscious | What one does not recall now but can retrieve | — | — |
| Unconscious | What was repressed in childhood, plus what the conditioned mind has "boxed up" | Speaks through dreams and images | Drains energy; drives projection and repetition |
| End point | "Fall of the conditioned mind": conditioning (not intellect) falls; mind serves the I | Inner silence, no comparison, fewer needs | A narrative arc, never a milestone to promise |

## Fear Gradient (Ch5)
Descriptive vocabulary for intensity. **Never a diagnostic scale.**
| Step | Definition in this model |
|---|---|
| Fear (paura) | Has a known object |
| Anxiety (ansia) | Fear without an object that attaches to any event ("something will happen to me") |
| Anguish (angoscia) | Paroxysmal anxiety: racing heart, sweaty hands |
| Panic attack (attacco di panico) | Strong anguish with no known cause ("something terrible is happening to me") |
| Madness (follia) | The far end of the scale |

## Defence Mechanisms (Ch1–Ch8)
| Mechanism | How this model describes it | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Repression (rimozione) | Memory erased, feeling stays active and resurfaces later | Freudian concept, real but debated |
| Projection (proiezione) | What was repressed is attributed to others; people read through the parents' template | Recognised psychodynamic concept |
| Displacement | Fear or anger moved to a safer target (sibling, promoted colleague, object) | Recognised mechanism |
| Phobia as false target (falso bersaglio) | Fear moved onto a containable object so the real cause stays hidden | This model's reading; phobias have many causes |
| Conversion (gambling sense) | Original pain swapped for a less painful event (matches, wins/losses) | This model's usage; not conversion disorder |
| Idealisation | Seeing only the good; searching for the good parent one never had | Recognised; the parental root is interpretive |
| Demonisation | Seeing only the bad; venting rage at a parent one cannot accuse | Recognised; the parental root is interpretive |
| Splitting (good/bad parent) | Keep an idealised parent, project the "bad" one outward | Linked to Melanie Klein |
| Identification with the aggressor | Side with the violent parent to avoid feeling like a victim; victim → perpetrator | Recognised concept; "whoever tortures was tortured" is not a rule and does not run backwards |
| Self-blame to preserve the parent | "I am not worth it" rather than "my parent does not love me" | Consistent with findings on maltreated children's self-blame |
| False ego / secondary identity | Approved persona built for parental approval | Close to the psychodynamic "false self" idea; this version is interpretive |
| Escapes (approved and disapproved) | Work, study, shopping or alcohol, gambling used not to feel | Consistent with experiential avoidance |
| Compensatory euphoria | Always "up" to avoid depression | This model's term |
| "Perfect childhood" | Insisting childhood was perfect to protect someone | Interpretive; risky if applied to real people |
| Personal importance / supremacy desire | Feeling special to escape feeling like nothing and to deny death | Interpretive |
| Black-and-white thinking | Filing people into fixed good/bad drawers | Recognised cognitive distortion (dichotomous thinking) |
| Imposed forgiveness | In this model, a form of repression that covers anger | Contested; many therapists disagree |

## Manipulation and Coercive-Control Red Flags (Ch2, Ch7, Ch8)
| Red flag | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Telling people what to feel | "There's no reason to be afraid", without exploring where the fear comes from |
| Blaming the follower | "It didn't work because you didn't let go enough" |
| Dependence on one figure | Leader as "beacon of light", the only one who knows |
| Detachment sold as wisdom | Cutting followers off from emotion or the body; the unreactive guru as "evolved" |
| Verdicts and one path | Pronouncing judgments, imposing a single route, no room for doubt |
| Devotees over competence | Surrounding oneself with the most devoted, not the best |
| "The secret" for devotion | Extraordinary results promised, no checkable process |
| Guilt and renunciation appeals | Sacrifice or guilt as the price of belonging |
| Over-insistence to convert | In this model, a sign of the persuader's own uncertainty |
| Loss of one's own compass | The core test: the person no longer trusts their own sense of right and wrong and needs someone to tell them |
| Healthy counter-sign | A real path makes people *more* sensitive to others, animals and nature, and invites them to check, not believe |

One flag is not proof; look for clusters. Real coercive control or abuse needs specialist or legal support.

## Relationship Dynamics (Ch3, Ch4)
| Dynamic | This model's reading | Observable signal | Agent-safe use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner-as-parent | Partners chosen and idealised as stand-ins for the parents | Same "type" chosen again and again | Fiction; open question "who does this remind me of?" |
| Saving the past | Cold partners resembling the withholding parent feel worth winning | Loving partners seem "boring" | Character backstory |
| Exogenous jealousy | Reaction to real behaviour | Concrete incidents | Talk about the behaviour |
| Endogenous jealousy | Arises even with an impeccable partner; early abandonment | Quizzing about exes; relief when fear is "confirmed" | Self-reflection only |
| Abandonment re-lived | Fear of losing a lover = fear of losing a parent | Grief out of scale with the bond | Acknowledge the pain; don't interpret it |
| Affective dependency | Depending on someone who doesn't love you, hoping they will | One serves, the other withdraws | "Is this reciprocal?" |
| Water up to the throat | Clutching the relationship without seeing the partner | Repeated "do you really love me?" | Behaviour answers, not words |
| Reciprocity | Answering in the same tone as received | Imbalance of tone over time | Health check of any relationship |
| Violence ≠ strength | Violence is inability to master aggression | Gentle partner felt as "soft" | Fiction; brand "strength" vs. dominance |
| Love vs. fear | You cannot love someone you fear | Aggression from fear | Reduce fear before asking for trust |
| Sibling displacement | Anger unsafe toward parents turned on siblings | Rivalry among the unloved | Workplace: peers hating the promoted colleague |
| Siding with the child | Choose the weaker party over the stronger | — | Ethical stance in communication |

Gender generalisations in this model ("women more soul, men more mind", etc.) are stereotypes: do not reuse them about individuals or audiences.

## Dream Symbols (Ch6)
**Always depends on the dreamer.** Meaning comes from the person's history, context and the dream's atmosphere; few images are universal. These readings are interpretive, not validated, and a dream is **never** evidence of trauma or abuse.
| Image | This model's reading |
|---|---|
| Water | Emotions, the unconscious, childhood. Stormy sea: agitated emotions; pool: controlled; ship: protection; swimming: moving through emotions |
| House / building | The ego or the conditioned mind; a collapse is a schema falling |
| Tree | Life; a felled tree that sprouts again is a lost principle reborn |
| Bag, shoes, suitcase, car | Containers of mind and identity; a lost bag marks schemas starting to fall; a car in traffic is the mind separating us from others |
| Dog | Domesticated instinct |
| Cat | Independence, attunement with the soul |
| Wild beasts | Feared instincts or destructive people |
| Snake | Primordial instinct bringing a truth that unsettles schemas |
| Toad / frog | Transformation |
| Pregnancy | Growing consciousness |
| Money, lost teeth | Energy being dispersed |
| One's own death | A deep inner change ("the unconscious does not know death") |
| Death of a parent or authority | The idealised image becoming a real person |
| Zombies, statues, ghosts, skeletons | Repressed parts returning, felt as autonomous |
| Vampire | The mind sucking energy |
| Dry "mental" dreams (lists, numbers) | Superficial; deep or enigmatic dreams speak of childhood |

This model contradicts itself on whether dream parents are "cover figures" or literal; flag it if citing either.

## Myth → Psychological Meaning (Ch7, Ch8)
| Myth / story | This model's reading | Story template |
|---|---|---|
| Orpheus and Eurydice | Descent to recover one's own soul; one *must* look back at childhood | Descent and return |
| Medusa | Former snake goddess of becoming and death, demonised later; seen only through a mirror | The monster that was once a goddess; approach the unbearable indirectly |
| Gorgon on the shield | Fear turned into protection | Flip a frightening symbol |
| Furies → Eumenides | A fearsome power tamed into a benevolent one | Rebrand a negative |
| Penelope's web | Nature's cycles and their acceptance | Patience, strategic delay |
| Gordian knot | The conditioned mind's impatience; solving by force | The shortcut that skips understanding |
| Parsifal and the Grail | Failure through not asking the question | The question not asked |
| Triple Goddess (nymph–mother–crone) | Life cycle; refusing death drives status-seeking | Life-stage triad |
| God as protective, strict father | Need for a parent stronger than the real ones (psychological reading only) | The protector figure audiences seek |
| Devil / serpent | Rebellion against parent and power; instinct that thinks for itself | Challenger positioning |
| Christmas over Sol Invictus | New meaning grafted onto a loved ritual | Ritual overlay |
| The Ring (Tolkien) | Power "for good" is still enslavement | The corrupting ring |
| Orcs as tortured elves | The abused turned abuser | Antagonist with depth |
| Frodo | Change from someone who is not the strongest | The improbable hero |
| Symbolic death (dreams, myth) | A passage, not a loss | Change story as transformation |

Readings are interpretive. Historical claims attached to them (primordial matriarchy, a single Great Goddess) are not scholarly consensus. No claim is made about any religion's truth.

## Mainstream Support vs. This Model's Own View (Caveats, Ch1–Ch8)
| Topic | Mainstream support | This model's own or contested view |
|---|---|---|
| Early bonds (Ch1, Ch3, Ch4) | Attachment and caregiver responsiveness shape self-worth and adult relationships | Self-esteem depends *only* on parents; most parents "could not love"; no later relationship compensates |
| Repression, projection, transference (Ch2, Ch6, Ch7) | Recognised psychodynamic concepts, still debated | Treated as universal and as the single cause of most suffering |
| Causes of disorders (Ch5) | Multifactorial: biological, genetic, environmental, social | Nearly every disorder comes from repressed childhood trauma, often abuse |
| Heritability and brain chemistry (Ch5) | Inherited vulnerability interacts with environment; chemistry matters | Mental illness is not inherited; chemistry is only a consequence of emotion (unsupported). Medication is still valued as a stabilising "container" |
| Therapies (Ch5, Ch6) | CBT has strong evidence for anxiety, social anxiety, OCD | CBT and EMDR only swap conditionings; only dream analysis reaches trauma |
| Recovered memories (Ch2, Ch5, Ch6) | Memory is reconstructive; recovered memories can be constructed or suggested | Dreams are the route to repressed trauma |
| Dreams (Ch6) | Freud's censor and Jung's symbolism are theories, not demonstrated facts | Fixed symbol readings; dreams as signs of abuse (must never be used that way) |
| Child symptoms (Ch4) | Nightmares, bedwetting, stuttering, shyness have many causes and are often transient | They signal a real hidden fear or harm |
| Parenting (Ch4) | Physical punishment harms; warmth, explanation, autonomy help; Harlow's experiment is real | The family is the base institution of power; birth order means nothing; rebellious adolescence is unnecessary |
| Temperament (Ch1, Ch4, Ch5) | Temperament research is nuanced; anxiety is partly innate | Only introversion/extraversion is innate; shyness is always parental fear |
| Forgiveness (Ch3, Ch4, Ch7) | Refusing *forced* forgiveness appears in some approaches | Forgiving is harmful unless the other repents |
| Psychosomatics (Ch5, Ch6) | Stress and emotion affect immunity and health | All illness has a psychic cause; anecdotes about cancer and platelets (dangerous as medical advice) |
| Violence and bullying (Ch5, Ch7) | Multiple causes: group, school, temperament, social, economic | Single cause in childhood mistreatment; victim selection by fragility (risks victim-blaming) |
| Conformity and contagion (Ch2, Ch7) | Conformity, social proof and crowd suggestibility are documented | Attitudes always flow from stronger to weaker |
| Belief (Ch8) | Beliefs are transmitted by family and environment; belief defence is studied | God as projected parent; anxiety not innate; suffering never fosters growth (contradicts post-traumatic growth) |
| Communication (Ch2, Ch6) | Vocal variety, plain language, empathic non-judgemental listening, separating observation from evaluation | Mind/soul split with masculine/feminine coding |
| Gender (Ch3, Ch4, Ch7) | Gender differences are debated; individuals vary widely | Sweeping generalisations: do not reuse |
| Free will, brain hemispheres (Ch2) | Philosophical question; lateralisation is a pop simplification | No free will; a right-brain to left-brain history of humanity |
| Workplace assertiveness (Ch7) | — | Personal opinion and experience; assumes the worker has options |

Excluded on purpose: esoteric material in the source (reincarnation, "astral", magic, entities, chakras) and the self-help tips flagged as not to relay.

## Quick Counts
- **3 parts of the person**: reasoning mind, conditioned mind, soul (+ instinct)
- **5-step fear gradient**: fear → anxiety → anguish → panic attack → madness
- **2 kinds of power**: fear-driven (reassure) vs. pleasure in dominating (set limits)
- **2 kinds of jealousy**: exogenous (real behaviour) vs. endogenous (own history)
- **2 kinds of sacrifice**: rational (real goal) vs. neurotic (barter with authority)
- **3-level respect ladder**: pause and resume → one private question → remove the extras
