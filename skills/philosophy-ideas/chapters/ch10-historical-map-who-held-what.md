# Chapter 10: Historical Map: Who Held What

## Core Idea
In this model, the history of Western philosophy is a chain of **responses**: each thinker corrects a premise of an earlier one. Use this map to place a thesis, then jump to the thematic chapter. Contested summaries are marked (position); anything added from background knowledge is marked (general knowledge).

## 10.1 Conditions for philosophy
- **Thesis (position)**: philosophy appears where cooperation frees time from survival: strength in numbers lets people spend cleverness on art, science, government and philosophy.
- **Argument**: early groups clustered where resources were abundant; the two early regions, an Ionian (Greek and Turkish coast) style and an Italian style, knew, influenced and refuted each other.
- **Limit**: the human-migration story behind it is contested; do not rely on it.

## 10.2 Presocratics, Socrates and the sophists
- Presocratic thinkers asked what the world is made of; the label "presocratic" is disputed but marks the shift when Socrates made philosophy open to everyone, not only elites (ch01, 1.9).
- Sophists: relativism, rhetoric, useful knowledge (ch01, 1.4).

## 10.3 Plato
- **Thesis**: a philosopher loves wisdom itself, not the spectacle; beauty itself is different from beautiful objects; non-philosophers live in a dreamlike state, mistaking objects for what they instantiate.
- He wrote dialogues, put in the mouth of his teacher, in three periods: early (the teacher's concerns), middle and late (government and metaphysics). Separation of what belongs to teacher and pupil is unclear.

## 10.4 Aristotle
- Plan of life; the unmoved mover; unity of soul and body (ch03, 3.4; ch04, 4.1; ch05, 5.5).

## 10.5 Hellenistic Stoics, then Plotinus
- Stoic ethics (ch05, 5.7). (General knowledge: Plotinus is the transition from late antiquity, a Platonist emphasising an ultimate One.) One discussion contrasts philosophy and religion as answers to fear of irreversibility (ch07, 7.5).

## 10.6 Augustine, Avicenna and Aquinas
- Augustine: a sceptical youth in a mixed household; a conversion; inherited guilt: even an infant is accountable.
- Avicenna: self-taught reader of Aristotle in a region where translation preserved Greek thought; "heals" Aristotle for compatibility with Islam; flying man (ch04, 4.1). One view: medieval philosophy is less a search for truth than a validation of a belief already held (position).
- Aquinas: Aristotle read as compatible with Christianity; the first-mover argument; essence and existence (ch03, 3.4, 3.9).

## 10.7 Renaissance and early modern
- Humanism; political realism (ch06, 6.1). Rationalists and empiricists (ch02, 2.1). Descartes starts from radical doubt; Spinoza; Locke on natural freedom (ch06, 6.2); Hume on is and ought (ch01, 1.6).

## 10.8 Kant and after
- Kant: categories of the mind; autonomy of morals (ch02, 2.3; ch05, 5.3). Hegel: history and dialectic (ch06, 6.4). Marx: internal conflicts of capitalism (ch06, 6.5). Schopenhauer: will (ch07, 7.3). Nietzsche: true-world theories (ch07, 7.4).

## 10.9 Twentieth-century currents
- Phenomenology (ch07, 7.1); existentialist treatments of absurdity (ch07, 7.2); logic and language (ch07, 7.6, 7.7); philosophy of mind and the science dialogue (ch04, ch08).
- **Limit**: only the opening of the later authors is covered, not their full systems.
