# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets. Entries marked (position) are argued stances, not consensus.

## 1. "It follows that..." too quickly
**When**: an argument jumps to a striking conclusion.
**Move**: list the premises; find the hidden one; look for what else would follow. [ch01, ch09]
**Cost**: slower exchange.
**Not when**: the step is trivial.

## 2. A forced choice between two options
**When**: a dilemma is presented.
**Move**: search for a third option; test whether both branches really cost what is claimed. [ch01, ch05]
**Cost**: the third option may be harder to defend.
**Not when**: the options are exhaustive by logic.

## 3. "Everyone has their own truth"
**When**: a relativist claim is used to end a debate.
**Move**: apply it to itself; separate context-dependent claims from truth claims. [ch01]
**Cost**: can seem uncharitable.
**Not when**: the claim is only that taste or custom varies.

## 4. A fact used to settle a value
**When**: "this is how it is, so this is how it ought to be".
**Move**: state the bridge premise; argue for it. [ch01, ch05]
**Cost**: an extra premise to defend.
**Not when**: the bridge is uncontroversial (harm).

## 5. Whom to believe
**When**: a claim reaches you through someone else.
**Move**: set the evidence bar by stakes; check track record, incentives and independent confirmation. [ch02]
**Cost**: effort.
**Not when**: trivial, low-stakes information.

## 6. Viral misconception
**When**: a widely repeated claim seems off.
**Move**: trace to the original study; note who simplified it and when; classify as misinformation or disinformation. [ch02]
**Cost**: slow.
**Not when**: the claim is cheap to ignore.

## 7. Distrust of experts
**When**: past expert error is used to discard expertise.
**Move**: ask about track record, incentives, naming effects, correction mechanisms; calibrate, do not abandon. [ch02]
**Cost**: nuance.
**Not when**: the expert has no relevant competence.

## 8. Free will argument
**When**: someone claims determinism removes responsibility, or that quantum randomness saves freedom.
**Move**: define "free"; run the determined/undetermined dilemma; separate control from freedom from obstruction. [ch03]
**Cost**: leaves the metaphysical question open.
**Not when**: the context only needs practical blame tied to responsiveness to reasons.

## 9. First-cause arguments
**When**: a regress is used to conclude a creator.
**Move**: accept a need for a first cause only as far as the argument goes; identify added premises. [ch03]
**Cost**: leaves the origin open.
**Not when**: a physical start already explains the regress.

## 10. "Why is there anything?"
**When**: asked as a final question.
**Move**: define the level of nothing; check whether the question presupposes possibility. [ch03, ch08]
**Cost**: deflation.
**Not when**: a specific physical claim is at issue.

## 11. Consciousness debate
**When**: someone claims machines will or cannot be conscious.
**Move**: ask which theory of consciousness is assumed; separate phenomenal from access. [ch04]
**Cost**: no immediate verdict.
**Not when**: the question concerns only behaviour or function.

## 12. Uploading a mind
**When**: mind is treated as software.
**Move**: ask whether a map of the brain is the brain; check whether neurons are digital; make functionalism explicit. [ch04]
**Cost**: invites technical dispute.
**Not when**: only a functional copy is of interest.

## 13. Trolley-style numbers
**When**: save the many versus the one.
**Move**: ask "better for whom?"; separate agent-relative permission from aggregate value. [ch05]
**Cost**: both answers carry costs.
**Not when**: the numbers are so extreme that all views converge.

## 14. Moral authority
**When**: someone says morality needs God.
**Move**: divide into three links; run the divine-command dilemma; ask whether duty must be a command. [ch05]
**Cost**: leaves a transcendent view possible.
**Not when**: the claim is only practical (belief helps).

## 15. Problem of evil
**When**: evil is used against God or answered.
**Move**: split logical from evidential; apply greater-good and free-will defences; note natural evil. [ch05]
**Cost**: probability judgments are contestable.
**Not when**: no definition of God is on the table.

## 16. Stoic move for a distressing event
**When**: anger, anxiety or insult.
**Move**: separate the event from the judgment; correct the judgment; act on what is up to you. [ch05]
**Cost**: practice needed.
**Not when**: clinical conditions (refer to a professional).

## 17. Why obey?
**When**: authority claims obedience.
**Move**: separate comply from obey; check the four features; test the consent ground. [ch06]
**Cost**: the grounds are weak.
**Not when**: moral duties apply independently.

## 18. Online influence
**When**: concern about information in politics.
**Move**: target coordinated campaigns, their assets and money, rather than single lies; consider organisation. [ch02, ch06]
**Cost**: harder than fact-checking.
**Not when**: a single claim needs correcting.

## 19. Meaning feels given
**When**: a scene looks arbitrary or an experience feels self-evident.
**Move**: separate what is given from what habit confers; use the lens as the object. [ch07]
**Cost**: can feel destabilising.
**Not when**: a practical decision is needed.

## 20. "That belief only exists to comfort us" (position)
**When**: genealogy of a doctrine.
**Move**: use it to prompt scrutiny; then test premises. [ch01, ch07]
**Cost**: risk of the genetic fallacy.
**Not when**: no evidence for the motive.

## 21. Fine-tuning and multiverse
**When**: a multiverse is used to explain tuned constants.
**Move**: check whether the evidence is specific or general; apply total evidence; mention anthropic selection. [ch08]
**Cost**: technical.
**Not when**: other explanations are on offer.

## 22. Reduction versus emergence
**When**: a whole is explained by its parts, or not.
**Move**: ask for boundary and initial conditions; use both levels. [ch08]
**Cost**: pluralism.
**Not when**: a single level is enough.

## 23. Intuition fails on counting
**When**: a probability or infinity claim feels wrong.
**Move**: flip the question; count pairs; calculate. [ch01, ch08]
**Cost**: needs a model.
**Not when**: assumptions are broken.
