# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets.

## 1. A headline inflation number
**When**: a rate is quoted without context.
**Move**: check basket and weights, base effects, core versus headline, and whether housing is included. [ch03]
**Cost**: slows the headline.
**Not when**: only a rough sense is needed.

## 2. "The central bank printed money, so..."
**When**: QE or balance-sheet growth is blamed for prices.
**Move**: draw the chain: bonds swapped for reserves; who sold; where the spending went (goods or assets). Compare with consumer prices over time and across countries. [ch02, ch03]
**Cost**: less satisfying than a slogan.
**Not when**: the central bank funds the government directly (monetary finance).

## 3. Demand or supply?
**When**: choosing a cure for inflation.
**Move**: list evidence for each: unemployment, labour shortages, consumption versus trend, sector shifts, shipping and energy prices; rates fix demand, not supply. [ch03]
**Cost**: data are ambiguous.
**Not when**: a clearly one-sided shock.

## 4. Is this debt a problem?
**When**: a ratio is called alarming.
**Move**: compute or find real r, g, interest share of revenue, currency of issue, maturity; look at the path. [ch04]
**Cost**: needs forecasts.
**Not when**: default is already underway.

## 5. Cutting to reduce debt
**When**: austerity is proposed in a slump.
**Move**: estimate the multiplier effect on output and revenue; ask which spending is investment; compare ratio and not only deficit. [ch04]
**Cost**: slower deficit reduction.
**Not when**: the economy is at capacity or the market demands credibility now.

## 6. Giving up the currency
**When**: a high-inflation country considers a peg, board or dollarization.
**Move**: weigh inflation and rate benefits against lost buffer, lender of last resort, seigniorage, reversibility; compare with a currency board. [ch05]
**Cost**: slower growth and deeper crises on average.
**Not when**: the domestic institutions cannot be trusted at all.

## 7. A bank is failing
**When**: deposit outflows, losses.
**Move**: separate liquidity from solvency; list the loss-sharing menu (bail-out, bail-in, split rescue, merger); note concentration and moral hazard. [ch06]
**Cost**: time during which confidence falls.
**Not when**: the bank is too small to matter.

## 8. A property bust
**When**: house prices fall for years.
**Move**: assess three non-financial channels (investment, consumption, sentiment) and whether credit also contracts; compare with the fast clean-up case and the slow one. [ch06]
**Cost**: guesses about the speed of adjustment.
**Not when**: the market is in a boom.

## 9. Expensive housing
**When**: a city or country wants to fix affordability.
**Move**: test shortage (construction per capita, vacancy), speculation (empty homes), credit (mortgages to GDP, rates); then match measures: upzoning, anti-speculation, lending standards. [ch07]
**Cost**: each measure has losers.
**Not when**: demand is falling (population decline).

For an individual household: compare yearly cash flows (rent vs owner costs plus opportunity cost) and the maximum possible mortgage payment. [ch07 7.10-7.13]

## 10. Rent control proposal
**When**: rents are out of control and a cap is popular.
**Move**: list the seven effects (rents, sales, supply, mobility, spillovers, quality, new building); ask who is outside; pair with a building plan; check design (exemptions, vacancy rules). [ch08]
**Cost**: political: the cap is quicker and visible.
**Not when**: a true monopoly landlord.

## 11. Claims with a school label
**When**: an argument says "monetarists say..." or "MMT says...".
**Move**: tag as position, state the mechanism, give the rival, ask which episodes each explains and which it fails. [ch03, ch09]
**Cost**: longer answer.
**Not when**: the claim is a definition.

## 12. A forecast request
**When**: someone wants the date of a crash or the price path.
**Move**: say what is knowable (direction, vulnerabilities), what is not (timing), offer scenarios and what would have to be true; give no investment advice. [ch09]
**Cost**: less quotable.
**Not when**: never.

## 13. A shortage after a price cap
**When**: a cap is set below equilibrium (rent, fuel, bread) and queues appear.
**Move**: list the non-price rationing rule (queues, favouritism, side payments), the quality response, the supply response over years, and who is outside the cap. [ch10, ch08]
**Cost**: weakens a popular measure.
**Not when**: the cap is set above equilibrium (not binding).

## 14. A cost falls on bystanders
**When**: a product harms or benefits people who are not part of the deal.
**Move**: separate private, external and social cost; ask if rights are clear and bargaining cheap (Coase), else weigh a tax, a rule or a tradable allowance, and the measurement burden. [ch10]
**Cost**: needs an estimate of the external cost.
**Not when**: the affected parties are few, known and can contract.

## 15. A resource nobody owns
**When**: a shared stock (fish, forest, grazing, a common kitchen) is thinning.
**Move**: compare rules on inputs, local norms and tradable quotas; check whether the stock can be bounded and monitored and whether it crosses borders. [ch10]
**Cost**: setting up rights takes time and conflict.
**Not when**: the group is small and stable and norms already work.

## 16. A trade barrier is proposed
**When**: a tariff, quota or ban is justified by jobs, security or leverage.
**Move**: name who bears the cost, who captures the rent (tariff revenue, licence holders, foreign exporters), the retaliation risk, and whether the stated aim is narrower than the barrier. [ch12]
**Cost**: slower political victory.
**Not when**: a narrow security need is explicit and costed.

## 17. Comparative advantage question
**When**: a richer or more productive partner seems to have nothing to gain from trade.
**Move**: compute opportunity costs of each good in each place; gains exist wherever the ratios differ; then ask who loses within each country. [ch12]
**Cost**: ignores adjustment frictions.
**Not when**: no input is mobile and no price can adjust.

## 18. Resource-rich but poor
**When**: a country with large reserves is struggling.
**Move**: test for Dutch disease, rent-based politics, spending that tracks price, weak property rights and absence of a savings rule; compare with a resource exporter that held a fund and spending cap. [ch11, ch13]
**Cost**: single-case comparisons are weak.
**Not when**: the shock was purely external and short.

## 19. Does aid work?
**When**: a programme or a budget line is defended or attacked.
**Move**: distinguish aid for a harm (health, food) from aid for national growth; ask about lags, reverse causality, the delivery chain and conditions. [ch11]
**Cost**: modest answers.
**Not when**: emergency relief is the aim.

## 20. Reading a country case sheet
**When**: a popular story explains a whole country's rise or fall in one cause.
**Move**: split facts, proposed causes, evidence and contested points; tag shock versus structure; ask what rival cause predicts the same facts. [ch13]
**Cost**: less dramatic.
**Not when**: the facts alone are needed.

## 21. A market that is unravelling from information gaps
**When**: prices rise, good buyers or sellers leave, quality falls (insurance, used goods).
**Move**: identify who knows more, the adverse-selection loop, and the repair (inspection, group pooling, mandate, signals) with its distributional cost. [ch10]
**Cost**: repairs rearrange who pays.
**Not when**: information is symmetric or the stakes are small.

## 22. Checking a "tax X funds benefit Y" proposal
**When**: a policy is sold as self-financing by one division.
**Move**: recompute rate = cost / base; test the base (valuation, liquidity, mobility); compare with the stated rate and with experience elsewhere; give the rival view. [ch04.11]
**Cost**: the proposal looks less tidy.
**Not when**: the funding is a one-off grant, not a recurring flow.

## 23. A tariff threat as a bargaining move
**When**: tariffs are announced and described as a bluff or as lasting policy.
**Move**: compare each side's exposure (trade share of GDP, market dependence), relocation arithmetic (wage gap versus tariff, time to rebuild supply chains), margins, and the counter-levers (export restrictions on inputs, bond sales). Treat "it will be reversed" as a prediction, not a fact. [ch12.9, ch05.11]
**Cost**: no clean verdict.
**Not when**: the question is only who pays at the border (ch12.2).

## 24. A market moved on a macro release
**When**: an asset move is attributed to one data release or to one central-bank sentence.
**Move**: compare the reading with consensus and with its revision; sort the shock family; name the channel (discount rate, policy path, currency); check what was already priced. [ch14]
**Cost**: a less tidy story.
**Not when**: the question is about the level of a series, not about a market reaction.
