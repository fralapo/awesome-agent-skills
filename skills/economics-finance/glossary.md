# Glossary

**Adaptive expectations** - people expect future inflation to resemble recent inflation (ch03)
**Adverse selection** - the informed side self-selects, leaving a worse pool at the going price (ch10)
**Aggregate demand / supply** - total spending and total productive capacity; prices rise when demand outpaces supply (ch03)
**Anchored expectations** - expectations that stay near the target and do not follow current inflation (ch03)
**Arbitrage** - profiting from price differences between markets; the engine behind parity theories (ch05)
**Austerity** - deliberate spending cuts or tax rises to reduce deficits; its effect depends on the multiplier (ch04)
**Balance of payments** - record of a country's transactions with the rest of the world; current account offsets financial account (ch05)
**Bank run** - many depositors withdrawing at once because they doubt the bank (ch01, ch06)
**Base effect** - a rate looks high or low because the comparison period was unusual (ch03)
**Bottleneck economy** - supply chains jammed so that output arrives later in a rush (ch03)
**Central bank balance sheet** - assets (bonds) and liabilities (reserves, currency) used as a policy tool (ch02)
**Coase theorem** - with low transaction costs and clear property rights, private bargains are efficient (ch10)
**Collective action problem** - individually rational acts produce a collectively bad result (ch07, ch09)
**Comparative advantage** - lower opportunity cost of producing a good; the basis of gains from trade (ch12)
**Consumption good / investment good** - used up as it is enjoyed, versus bought for future return; housing is both (ch07)
**Core inflation** - inflation excluding volatile items such as food and energy (ch03)
**Credit money** - money issued as a loan or debt, mostly by private banks (ch01, ch03)
**Currency board** - regime holding foreign currency for every unit of domestic currency issued (ch05)
**Deadweight loss** - the value of mutually beneficial trades that do not happen (ch10)
**Debt-to-GDP** - public debt divided by annual national income (ch04)
**Deflation spiral** - falling prices lead to falling wages and spending, which lowers prices further (ch02)
**Demand pull** - inflation from spending that exceeds capacity (ch03)
**Dollarization** - abandoning the national currency for another country's or a union's (ch05)
**Doom loop** - rising interest cost raises debt, which raises the rate demanded (ch04, ch06)
**Dutch disease** - a resource boom raises the currency and costs and hollows out other tradable sectors (ch11)
**Equation of exchange** - money times velocity equals prices times output; an identity (ch03)
**Event study** - measuring the market effect of an unexpected announcement (ch02)
**Externality** - cost or benefit falling on someone outside the deal (ch10)
**Fertility replacement rate** - births per woman needed to keep population steady, about 2.1 to 2.3 (ch11)
**Filtering** - new upmarket homes free older ones for lower incomes (ch08)
**Financial repression** - holding interest rates below inflation by regulation to erode debt (ch04)
**Financialisation** - growing weight of financial transactions and assets in the economy (ch02, ch06)
**Fiscal theory of the price level** - the price level adjusts so debt equals the present value of surpluses (ch03)
**Forward guidance** - central bank statements about the future rate path (ch02)
**Full-reserve bank** - bank holding a reserve for every deposit (ch01)
**Indifference curve** - bundles giving equal satisfaction; slope is the marginal rate of substitution (ch10)
**Individual transferable quota** - tradable right to a share of a total allowable catch (ch10)
**Inferior good** - demand falls as income rises (ch10)
**Institutions** - laws, rules and norms (property rights, courts, trust) that shape incentives (ch11)
**Lender of last resort** - central bank lending to solvent banks in a panic (ch02, ch06)
**MMT** - modern monetary theory: a currency-issuing government is constrained by inflation and real resources (position) (ch03)
**Monetarism** - school holding that inflation is a monetary phenomenon (ch03)
**Monetary finance** - central bank funding government spending with newly created reserves (ch02)
**Money multiplier** - textbook claim that banks lend out a fixed fraction of reserves; contested (ch01)
**Moral hazard** - protection against loss encourages risk-taking (ch06)
**New Keynesian** - school combining sticky prices and forward-looking expectations (ch03)
**NIMBY** - opposition to local development by those nearby (ch07)
**Opportunity cost** - value of the next best alternative given up (ch10)
**Owner's equivalent rent** - estimated rent of an owner-occupied home, used in CPI (ch03)
**Peg** - managed exchange rate fixed to another currency (ch05)
**Pigouvian tax** - tax equal to the external cost of a good (ch10)
**Policy rate** - rate at which the central bank lends to or pays banks (ch02)
**Primary balance** - budget balance excluding interest (ch04)
**Purchasing power parity** - theory that exchange rates adjust until price levels equalise (ch05)
**QE / QT** - quantitative easing: buying assets with new reserves; quantitative tightening: reversing it (ch02)
**Quota rent** - price gap between domestic and world price on a quota-limited import (ch12)
**r versus g** - real interest rate compared to real growth (ch04)
**Real interest rate** - nominal rate minus inflation (ch04)
**Rent seeking** - spending to obtain a privileged rent, which dissipates it (ch12)
**Rentier state** - a state living on resource rents rather than taxes (ch11)
**Reserves** - bank money held at the central bank (ch01, ch02)
**Resource curse** - tendency of resource-rich countries to grow slower than expected (ch11)
**Sanction** - trade or finance restriction imposed for political, not economic, reasons (ch12)
**Seigniorage** - revenue from issuing currency (ch04, ch05)
**Social cost** - private cost plus external cost (ch10)
**Sovereign wealth fund** - state investment fund, often from resource income (ch11)
**Stagflation** - high inflation together with high unemployment (ch03)
**Steady state (growth)** - level where investment equals depreciation (ch11)
**Sticky prices** - prices that adjust slowly, especially downward (ch03)
**Supply push (cost push)** - inflation from reduced supply or higher costs (ch03)
**Tragedy of the commons** - overuse of a rival, non-excludable resource (ch10)
**Upzoning** - changing rules to allow denser building (ch07)
**Voluntary export restraint** - exporter-imposed quota under pressure, giving the rents to the exporter (ch12)
**Zero lower bound** - the floor near zero on policy rates because cash pays nothing (ch02)
