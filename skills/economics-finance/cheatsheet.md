# Economics and Finance: Cheatsheet

Figures are dated snapshots; re-check. "Position" = judgment, not fact. Chapter references in brackets.

## Where to Start
| Question | First move |
|---|---|
| "Why did X rise?" | Say whether it is a price level, an asset price or a relative price; list channels [ch03, ch09] |
| A number is quoted | Level or ratio? nominal or real? date? what basket or denominator? [ch03, ch04, ch09] |
| A policy is proposed | Chain, who pays, who is outside, rival view [ch09] |
| A crisis is in the news | Promise to pay, backing asset, mismatch, trigger, loss-sharing rule [ch06] |
| A market moved on data | Surprise versus consensus, which rate channel, which shock family [ch14] |
| The curve inverted or steepened | Expectations plus term premium; bull or bear steepening; timing unreliable [ch14] |
| A digital money or CBDC is proposed | Name the design (retail or wholesale, holding limits, remuneration) and its effect on bank deposits [ch15] |
| Inequality is rising, or a rich-poor figure is quoted | Mean or median? stock or flow? which survey, year and denominator? [ch16] |
| Is the pension system sustainable? | Dependency ratio, replacement rate for the median, which part is assistance [ch17] |

## Money and Central Banks
| Item | Rule of thumb | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Bank lending | Creates a deposit; reserves arrive afterwards through the market [ch01] | Textbook multiplier is contested |
| Policy rate | Passed to loan and deposit rates [ch02] | Lags; asset-price first |
| Forward guidance | Markets trade the path, not the step [ch02] | Surprises relative to expectations |
| QE | Bonds for reserves; raises asset prices [ch02] | Not cheques to households |
| QT | Sells or runs off assets; raises long rates [ch02] | Financial stability fear |
| Monetary finance | Central bank funds spending with reserves [ch02] | Easy to overuse; credibility |
| Lender of last resort | Lend freely to solvent banks, good collateral, high rate [ch02] | Solvency judgment |

## Inflation Channels
| Channel | Story | Evidence caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Demand pull | Spending exceeds capacity [ch03] | Slack hard to measure |
| Supply push | Energy, chips, shipping, war [ch03] | Rates do not fix supply |
| Money (quantity theory) | Money growth above output [ch03] | Velocity and credit money break the link |
| Expectations | Wages and prices set in advance [ch03] | Thin empirical support |
| Wages/profits | Bargaining power and margins [ch03] | Hard to separate from costs |
| Sector shift | Demand moves, prices sticky [ch03] | Fades if shift reverses |
| Fiscal/government-centred | Price level tied to fiscal stance (position) [ch03] | Contested, hard to test |
| Exchange rate | Import prices follow depreciation [ch03, ch05] | Two-way causality |

## Public Debt
| Test | Reading | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Debt/GDP | Level vs income | No universal threshold [ch04] |
| Real r vs g | r below g: ratio falls on its own | r depends on expected path |
| Interest/revenue | Burden, not stock | Maturity matters |
| Currency of issue | Own currency: inflation risk; foreign: default risk | Reserve currency privilege |
| Spending cuts in a slump | Can raise the ratio via the multiplier [ch04] | Depends on multiplier size |

## Currency Regimes
| Regime | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Float | Buffer, own rate policy | Volatility, import-price pass-through |
| Peg | Credibility | Vulnerable to reserve shortage |
| Currency board | Strong commitment | Exit still possible, rigid |
| Dollarization / union | Inflation falls, rates fall | No lender of last resort, no buffer, slower and more volatile growth on average [ch05] |

## Crises
| Type | Slow cause | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Banking | Losses, maturity mismatch | Large deposit outflows [ch06] |
| Sovereign | Real rate above growth | Investor refusal; doom loop [ch04] |
| Property | Credit, speculation | Price decline; three channels [ch06] |
| Currency | Reserves below issued money | Bluff called [ch05] |

## Housing and Rents
| Claim | Test | Likely finding (as reported) |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough homes | Construction per capita; vacancy low | Supported in most booming markets [ch07] |
| Bubble | Vacancy, construction boom | Mostly China, some cities |
| Cheap credit | Mortgage credit/GDP, real rates | Strong in Australia, Canada |
| Rent control helps | Covered rent, supply, mobility, quality, new building | Covered rent down; supply, mobility, quality, building worse [ch08] |
| Build more | Rezoning case | Rents stabilised relative to comparator [ch08] |
| Fixed or variable mortgage | Maximum possible payment; can the loan move lender at no penalty | Fixed with a switching right is a one-sided option [ch07] |
| Rent or buy | Yearly cash flows incl. renovation, return on capital not spent | Close to a draw; driven by mortgage rate and appreciation [ch07] |
| Rental yield | Gross to net: tax, vacancy, service charges, renovation | Net often 1-3% before appreciation [ch07] |

## Micro, Growth and Trade
| Item | Rule of thumb | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Demand shifters | Income, population, tastes, related goods, expectations [ch10] | Move along a curve versus shift |
| Supply shifters | Technology, input prices, taxes, entry, opportunity cost, expectations [ch10] | Short-run fixed supply |
| Equilibrium | Maximises consumer plus producer surplus [ch10] | Ignores fairness and bystanders |
| Externality | Price too low if costs fall on others; tax equal to external cost [ch10] | Measurement of the cost |
| Coase | Low transaction costs plus clear rights gives efficient bargains [ch10] | Many parties, unclear rights |
| Commons | Rival and non-excludable gets overused; quotas or norms repair [ch10] | Cross-border stocks |
| Monopoly | Price above marginal cost; patents trade static for dynamic gain [ch10] | Rents from political favour |
| Adverse selection | Informed side self-selects; pool unravels [ch10] | Propitious selection |
| Comparative advantage | Specialise by lowest opportunity cost [ch12] | Losers inside each country |
| Tariff versus quota | Same price and quantity; rent goes to government, licence holders or foreign firms [ch12] | Rent seeking |
| Sanctions | Political restriction; costs to sender and third parties [ch12] | Evasion channels |
| Institutions | Rules and norms set incentives for capital and talent [ch11] | Reverse causality |
| Resource curse | Dutch disease, rentier state, price-tracking spending [ch11] | Funds and rules as counter-example |
| Aid | Modest growth effect; better for specific harms [ch11] | Lags, reverse causality |
| Fertility | Replacement about 2.1 to 2.3 births per woman [ch11] | Projections date |

## Digital Money and Payments
| Item | Rule of thumb | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Bank deposit | Bank liability, convertible to cash at par; the main form of money [ch01, ch15] | Insurance limits differ by country |
| Reserves | Bank deposits at the central bank; settle between banks [ch02, ch15] | Not held by households |
| Stablecoin | Safe only as far as its reserves are liquid, valued and redeemable [ch15] | Reserve quality and redemption rules |
| Payment network | Two-sided network effects; concentration raises fees [ch15] | Switching costs and interoperability |
| Retail CBDC | Central bank claim for the public; a rate or a generous limit makes it a savings product [ch15] | Deposit flight in a stress |
| Wholesale CBDC | Settlement among banks, as reserves do now [ch15] | Limited direct effect on households |
| Cash | Only money with no intermediary and no record [ch15] | Handling cost against privacy |

## Inequality, Wealth and Pensions
| Item | Rule of thumb | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Mean versus median | Mean is pulled by the top; median is the typical household [ch16] | Quoting one as the other |
| Stock versus flow | Wealth accumulates from income and gains; income is a flow [ch16] | Comparing them directly |
| Asset-owner channel | Asset purchases and low discount rates lift owners' assets first [ch16, ch02] | Employment effects work the other way |
| Positional goods | Fixed supply makes competition zero-sum; spending rises, positions do not [ch16] | Supply expansion is the usual remedy |
| Pay-as-you-go | Current workers fund current pensions; depends on the dependency ratio [ch17] | Notional balances are not funds |
| Replacement rate | Pension over earnings; judge the median and short careers [ch17] | Mixed denominators |
| Assistance versus contributory | Reclassifying spending moves the deficit, not the total [ch17] | Hidden assistance cost |
| Tax incidence | The less elastic side bears more of a tax, whoever pays it to the state [ch10] | Short-run prices can be sticky |

## Market Transmission
| Channel | Mechanism | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Real rates | Lower the value of distant cash flows; hurt non-yielding assets [ch14] | Estimates depend on the inflation measure |
| Policy path | Markets price the expected path; probabilities read from futures [ch14] | Probabilities are not certainties |
| Curve shape | Bull steepening (short rates fall) versus bear steepening (long rates rise) [ch14] | Inversion timing unreliable |
| Carry | Borrow a low-yield currency; unwinds when it rises fast [ch14] | Correlations jump during unwinds |
| Dollar | Shapes financial conditions for dollar borrowers and importers [ch14] | Depends on the currency pair |
| Oil | Direct weight in prices plus second-round effects; supply and demand shocks differ [ch14] | Quantities are needed to tell them apart |
