# Patterns

Recurring situation -> move. Each entry: When / Move / Cost / Not when. Chapter in brackets. Entries marked (position) are contested. Educational, not advice.

## 1. "Volatile means risky" or "calm means safe"
**When**: a risk figure is quoted from price history.
**Move**: ask: risk of what, over what horizon, for whom? Separate loss probability, volatility and goal failure. [ch01]
**Cost**: less tidy numbers.
**Not when**: a regulation or limit is defined in volatility terms.

## 2. A famous winner is used as proof
**When**: someone's track record sells a strategy.
**Move**: treat as an anecdote; ask for persistence, factor-adjusted results, costs and the full sample. [ch02, ch03, ch11]
**Cost**: sounds dismissive.
**Not when**: results are audited, rule-based and replicated.

## 3. "The data disproves efficiency"
**When**: an anomaly is found.
**Move**: apply the joint hypothesis: mispricing or a wrong model of normal returns? [ch02]
**Cost**: no clean verdict.
**Not when**: the same cash flows trade at different prices.

## 4. Premium that seems to have vanished (position)
**When**: a factor did badly for a decade.
**Move**: compare with simulated chance of such a period; look at other regions; separate "reason still holds" from "I am uncomfortable"; avoid timing it. [ch03, ch11]
**Cost**: patience; tracking error.
**Not when**: the reason for the premium has been falsified.

## 5. Concentration through a story
**When**: a few stocks, employer stock or one company becomes a large part of wealth.
**Move**: cap single positions (a study used about 10%), diversify, count correlated wealth (your job). [ch03, ch04]
**Cost**: forgoes the outlier win.
**Not when**: a small, affordable "play" slice.

## 6. Choosing a stock-bond split
**When**: allocating.
**Move**: use tolerance, ability and need; take the strictest; do not use last decade's return. [ch01, ch04]
**Cost**: may be below the return you wish for.
**Not when**: need exceeds capacity: change the goal.

## 7. "Is it cheap?"
**When**: a P/E or a DCF output is cited.
**Move**: say what growth and risk the price assumes; compare with peers and history; run a conservative case. [ch05]
**Cost**: slower.
**Not when**: the business has no forecastable cash flow.

## 8. Yield curve headline
**When**: curve inverts or flattens.
**Move**: explain expectations and premium; note timing is unreliable; check the plan against a recession scenario rather than trade. [ch06, ch11]
**Cost**: unsatisfying.
**Not when**: policy distortions change what the curve means.

## 9. High-yield "income" product
**When**: a covered-call fund shows a large yield.
**Move**: identify what you gave up: upside cap, higher fees, tax; compare total return to a plain index. [ch07]
**Cost**: smoother income is real; weigh it.
**Not when**: you want that payoff on purpose.

## 10. Chasing a recent winner
**When**: one asset or country has led for years.
**Move**: split the return into earnings and valuation change; remember you cannot pick the next winner. [ch02, ch04, ch08]
**Cost**: holding the laggards.
**Not when**: a structural change is documented.

## 11. Reading a boom
**When**: prices rise on a new story.
**Move**: run the anatomy: cash flow or next buyer? new issuance funding old price? credit faster than income? who is paid by volume? Do not predict timing. [ch09]
**Cost**: unpopular in the middle of a boom.
**Not when**: results justify the price.

## 12. Strategy that insures each holder
**When**: many follow the same rule that sells into a fall.
**Move**: ask what happens if all do it at once and liquidity vanishes. [ch07, ch09]
**Cost**: complexity.
**Not when**: the market is deep relative to the rule.

## 13. Promised steady high returns
**When**: an offer has smooth returns uncorrelated with markets.
**Move**: verify custodian, customers and assets independently; check dependence on new money. [ch10]
**Cost**: friction and awkwardness.
**Not when**: payouts are from audited real profits.

## 14. Complex firm with hostile response to critics
**When**: reporters question accounts and management attacks them.
**Move**: read the evidence, not the messenger; check revenue to customers. [ch10]
**Cost**: time.
**Not when**: answers are specific and verifiable.

## 15. A decision that went badly
**When**: loss after a sound plan.
**Move**: reread the written reason, risk and expected outcome; separate luck from process; revise only if the reason is falsified. [ch11]
**Cost**: requires a decision log.
**Not when**: the repeated pattern shows a process flaw.

## 16. Manager's or founder's own money
**When**: a person with control holds most of their wealth in their own fund or business.
**Move**: count the business as part of the exposure; size down using expected-utility logic. [ch04, ch11]
**Cost**: weaker signal to investors.
**Not when**: control matters more to you, knowingly.

## 17. A backtest with a striking result
**When**: a rule or fund "beat the market" in a chart or study.
**Move**: count independent observations, check survivorship, price versus total return, costs, currency; ask how many rules were tried and compare effect size with fees. [ch13]
**Cost**: fewer exciting conclusions.
**Not when**: the rule has a stated mechanism, was fixed before testing and replicates across markets.

## 18. A return quoted without a base or horizon
**When**: "4.6% a year", "up 70%" or an average coupon is offered as the yield.
**Move**: rebuild the figure as annualised IRR on dated, net cash flows over the same horizon; state the base. [ch12, ch06]
**Cost**: some arithmetic.
**Not when**: horizons are a few days (use the absolute gain after costs).

## 19. A yield far above the comparable
**When**: a bond, deposit-like platform or certificate pays much more than its peers.
**Move**: ask which risk is being paid (credit, currency, call, subordination, platform insolvency, issuer risk); price scenarios in money, not only the good case. [ch06, ch07, ch10]
**Cost**: the "bargain" is usually gone.
**Not when**: short, high-grade bonds with a transient mispricing.

## 20. A leveraged or structured product
**When**: "3x", "protected capital", "monthly coupon with barrier".
**Move**: find the wipe-out day, the barrier cliff, the issuer's solvency, the forgone dividends and the cost against a frictionless replica; test on rolling windows. [ch07, ch13]
**Cost**: less leverage or no product.
**Not when**: a specific hedge of a known exposure over a few days.

## 21. Money needed on a known date
**When**: tuition, tax bill, house deposit within 1-10 years.
**Move**: match the outflow to an instrument maturing on that date (single bond, deposit) rather than a fund with constant duration; keep equities for money not needed for ten years. [ch04, ch06]
**Cost**: lower expected return, concentration in issuers.
**Not when**: the wealth is so large that no outflow forces a sale.

## 22. Planning withdrawals from a portfolio
**When**: retirement or early-retirement spreadsheets use an average return.
**Move**: use real net return, a horizon-dependent rate, block-resampled history and the left tail; test a bad first decade. [ch14, ch12]
**Cost**: a larger required capital or flexible spending.
**Not when**: a guaranteed income covers the spending.

## 23. A message from a famous person or a "recovery service"
**When**: an advertisement, chat or call promises gains or recovers lost funds.
**Move**: do not pay any fee to release funds; hang up and call a number you found yourself; check the domain and the register. [ch10]
**Cost**: some missed offers.
**Not when**: you initiated contact with a firm you verified.

## 24. Rebalancing or switching on a rule
**When**: a portfolio drifts or a better allocation appears.
**Move**: use contributions and withdrawals before selling; act only after a weight has doubled or tripled; do not buy an asset that is failing. [ch04]
**Cost**: some drift.
**Not when**: taxes and fees are nil and the assets oscillate in small swings.
