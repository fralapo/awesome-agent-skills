# Chapter 3: Fit, Body Measurements and Pattern Alterations

## Core Idea
Fit is the relationship between a body, a pattern and a fabric. There is no single correct amount of ease and no "perfect block" for every cloth: the same pattern behaves differently with a different fabric, silhouette or wearer. Practitioners generally work in a fixed order: measure accurately, choose the size from the right measurement, make a test garment, read where the cloth pulls or gapes, then alter the pattern (never the finished garment) and carry every change to all connected pieces. Alterations are small, local and checked again on a new test. Most numbers quoted below are starting points to be confirmed on the person.

## 3.1 Ease
- **Principle**: Ease is the difference between finished garment measurements and body measurements at bust, waist and low hip. It is a design decision as much as a comfort need.
- **How it works / sequence**: Find finished measurements on the pattern. To learn a personal preference, measure a non-stretch garment you already like at the same points and compare.
- **Moves**:
  - Zero ease is only for very close or stretch fabrics; knits can have zero or negative ease.
  - About 4 cm (1.5 in) is described as a comfortable minimum for a close-fitting woven with princess seams; over about 9 cm (3.5 in) at the waist is loose (approximate).
  - Thick or low-stretch fabrics need more ease; very thin fabrics can wrinkle at the shoulder if given too little.
  - A high sleeve head needs more ease than a drop shoulder.
  - Voluminous sleeves carry large ease by design.
- **Evidence**: Practitioner for the principle; Observational for the centimetre values.
- **Trade-off**: More ease means comfort and drape but less shape; less ease means shape but less movement.
- **Does not hold when**: The fabric stretches a lot, or the silhouette is deliberately oversized.

## 3.2 Silhouette and fit level
- **Principle**: Fit should be judged separately at bust, waist and hip, and the silhouette (fitted, semi-fitted, straight, A-line) comes from side-seam shape and waist darts.
- **How it works / sequence**: Choose silhouette and fit level first, then the pattern. As ease increases, an anatomical block with darts flattens and the dart-based shaping disappears.
- **Moves**: Decide fit with the designer or wearer, not from a table; test on a body or form.
- **Evidence**: Practitioner.
- **Trade-off**: A fitted block shows every error; a loose one hides them but adds bulk.
- **Does not hold when**: The garment is knitted or draped.

## 3.3 Measuring the body
- **Principle**: Accurate measurements decide size and alterations.
- **How it works / sequence**: Tape parallel to the floor, snug but not pulling, relaxed breathing, arms relaxed, wearing the underwear the garment will be worn with.
- **Moves**: Measure bust, high bust, waist, low hip, back length; for sleeves, the widest bicep, both sides, using the larger. Do not rely on bra cup sizes, which vary between makers and countries.
- **Evidence**: Practitioner.
- **Trade-off**: Different underwear changes the numbers; use what will actually be worn.
- **Does not hold when**: The garment is worn without underwear or the fabric is very stretchy.

## 3.4 Choosing size and full or small bust adjustment
- **Principle**: Choose the size from the high-bust measurement so that shoulders and neckline fit, then add or remove bust width and length locally.
- **How it works / sequence**: Subtract high bust from full bust. A pattern drafted for a B cup assumes roughly 5 cm (2 in); each cup up adds roughly 2.5 cm (1 in).
- **Moves**:
  - Full bust adjustment adds width and height at the bust centre; small bust adjustment removes them.
  - Signs for a small adjustment: loose bust with correct shoulders, neckline and armhole, and a front longer than the back.
  - Choosing size by full bust makes shoulders and neck too wide.
  - A simple dart-width starting point is about 25% of the bust minus underbust difference, corrected by a test.
  - Blend between sizes where the body changes size.
- **Evidence**: Practitioner for the high-bust method; Position for the 25% dart rule.
- **Trade-off**: Alterations add steps and can distort other pieces.
- **Does not hold when**: Cup naming differs between systems, or the difference is very large; sources disagree on which measurement pair defines a cup (Contested).

## 3.5 Bust apex and darts
- **Principle**: Darts should point to the wearer's apex, the fullest point of the bust, which is not always the nipple.
- **How it works / sequence**: Mark the apex on the body in the intended underwear, front and profile. Take distances: between apexes (use half from centre front), centre front neck to apex, shoulder-neck point to apex, shoulder end to apex, centre front waist to apex. Average both sides and use only measures that agree with the pattern.
- **Moves**:
  - Estimating an apex on a pattern: with one dart, draw from the dart centre to its tip and extend; typically about 2.5 cm (1 in) beyond, within a range of about 1 to 5 cm (approximate). With two darts, the crossing of their centre lines. With princess seams, the strongest curve.
  - Small dart move: lengthen, shorten or redraw legs (beware a downward-pointing side dart).
  - Large move: box around the dart, about 1 cm (0.4 in) from edges, parallel to grain; cut, shift by the measured height and width, fill gaps, redraw legs and true seams. If the dart narrows, cut and respread to keep width.
  - Front gaping at the neckline: slash from bust point toward the armhole mid-point and overlap about 0.6 cm (0.25 in) (approximate).
- **Evidence**: Practitioner for the box method; Observational for the apex estimate.
- **Trade-off**: Moving only sideways without rebalancing loses dart width and unbalances side seams.
- **Does not hold when**: There is no dart or curve to read from.

## 3.6 Order of alterations and testing
- **Principle**: Alter in a fixed order and always test.
- **How it works / sequence**: Lengths first, then curves (bust, forward shoulder), then widths.
- **Moves**: Carry each change to back, facings, lining, sleeve and armhole; true all seams; check notches. Sew a toile in muslin or calico, with matching stretch for knits. Separate technical faults (seams that do not match) from design choices (ease, pocket position).
- **Evidence**: Practitioner.
- **Trade-off**: A test costs time and cloth but prevents larger errors.
- **Does not hold when**: The pattern is a trusted block in the same fabric.

## 3.7 Sleeves and bicep
- **Principle**: Widening a sleeve changes the sleeve head and armhole.
- **How it works / sequence**: Typical ease for a close non-stretch sleeve is about 2.5 cm (1 in) beyond the bicep.
- **Moves**: Up to about 1 cm per side (0.4 in): widen the underarm and blend. Up to about 2.5 cm (1 in): vertical slash through the sleeve centre and spread, armhole unchanged. Beyond that: redraw the sleeve head, compare with the armhole and reposition notches. Shortening a sleeve: cut, overlap and redraw the head.
- **Evidence**: Observational.
- **Trade-off**: Flat sleeve heads if the cap is not re-checked.
- **Does not hold when**: Raglan or very full sleeves.

## 3.8 Trousers and drag lines
- **Principle**: Wrinkles point to the cause. Three factors act together: pattern, body, fabric.
- **How it works / sequence**: Folds running from crotch toward the side seam suggest legs too narrow at the bottom; from side seam toward crotch, too wide. Try another fabric before altering.
- **Moves**: Test seated. For prominent or flat seat, alter the pattern, not the garment. Quantities were not consistently given.
- **Evidence**: Observational.
- **Trade-off**: Correcting one fold may move another.
- **Does not hold when**: The garment is stretch.

## 3.9 Grading
- **Principle**: There is no single industrial rule; increments depend on the target market.
- **How it works / sequence**: In one worked example with 10 cm (4 in) chest growth, each quarter grows 2.5 cm (1 in); the underarm point moves only horizontally; shoulder rises roughly 1.3 cm (0.5 in); bicep grows about 36% of chest increment; the grown sleeve-head length must match the body armhole.
- **Moves**: Move waistband points only along the horizontal axis to keep curves unchanged.
- **Evidence**: Position (single example).
- **Trade-off**: Fast but approximate.
- **Does not hold when**: Different garment type.

## 3.10 Visual proportion
- **Principle**: Finished-garment balance often follows the rule of thirds: top about one third, bottom two thirds.
- **Moves**: Top ends near the navel, at most three to four fingers below; tops and shirts at seat level; jackets at hip or seat, not mid-thigh; trouser hem just touches the top of the shoe; avoid loose over loose.
- **Evidence**: Practitioner.
- **Trade-off**: Rule of thumb, not for every figure.
- **Does not hold when**: Short torso or deliberate oversize.

## Quick use
| Situation | Start with |
|---|---|
| Choosing size | High bust, then bust adjustment |
| Gaping neckline | Slash from bust point, overlap, redraw dart |
| Dart misses apex | Measure apex, shift dart by box method |
| Several alterations | Length, then curves, then widths |
| Pulling folds on trousers | Read direction, try fabric, then move leg |
| Wide sleeve | Spread at centre, re-check sleeve head |
| Unsure of ease | Measure a loved garment |
