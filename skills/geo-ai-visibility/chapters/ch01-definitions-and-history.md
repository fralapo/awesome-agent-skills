# Chapter 1: GEO — Definitions and History

## Core Idea
**Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** is the practice of optimizing content and sites to raise the probability of being cited, mentioned or summarized in the answers of generative engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Copilot, Gemini, Claude). It is the counterpart of SEO for synthesized answers instead of lists of clickable links.

## What GEO is

- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)**: optimizing to be selected as a source or passage inside a single synthesized answer generated by an LLM, not to rank in a list of 10 links.
- **Mental model**: in SEO you optimize to **rank** in a list of clickable links, the user clicks, and the site gets traffic. In GEO you optimize to be **selected as a source or passage** inside one synthesized answer; there is often no click at all, and the value is the citation or mention itself (brand exposure), not direct traffic.
- **Related terms used interchangeably in the industry**: **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimization), **AI Search Optimization**, **AI Visibility**, **LLM Optimization (LLMO)**. For the exact boundaries between these terms see `glossary.md`.
- **A phrasing that recurs across industry sources**: *"LLMs don't rank pages — they select passages, reuse evidence, and cite what helps them answer with confidence."*

## The founding paper

- **"GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"** — authors Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan, Ameet Deshpande; affiliations Princeton University, Allen Institute for AI, Georgia Tech, IIT Delhi. Published on arXiv on 16 November 2023 (arXiv:2311.09735), then presented at ACM SIGKDD 2024 (KDD '24). It is the first academic paper to formalize GEO as a "black-box optimization framework" for content creators, with a dedicated benchmark.

### GEO-BENCH (the benchmark)

- **10,000 queries in total** (8k train / 1k validation / 1k test), aggregated from **9 source datasets**: MS MARCO, ORCAS-I, Natural Questions, AllSouls, LIMA, Davinci-Debate, Perplexity.ai Discover, ELI5, and queries generated by GPT-4.
- **25 different domains/categories** (Arts, Health, Games, Law & Government, Debate, History, People & Society, Science, Explanation, Opinion, etc.). Query distribution: **80% informational, 10% transactional, 10% navigational**.
- **Search backend**: Google Search (top 5 results per query) was used to build the prototype generative engine on which the interventions were tested, with real-world validation on Perplexity.ai as a deployed generative engine.

### The two proposed metrics

- **Position-Adjusted Word Count (PAWC)**: a normalized word count that weights the contribution of each source cited in the answer, with exponential decay based on the position of the citation (citations closer to the start of the answer weigh more — analogous to positional CTR in the classic SERP). Formula: `Σ(s∈Sci) |s|·e^(-pos(s)/|S|) / Σ(s∈Sr) |s|`.
- **Subjective Impression (SI)**: a composite metric with 7 components, evaluated with GPT-3.5 (a methodology similar to G-Eval) — relevance to the query, influence of the citation, uniqueness of the content, subjective positional prominence, perceived volume of the content, likelihood of a click, diversity of the cited material.

### The 9 techniques tested and their measured impact

| Technique | Δ PAWC | Δ Subjective Impression | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quotation Addition** (expert quotes) | +41% (top performer) | +28% | Best technique overall |
| **Statistics Addition** (data/statistics) | +33% | +22% | Also strong on real Perplexity (+37% SI) |
| **Cite Sources** (citing authoritative sources in the text) | +30% | +15% | Much stronger effect on low-ranking sites |
| **Fluency Optimization** (smoother, more readable prose) | +28% | +13% | |
| **Technical Terms** (domain-specific technical terminology) | +18% | +10% | |
| **Easy-to-Understand** (simplifying the language) | +13% | +5% | |
| **Authoritative** (authoritative/assertive tone) | +12% | +17% | Best for Debate/History/Science |
| **Unique Words** (rare/distinctive vocabulary) | +6% | minimal | Weak effect |
| **Keyword Stuffing** | -8% (down to -10% on real Perplexity) | — | Counterproductive: contradicts classic SEO |

- **Validation on real Perplexity.ai**: Quotation Addition +22% PAWC; Statistics Addition +37% SI; Keyword Stuffing -10% (confirming that it is counterproductive).
- **Best combination**: combined strategies beat single ones — Fluency Optimization + Statistics Addition is the best combination in the tests.

### Redistributive effect (key finding)

- **Redistributive effect**: the lift from GEO is much stronger for sources that already rank low — "Cite Sources" gives **+115% for sources in position 5** against **-30% for sources already in position 1**. Implication: GEO tends to "democratize" visibility — sites that are disadvantaged in the traditional SERP have more room to grow in generative answers, while sites that already dominate have less to gain (or lose in relative terms).

### Effectiveness by domain (it varies)

- **Authoritative**: best for Debate, History, Science. **Quotation Addition**: best for People & Society, Explanation, History. **Statistics Addition**: most effective for Law & Government, Debate, Opinion. **Cite Sources**: strongest on factual/declarative queries. There is no universal technique — optimization has to be adapted to the domain and type of query.

### Limitations stated by the authors

- **Stated limitations**: the techniques need continuous adaptation as generative engines evolve (the underlying models change); query datasets can age and require the benchmark to be updated; the study does not evaluate the impact on traditional SEO ranking (it is orthogonal); the tagging of queries by domain is subject to subjective interpretation (despite manual verification).

## Essential timeline of the field

- **Nov 2023**: publication of the GEO paper (Princeton/Georgia Tech/Allen AI/IIT Delhi) — the academic term is born.
- **2024**: paper presented at KDD 2024 (ACM SIGKDD).
- **2024–2025**: rapid growth of commercial AI-visibility tools (Profound, Peec AI, Otterly.AI, AthenaHQ, Goodie...) and of "how-to" content from SEO vendors and publications (Ahrefs, Semrush, HubSpot, Search Engine Land).
- **2025–2026**: market consolidation — large funding rounds (Profound $96M Series C at a $1B valuation, Feb 2026; Peec AI €18M Series A, Nov 2025); emerging standards such as **llms.txt** appear (reported adoption on more than 844,000 sites, including Stripe, Cloudflare, Vercel, Anthropic); Google adds an llms.txt check to **Chrome Lighthouse** (Apr 2026).
- **2026**: further growth of "query fan-out" in Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, which changes how citations are formed (see `ch02-how-generative-engines-work.md`).

## GEO vs traditional SEO

| Dimension | Traditional SEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|
| Output for the user | List of 10 links (SERP) | One synthesized, multi-source answer |
| Unit of optimization | Whole page (title, meta, backlinks) | **Semantic chunk/passage** inside the page |
| Main signals | Keywords, backlinks, metadata, PageRank | Fact density, structured data, entity relationships, citability |
| Typical queries | Short, navigational keywords | **Conversational**, multi-turn, informational/comparative |
| Success metric | Ranking position, CTR, traffic | Citation/mention frequency, share of voice, sentiment in the answer |
| Click-through | Central | Often absent (zero-click) — the "win" is being mentioned even without a click |
| Backlinks | Primary authority signal | Secondary; entity co-occurrence and unlinked mentions count more |
| Multiple ranking | 1 page = 1 position | 1 answer can cite 3-10 different sources at the same time (no exclusive "position 1") |
| Feedback loop | Google Search Console, rank trackers | AI-visibility tools (Profound, Peec, Otterly...) — a new category with no established standard |

- **What stays the same**: authority, clarity, structure and alignment with user intent count in both — but the way these signals are "read" changes: SEO reads them through backlinks/CTR, generative engines through entity recognition and semantic coherence. A solid technical SEO base (speed, crawlability, clean headings, no robots.txt blocks on AI crawlers) remains a prerequisite for GEO: GEO is built *on top of* SEO, it does not replace it.
- **Why "ranking well on Google" does not guarantee AI visibility**: Google AI Overviews synthesizes (in part) from pages already indexed and ranked by classic Google, but with query fan-out (the original query is split into several sub-queries, and the pages that appear most often in the sub-query results get cited) only **38%** of AI Overview citations now come from pages in the Google top 10 (2026 figure, sharply down from 76% in mid-2025). ChatGPT Search and Perplexity have retrieval pipelines that are independent of Google: only **11% of domains** are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity (out of 680M+ citations analyzed) — each engine has to be optimized for separately; one channel is not enough.
- **Query fan-out (Google) — a key concept in 2026**: the user query is not treated as a single search. Google breaks it down into related sub-queries, runs multiple searches, and synthesizes. Pages that appear repeatedly across several sub-queries are more likely to be cited in the AI Overview. Practical implication: cover a **broad topic cluster** (all the sub-questions connected to the theme), not just the main keyword.
- **Brand mention vs linked citation**: in a commercial context what often matters most is the brand mention (the AI recommends the company by name), not necessarily a clickable link — mentions without a link still contribute to the brand's visibility in AI answers. The co-occurrence signal (the brand name appearing in the same paragraph/context as the category keywords on authoritative third-party sites) is what "tells" the model that the brand belongs to that category.

## Key Takeaways
1. GEO optimizes for being cited or mentioned inside a synthesized answer, not for ranking in a SERP — the unit of optimization is the semantic chunk, not the whole page.
2. The founding paper (arXiv:2311.09735) shows that adding expert quotes and statistics (Quotation/Statistics Addition) is the most effective lever, while keyword stuffing is counterproductive in GEO.
3. The lift from GEO is redistributive: sites in low positions in the traditional SERP have the most room to grow in generative answers.
4. No GEO technique is universal — effectiveness varies by domain and type of query, so optimization has to be adapted case by case.
5. Classic Google ranking does not guarantee an AI citation: with query fan-out only 38% of AI Overview citations come from the Google top 10, and ChatGPT/Perplexity have independent pipelines (only 11% overlap in the domains the two cite).
