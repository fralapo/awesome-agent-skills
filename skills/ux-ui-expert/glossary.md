# Glossary — UX/UI Research & Leadership Expert

**Affinity Diagram** — grouping raw qualitative data points into emergent themes during analysis (Ch7)

**Affordance** — what an element can do (e.g., a button can be pressed); communicated to the user via a signifier (Ch18)

**Anticipated Use** — user expectations formed before actual use; part of what UX design accounts for, alongside actual use (Ch12)

**Attrition/Drop-off Rate** — participants leaving a longitudinal study before completion; the main risk of long diary studies (Ch8)

**Baseline / Benchmark** — the first measurement of a metric, used as the comparison point for all future measurements (Ch10)

**Belmont Report** — 1974 ethics document (created in response to the Tuskegee syphilis experiment) defining five research-ethics principles: Respect for Persons, Beneficence, Justice, Fidelity and Responsibility, Integrity (Ch9)

**Between-Subjects Design** — experimental design where each participant experiences only one condition/prototype (Ch9)

**Card Sorting** — method where participants group content/labels into categories to inform information architecture; open (no predefined categories) or closed (validate predefined categories) (Ch8)

**Central Limit Theorem (practitioner rule)** — statistical rule of thumb: don't report percentages for sample sizes under ~30 (Ch7, Ch8)

**Classical Conditioning** — pairing a neutral stimulus with a response until the stimulus alone triggers it (Pavlov); underlies notification-sound design (Ch20)

**Coercion** — forcing/threatening someone against their will; the most unethical influence tactic (Ch11)

**Cognitive Empathy** — knowing how someone else thinks and feels, one of Goleman's three empathy types (Ch11)

**Cognitive Overload** — the stress placed on the brain when asked to process too much information at once (Ch20)

**Color Ramp** — a systematic range of tints/shades derived from a primary color, used for chips, states, and charts (Ch18)

**Compassion** — taking action to help based on empathy; the empathy type most relevant to UX practice (Ch11)

**Component State Set** — the minimum required visual states for an interactive element: default, hover, active/pressed, disabled, often loading (Ch18)

**Concept Testing** — validating/invalidating whether an idea resonates with users, using any method; not itself a method (Ch3)

**Contextual Inquiry** — combining observation with interviews to study behavior in a participant's natural environment; NOT true ethnography (Ch8)

**Conversion Rate** — the number/percent of visitors who complete a target action (buy, donate, register); the core metric in most ROI-of-UX calculations (Ch13)

**Cost-of-Error Multiplier** — fixing a defect after development costs roughly 100x fixing it before/during development (Ch13)

**Counterbalancing** — systematically varying the order conditions are shown to participants to cancel out order effects (Ch9)

**CX (Customer Experience)** — the full journey across every touchpoint (comparing, purchasing, support), encompassing UX and UI as a superset (Ch12)

**DACI Framework** — documenting who's working on what and when within a study plan timeline (Ch5)

**Dark Mode Adjustment** — light-mode techniques (borders, shadows, saturated fills) don't transfer directly; require softened borders and lighter surfaces instead of shadows for depth (Ch18)

**Decision Point** — a moment in a user flow where the path branches based on a condition, shown as a diamond (Ch16)

**Design System** — a shared set of rules (spacing, typography scale, interaction patterns) and reusable components sized to a team's actual scale, valued as much for its creation process as its output (Ch19)

**Diary Study (Cultural Probe)** — longitudinal method where participants self-log behaviors/attitudes over time (Ch8)

**Dominance Hierarchy** — leadership theory based on power/authority ranking, seen in animal social groups (Ch11)

**Double Diamond** — design-thinking diverge/converge pattern, often repeated multiple times per project; applied concretely as Discover/Define/Develop/Deliver (Ch3, Ch14)

**Embedded Model** — research team structure where a researcher is dedicated to one product team (Ch2)

**Empathy Map** — a "user says/thinks/does/feels" deliverable often considered redundant with journey maps (Ch7)

**Evaluative Research** — tactical research testing an existing idea/prototype; splits into formative and summative (Ch3)

**Evergreen Insights** — findings durable enough to remain useful for months or years (Ch10)

**Formative Research** — early-stage evaluative research (e.g., usability testing) done before launch to improve a design (Ch3)

**Foundational/Strategic/Generative Research** — synonymous terms for big-picture, exploratory research building the knowledge base of who users are (Ch3)

**Four Communication Styles** — Direct, Spirited, Considerate, Systematic — a 2x2 model of assertiveness × expressiveness (Ch11)

**Genetic Set Point** — a baseline satisfaction level a person tends to return to regardless of temporary positive or negative circumstances (Ch20)

**Ghost Button** — a button with no visible background until an interaction state (e.g., hover) reveals it (Ch18)

**Goleman's Six Leadership Styles** — Affiliative, Visionary, Coaching, Democratic (good defaults), Coercive/Commanding, Pace-Setting (situational) (Ch11)

**Great Man Theory** — Thomas Carlyle's discredited theory that leaders are born with innate charisma, not made (Ch11)

**Hawthorne Effect** — behavior changing because participants know they're being observed (Ch8)

**Hedonic Adaptation** — acclimating to positive/negative stimuli over time, drifting back toward a baseline satisfaction level; erodes engagement with any static experience (Ch20)

**Influence** — earned, long-term, win-win relationship-building, distinct from persuasion ("influence is something you earn") (Ch11)

**Information Architecture (IA)** — how content/features are organized and labeled for navigation (Ch8, Ch12)

**Institutional Review Board (IRB)** — academic/biomedical ethics review body; most UX researchers operate without one (Ch9)

**Latin Square Design** — a counterbalancing shortcut that still embeds systematic order bias; best avoided in favor of true pseudorandom assignment (Ch9)

**Magic Number Five** — 5 participants in a usability test uncover ~80-85% of usability problems (Nielsen/Virzi/Lewis) (Ch8)

**Manipulation** — controlling others for short-term selfish gain with no cooperation (Ch11)

**Micro-interaction** — a small, purposeful animation confirming an action succeeded, beyond a static hover/click state (Ch18)

**Milgram Experiment** — 1961 Yale obedience study cited as a research-ethics cautionary tale (Ch9)

**Order Effects** — bias introduced by the sequence in which conditions are presented (fatigue, practice/learning) (Ch9)

**Payback Period** — time for recovered revenue/cost savings to equal the cost of a UX fix (Ch13)

**Persuasion** — short-term, one-directional convincing; "something you do," distinct from influence (Ch11)

**Persuasive Design** — designing interaction loops (e.g., post → notify → react) that prompt repeated re-engagement (Ch20)

**Positive Intermittent Reinforcement (Variable Reward)** — reward delivered unpredictably rather than every time; the strongest known engagement driver and the one requiring the most ethical scrutiny (Ch20)

**Prevalence / Severity / Frequency** — three-factor framework for triaging and prioritizing usability issues (Ch7)

**Processing (Perceptual) Fluency** — the ease with which a person can process presented information; perceived quality often correlates inversely with information density (Ch20)

**Progressive Disclosure** — revealing information or controls incrementally, as the user needs them, rather than all at once (e.g., "load more" over infinite scroll) (Ch19)

**Psychological Ownership** — the bias a feature's builder develops toward it, which makes self-assessment of that feature unreliable; a core rationale for independent research (Ch15)

**Research Roadmap** — a timeline of planned UX research studies, scoped to research only, revisited as priorities shift (Ch4)

**Screener Table (Track/Question/Why)** — a table format for organizing recruiting screener logic and rationale (Ch5)

**Semantic Color** — a color chosen for conventional meaning (blue=trust, red=danger, yellow=warning, green=success) rather than pure decoration (Ch18)

**Signifier** — the visual cue (container, highlight, hover state) that communicates an element's affordance (Ch18)

**Stakeholder** — anyone with a vested interest in a product, initiative, or project (Ch1, Ch2)

**Stanford Prison Experiment** — 1971 Zimbardo study cited as a research-ethics cautionary tale (Ch9)

**Study Plan** — the living document centralizing a study's background, objectives, research questions, timeline, methodology, and procedure (Ch5)

**Sub-flow** — a separate, linked user-flow diagram (e.g., registration, password recovery) branched off a decision point in the main flow (Ch16)

**Summative Research** — post-launch, usually quantitative evaluative research tracking real-world metrics (Ch3)

**Superordinate Goal** — a shared goal above individual self-interest, used to realign resistant stakeholders (Ch11)

**Tactical Research** — synonymous with evaluative research; implementation-focused (Ch3)

**Tactical Research Death Spiral** — the risk of over-relying on easy evaluative/usability testing at the expense of foundational research (Ch3)

**Transformational Leadership** — leadership style where anyone can lead by prioritizing followers' growth over self-interest (Ch11)

**T-Shirt Sizing** — estimating relative task size (small/medium/large) without precise time estimates, used to prioritize a backlog (Ch14)

**Two-Step Content Structuring** — (1) decide which content to display based on real user need, (2) structure it to survive real-world content variance (long titles, low-contrast overlays) (Ch19)

**UCD (User-Centred Design)** — a design process centering user behavior, needs, and context at every decision, rather than designer or engineering convenience (Ch12)

**UI (User Interface)** — the visible layer of a product: visual design, color, layout, branding; a subset of UX (Ch12)

**User Flow** — a diagram mapping every step a user can take to complete a task, using circles (start/end), lines (paths), and diamonds (decisions) (Ch16)

**User Intent** — the specific goal a user is trying to accomplish in a given moment, which should drive UI decisions rather than being reverse-engineered from a layout (Ch19)

**UX (User Experience)** — a person's perceptions and responses from using or anticipating use of a product, system, or service (Ch12)

**UX Iceberg** — a model showing visual design as the ~10% visible tip, with interface design, functional specs, and strategy stacked beneath it (Ch12)

**Who / Question / Why Framework** — a 3-column table for structuring research questions with intent (Ch5)

**Within-Subjects Design** — experimental design where each participant experiences all conditions (Ch9)
