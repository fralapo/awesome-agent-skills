# Chapter 4: The Research Roadmap

## Core Idea
A research roadmap is simply a timeline of what research happens when — useful for alignment and prioritization — but confidence in sticking to it degrades sharply past 3-4 months, so the real skill is planning with intentional buffer time and staying adaptive rather than chasing a perfect long-range plan.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Research Roadmap Scope Rule**: a research roadmap contains ONLY UX research studies and their timelines — not marketing, engineering, or customer-support workstreams (those have their own roadmaps), though overlap/collaboration with those teams is still worth discussing.
  - When to use: scoping what belongs on your roadmap vs. what's a cross-team discussion.
- **Roadmap Planning Walkthrough Pattern**: (1) gather stakeholder priorities/projects for the quarter, (2) plan highest-priority project first, (3) lay out kickoff → desk research → recruiting → drafting plan → stakeholder feedback → pilot → sessions → analysis → readout → (if foundational) ideation workshop, (4) stagger a second/lower-priority project's studies so you're never running two studies in the same week, (5) always add buffer time for recruiting delays and scope changes.
  - When to use: quarterly planning exercises with 2+ concurrent projects.

## Key Concepts
- **Research roadmap / research timeline**: a chart (Gantt-style or calendar-style) mapping research activities to weeks/months, updated as new priorities emerge.
- **Buffer time**: intentional slack built into a timeline to absorb recruiting delays, scope creep, or shifting priorities — always add buffer time whenever planning projects far in advance.
- **Roadmap adaptability**: the expectation that roadmaps beyond one quarter are directional, not commitments.

## Mental Models
- Racetrack analogy: look toward the next turn, not five turns ahead — you can't plan the next turn well without having executed the current one, but you still need a long-term vision to aim toward.
- "If the plan doesn't work, change the plan, not the goal" — treat the roadmap as a living document, not a contract.
- Be like water — anticipate roadblocks so priority shifts and scrapped projects don't derail your composure.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating the roadmap as fixed**: virtually no researcher sticks to timelines planned more than a quarter out — presenting a roadmap as a firm commitment sets up credibility problems later.
- **Planning without buffer**: back-to-back scheduling with no slack means any recruiting delay or scope change cascades into missed deadlines.
- **Running two studies the same week**: without staggering projects, researcher bandwidth and stakeholder attention get split, degrading both studies.

## Key Takeaways
1. Scope the roadmap to UX research studies only — cross-team overlap is a separate conversation.
2. Plan the highest-priority project first, then stagger lower-priority projects around it.
3. Always add explicit buffer time for recruiting and scope shifts — this is a repeated, deliberate practice, not an afterthought.
4. Present the roadmap as a directional planning tool, not a fixed commitment; revisit and reprioritize as new initiatives arrive.
5. Use the roadmap itself as a diagnostic tool: if it's dominated by one method (e.g., usability testing), that's a signal to rebalance toward foundational research.
