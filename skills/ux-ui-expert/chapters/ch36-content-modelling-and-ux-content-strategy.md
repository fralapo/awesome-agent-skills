# Chapter 36: Content Modelling and UX Content Strategy

## Core Idea
Content shapes the interface. Design that ignores content structure fails late, because real content is longer, shorter or missing compared with the placeholder text the layout was built around. Model content before deciding layout, keep it working over time, and treat the tools and process used by the people who maintain it as a user experience problem too. Extends ch19 (two-step content structuring, worst-case content), ch21 (UX writing) and ch29 (content-first site build).

## Principles
- **You do not need every word to start, only the structure and size ranges** (short or long, big titles or small subtitles).
- **Bring content strategy in at the start**, before design and development; do not ask the client for content at the last minute.
- **Design for variance**: write rules that survive longer, shorter and missing content (truncation, fallbacks, optional blocks).
- **Ground decisions in what the visitor needs to find and do**, not in what the business wants to say. Check messaging against user needs.
- **A site is a system maintained over time**, not a launch-day deliverable like a printed magazine that is "finished" at print.
- **Editorial tools are UX**: the authors and editors who maintain the site are users; their workflow, templates and approvals deserve design.

## Content Model
For each content type define:
1. Purpose and audience.
2. Expected size and shape (length ranges, number of items, required vs optional parts).
3. Source (who or what produces it) and owner (who keeps it current).
Treat the model as building blocks or specs given to designers before they design; layouts then fit the model, not the other way round.

## Engagement Shape (typical sequence, example of 6-8 weeks starting before design)
1. **Content audit** of existing material: what exists, what is outdated or duplicated.
2. **Content model** per type.
3. **Copy guidelines** for writers, with an editorial style guide for voice, tone and word choice tied to the brand (ch21).
4. **Migration** of content into the new structure.
5. **Liaison role** between content, design and development so the three stay aligned.

## Procedure
1. Audit what exists and what visitors need to find or do.
2. List the content types; fill in purpose, audience, size, source, owner.
3. Hand the model to design; test layouts with the shortest, longest and a missing example.
4. Write guidelines and the style guide; assign owners.
5. Design the maintenance workflow for the editors.
6. Review content after launch on a schedule.

## Mistakes
- Pixel-perfect pages around placeholder text that real content breaks.
- Asking for content at the last minute.
- Letting the client decide the messaging without checking user needs.
- Treating the website as finished at launch; no owner after go-live.

## Checklist
- [ ] Every content type has purpose, audience, size range, source, owner.
- [ ] Layouts tested with shortest, longest and missing content.
- [ ] Style guide covers voice, tone, word choice.
- [ ] Editors' workflow considered as a user journey.
- [ ] Named owner for post-launch upkeep.
